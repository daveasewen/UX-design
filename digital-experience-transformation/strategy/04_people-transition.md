# People & redundancy transition

*DRAFT v0.1 for review. The honest part. This needs your judgement and your HR/People partners more than any other file here.*

---

## Executive summary

This transformation reduces the number of pure-production design roles. Pretending otherwise would discredit the whole case. But the honest, defensible position is not "AI lets us cut the team" — it is "**we are moving the team up the value chain, and that means a different, smaller, more senior shape, with a real plan for the people affected.**" Some production roles **convert** to higher-value roles (canon steward, criteria author, experience engineer, governance lead); some people will be **better served elsewhere**, and that should be handled with transparency, time and genuine support, not silent attrition. The single biggest mistake to avoid is cutting the scarce capabilities — research and judgment — to preserve production headcount. That is the transformation in reverse.

## Principles for handling it well
1. **Honesty over euphemism.** Name the reduction in production roles plainly. People can tell when a "transformation" is a layoff in costume; trust is the currency the whole change runs on.
2. **Convert first, exit last.** Map every affected person to a possible new role before treating any role as surplus. The new team needs curators, criteria authors, governors and engineers — many can come from within.
3. **Protect the scarce capabilities.** Research, judgment, systems-thinking and design-engineering are the *destination*. Never fund short-term savings by cutting them; that is cutting the future to pay for the past.
4. **Capability gates the change.** No role is changed or removed ahead of proven capability (see the roadmap). People changes follow evidence, in step, not in a single upfront cut.
5. **Support the exits properly.** Where someone is genuinely better placed elsewhere, give time, reskilling options, references, and a dignified process. How leaving people are treated is watched closely by those who stay.

## Conversion map (who can move where)
| From (production role) | Most natural new role(s) | Reskilling needed |
|---|---|---|
| UI / production designer | Experience designer & criteria author; canon steward | Writing success criteria as checks; judgement-at-the-gate; systems thinking |
| Design-system / component designer | Canon steward; content & tone governor | Governance mindset; token/gate fluency; "spend judgment once" discipline |
| Front-end-leaning designer | Experience / design-system engineer | Harness, gates, code-binding, generation pipeline |
| Researcher | Experience researcher (elevated) | Mostly growth, not reskill — moves to the front of the value chain |
| UX writer | Content & tone governor | Encoding tone-of-voice as enforceable checks |
| Design lead / manager | Experience lead / orchestrator; governance lead | Managing a system and outcomes rather than throughput |

Not everyone maps cleanly, and not everyone will want to. That is legitimate. The plan's job is to make the *opportunity to convert* real and well-supported, and the *exit* humane where conversion isn't right.

## The capability the team must net-gain
Even as total headcount falls, the team must **gain** capability in four areas it was thin on: **criteria authoring, canon governance, design-engineering, and research depth.** If the transition only subtracts, it has failed; the test is whether the team is *more* capable in the scarce skills afterwards, not just smaller.

## Risks to manage
- **Cutting the wrong half.** Trimming research/judgment to keep production is the most common and most damaging error. Guard against it explicitly in every phase.
- **Reskilling as theatre.** A reskilling promise with no real runway, training budget or role to land in is worse than honesty. Fund it or don't claim it.
- **Survivor trust collapse.** If exits are handled coldly, the people you most want to keep disengage. Process quality is retention strategy.
- **Morale during parallel-running.** Phase 2 runs old and new side by side; people can feel they are "training their replacement." Name this directly and frame it as building the system *they* will operate.

## What to say (and not say) to leadership
- **Do say:** the production-role reduction is real; here is the conversion map, the protected capabilities, the reskilling runway, and the support for exits.
- **Don't say:** "AI lets us do the same with fewer people." That is the shrink-only trap (`01`), and it concedes the team is just a cost. The line is: *we are building a more valuable, differently-shaped team — and treating the people in the transition properly is part of why it will work.*

## The framing that keeps it humane and credible
This is a **workforce transformation with a plan**, paced to proof, honest about cost, and built around moving people up rather than simply out wherever possible. It is also part of an **industry-wide structural shift** — design teams everywhere are becoming smaller, more senior and AI-augmented — so this is not a local failure to absorb, but a change to lead deliberately and decently.
