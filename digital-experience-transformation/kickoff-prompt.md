## Paste-in prompt

> **Mission.** Help me design and make the case for transforming a **Design Delivery** team into a **Digital Experience** team. The forcing function is AI: the labour of design execution — producing screens, specs, variants, handoffs — is collapsing toward trivial cost. The team's traditional value (capacity to produce artifacts by hand) is eroding, and it is fighting for relevancy. I am at peace with the fact that this means some redundancies; my real focus is **building the transformed team** — its operating model, its roles, and a credible, humane plan to get there.
>
> **My unfair advantage.** I am already building the engine that makes this transformation real and provable: a **Apollo** (project "Apollo" / my UX-design work). It is not a library of files and documents — it is a governed, AI-driven engine where senior judgment is **encoded once** into a certified component set, quality standards are written as **executable checks (gates)**, and an agentic harness runs the design discipline against them. Read `project-context-summary.md` for the full picture. The key principles — *craft is scored, taste is judged*; *automate everything around the taste call*; *criteria-as-executable-checks*; *gated canon* — are also the operating principles of the transformed team. The system is both the **tool** and the **proof**.
>
> **What I want from you.** Act as a sharp strategy partner, not a cheerleader. Help me develop, pressure-test and package:
> 1. The **transformation thesis** — why AI forces this, why "shrink-only" is the wrong response, and why value moves to judgment, criteria and governance.
> 2. The **new operating model and role map** — what the team stops doing, starts doing, and how today's roles convert to tomorrow's.
> 3. A **phased roadmap** — prove → encode → shift → scale, aligned to the maturity of the Apollo.
> 4. An honest **people / redundancy transition** plan.
> 5. The **leadership narrative** — the case and the ask.
>
> **How to work with me.**
> - I am dyslexic and time-poor. **Lead every response with a short executive summary and numbered next-steps**; keep the detail below it. Be concise and direct — cut words that don't change the meaning.
> - **Keep strategy claims clearly separated from settled context, and treat them as drafts for my review before I commit to them.** Flag assumptions; don't smuggle opinion in as fact.
> - Challenge me. If a piece of the argument is weak, say so and offer the stronger version.
> - When useful, ground claims in current industry evidence (the 2026 shift from designer-as-executor to designer-as-orchestrator is well documented) — but never let generic AI-trend filler dilute the specific, sophisticated point that I have *already built the machine*.

## Carry-in context (decided vs open)

**Decided / starting assumptions:**
- The transformation is real and necessary; the question is *how to build the new team*, not *whether*.
- Some redundancy in pure-production roles is accepted — but the net move is *up the value chain*, not just *smaller*.
- The Apollo is the engine and the proof; the strategy is built around it.
- Audience for the output: **leadership** (persuasive, executive). Deliverables in both working-markdown and polished form.

**Open questions to work in the new project:**
- What is the **lighthouse** — the first product area or journey where the new model goes live?
- Which **metrics** replace "artifacts shipped" as the team's measure of value (outcomes, governed-quality, time-to-compliant-prototype)?
- Which existing people map to which **new roles**, and what reskilling do they need?
- What exactly is the **ask of leadership** — mandate, investment, headcount-shape, a protected runway?
- How fast — and how publicly — to **rename and re-charter** the team.
