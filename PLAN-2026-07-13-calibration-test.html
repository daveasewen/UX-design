03

The two experiments

One tests which generation approach wins; the other tests how much the engine needs to be told.

Experiment 1 — which approach wins

Same screen, same criteria contract, three generation approaches, judged blind against the shipped design. Settles your open question with data on real, external material — not payments, not a vibe. Arm (b) is your lead; (c) earns more attention only if it beats (b).

Experiment 2 — how much to tell it

Hold the screen and the winning arm constant; feed progressively richer input. If quality jumps most between brief→research, the leverage is discovery scaffolding; if between research→wireframe, it's composition/layout — which points straight at the layout-tier build. Directly tests "richer substrate → better strict runs."

04

Method & discipline

Four rules that keep the result honest.

Blind, or it's worthless. Extract inputs and outputs separately; the shipped finals stay sealed until the judge step. Generation happens before the finals are ever opened (or in an isolated subagent that can't see them).

Render, then look. Every prior experiment's fatal flaw was scoring on structural greps, never a rendered picture. A minimal Playwright screenshot step runs before any arm is judged — this is R3, promoted from "later phase" to test prerequisite.

Isolated arms. Each of the three arms is generated cold from the same signed contract, none seeing the others — no anchoring toward a shared mean (the §9 machinery, used for real).

Judge on three axes, ideally two sets of eyes. Organising idea · brand read · would-ship. Your design eye is the bar; a second reviewer (the project's original designer, if reachable) makes the calibration verdict defensible.
