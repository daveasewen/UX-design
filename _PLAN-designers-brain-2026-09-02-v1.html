The designer's brain

A plan for putting design principles, laws and legal requirements into Apollo's knowledge graph — assembled from two filed research reports, so that Apollo can defend a choice and not only make one.

Nothing on this page is ruled

Every recommendation below is attributed to the report that made it — “R1 recommends…”, “R2 recommends…”. Twelve questions are open and they are Dave's; they are collected in section 8. This page describes a proposed schema; it does not create one, and no knowledge-graph file is touched by it.

Goal worked back

What a node must carry

Working back from “Apollo defends a choice, not just makes one”, the research says a principle node needs eight fields — and a node missing any one of them cannot support a defence.

  1. Our-words statement. Every one of the 145 rows is written in the lane's own words, ≤25 words, machine-checked with 0 violations. This is also what makes a licensed source usable at all. R1 §2 · assets/…/principle-register.json field statement
  2. Grade. R1 recommends the evidence grade as a first-class field so retrieval can rank and refuse (Q1a) — “a graph without it will let Parkinson's Law outrank Fitts”. R1 states plainly that the letters A/B/C/D/L are the conductor's working labels and the names are Dave's. R1 RULING-SHAPED Q1 · field grade, grade_alt, grade_reason
  3. Scope conditions. Fitts is derived from “rapid aimed movement… tapping and disc-transfer tasks, not reading or decision tasks” — the condition is the difference between a law and a slogan. R1 register field scope_conditions
  4. Tension edges carrying the mediating variable. 30 tensions, each with the variable that resolves it; 21 carry an Apollo touch. R1 leaves “edges or nodes?” explicitly undecided (Q4, no recommendation — schema was fenced from that lane). R1 §3 · assets/…/tensions.json field mediating_variable · R1 Q4
  5. Legal fence. R1 idea 3: pair every persuasion node with its legal node. Tension tn-19 is the case — “the persuasion literature is a toolkit for exactly the behaviours Article 25 prohibits”. R1 §5b idea 3 · tensions.json tn-19
  6. superseded_by / author_revised. R1 finding 7: RFC 9413 walks back Postel; NN/g's own page calls the F-pattern “bad for users and businesses”; Norman replaced affordance with signifier. “The register needs this field or it will ship 1988 forever.” R1 finding 7 · §5b idea 2
  7. Licence. R1 idea 5: the graph must know it may not emit text from a CC BY-NC-ND or proprietary source. Tension tn-30 is licence-vs-generation chain. R1 §5b idea 5 · §1 licence flags · tensions.json tn-30
  8. Expiry / re-check. R1 idea 13, citing s129-D5 (conclusions are debt): “Cowan superseded Miller in 2001 and the field did not notice for twenty years; a graph with no expiry will do the same.” R1 §5b idea 13 · finding 8

Two fields already exist in the register and are not on the brief's list: known_misreadings (Fitts “read as ‘make buttons big’… it predicts TIME, not preference”) and refutation_probe (what was searched for to try to break the row). Both are populated across all 145 rows; whether they survive into the node shape is part of question 1's answer. assets/2026-09-02-236-R1-principles-survey/principle-register.json

2.4 · Five headline tensions

Thirty were mapped; these five are the ones the phases below turn on. The mediating variable is the product — not the verdict.

#Pulls againstMediating variableHow it resolvesApollo touch
tn-01 Jakob's Law ↔ Von Restorff Layer. Convention governs MECHANISM (where things are, how they behave); distinctiveness governs SIGNAL (which single element carries the moment). Also brand mandate. Never trade them at the same layer. Diverge on signal, converge on mechanism. A brand that diverges on mechanism pays in error rate. s151-D1 two-red law: one signal colour, background-keyed. The law is a Von Restorff budget with a legal contrast floor attached.
tn-14 Miller's 7±2 ↔ Cowan's four chunks Neither applies. Both are recall-capacity claims and a visible menu is a recognition task. Cite chunking (the mechanism) for grouping decisions and cite neither number for item counts. Use scanning cost instead. Bento module counts (s217-D2/s217-D5) should be justified by grouping and scanning, NOT by 7±2.
tn-19 DSA Art. 25 ↔ social proof · goal-gradient · loss aversion Truth and reversibility. A real count shown honestly is persuasion; a fabricated or non-reversible one is prohibited design. Every persuasion node in the brain needs a paired legal node. Never let a B-grade effect be retrieved without its L-grade fence. A concrete argument for the graph carrying grade L at all — the brain must know “wise” from “mandatory”.
tn-25 Anti-preselection ↔ good defaults Consent vs convenience, and reversibility. A default on a data-protection choice is unlawful; a default on units or theme is a kindness. Defaults are legal for preferences, prohibited for consent and for anything that costs the user money. Grade the SLOT, not the mechanism. A generator that mints defaults needs this distinction encoded, or it will pre-tick a marketing consent by symmetry with a theme toggle.
tn-27 Evaluator effect ↔ heuristic scores used as a rubric Mechanical vs inferential. Mechanical checks (contrast, target size, semantics) are reliable; judgement checks are not. Rank on mechanical criteria and report judgement criteria as flagged observations, never as a score. s234-D2 factory goal and s234-D6 the one path-taking gate. R1 calls this the strongest reason the rubric must be split by check type.

06

Externalities

Five forces this programme does not control

Regulation reaches design directly, licences decide what may be stored at all, positioning constrains what may be said in public, one human ruler binds the schedule, and the field itself keeps revising the sources.

1 · Regulation, arriving as a market driver

R1 finding 9: the FCA's PRIN 2A names four outcomes — “product governance, price and value, consumer understanding and supporting consumers”. Consumer understanding and consumer support are design outcomes with an enforcement mechanism behind them, and Apollo's clients are exactly the firms bound by them. DSA Article 25 prohibits interfaces that deceive; the European Accessibility Act sits behind EN 301 549. Twenty-seven of the 145 rows are legal. R1 finding 9 · §1 families 7–12 · principle-register.json grade L = 27

2 · Licence owners

Three families can only ever be pointers: Yablonski's Laws of UX (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — no derivatives, non-commercial), NN/g (“All Rights Reserved”), Baymard (proprietary/subscription). Separately, the SDLC playbook R2 read is Anthropic's copyrighted publication — R2 borrowed its structure and quoted it in ≤15-word receipts, which is the same discipline the principle register uses. R1 §1 licence flags · R1 Q5 · R2 §1

3 · The positioning rule

Apollo's public copy stays abstract and never describes gate or token mechanics. Anything from this programme that reaches a client-facing surface — a claim about grades, a compliance harness, an eval suite — is subject to that rule before it is subject to this plan. standing positioning rule · echoed at R2 idea 10

4 · One human ruler

R2's non-transfer 1: the playbook routes every governance decision to a different person — seven roles. Here they collapse onto one. R2's non-transfer 8 draws the consequence: the constraint is Dave's eye, which “is not a process step waiting to be automated — it is the acceptance function of the product”. Decision load, not tokens, is what binds this programme's schedule. R2 §3 non-transfers 1 and 8

5 · The field's own drift

R1 finding 7: RFC 9413 (IETF, 2023) walks back Postel's robustness principle; NN/g's own page calls the F-shaped pattern “bad for users and businesses”; Norman replaced his most-quoted use of “affordance” with “signifier”; and Cowan's 2001 correction to Miller went unnoticed for twenty years (finding 8). Sources move after import, which is why the expiry field is an externality and not a nicety. R1 findings 7 and 8 · families 30, 32

From R1 — the principles survey

  1. Does the evidence grade go into the graph at all?
    • (a) Yes — a first-class field on every principle node, so retrieval can rank and refuse.
    • (b) No — keep the grades in the report and import only what survives a manual cull.

    R1 recommends (a), because the grade is the product of that lane and a graph without it “will let Parkinson's Law outrank Fitts”. R1 adds that the letters A/B/C/D/L are its own working labels — the names are Dave's. receipt: R1 RULING-SHAPED Q1

  2. Is “legal” a separate node type rather than a grade?
    • (a) A fifth rung on one ladder.
    • (b) A distinct “requirement” type that a principle can never outrank.

    R1 recommends (b): 27 rows are legal obligations, and the ladder metaphor implies they can lose an argument. They cannot. receipt: R1 RULING-SHAPED Q2 · finding 3

  3. Do the discredited “grade D” principles enter at all?
    • (a) Exclude them.
    • (b) Include them as inoculation — nodes whose purpose is to fire when someone cites the folk rule, carrying the refutation.

    R1 recommends (b): “someone will say ‘three clicks’ in a client meeting and the brain should have Porter's data ready.” receipt: R1 RULING-SHAPED Q3 · the 9 D rows in §2.3 above

  4. Are tensions edges or nodes?
    • (a) Typed edges between two principles, carrying the mediating variable.
    • (b) Their own node type — several tensions are triples, and several carry an Apollo ruling as the resolution.

    R1 makes no recommendation. It states this is a schema question and schema was fenced from that lane. no recommendation receipt: R1 RULING-SHAPED Q4

  5. Which source families enter first?
    • Safe now — GOV.UK (OGL), W3C / WCAG / APG / COGA, EN 301 549, FCA, DSA, EDPB, and every peer-reviewed primary (facts, not text).
    • Pointer-only — Laws of UX (ND licence), NN/g, Baymard.

    R1 recommends starting with the legal set plus the A/B primaries: “highest value, lowest licence risk, smallest count.” receipt: R1 RULING-SHAPED Q5 · source-register.json licence column

    • The 12 touchpoints in R1 §5a are proposals only; every id was confirmed present in _rulings.json by name.
