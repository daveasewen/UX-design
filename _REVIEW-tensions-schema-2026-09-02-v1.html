The answer, before the argument

01

Settling the 30 costs you nothing at the grill and 21 lines of declaration.

Six of the thirty are settled by a legal obligation, so there is nothing to weigh. Three are already answered by a ruling you made. The remaining twenty-one all take a conservative standing answer the factory can reach on its own — none of them needs a new standard question, and four ask one only when a named trigger fires.

The cost lands somewhere else, and it is the finding: the "Defaults used:" line the grill writes today was built for six answers and would carry twenty-one. Question fatigue is not the risk. Declaration fatigue is.

What "settled by an obligation" means

An obligation is a rule written by someone outside this house — a WCAG success criterion, a DSA article, an FCA rule. s237-D2 makes it a node type that no principle can outrank. So when one side of a tension is an obligation, the tension is not a trade and there is nothing to weigh: the obligation wins by construction. What is left is not a choice but a route — the mediating variable stops being the thing that decides the winner and becomes the thing that decides how you comply. Brand does not lose to contrast; brand changes size, weight or ground until it complies.

The sort is sensitive to its own rule, and this matters more than the counts. R-RESOLVED reads only how_it_resolves. If it also read the apollo_touch field, six more tensions would close and the count would become 6 · 9 · 15.

Four of those six would close wrongly. apollo_touch conflates three different links: tn-22 and tn-02 genuinely name a ruling that resolves them, but tn-01's s151-D1 explains the tension (s237-D5, advisory), tn-14's and tn-11's rulings are challenged by the tension, and tn-08's s135-D1 says in its own words that the shell is ruled and the routing rule is not. A generator that reads one link type where there are three would silently close four open questions.

What each reading actually buys — both sides

Edges are better at
  • The commonest query. "What pulls against X" is one hop with no join.
  • Fewer objects. 24 edges against 30 nodes plus 50 party links.
  • No second store. No new retrievable node type means a tension can never be quoted on its own as if it were a ruling — consequence (a) becomes structurally impossible rather than merely mitigated.
  • No status field to maintain. Nothing to let rot.
  • It fits 17 of the 30 exactly, which is a real majority.
Nodes are better at
  • Arity. One triple and one four-party row survive untouched; edges fan them to 3 and 6.
  • Parties that are not principles. 13 of 30, including a ruling as a party.
  • Typed ruling links, which s237-D5 needs and which stop four open questions being closed by accident.
  • Sides. "Three persuasion principles against one article" stays one side against one.
  • One home for one sentence — no copied mediating variable.
  • Cost: a status field, which must be derived (s237-D9), never typed.

Knowable is a keyword match of the mediating variable against the grill's six questions, plus a named standing rule where the answer is a property of the artefact or the pipeline rather than of the client.

Disposition is a two-clause test: a conservative standing answer exists and a wrong answer is recoverable in the edit pass. Both true → default and declare. True but with a named trigger → conditional. Neither → a new grill question.

Four rows are reached by no grill question at all — tn-08, tn-21, tn-22, tn-26 — and two of those still default, because the answer lives in a ruling you already made (tn-22 → s217-D8) or in a check on the artefact.

More problems than we solve — replayed

05

Eight ways this goes wrong, each with the thing that stops it.

A

A second rulings store

Thirty retrievable judgement objects beside 321 rulings is a place to look up "what does Apollo think" that is not the rulings store.

Stops it: point, never restate — the tension holds an id, the ruling holds the words (ADR-0017); status is DERIVED, never typed (s237-D9); retrieval returns the ruling first.

Owner — whoever writes the retrieval layer

B

A default that pre-empts consent

tn-25 is the live one. "Good defaults help people" and "never pre-tick" differ by consent, not by mechanics — a generator that treats a marketing consent like a theme toggle will pre-tick it by symmetry. DSA Article 25 is the line.

Stops it: the derivation already puts tn-25 in the obligation bucket, so the factory is never allowed to default there. Grade the slot, not the mechanism.

Owner — the generator's default-minting path

C

The grill grows by thirty

The failure this lane was told to avoid.

Stops it: ask-when. Derived count: 0 new standing questions, 4 conditional. But the real growth landed on the other side — 21 default lines in a "Defaults used:" field designed for six. Cap that, or the declaration becomes the thing nobody reads.

Owner — the grill skill, unresolved

D

Split edges that disagree

tn-03 becomes three edges and tn-19 six, each carrying a copy of one mediating variable. Copies drift.

Stops it: the node reading, or — if edges win — one canonical edge with the others generated from it, never hand-written.

Owner — the schema decision itself

E

"Tension" and "default" already mean things

Probe: 43 hits. In s143-D1 four $status tension flags were downgraded from TENSION to CROSS-REFERENCE — "they are not conflicts". In the usage entities, a tension is an unreconciled disagreement between two source documents, queued for settlement. R1's tensions are neither: they are permanent pulls between two true things.

"Default" carries two senses already — the shipped starting value inside a ruled option set (s219-D3) and the grill's value-used-because-you-skipped. A factory default for a tension would be a third.

Stops it: pick different words before anything is generated. This is the s202 class and it costs one decision now, or a rebuild later.

Owner — Dave, before P1

F

A default that contradicts the pack

Probed against the shipped rules: tn-22's default restates s217-D8 rather than contradicting it; tn-15/tn-16 route through s151-D1 and s116-D1 as the obligation's compliance path, not against them.

Stops it: no proposed default may restate a ruling — it must point. Unproven: the 17 default-and-declare rows were checked against the rulings named in tensions.json, not against every rule in apollo-spider/.

Owner — a gate at generation time

G

An immortal open tension

An open thing with no default and no ask-when never closes.

Stops it: every one of the 21 rows carries a close condition — a default, or a named trigger. "never" in the ask-when column means closed by the default, not unclosable. A row with neither should fail the build.

Owner — the tension generator

H

Hand-typed tensions are a copy chain

s234-D1: the graph is generated, never copied into.

Stops it: name the generator. tensions.json is the source; the two scripts in this lane's assets are the working shape of it — a sort keyed on the principle register's own grade and on _rulings.json membership, re-derived on every build. It already found a dangling party id (pr-info-scent) that a hand-typed import would have carried in silently.

Owner — the P1 build

Ruling-shaped — your call, not this lane's

06

Five decisions. Nothing below is decided.

Question 1

Edges or nodes?

(a) a typed edge between two principles, carrying the mediating variable · (b) its own node type with N parties and typed links out.

Recommend (b), nodes — because 13 of the 30 have a side that is not a principle at all and would produce no edge, and because the single untyped ruling slot an edge affords would silently close four open questions.

Question 2

Who settles the open ones?

(a) you sit down and rule 21 · (b) derived defaults, and you rule only the ask-when list · (c) derived defaults with no ruling at all until one bites.

Recommend (b) — it puts 4 decisions in front of you instead of 21, and the four are the ones where a wrong default is expensive.

Question 3

Does "tension" keep its name?

The word already means a downgradeable conflict flag in this house (s143-D1) and an ingestion disagreement in the usage entities.

Recommend renaming — the s202 "switch" case says a vocabulary collision is cheapest to fix before anything is generated.

Question 4

Does the declaration get a cap?

21 default lines would join a "Defaults used:" field built for six. (a) declare all · (b) declare only the ones that bent away from the conservative side · (c) declare on demand.

Recommend (b) — a declaration nobody reads is the same failure as a question nobody answers.

Question 5

Which field carries a ruling link?

apollo_touch currently mixes resolves, explains (s237-D5), challenges and merely touches.

Recommend typed links, and a generator that refuses an untyped one — the sort moves from 6·3·21 to 6·9·15 on this single choice.
