Priority — a PROPOSAL, not a ruling

My proposed order, and the weights it came from

You ruled that I generate the priorities and you overrule them. This is the generated half. It is computed at build time from the store, never written back into it, so it cannot rot and it cannot quietly become a decision. 5 criteria, each scored 0–1, combined with the declared weights below into a score out of 100. The weights themselves are a proposal too — change any number in the table and the order changes; nothing about them is ratified.

There used to be a sixth criterion, and you removed it. “Effort (inverse)” carried 0.15 of the weight and, with no real effort values in the store, its only input was the BYTE LENGTH of an item’s body text — a proxy for how much had been written about a job, not for how big the job is. Dropped on your #168 review (DC2). The remaining weights are your original ratios — 30 : 20 : 15 : 10 : 10 — divided by their own sum, so every criterion stands in exactly the relationship to every other that it did before; only the removed 0.15 left the denominator. It has since come back as a CONDITIONAL criterion — the block under the table — but the byte-length proxy has not, and will not: the criterion now has one input, and no input means no criterion.

Effort is back — as a rung measured in TOKENS, and only where you have written one. The unit is real Claude tokens of job window (boot and wrap excluded) — the unit this project already rules and logs, not t-shirt sizes and not bytes of prose. Delegated sub spend is excluded too: a sub’s tokens are weekly quota, not this window’s fill, and they are not in the logged blocks these edges come from — so an L that needs a sub is a claim about this window, never a total bill. They move as the log grows; that is why they are printed rather than stored.

A rung is your ESTIMATE against a measured standard — it is not a measurement of the job, and the page will never print it as one. Writing "effort": "M" on an item is a forecast; the edges it is judged against are the measured history. Two more things worth knowing before you author any: (1) absent is legal and costs nothing — an item with no rung has no effort criterion at all, and its remaining weights renormalize, so it is scored on what it has rather than penalised for what it lacks; (2) a rung changes the whole row’s weighting — with a rung present, effort takes 0.15 and your other five snap back to their absolutes (Unlock 0.30 / Rot risk 0.20 / Deadline 0.15 / Risk reduction 0.10 / Decision relief 0.10). So a partly-authored store ranks two populations under two weightings; that is honest per item, but it is not a clean comparison across items.
