3 · The read-side mechanism — one, designed to build-ready depth

The brief offered four candidates and said pick one. Picked: the consult step — a problem-domain query surface plus the pre-flight protocol that requires using it. The other three, and why not, at the end of this section.

DIAGNOSIS

Every failure the brief lists shares one shape: at the moment of design, nobody asked the record "what already governs this?" — because there is nothing to ask. The corpus is superbly ID'd (465 rules, T-D/R-D/DV-D rulings, ASSERT-00x, DEF-00x, ADRs, gates) but lives in nine-plus stores with grep as the only join. A rule that is written, correct, and unretrieved is indistinguishable from one that does not exist (§2 of the brief). So the mechanism must supply both halves: an answerable index (else asking is grep by another name) and a required moment of asking (else the index joins the pile of unretrieved correctness).

DESIGN

_consult.py — "what governs X?", answered in one step, with an enforcement path.

Part 1 — the index, generated so it cannot rot. _build_consult_index.py, wired into _build_all.py, emits knowledge/_consult-index.json from sources that all already exist:

record := { id, kind, text, file, status, bite }
  kind ∈ rule | ruling | assertion | gate | adr | defect | open-item
  sources:
    guidelines/_rules-index.json            → 465 rule records (id, destiny, text)
    _proforma/_TYPE|_RAG|_DATAVIZ-DECISIONS → ruling records (parse **T-D\d+ / R-D\d+ / DV-D\d+** + first sentence)
    _assertions.json                        → assertion records (claim + consequence + last-verified)
    _validate_*.py                          → gate records: docstring line 1 + the gate's GLOB(s)
                                              → bite = the resolved file list ("where this rule actually applies")
    docs/decisions/ADR-*                    → adr records (title + status)
    _DS-IMPROVEMENTS / _ICON-GAPS           → defect records
    _LIVE-STATE OPEN entries                → open-item records (heading line only)

Part 2 — the query. python3 knowledge/_consult.py "amber indicator on white" → ranked governing records grouped as: rulings first, then blocking rules, advisory rules, assertions, open items — and for every rule, its enforcement state: gated (by which gate, over which files) or asserted only. That last column is the gate-glob audit answered per-query instead of as a separate campaign — the {#type26-019} failure becomes visible at the moment someone consults, not weeks later. Matching = keyword over text + a small hand-authored synonym lexicon (_consult-lexicon.json: "ochre→amber", "leading→line-height", "webfont→woff") that grows one line each time a query misses — curation is part of the job.

Part 3 — the protocol (the required moment of asking). One new step at the top of _RUNBOOK-gated-component.md and _RUNBOOK-compose-from-canon.md, and in GOOD-MORNING §A's bite-rules: before designing, consult; paste the receipt (query + retrieved ids) into the work's review sheet or meta. Enters at the advisory tier per AGENTS principle 5; the promotion path to blocking is a presence-check gate (_validate_consult.py: new/changed review sheets and metas must carry a consult receipt) once the tool has earned trust by being bite-tested.

Part 4 — the bite test, built in. --selftest replays the three 07-18 failures as regression queries; the tool fails its own build step if any misses its known answer:

"amber glyph contrast white"   must surface the 1.4.1 label-carries-meaning waiver + R-D3
"inline fonts portability"     must surface the delivery-=-link ruling (T-D9 block) + the portable-unit ruling
"univers sandbox render"       must surface ASSERT-002/006

Reuse, not rebuild: the enforcement half of the receipt check extends _trace_knowledge_usage.py's claimed-vs-actual cross-match technique; the index generator reuses _rules-index.json and the assertions register as-is. Sequencing: the index + query + selftest are throughput work to this spec — Sonnet, one session, per MODEL-ROUTING. The lexicon's first ~30 entries and the runbook step are judgment — they ride along with the enactment of this sheet. This is also the seed of the chat-to-KB bot target: same index, same join, conversational surface later — the read side built once, used twice.

WHY NOT THE OTHER THREE

Gate-glob coverage audit — real, but enforcement-side: it audits rules already wired into gates, and it is already §7 candidate #2 on the Fable list (my read: Opus-shaped, and partially answered per-query by the consult tool's bite column). Assertion re-test dates — too narrow: today's ASSERT-001 finding shows the live failure is stale consequence prose behind a passing predicate; the fix for that is a small amendment (see §5 note), not this session's one mechanism. Problem-domain index alone — an index nobody is required to consult joins the corpus of unretrieved correctness; the trigger is half the mechanism. The consult step is the index plus its trigger, which is why it is one design and not two sketches.
