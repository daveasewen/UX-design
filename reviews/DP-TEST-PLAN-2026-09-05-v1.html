Method

Careful means one group at a time, and the control re-run after each

The #246 finding was that four dashboards from two models were the same specimen — the library dictated the result. So the experiment is on the library, and the model is held constant: Fable, the same prompt, blind and sighted arms as before. Each wave lands ONE group at its ONE landing source (the enactment table's rule: additive only), gates run, and P0 is re-run. The score delta between wave n−1 and n is that group's effect, alone. A group that moves nothing across two waves is a candidate to drop — Dave's call, named in the wrap.

Conflicts are the thing Dave said he is wary of. Every wave keeps a conflict ledger: any pair of principles that pulled in opposite directions on the same tile, with the file and line. Three pairs are already suspected and are listed under Ideas.

What a wave is NOT
A wave does not touch a lane report, a ruling, a constant or a version. It does not re-word a principle. It does not fix a defect the group did not name — that is a finding for the next wave, not a side edit.
What a wave IS
One landing edit · gates at four bands · P0 re-run, both arms · the 29-row sheet filled with one fact per row · the six seeds pass/fail · a conflict ledger · a one-line verdict: moved / did not move.

DP-17 · three-way

Test all three, then Dave picks

s246-D7 rules a test, not an option. The design: after W2 has landed DP-14/15/16/18, three lanes build P0 sighted, differing only in what DP-17 is — (a) a named TASTE chord in the rails · (b) an ADVISORY line with the display bento as the worked example · (c) absent, DP-14/15 alone carrying the rhythm. The three renders at 1440 light are stripped of any arm label and handed to a fourth lane, a judge who has not read this page, with one instruction: rank the three walls for order, and say in one line what ordered them. Dave then sees the ranking, the judge's line, and only then the arm key.

What would make the test fail
Three identical walls — DP-17 changed nothing, and (c) wins by parsimony. Or the judge's line names something other than count and break — the rhythm is coming from DP-14/15 and DP-17 is a restatement.
What would make it decisive
The judge ranks (a) or (b) first and names the odd count or the one break unprompted. That is the taste, seen by an eye that was not told to look for it.

Other ideas

Six things worth adding, none of them ruled

  • A blind judge on every wave, not just DP-17. The conductor who briefed the wave should not score it. A judge lane gets the render and the score sheet and nothing else; ≈ 60K quota per wave.
  • The conflict ledger, seeded. Three pairs to watch from day one: DP-06 (one compact row) vs DP-14 (one dominant tile) when a KPI is the evidence the role came for · DP-20 (three signals above the fold) vs DP-10 (attention counts) on a bad morning · DP-02 (collapsed filters) vs DP-13 (declared consumer) when the consumer is off-screen. A conflict is recorded, never resolved by the lane.
  • Opus as the second eye at W7 only. The GPT proxy costs 347K a lane. One run at the end tells whether the library's fix survives a different model; running it every wave would only re-prove #246.
  • A "which sentence did it" probe. Once P1 (sentence one alone) scores level with P0, drop sentence two from the demo prompt. The demo is stronger if the library, not the prompt, carries interactivity.
  • Persist the sheet as data. Each wave writes notes/_dp-scores/<wave>.json in the score-sheet shape; a tiny generator renders the trend table on this page at W7. No number typed twice.
  • Freeze early. Dave's cold VS run (W8) is the demo condition; the last enact wave before it is the version the demo ships. Anything W8 finds is fixed as a patch, and nothing new lands after 09-16.
