The capsule correction — three readings

One sentence from Dave supports three different rules. They do not ship the same page. All three are drawn below, live, so the choice can be made by eye rather than by argument.

Console theme. Every specimen is shown in light and dark, side by side. The markup and the stylesheet under every specimen are copied from the approved page — nothing here is re-drawn, and only the dials differ between one specimen and the next.

Nothing on this page is enacted. No rule, no manifest and no generator has been changed.

The card he rejected

“This would never exist”

“almost right, this would never exist. its ether cohesive capsule or rounded full image if there is no background colour on the caption.”

Dave, 2026-08-26, on the Capsule — White card of the bento rails page.

Here is that card, exactly as it stands on the page today.

The card as it stands: a capsule whose caption is white, on a page that is also white

Console gallery · capsule · caption ground white · page ground the page itself

What you are looking at

  • In light the caption’s white and the page’s white are the same colour, so the bottom half of the capsule has nothing to draw it. The picture reads as rounded on top and cut off square at the bottom, and the caption floats loose underneath.
  • In dark the same card is fine. There the caption ground and the page ground are different colours, so the capsule draws all the way round. The defect is light-mode only — and that is already a fact about how wide the fix ought to be.
  • The capsule is doing its job. It is being asked to draw an edge in a colour that is already there.

And this is why the readings matter. His sentence names a condition (“if there is no background colour on the caption”) and an answer (“either cohesive capsule or rounded full image”). Which of the two he is ruling on — the colour, the condition, or the shape — changes what gets built, how far it reaches, and whether white survives at all.

Reading A

White leaves the capsule’s list of caption grounds

Take white off the list. The console gallery’s capsule keeps two caption grounds and no others: the lightest grey, and the dark grey with its text reversed out. A caption with no ground of its own stops being the capsule’s problem — it belongs to the other chord, the rounded picture with the caption bare beneath it.

This is the reading the receipt already wrote down, and it is unconditional: white is gone from the capsule on every page ground, in both modes, with no exceptions to compute.

What survives

The lightest grey — today’s default, unchanged

The dark grey — the ramp’s dark step, text reversing out on its own

capsule · caption ground dark grey · page ground the page itself

Look at the dark cell. In dark, this caption ground and the page ground behind it are the same colour. So this capsule has no edge either — rounded on top, cut off square at the bottom, caption floating loose. It is the picture at the top of this page, in the member Reading A keeps, shipping today as a ruled part of the chord.

Measured in the browser, not judged by eye: caption ground and page ground both resolve to the same value in console dark.

Where a ground-less caption goes instead

Not to a capsule. To the other chord, which already exists and is already ruled: four rounded corners on the picture, and the caption bare beneath it on whatever is behind.

The rounded picture — the caption has no ground and does not need one

rounded corners · caption ground none · page ground the page itself

What it costs

  • The white caption ground stops existing for the console capsule. There is no page you can put it on to get it back.
  • The chord goes from three grounds to two.
  • It takes away a ground that is correct in dark and wrong only in light — while leaving in place a ground that is broken in dark. See the note under the dark-grey specimen above.
  • Nothing new has to be worked out at edit time. A list gets shorter, and that is the whole change.
  • The card at the top of this page becomes unbuildable — which is the plainest reading of “this would never exist”.

Reading B

White stays, and is refused only where it cannot be seen

Keep white on the list, and refuse the one pairing that breaks. A white caption is a legal capsule ground as long as the ground actually painting behind the wall is not itself white or the page. Where it is, the option is struck out at edit time and the reason is printed beside it.

There is already a rule of exactly this shape on the rails page: a caption cannot sit on its own colour. It compares the caption’s ground to the bento’s ground — and the bento’s ground here is transparent, so it has nothing to compare against and waves the pairing through. Widening it to compare against the ground that actually paints is the whole of this reading.

Legal under B

Put something behind the wall and the white caption has an edge again. This specimen is illegal under Reading A and perfectly good under Reading B — it is the single clearest place the two readings part company.

A white caption on a capsule, over a grey page ground

capsule · caption ground white · page ground lightest grey

And look at the dark cell. In dark, white and the lightest grey resolve to the same colour — so the pairing that is legal in light is broken in dark, and the capsule disappears again.

That is not a detail of this specimen. It means Reading B’s verdict is not a property of the pairing at all: it is a property of the pairing and the mode, and the rule has to be worked out twice.

Refused under B

Refused: the caption ground and the ground painting behind the wall are the same colour, so the capsule has no edge to draw. Pick another caption ground, or change the page ground underneath it.

A white caption on a capsule, over the page’s own ground

capsule · caption ground white · page ground the page itself · refused

The strike and the dimming are the refusal vocabulary the explorer already uses: weight, a line through it, and the reason in words. No colour carries the refusal.

What it costs

  • White stays available, and there are pages where it is the right answer — the grey-ground specimen above is one of them.
  • Legality becomes conditional. Whether a caption ground is offered now depends on a decision two levels up, which the person setting the dial is not looking at.
  • Something has to resolve the effective ground — caption, then wall, then page — and today nothing does. The existing rule stops one level short, which is exactly how this card got onto the page.
  • The card at the top of this page becomes a refusal rather than an impossibility: still wrong here, still fine one page ground over.
  • And the verdict flips between light and dark, measured on this page: the pairing refused above is fine in dark, and the pairing called legal above breaks in dark. Two verdicts per pairing, not one.

Reading C

A caption with no ground re-forms the whole tile

This reading is not about white at all. It takes “if there is no background colour on the caption” as the condition and “either cohesive capsule or rounded full image” as the complete answer: when a caption has no ground of its own, the tile may take exactly two shapes, and there is no third.

The first legal shape — the cohesive capsule

One capsule holds the picture and the caption together, and the caption is clipped inside it. The capsule is filled, so its edge is drawn all the way round.

Cohesive capsule — picture and caption clipped inside one filled capsule

capsule · caption ground lightest grey · page ground the page itself

The second legal shape — the rounded full image

The picture is rounded on all four corners and the caption sits bare beneath it, on whatever is behind. Nothing is clipped, and nothing pretends to be a capsule.

Rounded full image — the caption bare beneath it

rounded corners · caption ground none · page ground the page itself

The shape that would never exist

A capsule asked to hold a caption that has no ground. The clip rounds the top of the picture and the bottom of a strip you cannot see, so what reads is half a capsule and a caption floating below it. This is the same picture as the card at the top of this page, arrived at from the other direction.

Half a capsule, and a caption strip with nothing under it

capsule · caption ground none · page ground the page itself · the thing he says would never exist

What it costs

  • White is untouched. It stays a legal capsule caption ground everywhere, on every page ground.
  • The rule moves off the colour and onto the shape, so it covers cases nobody has hit yet — every future ground-less caption, not just this one.
  • It reaches wider than the console gallery. Ground-less captions exist in every theme, so this is a bigger ruling than the card that prompted it.
  • A proposed fence already says a capsule with no caption ground needs keylines turned on or it is not a capsule. Under C that fence stops being a separate rule and becomes a consequence of the two-shape law — one rule instead of two.

The sharp edge of Reading C, said plainly. Read strictly, C does not refuse the card at the top of this page. That caption has a background colour — white — so the condition never fires, and a white capsule on a white page stays legal and stays broken. C only catches it if “no background colour” is read as “no ground you can actually see”.

That is not a detail. It is the difference between C being the answer and C being a second, separate ruling that still leaves this card standing.

Measured, not asserted

The first thing the table says

In dark, white and the lightest grey are the same colour. The ramp has three steps in light and two in dark. Any rule written about “white” is therefore a rule about the lightest grey as well, in half the modes.

The second thing, and it is the sharper one

In dark, the dark-grey caption ground is exactly the page ground. So the dark-grey capsule — a ruled member of the chord, shipping today, and the member Reading A keeps — has no edge at all in dark. It draws the same broken picture as the card Dave rejected, one mode over, and nothing on the rails page says so.

What this does to the three readings. It is evidence that white is not the problem — the problem is a caption ground landing on the ground behind it, which happens to three different members in three different mode-and-page combinations. Reading A fixes one of them. Reading B fixes them per mode, and has to be computed twice. Reading C is the only one whose rule does not care which colour happened to collide.

Stated honestly: this is a finding, not a fourth reading, and not a recommendation. It is the same defect showing up in places the correction did not name.

Not decided here

One thing this page deliberately leaves alone

Mono’s access to the dark caption ground is expressly open, and is NOT decided by this page. It is open by ruling, not refused on taste, and none of the three readings above touches it.
