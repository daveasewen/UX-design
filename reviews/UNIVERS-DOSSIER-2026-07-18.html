The headline. Received wisdom says Univers is tight. Measured, this cut is looser than Helvetica, Arial, Calibri and Lato. Frutiger's famous "too tight" remark was about apertures — a different property, and one letter-spacing cannot fix.

4 · What it means

MEASURED

M1This cut is spaced looser than Helvetica, Arial, Calibri and Lato.

The n sidebearing is 15.6% of x-height; Arial is 12.4%, Calibri 13.5%, Lato 13.4%. Only DejaVu Sans — a face drawn deliberately open for low-resolution screens — is looser, at 15.7%. Relative to its own stroke weight the sidebearing/stem ratio is 0.90 against roughly 0.75 for the Helvetica-lineage faces.

For us: This weakens C1 on the contact sheet. I recommended +0.01em on 12px component labels on a crowding argument that silently assumed normally-spaced type. Univers already carries more air than Arial. The crowding case does not disappear — but it starts from a looser baseline than I assumed, and C1 should probably fall back toward +0.005em or zero.

MEASURED

M2Sidebearings barely move across the weight range. Stems increase sevenfold.

From Ultra Light to Bold the sidebearing falls 92 → 68 (−26%), while the stem goes 20 → 146 (+630%). So the sidebearing/stem ratio collapses 4.60 → 0.46: at Bold the space between letters is under half a stem, at Ultra Light it is over four stems.

For us: Tracking in this family is weight-dependent, and none of our rules account for that. Heavier weights are relatively far tighter and need more relief. Note where this lands: col26-020(c) mandates Medium for small reverse text, and Medium sits at 0.64 — already tight-side. Bloom then closes the apparent gap further. C2/C3 hold or strengthen; C1 weakens.

MEASURED

M3Kerning is present in every weight, and consistent. One real gap.

All six weights kern via GPOS extension lookups. Across ten common problem pairs the values track closely (Ta −73 to −119, LT −93 to −114). The one inconsistency: Fo is unkerned in Regular only (0, against −25 to −30 in every other weight). Digit pairs are unkerned throughout, which is correct for tabular figures.

For us: Not a systemic defect. The Fo gap is present in stock too (§5), so it is upstream, not an HSBC error. See the correction note below — my first pass reported this section very differently and was wrong.

RESEARCH

R1Frutiger's "too tight" was about apertures, not spacing.

The often-repeated line is that Frutiger found Univers "too tight" for airport signage and drew Frutiger in response. The sources are specific about what he meant: Univers had "too round and closed an effect for the easy recognition of word-signs". He wanted larger apertures — the openings in c, e, s, a — so letters would not blur into circles at a glance. Aperture is a counter property. It is not sidebearing.

For us: The negative finding, and the important one: tracking cannot fix this. Letter-spacing does not open a counter. The known weakness of Univers for glance-reading is not addressable by the lever we have been designing. Size, weight and the halation levers can help; tracking cannot. That should stop us reaching for tracking as a general legibility cure.

RESEARCH

R2Frutiger drew the same Editorial / Component line you did.

His judgement was that Univers was "perfect for printed books" but wrong for someone moving through an airport at 5 mph — continuous reading versus recognition at a glance. That is exactly the split you made: Editorial is read, Component is recognised.

For us: The typeface's own designer put Univers on the Editorial side of your line. Not a reason to abandon it — it is the brand face — but it means the Component tier is working against the grain of the design, and compensations there (size, weight, ground) are carrying more load than they would in a face drawn for glance-reading.

RESEARCH

R3Lineage: the real revision was 1997, not 2010.

Original Univers, Frutiger for Deberny & Peignot, 1957 — 21 coordinated variants on the two-digit grid. Linotype Univers (1997) was the substantial rework, done with Frutiger, in which stroke weights were revised for consistency within each face and between weights. Univers Next (2010) added true small caps and renamed the family; sources describe it as extension and rebranding rather than a spacing redesign.

For us: The stroke-weight consistency work in 1997 is visible in M2's clean progression. It also means "Univers" advice written before 1997 may not describe what we have.

5 · HSBC's cut vs stock Univers Next Pro — ANSWERED

Dave supplied Univers Next Pro mid-session, so this moved from "cannot answer" to measured. Six weight pairs, matched UltraLight–Thin–Light–Regular–Medium–Bold.

Horizontally, HSBC's cut IS stock Univers Next Pro. Not "close to" — identical, to the font unit.

• Sidebearings: 75 glyphs × 6 weights, LSB and RSB both compared. One glyph differs in the entire set.
• Advance widths: 82 glyphs × 6 weights. Same one glyph.
• Kerning: 10 problem pairs × 6 weights = 60/60 exact matches, value for value.
• Cap-height and x-height: identical to the unit at every weight.

The single differing glyph is the ampersand — redrawn for HSBC (ink 680×751 vs 664×738; right sidebearing −1 vs 15, so the brand ampersand runs right to its edge). A deliberate brand glyph, not a spacing change.
What this settles. Published Univers Next guidance on spacing applies to us directly — that is now an empirical finding, not an assumption. Everything in §1–3 describes stock Univers Next Pro as much as it describes our cut.

It also relocates the Fo defect. The unkerned Fo in Regular is present in stock too, at the same zero. It is an upstream Linotype/Monotype omission, not an HSBC error — so there is no point raising it with brand. Worth knowing before someone files it.
⚠ Where they genuinely differ: VERTICAL metrics. This is the one place published Univers advice will mislead us.

Both ship USE_TYPO_METRICS OFF, so the browser reads hhea. The line boxes are only 8% apart — but the baseline sits ~11 percentage points lower in the box in our cut, because HSBC put everything into ascent and dropped the lineGap to zero. That changes where text sits inside its line, which is exactly what the cap-trim and grid-slot work depends on. HSBC also extended the charset substantially (669 → 1011 glyphs).

A number I nearly got wrong: reading ascents alone (1068 vs 750) suggests a 30% difference. It is 8%, because stock carries a 200-unit lineGap that HSBC folded away. Recorded because the ascent figure is the one that looks decisive and is not.

6 · What this changes on the contact sheet

  1. C1 weakens — 12px component label, ordinary ground. I recommended +0.01em on a crowding argument that assumed normally-spaced type. This face is looser than Arial. Revised suggestion: +0.005em or zero, and I would not defend the higher number now.
  2. C2 / C3 hold, and arguably strengthen. They specify Medium, which measures 0.64 SB/stem — tight-side for this family — with bloom closing the gap further. Two compounding effects, now with a measured basis rather than practitioner lore.
  3. E1 strengthens — editorial display. A relatively loose face at 40px drifts apart more, not less. −0.02em stands, and the brand's −15/−30 range looks better-founded than it did.
  4. NEW — tracking may need to vary by WEIGHT, not just size and role. The SB/stem collapse from 4.60 to 0.46 is the largest single effect measured here, and nothing in the current rule sketch accounts for it. This is the open question I would take to a ladder next.
  5. A limit on the whole exercise. Univers' known glance-reading weakness is closed apertures. Tracking does not open a counter. Some of what we might want from tracking on the Component tier is simply not available from this lever, and size, weight and ground have to carry it.
