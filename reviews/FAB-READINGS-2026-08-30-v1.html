Corner zone

The overlays cannot reach inside the frames on file://. Chrome treats every file:// page as its own origin, so an overlay sitting on this page is not allowed to set data-apollo-theme on a dashboard inside an iframe — or to hover its components. Each FAB has fallen back to driving this page instead, which is why the page itself re-themes when you use one, and why the practice strip below is what the inspector can see. To drive the real dashboards, serve the repo over HTTP: cd <repo> && python3 -m http.server 8000, then open http://localhost:8000/reviews/FAB-READINGS-2026-08-30-v1.html.

The question on the page. The controls are the same in all three. What differs is how the FAB arrives — and that is the only thing being asked. Open each one, switch a theme, switch light/dark, turn Inspect components on and hover something.

What it is not. The overlay is never part of the design. It is one fixed layer with its own z-index, it changes no layout, and it leaves when the one <script> tag that loaded it leaves. The dashboards in these frames have not been edited.

Reading A

The FAB is simply there

How it appears: a 56 px disc in the bottom-right, present from the moment the page loads. Click it and the panel opens above it.

The trade-off: nothing to discover and nothing to teach — but you have permanently spent the bottom-right corner of every design you show, and it sits on top of whatever lives there.

Reading B

A hot corner reveals it

How it appears: nothing is drawn until the pointer enters a 72 px × 72 px zone in the bottom-right corner; then the FAB fades in. Move away and it fades out again. On touch there is no hover at all, so a tap in that same corner reveals it.

The trade-off: the design is clean until you go looking — but anyone you hand the file to has to be told where to look, once.

Reading C

Nothing at all, until you summon it

How it appears: it does not, until you double-press Shift — or, on touch, double-tap the bottom-right corner. Esc puts it away again.

The trade-off: presentation-grade — the screen is only the design, with no corner to avoid and nothing to find by accident. The cost is that the gesture has to be known, so this one is for you, not for a client clicking around on their own.

What is being asked. Which of the three, and what the corner should be. Nothing here is ruled — the 72 px zone in reading B is a proposal, and it is a single config value either way.
  • One file, three modes. All three panels load the same knowledge/_render/apollo-fab.js. The only difference between them is reveal: 'always' | 'hotcorner' | 'summon' and cornerSize. Whichever you pick is a config value in the script tag, not a rebuild: <script src="…/apollo-fab.js" data-reveal="hotcorner" data-corner="72"></script>.
  • Theme labels. The Common button still writes data-apollo-theme="legacy" — the label was renamed for the demo, the attribute value was not, because canon.css keys its cascade on that value. The rename is a separate lane.
  • The inspector. With the component map loaded it gives the component's name, category, purpose and token verdict; without it — the normal case on file:// — it still names every component from its cn-* class. The nameless path is the default, not a failure. Its highlight and tooltip are pointer-events:none at all times, so it never steals a click from the design underneath.
  • The corner is measured, not hit-tested. Readings B and C watch pointer coordinates rather than putting an invisible box in the corner, so even the hidden variants take nothing away from whatever sits under that corner of the design.
  • z-index. The layer sits at 2147483000, above the dashboard's own sticky masthead and above anything a generated page plausibly declares. Overridable per mount with data-z.
