WHAT CHANGED SINCE -v1 (which you approved by eye). Two things, and nothing else.
1. The thumb corners are tighter: 0 / 2 / 4 / 6, re-minted at s202-D2. The s199-D3 carve-out you asked for — “the switch, not the container” — turned out to BE this thumb; there is no separate two-state switch. Its radius no longer derives from the track inset, it derives from the dial you tuned: container − 6, floored at 0. At xs the thumb is now square (6 − 6 = 0).
2. The label sizes are on the real type scale: 12 / 14 / 16 / 16 (font-7 / font-6 / font-5 / font-5). The old 11px at xs was off-scale — no such size exists. These bindings are still PROPOSED: the sizes are real tokens, but nothing rules that a segmented label must take them.
Everything else — heights, insets, container radii, the 44px hit zone, the greys, the layout — is -v1 verbatim.

The geometry on this page is MINTED, not proposed. Every height, track inset and radius below is a literal value read out of the console overrides file and printed next to its token key.

The label type sizes are REAL TOKENS on a PROPOSED binding; the horizontal label padding has no token at all. Sizes are typography/font-size/font-7|font-6|font-5 = 12 / 14 / 16 / 16. Paddings (8 / 12 / 14 / 16) come from the tuner v3 export draft and no token exists for them. Both are flagged PROPOSED in every table.

Colour is not under review here. Neutral greys are used deliberately, in both modes — the judgement asked for is geometric and dimensional. No console palette has been applied or invented, and no new token key appears anywhere on this page.

Values are inlined, not read from canon.css. The built stylesheet does not yet carry the #200/#201 tokens, so relying on it would render stale numbers silently.
What you are looking at, in plain words
  • Four sizes, tuned by height. You pick the total outer height (24 / 36 / 44 / 48) and the track inset (2 / 2 / 4 / 4). Everything else follows.
  • The thumb is tuned, not calculated from the inset. Its radius is the container’s radius minus 6 — the dial you set on the tuner — never below 0. That is what makes the thumb read as a distinctly tighter shape inside its track rather than a scaled copy of it. A future tuner pass re-tunes that one number and every size follows.
  • Those thumb radii are real numbers on disk, not a live sum. The subtraction was done once, at mint time, and the answer written down (s200-D1 clause a). Nothing computes at runtime.
  • 44 is a finger, not a box. The two small sizes stay visually small — 24 and 36 — but the area that actually responds to a tap is stretched to 44 tall. Turn the overlay on to see the difference between what you see and what you can hit.
The controls are live — click a segment and the thumb moves.

Note the two coincidences that are not the same fact: scale m’s visual height happens to be 44, the same figure as min-hit-area. At m and l the hit zone therefore adds nothing; at xs and s it is the whole point. And m and l share a label size of 16 (font-5) because the type scale has no step between them — that is the scale, not a tuning choice. The thumb radii no longer collide: the single dial makes them step 0 / 2 / 4 / 6, one per size.
