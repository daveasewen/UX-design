Why 47 became 6 — the two subtractions, proved

Alias-target. Supercharge overrides color/neutral/15. In the store, background/default carries "$alias": {"light": "color/neutral/15"}. In the built file, base :root reads --color-neutral-15:#FFFFFF and --background-default:#FFFFFF; the supercharge block reads --color-neutral-15:#F7F6F4 and --background-default:#F7F6F4. The second value can only have arrived down the alias edge — and the name --color-neutral-15 is never used in a var() anywhere. The primitive is consumed; the consumption happens in the generator, not in the browser.

Alias-shadow. Twenty of the twenty-four are not in supercharge’s override file at all. tooltip/background, primary/border/hover, tertiary/text/pressed and their kin are alias-reachable from the neutral rungs supercharge does override, so the cascade materialises an effective override for each. Nobody chose them. Reporting these as “forgotten wires” would be twenty findings where there is one generator-scope question — asked at the end.

Row A · --tabs-inactive — the fade versus the colour

R-D23 and ADR-0014 clause 4 rule the state mechanism as a theme property: mono and console fade with opacity, supercharge uses colour (“a fade drags the hue off-ramp”), legacy has no operational fade. The token tabs/inactive is the seat that mechanism would paint from — supercharge’s dark leg was even re-picked to warm/11 #AA9B92 to clear 4.5:1.

The tab snippet paints every theme the same way: color:var(--text-default); opacity:var(--alpha-72). The colour mechanism is minted and unread.

Do not read this as a defect to fix. I measured the consequence before proposing the wire, and it does not point one way. Legacy and supercharge-light get markedly better (5.25→12.63, 7.32→9.47); supercharge-dark gets worse (7.93→5.59). Nothing is below 4.5 today, so there is no live accessibility breach to repair — which is exactly why this is your call and not a repair. The readings under each cell are taken live in your browser, not typed here.

The last two pairs — mono and console — are shown for completeness only. ADR-0014 clause 4 rules those two onto the opacity mechanism, so their right-hand cell is what the seat holds, not a proposal. Both go slightly down if read (6.90→6.10, 9.06→8.22).

Row B · --padding-card-internal — ruled formula, no home

s201-D4 ratified the formula max(border-radius/surface, 8) snapped to 2px, and s200-D3 narrowed the mint to console only. Console’s 20 is on disk. Every card in the library is still padded with a hand-typed 16px.

The mechanical blocker, first. --padding-card-internal has no base declaration — it exists only inside the console block. It is the only one of the 394 per-theme mints in that position. A component wired to it renders with no padding at all in mono, legacy and supercharge, because an unresolvable custom property is invalid at computed-value time and the property falls back to its initial value. The third cell below is that, rendered, not described. Wiring therefore needs either a base mint (yours, s200-D3 says nothing mints for the other three) or a declared fallback token.

And which card? Five snippets carry a card surface and three different paddings: Cards (16 body, 12 action, 16 media, 16 option), Account-card, Stat-card, Card-header-lockup, Payment-card-visual. The ruling names a formula and a value; it does not name a surface. Picking the set is the taste call.

The twenty-four nobody authored

These have no visual row because there is nothing to show: no component names them, in any theme, and no override file declares them. They exist because supercharge overrides the neutral DNA and the cascade expands every alias edge that reaches it.

--tooltip-background · --tooltip-border · --primary-border-{hover,pressed,disabled} · --secondary-border-{hover,pressed,disabled} · --tertiary-{background-active,pressed,text-disabled,text-pressed} · --tabs-{badge-background,hover,pressed} · --data-vis-border-on-{dark,light}-{baseline-2,gridline} · --divider-border-subsectioninset · --form-border-pressed · --badge-host · --drop · --pri-icon

Two are worth your eye on their own terms, and both are recorded as questions rather than acted on: --tabs-badge-background carries a value you ratified at s123-D4 (“SC badge is fine”) which s149-D1 then fenced supercharge’s badge off — so a ratified colour paints nothing. --pri-icon carries s170-D4’s ruled white for the legacy primary button icon, and the Button snippet contains no icon at all.

The questions, plainly

  1. Does the inactive tab keep its fade? Row A. Wiring the ruled colour mechanism moves supercharge dark down; leaving it means tabs/inactive stays a value with no picture.
  2. Which surfaces are “the card”? Row B — and does the other three themes’ card padding mint, or does the consumer carry a declared fallback?
  3. Should the cascade emit an alias-shadow at all? Twenty-four declarations no one authored and no one reads. Suppressing unconsumed shadows is a generator-scope change, not a repair.
  4. Do reserved slots get to say so? A $reserved / $consumer field on a token is the only thing that can tell a forgotten wire from a deliberate one. It is also the only thing that would let the new inventory gate be promoted from advisory to blocking.
  5. The ratified-but-fenced pair — --tabs-badge-background (s123-D4 vs s149-D1) and --pri-icon (s170-D4, no icon exists). Stand, retire, or build the consumer?
