What the exclusion can and cannot do

The exclusion table is now theme-scoped: an entry names the themes it applies under, so Mono can drop a forbidden pairing without blinding the other three. Two mutation proofs were run against the live tree and reverted:

  • It does not over-suppress. Planting a genuinely bad Mono ink (rag/text/on-dark → #333333) made the gate go red at 1.33:1 on #1D1D1D — the token is still measured.
  • It does not blind other themes. Planting a light error fill in palettes/rag/legacy.json (#A8000B → #F6604C) made Legacy’s white-on-error go red at 3.14:1.

Four permanent selftest arms (A7a–A7d) now hold the ruling: the Mono scope, the non-scope for the other three, the family-accumulation fix, and the three retained rows by name. Selftest: 30 arms, 0 failed.

What this review does NOT settle

It shows what the ERROR TREATMENT looks like and what the AUDIT now measures. It does not prove that no component anywhere still paints white on an error fill in Mono — that is a separate sweep of the snippets, not of the token audit. If you want that fenced too, it needs its own gate.
