The consequence nobody had joined up: the defect has two different faces

In the snippet (which is what the render-proof measures) neither property resolves, so both declarations are invalid-at-computed-value-time: the border falls to currentColor and the label to inherit. Both land on ink. That is the inversion Dave saw.

In a real canon page the border still falls to ink — but the label now does resolve, to :root's #E1E1E1: near-invisible on a white ground. So "promote to :root" is not an untried option — it is already the state of the label token, and it produces the exact defect B-D4 was minted to fix. That is call A's answer, mostly.

2The finding that changes the shape of the call

The record says: make the disabled state recessive. Measure what "recessive" has to be quieter than, and the border channel turns out to be already spent.

The Reset's resting enabled border is var(--line) — #E1E1E1 on a white page. Numbers computed live below, from the specimens themselves:

Live measurement — the squeeze read from getComputedStyle on the rendered specimens, not typed

Read it as a ladder with no rung below the bottom one

Hover sits at ink. Enabled-resting sits at --line, which is already at the floor of perceptibility as a border. Any disabled border quieter than enabled is below that floor — invisible — and any disabled border equal to it erases the distinction entirely. There is no third value.

The label channel is the opposite: enabled label is ink at maximum contrast, so there is a wide band of genuinely recessive-but-visible values beneath it — which is precisely the band B-D4 already ruled acceptable when it chose "faint but visible by choice". The honest fix is label-led, and the border question becomes secondary.
