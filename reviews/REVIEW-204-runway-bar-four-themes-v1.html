Coverage / runway bar

Itinerary row 94, PROPOSED #204 — and the first question is whether it should exist at all.

This row originates in a test fixture, not a product need. Read that before you look at the drawing. The itinerary’s own note says “fitness tests invented this”, and the chain is now proven rather than assumed. knowledge/_COMPONENT-GAPS.md, under the heading “Token / system gaps (SME-Payments fitness tests, 2026-06-30) — exposed by the portfolio run: the canon was silent in exactly the places it had to invent”, records verbatim: “the waterfall / runway / proportional bars were invented”. Four synthetic pages in knowledge/_fitness-test/ hand-rolled a .runway section in raw hex. That invention was logged as a gap, the gap was promoted into _COMPONENT-LIBRARY-TARGET.md line 104, and the target table became the itinerary. A direct grep of knowledge/_rulings.json for runway / coverage / burn-rate returns zero hits naming this component. There is a real question underneath — “does my money cover what is already scheduled, and until when?” — but nobody has ruled that it should be a component. That is your first decision.

The overlap adjudication: it is not a Chart-bullet, it is a Progress bar. The brief flagged Chart-bullet as the nearest gated grammar. Reading both metas in full says otherwise. Chart-bullet is, in its own words, “a measure bar against a comparative target marker and qualitative performance ranges” — an SVG figure at a fixed 580px with a real data table and a series fill. The tell: a Chart-bullet needs a target; a runway bar has no target, it has a horizon. Progress bar, meanwhile, already lists “savings goal / limits meter” in its own common patterns, and its third specimen is exactly this shape — a non-percentage determinate bar carrying its domain in aria-valuetext. So the bar below is Progress bar, copied byte-for-byte. This file draws no new bar. What is actually absent is the readout: the verdict with a date, the arithmetic that produces it, and the two-key legend. A runway bar without those is a Progress bar and should be one; a runway bar with a target is a Chart-bullet and should be one.

⛔ Look at the “short” specimen: there is no red. That is the most deliberate thing on this page. A cash-runway meter is precisely where a healthy/critical green-red binding gets invented, and I invented none. The bar is ink on track in every state, inherited from the gated Progress bar, whose meta gives the reason in your own terms: “Progress is structure, not status (Dave 2026-07-21) — hence the ink pair rather than a RAG colour.” Coverage is a quantity. The verdict is a separate object — a Status-indicator chip carrying a word and a date (“Covered through 31 May”, “Short from 12 May”). Which verdict maps to which chip is a semantic binding and it is yours. Strip every colour from this component and it still says everything it means.

One open ARIA question, with the search that failed to settle it. The bar carries role="progressbar" because that is what the gated parent carries. role="meter" is arguably more correct for a gauge that is not task progress. Probes: grep -rn 'role="meter"' knowledge/snippets/ → 2 hits, both of them the new file’s own prose, i.e. zero in the 87 pre-existing snippets; grep -icE '\bmeter\b' knowledge/_rulings.json → 0. Nothing in the store rules it. Yours.

One honest geometric limitation. In the “short” state the bar is pinned at 100% and aria-valuenow is clamped, because committed money exceeds the balance and a progressbar cannot express overflow. The true committed figure appears in the legend, the arithmetic and the aria-valuetext. A dedicated overflow treatment is a decision, not a defect — yours.
