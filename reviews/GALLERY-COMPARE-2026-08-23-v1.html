Proposed — not ruled

Is a justified gallery visually different from a span gallery?

You asked whether the gallery should stay a span grid with orphan tolerance or become justified rows with a widow switch, and whether that would even look different. It looks very different, and the difference has one name: cropping. A decides the compartment first and cuts the photograph to fit it. B decides the photograph first and builds the compartment around it.

Both walls below hold the same 15 photographs in the same order, read from the same manifest rows, with the same 86px caption space and the same per-theme gutter. Only the layout differs. The page opens in console; the theme and mode switches are above and everything is live in all four.

Nothing here is ruled. s217-D3 stands: the gallery role is a span grid with orphans tolerated, and it stays that way until you say otherwise. This page is the decision surface, not the decision.

Candidate A

The ruled gallery role — span grid, orphans tolerated

Canon exactly as it renders today: each photograph’s aspect ratio picks a compartment (3 columns at 6:1 and wider, 2 columns at 2.6:1, 2 rows for portraits), every 6th tile is promoted to 2×2, and the squaring pass is off so the bottom edge may be ragged. The picture is then fitted to the compartment with object-fit: cover — which means it is cut.

A1 — canon’s own default: 6 columns, 320px row unit

Where the cropping happens: everywhere. At six columns a 1×1 compartment is taller than it is wide, so a 3:2 landscape loses roughly 42% of its area (mean across the set 44%, worst 50%, 15 of 15 pictures cropped by more than 2%). Portraits are cut too, in the other direction. Nothing on this wall is showing you the whole photograph.

Where the ragged edge sits: at the bottom, and also inside the wall — a tall portrait leaves a hole beside it that dense packing may or may not backfill. Measured on this set: 6 col: 6 empty cell(s) over 5 rows · 3 col: 3 empty cell(s) over 9 rows · 2 col: 2 empty cell(s) over 13 rows · 1 col: 0 empty cell(s) over 15 rows. That is the ruled tolerance doing exactly what you exempted it for.

A2 — the same role dialled to 4 columns (what the roles page used)

The dial is most of A’s crop cost, so both are here. At four columns the compartment is much closer to 3:2 and the mean crop falls from 44% to 13% (worst 24%). Judging A on the six-column wall alone would be judging a strawman — but note that even at its kindest, A still crops, and the amount depends on a dial rather than on the picture.

A3 — the keyline trial (s217-D3, also proposed): no borders, 1px gutter

Your “let’s try dropping the keylines” variant, carried forward from the roles page so the two open questions can be looked at together. Still proposed, still not ruled. Note what it does to the crop question: nothing. Removing the keylines makes the wall read as one surface, which arguably makes the inconsistent cropping more visible, not less.

Candidate B

Justified rows — Flickr-style, with a widow switch

Photographs are taken in order into a row until the row would drop below the target height of 320px, then the whole row is scaled so it ends flush at both edges. Row height varies; row width never does. Every box carries its picture’s own aspect ratio, so nothing is cropped anywhere on this wall.

The target is not a taste pick: 320px is the ruled row unit (layout/bento/row-unit, s217-D2) and it is also Flickr’s own default target row height. The two agree by coincidence, and the coincidence is worth knowing.

The last 3 photographs cannot fill a row at that height. Flickr calls them widows, counts them, and gives you a switch. They are not blown up to justify — they keep the target height and the row simply ends where it ends.

Where the row heights vary: 4 justified rows at 286px / 351px / 291px / 286px — a range of 65px around the 320px target (11% either side). The row holding the portrait is the tall one: a portrait eats far less width per unit of height, so its row has to grow to fill the container. That variation is the mechanism, not a defect — it is what buys the flush edges.

Where the ragged edge sits: in one place only, the last row, and it is switchable. There are no holes anywhere else — that is the whole point of justification, and it is the sharpest visible difference from A.

The honest cost of doing this at mint time. The row membership is decided here, in the generator, against the page’s own 1336px content width. The rows stay flush at every width — the browser redistributes the space — but they stay the same rows, so they get shorter as the window narrows instead of re-packing. Flickr re-packs at runtime. A shipped B would need either a packing per responsive band or a runtime pass; A re-flows for free. Narrow your window and you will see both behaviours.

What is actually being decided. Not "which is prettier" — whether a gallery of licensed photography may crop the photographs. If the answer is no, A cannot do it at any dial and B is the layout. If cropping is acceptable, A is already ruled, already responsive, already nests, and B buys a flush bottom edge you have already said you do not need.

What is canon here and what is proposed

Canon, untouched: candidate A’s container, grid, span vocabulary, responsive bands, gallery role rule and the 86px caption space all come from knowledge/canon/canon.css. This page declares no grid, no span and no role rule for A. Proposed: everything in candidate B, whose rows are a page-local flex layout because canon has no grammar for justified rows — that is what the decision would create.

Both candidates read the same manifest rows through the same function as the ruled roles page (gen_bento_roles_217.read_photos), so the photographs, their order and their derived spans cannot differ between them. Photographs are the committed web derivatives (s217-D1); licences are on every caption.
