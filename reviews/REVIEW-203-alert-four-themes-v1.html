Alert / inline callout

Itinerary row 68. This component was already built and gated — it landed 2026-07-22 (Phase-2 wave 1), eight days after the itinerary that still lists it as a Gap. Nothing below was re-drawn for this review: every pane is the approved knowledge/snippets/Alert.reference.html markup rendered through the generated canon.css.

What is new here is the spread. Until now Alert had only ever been seen in Apollo Mono, light and dark. These are its first eight panes — four themes, each light and dark. Flexibility across themes is the requirement, so this is the first view that can actually test it.

Amber, for your eye. The warning shape-vs-tint pair sits at 2.44:1 in Mono and is deliberately not declared as a gated pair — amber is exempt by convention (R-D3/R-D6 A′) because the glyph shape and the title text carry the meaning. Look hard at the warning row in each theme: if amber-on-tint reads as decoration rather than as a warning anywhere in the spread, that convention is the thing to revisit, not the colour.

PROPOSED #203, your eye owed — the callout grammar exists twice. Alert renders inline status messaging as a tinted shell with icon, title and dismiss. Form layout (row 13) renders its own, independently: .fl-summary for the form-level error box and .fl-msg per field, sharing no markup or CSS with Alert (verified: zero .alert references in the Form-layout snippet). The brief for this lane assumed Form would use Alert. It does not. Whether the form error summary should become an Alert, or whether the two are legitimately different objects, is a design decision and therefore yours — nothing has been changed either way.

PROPOSED — type debt. This snippet contributes 4 of the 1,097 type-composite violations (raw font-size:14px and font-weight:500 on demo headings and the inline link). The ratchet is shrink-only, so these are fixable at zero risk, but the fix touches an existing shared artefact and was left to the conductor.
