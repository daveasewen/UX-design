A role is the thing a slot asks for — wants: status-surface — and a component declares provides: status-surface. Co-providers are the substitutes; priority orders them, when gates them (s251-D4/D5/D11). This page fixes only the names and the membership. Priorities and when clauses are illustrative and get authored properly in the 25-meta pass.

How to answer. Strike a role by number. Rename by number ("3 → …"). Move a provider by slug ("table → 4, not 3"). Anything unstruck at the end is adopted as the list. Six calls I made without asking are in §14 — strike those too if they are wrong.

01

Role

headline-metric

FFF: function "how big is it, and did it move?"

One number the reader takes away, optionally with its movement. The tile a dashboard's top row is made of.

ProviderPriorityWhen

kpi-tile50a series exists to show (s247-D3 / DP-08: no series ⇒ not a KPI tile)

with (complements, off the role, once): chart-sparkline Suggests · stats-band-lockup Recommends as the board that holds 3–6 of them.

not-with: kpi-tile ↛ status-surface when shape lacks series (the DESK example, kept).

03

chart-panel

FFF: function is the chart-intent word, gated by answers, not by the role

A charted reading in a panel. ONE role for all fourteen charts: the five intent words (comparison · change-over-time · distribution · relationship · composition) already live on the answers axis (chart-intents.json, s251-D11), so they gate; the role only names the slot. Five roles named after the intents would duplicate that axis and break "three independent axes" (s251-D3). This is the call most worth striking if you disagree — see §14 call 1.

14

Calls made

Six things I decided rather than asked

Per s251-D15. Strike by number.

  1. One chart-panel role, not five. The intent words gate on the answers axis; a role per intent would carry the same fact twice. Cost: priority among the 14 charts only means something after answers has filtered — which is the intended order (D11: when gates, priority orders).
  2. Slot-level roles, not intent-level or atom-level. Twelve names a brief would actually use ("give me a headline-metric"), not 40 fine ones. Debian's virtual packages sit at this grain.
  3. One component may appear once as a provider and again as a complement (breadcrumbs: provides 07, with 06). No component provides two roles. If you want multi-provides, summary is the first candidate (02 and 04).
  4. Templates are consumers, not providers. They hold the wants list; the bento template becomes the first worked consumer.
  5. Priorities are 10–60 in steps of 5, compared only within a role (update-alternatives). Illustrative on this page; authored for real in the 25-meta pass.
  6. Roles 11 and 12 are named now, authored later. They exist so the validator can say "every meta has a role" from day one, without the first pass growing past 25.

What your answer settles

Unstruck roles become the provides vocabulary — one JSON home, knowledge/roles.json, same shape as chart-intents.json (address set is the fence; the validator refuses a slug not in it). The 25-meta pass then authors provides · shape · span · priority · when per meta against this list, one review page per batch.
