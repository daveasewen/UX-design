Opacity primitives — the 4% ladder, for your eye (#96-D2, steps ruled #98-D3)

In one breath: the design system has no opacity values of its own — every see-through surface in the library today either hardcodes a number or leans on a temporary dial you asked us to replace. You ruled that a proper set gets minted, spaced at four percent, and that you promote what survives your eye. This page is that spread: the full ladder shown over light and dark, over the five chart colours, and driven live into the two places waiting for it — stacked-area bands and bullet range bands. Nothing is minted yet; four decisions below settle what gets promoted, what it's called, and what happens to the odd values already in the wild.

Technical detail — what the specimens are and aren't

Inline SVGs using the canon series hexes and band geometry from Chart-stacked-area.reference.html / the #96 bullet build — NOT the reference snippets themselves (an iframe would need copies in this directory per the #66 cross-origin lesson, and the property under review is a single fill-opacity number, which these carry faithfully). The four decisions

D1 — What gets promoted from the ladder?

Three ways to cut it. Mint the whole ladder as the primitive tier and let roles bind to steps later (recommended — primitives are cheap, roles are the governed thing). Or you pick a shortlist from this page and only those exist. Or mint nothing yet and keep dialling per-consumer until a pattern earns it.

Technical detail

Full ladder = 24 unitless number tokens; gen_canon_tokens.py:36–38 already classes opacity/alpha as UNITLESS so generation is a data change, not a generator change. Promotion is yours per derivation governance; whatever you rule lands via the serial/conductor mint path the stacked-area receipt reserved.

D2 — Naming

The house pattern elsewhere is family-then-value. Zero-padded keeps them sorting correctly everywhere.

D3 — The off-grid legacy values

Five opacity numbers already live in canon and two of them don't sit on a 4% grid: the ruled hover strength (seventy percent) and the modal text fade (eighty-five). The rest land cleanly (80, 60 — and the pulse's 50 is animation, arguably exempt like text). Moving the hover means touching a value you ruled in ADR-0009, so it is flagged, not proposed quietly.
