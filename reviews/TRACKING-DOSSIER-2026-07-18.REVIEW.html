Letter-spacing — what the evidence supports

Apollo SDS · 2026-07-18 · evidence dossier, tiered by strength. Nothing proposed for promotion.

The gap. The canon governs letter-spacing nowhere. canon/type.css declares none on any composite. The token store carries a letter-spacing ramp set to zero at every size but one. Twenty-four hardcoded values float loose in the corpus. The only rule on the books is {#type26-018} — "no wide kerning that breaks legibility; not too tight either" — tagged [TASTE], with no number.

That is precisely the shape dv-019 had before its 135° and col26-020 had before its 0.72: a real perceptual rule that cannot be enforced because nobody pinned a number to it.
How to read the tiers. Mixing replicated experiment with "what typographers say" produces rules nobody can audit later. Each finding carries its tier, and the contested ones are written as contested — including where that is inconvenient for us. The literature's job is to say where to point the specimen, not to set the number. Every threshold this system enforces came from what you could see.

1 · The medium question

Dave, on reading the first cut: "what the display was — I suspect screens and print might be different, especially for astigmatism." Correct, and it cuts at the foundation of the section below. Most of the classical typographic authority here is print-derived, and the single strongest experimental finding turns out to be print too. This section comes first because it changes how the evidence below should be read.

Zorzi 2012 — the dyslexia headline — was PRINT.

Times-Roman, 14 point, inter-letter spacing widened by 2.5pt on a 14pt body — about 18% of body size, i.e. roughly +0.18em. Paper. Serif face. Italian and French children aged 8–14.

For us: That is 50% above WCAG 1.4.12's 0.12em ceiling, on paper, in a serif, with children. The distance to a 14px Univers label on an emissive screen is enormous. The finding may still hold on screen — but nothing in that study says so, and I presented it yesterday as though it did.

Butterick is a PRINT typographer. So is type26-016.

The 5–12% caps range comes from book and document typography. Our own brand rule states tracking in InDesign thousandths-of-an-em and leading in points ("50pt → 53pt") — it is a print specification that was never re-derived for screen.

For us: Both are still useful, but as inherited practice, not screen evidence. Contradiction 1 gets more interesting: maybe the token store zeroed the ramp because nobody could say what the print numbers meant digitally.

Halation is SCREEN-SPECIFIC — and it is the mechanism behind col26-020.

Print and e-ink reflect light; displays emit it. Emitted light from bright letterforms scatters within the eye and blooms across the boundary into the dark ground. Paper does not do this. Level Access put it plainly: white text on pure black creates "a visual fuzzing effect for people with astigmatism called halation" — and "automated contrast-ratio checkers would not detect a problem".

For us: That is your badge, described by an accessibility body, three years before you saw it. It also independently confirms the thing that made col26-020 worth writing: the effect does not fold into the contrast gate. Our rule was right and is better-founded than we knew.

Prevalence is high enough to change the priority.

WHO: refractive errors account for 43% of global visual impairment. In a UK study of 11,000+ spectacle wearers, 47.4% had astigmatism of 0.75D or greater in at least one eye. Roughly half the population has at least 0.5D. Halation is reported as worst on body text at 14–16px.

For us: 14–16px is exactly the band col26-020(c) governs, and exactly where the retrofit's 100 declarations sit. Under ADR-0004 this stops being a refinement and becomes a mainstream legibility case.

⚠ The method caveat — thresholds observed by one pair of eyes.

0.72 and 135° were both fixed by what you could see, on your screen. That has been the right call — it is why they are gateable at all. But halation severity varies with the observer's own refraction, and with panel type, brightness and subpixel rendering.

For us: Not an argument against the method — an argument for recording the conditions. A threshold with its display, brightness and observer noted can be re-tested later; one without them cannot be distinguished from a preference. Cheap to add now, impossible to reconstruct afterwards.

2 · The evidence

Each finding carries its medium alongside its tier — print-derived, screen-derived, or both. Print provenance is not a disqualification — it is a reason to specimen the value rather than adopt it.

SETTLED

Capitals need +5–12% tracking. Lowercase does not. PRINT-DERIVED

Butterick states it as a hard CSS range: 0.05em–0.12em for all-caps and small-caps, "particularly important at small sizes". Capitals are spaced to sit beside lowercase; set together they read too tight. This is the least contested statement in the whole of typographic practice.

For us: We already do this — four tranches carry .05em on uppercase runs, dead centre of the range. But see contradiction 4: those runs should not be uppercase at all.

SETTLED

Tracking should tighten as size rises — optical sizing. BOTH

At large sizes the gaps between glyphs grow faster than the strokes, so display type "drifts apart" at the spacing that suits body text. The inverse holds below ~9pt, where extra tracking keeps counters distinct. Apple ships this as two physical fonts — SF Pro Text under ~20px, SF Pro Display above, the Display cut tracked tighter.

For us: This is a ramp, not a constant — tracking is a function of size, exactly like the weight rule col26-020(c) turned out to be. Our token store has the ramp and sets it to zero at every step.

SETTLED

WCAG 1.4.12 is a survival requirement, not a design target. SCREEN

Level AA. Content must lose no meaning or function when the USER overrides letter-spacing to 0.12em (plus line-height 1.5, word-spacing 0.16em, paragraph 2em). It does not ask us to ship 0.12em. It asks that nothing breaks when someone else sets it.

For us: Directly gateable, and currently ungated. If we ship negative tracking the jump to 0.12em is larger, so tight display type raises overflow risk in exactly the components with least slack. A gate can re-render at the 1.4.12 values and diff for clipping.

CONTESTED

For general readers, wider tracking does not speed reading — and can slow it. SCREEN — eye-tracking

Reading speed peaks at or near a font's designed spacing and falls off in BOTH directions. The 2020 Frontiers study found wider spacing shortened fixation durations for everyone, yet lowered words-per-minute for fast readers — it cut word-skipping and added fixations. Slower readers benefited more. Wider spacing also pushes text further into peripheral vision, where acuity is worse; that partly cancels the crowding relief.

For us: Kills any "more air = more readable" instinct. Positive tracking on body text needs a specific justification (caps, small size, reverse) — it is not a free improvement.

CONTESTED

Dyslexia: real effect in the headline study, poor replication since. PRINT

Zorzi et al. (PNAS 2012) found extra-large spacing roughly doubled accuracy and raised speed >20% in dyslexic children 8–14, with no training — attributed to abnormal sensitivity to crowding. The study was PRINT — Times-Roman 14pt, spacing widened 2.5pt on a 14pt body (~+0.18em). Later work has struggled to reproduce it; one study found no effect on accuracy, comprehension or speed in either dyslexic or typical readers. The 2020 slow-reader finding is consistent with a real but narrower effect.

For us: Handle honestly. Two separate problems: replication is weak, AND the medium is wrong — paper, serif, children, at a tracking 50% above WCAG's ceiling. Nothing in it speaks to a 14px sans label on an emissive screen. The defensible position stays user-adjustable spacing — which 1.4.12 already requires — not shipping wide tracking by default.

PRACTITIONER — not experimental

Reverse type reads tighter than it is; open the tracking to compensate. SCREEN

Consistent practitioner advice, not experimental: light-on-dark letterforms bloom into the surrounding dark, closing the apparent gaps. Standard remedy is to open tracking, increase leading, and reduce weight. The same sources give a third lever — dim the text from #FFF toward #CCC–#D4D4D4 to cut the bloom.

For us: This is {#col26-020} again, from the other side. Same phenomenon Dave observed on the badge. Our rule has two levers (ground chroma, ground luminance); the literature offers two more — text luminance and tracking. Both are candidates to extend it.

TENSION WITH OUR OWN RULE

Reverse-type practice says LIGHTER weight. Our col26-020(c) says Medium minimum.

Practitioner guidance for reversed type is to drop weight, because bloom thickens strokes. col26-020(c), from Dave's own observation, sets Medium (500) as the floor at 12–16px and Light (300) at 20px.

For us: Probably not a real conflict — probably a size split. The reverse-type advice is written for display sizes; col26-020(c) was observed at 12–20px, and its weights already FALL as size rises. Read together they may be one curve. Worth testing, not worth resolving on paper.

3 · Four contradictions already in our system

Found by grepping the repo before going outside. All four are independent of what the research says.

1The token store flattens the brand rule to zero.

tokens/typography.json → letter-spacing is 0 at every size except font-00 = −1. But brand {#type26-016} specifies Latin headlines at −15 to −30 tracking and body at −5 to −10 — InDesign thousandths-of-an-em, i.e. −0.015 to −0.030em and −0.005 to −0.010em. The tokens do not carry the brand standard; they erase it.

Action: Reconcile. Either the export dropped it or the brand rule was never applied digitally.

2Authors are hand-applying the brand rule the tokens don't carry.

Headers, Hero, Navigations, Account-card and DataViz all set −.01em or −.015em by hand — squarely inside type26-016's headline range. Somebody knew the rule and applied it manually because the system offered no way to inherit it.

Action: Evidence FOR the ramp being real. Retrieval-not-recall: the value is right, the mechanism is missing.

3The composites are silent, so 24 values float free.

canon/type.css declares no letter-spacing on any composite. So every tracking decision in the library is a hardcoded local override — 24 of them, seven distinct values, governed by nothing. Butterick's own advice is that letterspacing belongs in the style definition.

Action: This is the same gap the TYPE-002 retrofit exists to close, one property over. Whatever shape the tracking rule takes, it belongs ON the composites.

4⚠ Four files carry uppercase past a BLOCKING gate.

Tranche-3 (×2), -5, -6, -8 contain text-transform:uppercase. {#type26-019} bans uppercase outside acronyms brand-wide — on a dyslexia rationale — and was promoted from advisory to blocking by Dave's 2026-07-02 ruling. It doesn't fire because _validate_snippets.py globs snippets/*.reference.html only. _proforma/ is outside its scope.

Action: A gate blind-spot, not a design question — same class as the type gate reporting clean on the badge that motivated it, and Cards scoring 9/9 in June. Found incidentally while grepping for tracking. Fix is independent of every ruling on this sheet.

5 · To rule

  1. T1 — Is tracking a ramp, or a constant? The optical-size evidence says tracking is a function of size — negative at display, zero through body, positive below ~12px. That makes it a per-step token, like line-height. The alternative is one value plus named exceptions (caps, reverse). [ per-size ramp / constant + exceptions ]
  2. T2 — Whose numbers — brand's, or observed? type26-016 already gives numbers (−15/−30 headline, −5/−10 body) but they are print numbers, and this system's enforced thresholds have all come from what you could see on screen. Adopt the brand numbers as the digital ramp, or cut a specimen and pick the way you picked 0.72 and 135°? [ adopt brand / observe on specimen / brand as start, confirm by eye ]
  3. T3 — Does col26-020 gain a tracking leg — and a text-luminance leg? The reverse-type literature describes your badge effect exactly, and offers two levers you don't have: open the tracking, and dim the text off pure white. Both fit "reduce the extremity of the edge" without adding contrast. [ extend col26-020 / keep separate / specimen it first ]
  4. T4 — Do we gate WCAG 1.4.12 survival? Distinct from every other question here: not what we ship, but whether we break when a user overrides to 0.12em. Testable by re-rendering at the 1.4.12 values and diffing for clipping — and it gets sharper if we adopt negative tracking. [ build the gate / log as debt ]
  5. T5 — How do we speak about dyslexia and spacing? The headline study is strong and the replications are weak. Given ADR-0004 the temptation is to claim the benefit. The defensible line is user-adjustability — 1.4.12 — rather than shipping wide tracking on a contested finding. Your call, and you have standing here that the literature doesn't. [ user-adjustable only / ship wider small-text tracking / specimen it on yourself ]
  6. T7 — Do observed thresholds start carrying their conditions? Prompted by your own question. 0.72 and 135° were fixed by eye, on your screen — and halation severity varies with the observer's refraction and with panel, brightness and subpixel rendering. Recording display + brightness + observer alongside each observed number costs a line; reconstructing it afterwards is impossible. It would also let a future session tell an observed threshold from a preference. [ add to the ruling template / not worth it ]
  7. T6 — The uppercase blind-spot — confirm the fix. Four _proforma files breach a blocking rule the gate cannot see. Two parts: de-cap the instances, and widen _validate_snippets.py's scope so _proforma/ is covered. Note the interlock: de-capping removes the .05em caps tracking with it, so contradiction 4 partly dissolves contradiction 3. [ confirm both / de-cap only / separate session ]
