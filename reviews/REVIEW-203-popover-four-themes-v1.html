Popover

Rich anchored content that opens on click — Tooltip's interactive sibling. The panes below show the real gated markup rendered through the generated canon.css, held open by a declared review-surface override: the component has no static specimen of its own, which is itself a finding below.

Read this first — the brief's premise did not hold. Wave 3 was briefed from the itinerary of 2026-07-14, which marks this component Gap. It is not a gap: the gated snippet, the meta, the showroom page and the MIGRATED_SNIPPETS entry all landed on 2026-07-22, eight days after that snapshot. Nothing was re-built here. How Popover differs from its gated siblings

  • vs Tooltip The distinction is clean, and enforced by the markup. Tooltip opens on hover / focus, is role="tooltip", is tied to its trigger by aria-describedby, holds plain text only, and nothing inside it is focusable. Popover opens on click, is role="dialog" with aria-labelledby, its trigger carries aria-expanded / aria-controls / aria-haspopup="dialog", and it holds focusable content — title, body, link, close. The test: if the user can act inside it, it is a Popover; if it is a sentence of help, it is a Tooltip. They share only the placement mechanics — flip above / below, clamp to the viewport, tail tracks the trigger.
  • vs Modals / Drawer Popover is non-modal: no aria-modal, no scrim, no focus trap. Tabbing out of it closes it, and the page stays live throughout.
  • vs Toast Popover is anchored and user-invoked; Toast is viewport-fixed and system-invoked.

Finding — the Popover cannot be seen standing still. Toast ships a .spec block and Drawer ships a .specimen class precisely so the component can be shown in-flow, at rest, in a review pane. Popover ships neither. .pop is position:fixed with opacity:0, revealed only by the snippet's placement JavaScript — so it is invisible in any still: this review page, a showroom thumbnail, a screenshot, a paste into Figma. The panes above are visible only because this page carries a declared local override (see the <style> block, marked REVIEW-SURFACE OVERRIDE). PROPOSED #203, Dave's eye owed: give Popover a static-specimen affordance matching Drawer's .specimen.

The rest of the audit — all PROPOSED, all yours

  • The trigger button is unstyled demo chrome. .pop-trigger has no canon rule at all, so it renders as the raw browser button above — grey and round-cornered in every theme, and conspicuously wrong on the dark panes. It is not part of the component, but it is the first thing anyone opening the snippet or the showroom page sees. Square corners are the default rule; this one borrows the browser's.
  • The tail is a rotated square (::after, 10px, 45°) inheriting the surface and the elevation border. In light the border is transparent and the shadow carries the lift; in dark a grey hairline outlines it. Look closely at the tail on the dark panes — a rotated bordered square is where a seam usually shows.
  • Corners are square in three themes and 20px in Console — and that is correct. Measured in the rendered panes: --border-radius-surface resolves to 0 in mono, legacy and supercharge, and to 20px in Console, light and dark. That is not a drift: you ruled it at s199-D3 off the radius tuner (console surface 12 → 20). Flagged only because the square-corner default makes a rounded pane look like a defect at first glance — it is your rounding, arriving correctly.
  • 44px hit area — checked by hand, passes. The close × is a 24px visual with a 44×44 ::before.
  • Type-composite debt: 2 violations — body font-family (demo chrome) and .pop a { font-weight:500 }. The second one is in the component: an inline link's weight set raw instead of by a composite.
  • Popover is painted the page's own colour — alone among the three. Its --surface binds to background/default, the page token. Toast and Drawer both bind to tertiary/background/default, the raised-surface token. The consequence is measurable: in dark, a Toast lifts off the page by 1.06:1 (mono) and 1.16:1 (supercharge), while a Popover sits at exactly 1.00:1 — the same colour as the page — in all four themes, both modes. Everything separating it is the hairline, and in Supercharge dark that hairline is the 1.96:1 one above. Deliberate, or an inconsistent binding across three siblings that share one elevation recipe? Never ruled.
  • Focus behaviour. On open, focus moves to the first focusable child — which is the link, not the close button. Esc, outside click and re-toggling all close it and return focus to the trigger. Correct, but that first-stop choice was never ruled.

Measured finding, all four themes — in Supercharge dark the only thing holding this overlay off the page is a hairline at 1.96:1. These overlays are lifted by the elevation recipe: a shadow in light, a 1px border in dark. In light the panel is exactly the page colour in every theme (1.00:1) and the shadow does all the work — that is the canon recipe and it reads fine. In dark the border takes over, and it is not equal across themes: mono's hairline is #808080 on #1A1A1A = 4.41:1, comfortable; Supercharge's is #524842 on #1A1A1A = 1.96:1, under the 3:1 non-text floor. Compare the mono-dark and supercharge-dark panes below. Pre-existing and theme-level, not this component's — but it is the component that shows it. PROPOSED #203, Dave's eye owed.
