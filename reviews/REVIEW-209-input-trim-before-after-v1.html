Review 209 — leading-trim applied to text-bearing inputs

Dave’s ruling (live, session 209): the line-height trim “should be applied to inputs such as searches and multiselect”. Every pane below is a REAL artefact in an iframe — left = the snippet as it stood at HEAD, right = the snippet after the sweep. Nothing re-drawn.

The headline, before you look at anything else. The vocabulary is now authored on the inputs and the browser accepts it — getComputedStyle(input).text-box-trim returns trim-both where it returned none. But Chromium does not act on it inside a form control. Across the four affected components the biggest measured movement of any input’s text was 0.75 CSS px, and no input’s box changed height at all. Three of the five components you named (Multi-select, Tags-input, Combobox) do not carry a leading-trim block in their snippet at all, so they were not touched and did not move.

The isolation probe at the bottom of this page proves the mechanism rather than asserting it: at line-height:2 a <span> collapses 32.00 px → 11.56 px and a <button> 32.00 px → 11.02 px, while an <input> and a <textarea> both stay at 32.00 px with the same declaration.

Your call: keep the inert vocabulary (harmless today, self-enacting the day Chromium ships trim inside form controls — which would move every field in the library without a review), or revert the sweep and enact the intent a different way (explicit line-height / padding on the field, which is a design decision, not a CSS capability).

What changed in the source

54 leading-trim blocks across knowledge/snippets/*.reference.html — every block in the library — gained nine entries in the :is(…) vocabulary: the seven text-bearing input types, input:not([type]), and textarea. Checkboxes, radios, ranges, colour and file inputs are deliberately excluded. The :not(:has(svg)) guard is untouched. canon.css was regenerated from the snippets (91 components), and its hand-tier .canon base trim rule carries the identical vocabulary so canon consumers match the snippets. 91 showroom pages regenerated.

Multi-select — untouched, and why no trim block in the snippet

Multi-select, Tags-input and Combobox carry no leading-trim block at all — the idiom was never authored into them, so the sweep had nothing to extend. Eleven input-bearing snippets are in this state (Amount-input, Combobox, Date-picker, Date-range-picker, Form-layout, Multi-select, Secure-entry, Stepper, Tags-input, Textarea, Time-picker). Giving them a trim block would also trim every label, span and button in those components — a far larger visual change than the one you ruled — so that is a decision for you, not a sweep.

What closes this review

Your eye on the pairs above, and one ruling: keep the input vocabulary as authored (inert now, self-enacting later), or revert it and treat input text alignment as an explicit design decision. Everything is reversible in one sweep either way.
