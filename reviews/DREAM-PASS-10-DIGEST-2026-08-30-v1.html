First finding · open

The reports your subs file no longer fit the parser you built for them — and it got worse after the last fix

P1 · governed by s218-D7 · touches knowledge/_capture_gate.py:4655–4656

In one sentence: you ruled that every sub’s filed report must carry three lines a machine can read — a counts line, a priced replay line, a questions heading — and roughly half of them now write those lines in a shape the machine skips over.

What you ruled

“the FILE is sole authority; stub figures are parsed from it, never retyped … Stub carries a priced REPLAY-THESE line so deferral is declared.”

Your words, recorded at s218-D7, 25 August.

What the parse rate actually is

Measured this session over every filed report in notes/_subreports/ — 67 files, the skeleton excluded, using the gate’s own three regexes. The bars show how many of the 67 the machine can read.

The pass read 64 files and got 28 / 21 / 26, and 3 / 3 / 4 among the 15 filed since the fix. Three more reports have landed since it ran; the picture is the same, marginally better, still under half.

It is not that the lines are missing — they are decorated

Nearly every report carries the words. What has drifted is the shape. The newest reports write a section heading where the contract asked for a field:

WHAT THE GATE READS      REPLAY-THESE: notes/foo.md (~2,400 tk) · knowledge/bar.py (~900 tk)
WHAT IS BEING WRITTEN    ## REPLAY-THESE
                         ## 8. REPLAY-THESE
                         COUNTS: findings 8 / ruling-shaped 4 / UNPROVEN 2

What enacting it would change

  • Two regexes in one file: SUBREPORT_REPLAY_RE and SUBREPORT_QUESTIONS_RE at knowledge/_capture_gate.py:4655–4656, widened to accept the same ordinary decoration — leading # or **, a numbered prefix — that the counts pattern beside them already accepts.
  • The parse rate re-measured in the same motion, so the figure is known rather than assumed.
  • Nothing else. Not the 67 filed reports — they are dated history and may not be edited (ADR-0017).

What it costs

Small. Two pattern edits, one re-measurement, one selftest read-back. This is the fix that was already written once, at #221, for the third regex — the two either side of it were never re-measured and never widened.

What could go wrong

1. Widening the regex can make the report parse while the deferral stays silent. This is the sharp one. ## REPLAY-THESE as a heading has no path (~N tk) items under it — the priced half, the half that makes a deferral declared, is genuinely gone, not merely unread. Widen the pattern to accept the heading and you buy a green parse that certifies nothing. If this is enacted it needs the pattern to keep demanding the priced items, wherever they sit.

2. It is tempting to widen the vocabulary too, and #221 deliberately refused that. The comment beside the fix declines to loosen the field names or their order. A sub asked to “make the reports parse” will reach for that, and a gate that accepts any wording is not a gate.

3. On its own, nobody will see the result. The check that would report this number reads an empty population — that is the second finding. Enacted alone, this fix improves a figure no instrument prints.

4. SUBREPORT_CITE_BLOCKING is not in scope. Whether the citation check blocks a wrap or merely warns is your word, and the pass explicitly did not propose flipping it.

Your word would settle

  1. Fix the gate, or fix the writing? Widen the two patterns to meet where subs actually write — or leave the patterns alone and tighten the skeleton and briefs so subs write the field form. The pass proposes the first; the second is the one that keeps the priced replay items alive.
  2. If the gate is widened, must the priced items survive? Accepting a bare ## REPLAY-THESE heading with nothing priced under it, yes or no.
Second finding · open

The thing that answers “when was the last wrap?” is looking for the wrong shape, so two checks have nothing to check

P2 · touches knowledge/_capture_gate.py:4657 and :4669–4678 · consumers at :4386 and :4710

In one sentence: the detector matches commit subjects beginning after #N — which the commit script itself documents as the non-wrap shape — so it hands back the newest commit of any kind, and the two gates that ask it “what has changed since the last wrap?” get back an empty list every time.

The two shapes, from the authority

“The prefix T3 generates has TWO legal shapes: after #<n> <date> — (non-wrap) and #<n> <date> — (wrap).”

knowledge/_git_commit.sh:138–139 — the script that generates the prefixes.

And the detector, one line: WRAP_COMMIT_SUBJECT_RE = re.compile(r"^after\s+#\d+\b"). It is named for the wrap shape and matches the other one.

How unspecific that is

Commits it matches 130 of 150
… of those, ones that mention a wrap 11 of 150

A pattern satisfied by 87% of the log will always return the newest commit. It is not wrong about wraps — real wrap commits do use both shapes — it is not specific, and any fix has to key on something other than the prefix.

The live consequence, run from this seat just now

_last_wrap_commit('.')  →  ('e29c25b…', 'after #225 2026-08-30 — #225: v1.0.3 ratified and
                             baked, and the carry list left the banner')
_changed_since('.', <that sha>, 'notes/_subreports')  →  []

The citation check’s population is empty. A check that cannot fail. The pass got the same empty list at the previous HEAD; the tree has moved and it is still empty.

What enacting it would change

  • _last_wrap_commit at knowledge/_capture_gate.py:4669–4678 keys on a mark the wrap itself owns rather than a prefix both kinds share — the proposal’s suggestion is that the wrap is the commit that moves GOOD-MORNING.md’s LATEST banner, so a path-scoped log plus the existing subject match is roughly two lines.
  • One new arm in selftest_subreport_citation that plants an ordinary work commit after the wrap commit — the arm that would have caught this.
  • Nothing in _git_commit.sh. The two commit shapes are ruled and three other gates count them.

What it costs

Small to build, noisy to land. Two lines and a test arm. The noise is the point: see below.

What could go wrong

1. The second consumer is the regen serial, and it has a history. regen_serial_check at :4386 uses the same start point. It is the ordered serial that cost about six CI reds at #210. Today it is looking at nothing. Give it a real population and it will start speaking — correctly, and probably loudly, on the first wrap after the change. That is the fix working, but it should not arrive as a surprise mid-wrap.

2. The existing selftest is green and will stay green either way. Its fixture plants the two commit shapes in the documented mapping, which the live log inverts. It proves the clause — “a subject starting after # is found” — and never the feature. Without the new arm, a fix here is unproven no matter how green the run is.

3. Keying on the LATEST banner is a guess that needs probing before it is built. If a non-wrap commit ever touches GOOD-MORNING.md — and the carry-list move ruled this morning at s225-D2 changes what that file holds — the new key inherits the same non-specificity in a new costume. Worth one probe over the log before anyone writes it.

4. Fixing this makes P1 visible, and P1 is currently failing. Land P2 first and the citation check starts grading reports that are, by the first finding’s measurement, about two-thirds unparseable. Order matters: either widen the patterns first, or expect the first honest report to be a red one.

Your word would settle

  1. Which mark identifies a wrap? The LATEST-banner move is the pass’s suggestion, not a fact — a wrap-specific token in the subject, or a git trailer, are the alternatives, and one of them is cheaper to prove.
  2. P1 or P2 first? P2 first gives an honest red; P1 first gives a quiet landing. Both are defensible and the choice is a temperament call, not a technical one.

The sentence every cold session reads first has been contradicted by this project’s own record for six sessions

P3 · the class s125-D1 named · touches knowledge/_gen_chain.py:280, knowledge/_gen_schematic.py:354–360

In one sentence: the chain banner still announces “75 of 140 steps green — 65 steps have never been in a green verdict”, and your own committed record from #218 calls that figure an artefact of a sandbox missing tiktoken, giving 126 of 128 instead.

“CI: #413 GREEN on e645df2” — and that commit has 128 steps. A green CI run at a 128-step commit cannot leave 65 steps never-green. Two independent contradictions, not one.

Why it cannot correct itself

The two counts in that sentence are generated — that was s125-D1’s whole point. The anchor was left typed: VERDICT_SHA = "18c7789", with a comment defending the choice on the grounds that a SHA names a fixed historical event and so cannot go stale. True of the event. But the published sentence is not the event — it is the conclusion “no later green verdict exists”, and that is exactly the kind of claim that ages. Nothing re-checks it. It reaches three surfaces from one constant: the chain banner, GOOD-MORNING.md via the splice, and the rendered schematic page.

Three ways out, and they are not equal

OptionWhat it doesCostLeaves the class…
(a) Generate itDerive the anchor from the newest commit carrying a recorded green verdict, instead of typing it.Largest. Needs a machine-readable record of green verdicts to exist.Closed.
(b) Expire itHave build_verdict_line() refuse once the anchor is older than N sessions, rather than restate a superseded figure.Moderate. The module’s UNMEASURED refusal posture is already written and tested.Closed, differently — it goes quiet rather than lying.
(c) Re-point itMove VERDICT_SHA to adb5130 and carry the 126/128 reading.One line.Open — and see the warning below.

What could go wrong

1. Option (c) is the shape of the act you already refused, and a ruling stands watch over it. s125-D1, in your words: “a value that has gone stale twice will go stale a third time, so the fix is not a fresher value but removing the human from the loop that produces it.” Its watch field says: “A future session ‘helpfully’ correcting 75 to 98 by hand would enact the alternative Dave rejected, under cover of tidiness.” The re-stamp being proposed here is one level up — the anchor rather than the count — but it is the same motion. It should be picked knowingly or not at all.

2. The replacement figure is itself a conclusion, not a machine record. “126/128 driven individually” comes from a #218 seam note. Re-pointing the anchor to it swaps one inscribed conclusion for a fresher inscribed conclusion — which is precisely the debt s129-D5 says must be triaged, not refreshed.

3. Option (a) may discover there is nothing to derive from. If no green verdict is recorded machine-readably, (a) collapses into (b). That is worth knowing before it is commissioned, and it is one probe.

4. Settling it by running the build is barred. The chain forbids partial runs of _build_all.py, the sandbox cannot complete one inside a call boundary, and a tiktoken-less run produces exactly the artefact reds that caused this in the first place.

Your word would settle

  1. (a), (b) or (c)? The pass recommends (a) and offers (b) as the fallback if nothing machine-readable exists. (c) is named as the minimum and flagged as leaving the class open.
  2. If (b): how old is too old? The refusal needs a number of sessions, and that number is yours.
