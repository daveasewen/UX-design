The glyph — and your ruling dissolves most of the problem

“Contrast with a white background is a luxury — the label carries the meaning.” That is not a new position, it is already this system's canon, stated in at least five snippets. Account-card: “Status dot is < 3:1 standalone (1.4.11) so meaning is carried by the label text (1.4.1).” Same in Status-indicator, Confirmation, Countdown-timer and Links. I built a dark amber glyph to satisfy a rule this system had already and deliberately waived.

What follows: where the icon is aria-hidden and the LABEL carries the state, the icon is decorative — 1.4.11 does not apply, and the glyph can stay light and match the fill. The ochre disappears.

The one case that survives: amber as TEXT. Text IS the label, so 1.4.3 applies at 4.5:1 with no waiver. But that is answered by a rule rather than a token — “amber is never a text colour” is simpler than maintaining a second amber, and it costs nothing: the label sits in #1A1A1A and the amber does the signalling.

Belt and braces — Dave's framing, and it is the mechanism. Red, green and blue clear 4.5:1 on a white page unaided, so they carry the label and the contrast. Amber only ever has the belt. Remove the label and the belt is gone — which is exactly when tier 2 is required, and why it exists at all.

Correction on the record: I first built the lone-glyph value at 4.5:1 (#A16C00, L 0.573) — that is the TEXT threshold. A lone icon needs 3:1, giving #C28B14 at L 0.674: one rung lighter, and still amber rather than the ochre. Wrong rung, not wrong idea.
