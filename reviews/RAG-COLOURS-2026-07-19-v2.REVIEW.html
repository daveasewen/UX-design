§1 decision — pick the canon manifestation(s)

M1 Cell — strongest scan in dense tables; the fill carries the meaning. Needs the background role + text-colour rules (white, amber-black).

M2 Pill — same fill, softer; reads as a tag. (Square in mono.)

M3 Dot + label — the "belt & braces" form: never colour alone (icon-013). Lightest touch, best for accessibility, works inline in prose. R-D6 (your ruling): because the label carries the meaning, the dot's contrast can relax — the brighter amber is fine here. Contrast only becomes strict when the glyph itself carries the meaning (arrows = direction, colour-only status, or a bare-number label).

M4 Bar / edge — for cards and grouped rows where the whole object has a status.

My read (yours to overrule): these are context-scoped, not competing — M1 for table status columns, M3 for inline / dense / accessibility-first, M4 for cards. M2 only if a tag pattern already exists. If you'd rather name one canonical form, say which.

Your call: which form(s) are canon, and is the mapping to surface right?

§2 · Dark mode — a balanced (isoluminant) RAG set

Your steer: match the intensity across the hues in dark mode. The term is isoluminant — holding the same perceptual lightness (OKLCh L) across red / green / blue so none reads brighter or muddier than the others (amber excluded — it's the carve-out and stays #F0B13A / #C58900). Doing this reveals the real structure: you can't get both roles at one lightness. White-text-on-fill wants the hues dark; a mark on the dark page wants them light. So a matched set lands as one of two bands, each internally balanced — measured on the real ground #1A1A1A:

What each band costs. Band A keeps the light-mode logic (white text on fills) and changes the least — but red/blue nudge slightly darker off their R-D1 dark values (#CC4333→#C33A2B, #2674DC→#1F6ED5) to sit at the matched L, and bare coloured RAG text on dark stays disallowed (marks are icons or label-paired — the "belt & braces", icon-013). Band B is the more unified end: every fill takes black text, so amber stops being a carve-out in dark mode and marks work as bare text — but the fills read lighter/pastel and it moves red/blue further from their ruled values. Both bands are equally balanced; the choice is which role you privilege.
§2 decision — the balanced dark set

Band A: matched white-text fills; marks icon / label-paired. Closest to light mode and to the existing dark red/blue intent; smallest change. But white-on-dark-fill still blooms (§3) — the light-bleed edge stays.

Band B: matched black-text fills; marks self-sufficient as text. Maximally unified (amber no longer special) — but uncapped, the saturated fills/marks dance (§3).

Band B′ (sat-capped): Band B with the dance lever pulled — chroma −28% at ~zero contrast cost. The most likely to sit still for you, and it makes black-text fills bloom least (§3 step-down finding).

Hold red/blue as ruled, cut green only: least churn; gives up the balance.

Recommendation flips to Band B′. Once halation is on the table, the black-text-on-light-fill combo blooms least, sat-capping calms the dance, and it unifies the text colour — the most likely to sit still to a sensitive eye. Band A remains the conservative pick if you'd rather not move red/blue far. This is the one to eyeball on dark.

Your call: A, B, or B′ — and if B′, is the ×0.72 cap right, or pull further / less?

§3 · The balance model — bloom & dance, by stroke thickness (R-D6 · Apollo Labs seed)

Your question: a calculation that includes line thickness. WCAG's flat 4.5 ignores it; this does not. Halation has two modes, and thickness (as degrees of visual angle) selects which: thin → dance (saturated colour shimmers at a high-frequency edge), thick → bloom (a bright field spills across its border). Both levers are already in canon — luminance step (bloom) and the saturation ceiling (dance). First-cut indices (0–100, relative), grounded in the contrast-sensitivity function + chromatic aberration. v0 — to be calibrated against your eye, not taken as final.
