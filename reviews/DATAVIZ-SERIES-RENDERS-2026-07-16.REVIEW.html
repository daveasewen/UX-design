01The constraint map

  • col26-017 — dark colours on light backgrounds, light on dark. The 10 families split cleanly: midnight-blue · forest-green · olive-green · burnt-orange · dusk-purple are the LIGHT-mode half; rose-pink · sky-blue · mint-green · sun-yellow · apricot-orange the DARK-mode half. (olive-green is the one family dual-legal at every step.)
  • col26-011 / dv-016 — every chart colour ≥3:1 vs its background; the per-step indicatorOK receipts encode exactly this and are shown per swatch below.
  • dv-018 / dv-014 — different colour per series; same data = same colour across the journey (scope resolved by your C pick: consistency holds ACROSS the theme switch — see 02).
  • Palette guidance — "don't create rainbow-like colour treatments": five anchored hues per mode, cycled in a fixed order, rather than ten hues at once.
  • Dave's legend ruling — every legend below is colour + LETTER + name; letters repeat on the elements themselves (the colour-independent channel).
  • Vibrating boundaries (NEW — Dave 2026-07-16, from the Tuts+ article) — adjacent SATURATED, near-COMPLEMENTARY colours at near-EQUAL VALUE shimmer at the boundary: an accessibility hazard (astigmatism, sensory processing; equal-value pairs also vanish for CVD). All three legs together = risk; any leg broken = calm. Every candidate, delta pair and range below now carries vibration receipts (value-ratio ≥1.25 · hue-sep <135° · saturation <0.5 — one leg broken is enough). Structural defence in charts: the dv-004 ≥2px surface-coloured gap is precisely the neutral boundary that kills the effect; the receipts protect legends, adjacent text and gapless edges. Goes to the gate as an advisory check in _validate_dataviz.py.
