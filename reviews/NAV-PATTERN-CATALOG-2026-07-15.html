00Principles & the evidence spine

The findings that survived adversarial fact-checking. They shape defaults for every pattern below.

Exposed beats hidden — by a lot. Hiding the main nav (hamburger / icon-only) cut content discoverability by >20% and slowed tasks ~39% on desktop, ~15% on mobile; a hidden hamburger drew 17% usage vs a 42% desktop-nav average. On mobile, a labelled combination pattern (big “Browse” button) hit 89% usage vs 44% for a low-salience hidden icon.
NN/g — Hamburger Menus & Hidden Navigation; Find Navigation (desktop & mobile).

What this means for Apollo:

  • Default Expose top-level items on desktop. Collapse to a menu only when space genuinely runs out, and then label the toggle (“Menu”), don’t rely on the bare ☰ glyph — icon recognition isn’t universal.
  • Prefer a combination / “priority+” pattern (show what fits, roll the rest into “More”) over an all-or-nothing hamburger.
  • Click, not hover, opens submenus — hover excludes touch and keyboard users. If a hover affordance is added on top, use hover-intent timing (~0.1s to open, ~0.5s to close) to avoid accidental triggers. (NN/g — Menu Design; Timing Guidelines.)
  • Don’t cascade. Multi-tier fly-out dropdowns are error-prone past two levels; use a mega menu (2–3 tiers in one panel) or route to a landing page instead. (NN/g; Baymard; Microsoft.)
  • Put the real destinations at the top level, not under generic umbrellas (“Products”, “Shop”). 33% of sites bury them and users abandon the menu. (Baymard.)
Accessibility spine (normative, least time-sensitive). For site navigation use the Disclosure pattern — a real <button> with aria-expanded + aria-controls — not the ARIA menu/menubar roles, which are reserved for application-style command menus. Esc closes an open panel and returns focus to its trigger (also helps WCAG 2.1 §1.4.13). Keyboard model is two-tier: Tab moves between components, arrow keys move within one. Wrap regions in <nav> landmarks with distinct aria-labels, mark the current item with aria-current, and provide a “skip to content” link.
W3C ARIA APG — Disclosure Navigation, Keyboard Interface. One refuted claim: general site nav is not a single roving-tabindex composite — each top-level link stays independently tabbable.
Read the evidence honestly. The headline NN/g percentages come from ~2016 studies on browse/e-commerce sites; the direction is robust and repeatedly reconfirmed, but exact figures predate current touch norms, and their transfer to authenticated, task-heavy banking flows (accounts, transfers, servicing) is reasonable but not directly measured. Treat as strong defaults, not immutable law — and validate against HSBC’s own analytics where possible.

APrimary / global navigation

The persistent top-level wayfinding for the whole property. The organism everything else hangs off.

Global header / top navigation bar organism

What
The persistent bar carrying brand, primary destinations, and utility (search, account, region, sign-in). The IA’s front door.
Variants
Exposed top-level (items visible in the bar) · with dropdowns / mega menus · burger-only (all nav hidden — avoid on desktop) · hybrid / responsive-collapse (exposed on wide, collapses on narrow) · with utility rail (secondary bar above/below for locale, login, contact).
Use when
Always, for a browsable site. It’s the anchor. On task-apps (post-login banking) it often thins to brand + account + a few key actions, with the real nav in a side rail.
Avoid
Burger-only on desktop; more than ~7 top-level items (chunk the IA instead); a logo that isn’t a home link.
Responsive
Exposed items → “priority+” (overflow into “More”) → labelled drawer/off-canvas at the smallest sizes. Utility items usually move into the drawer or a bottom bar.
A11y
<header> + <nav aria-label="Primary">; skip-link first in DOM; brand link labelled “Home”; each disclosure a real button; visible focus.
Pitfalls
Sticky headers eating viewport on mobile; utility drowning primary; “mystery-meat” icon-only actions with no labels.
Apollo: organism GlobalHeader composed of atoms Brand NavLink NavToggle (disclosure) and molecules UtilityBar Search AccountMenu. Slots: brand / primary / utility. States per link: default·hover·active·current (aria-current)·focus. Density + sticky as tokens. This is the composition root for B–D.

Utility navigation molecule

What
Secondary, always-available links/actions distinct from primary IA: locale/region, language, login/register, contact, search, basket, accessibility.
Variants
Top utility strip · inline-right in the header · icon cluster · “meatball/kebab” overflow at narrow widths.
Responsive
Collapses earliest — into the drawer header or an overflow menu. Region/language often persists (regulatory).
A11y
Separate <nav aria-label="Utility">; icon-only items need aria-label; login state announced.
Apollo: UtilityBar reusing NavLink, IconButton (built), Badge (built, T5). Region/locale switcher may itself be a small menu.

BMenus — mega, dropdown, fly-out

The disclosure panels that hang off a top-level item. Where most of the depth (and most of the risk) lives.

Mega menu organism

What
A large, structured panel showing many destinations at once in a scannable layout — the recommended way to handle a broad/deep IA (handles 2–3 tiers without cascading).
Variants
Multi-column list (grouped links) · featured / promotional panel (links + a highlighted card/CTA) · tabbed / category-switching (left rail switches the right panel) · image-led (visual product/segment tiles) · mixed (columns + feature + media). For HSBC scale, tabbed and featured-panel variants cover most needs.
Use when
Broad IA with many peer destinations you want discoverable in one move (products, segments, support). Replaces fragile multi-tier dropdowns.
Avoid
As a dumping ground (structure it — headings, ≤~7 per group); hover-only open; trapping keyboard users; opening on accidental mouse-through (use hover-intent / click).
Responsive
Collapses into the mobile drawer as nested accordions — each top item expands its groups in place. Tabbed variant → sequential accordion sections.
A11y
Disclosure per top item (aria-expanded/aria-controls); panel is grouped links (headings + lists), not role=menu; Esc closes + returns focus; first-focus into panel optional; no focus trap (it’s navigation, not a dialog).
Pitfalls
Diagonal-movement problem (cursor slips out crossing to the panel — solve with an intent “triangle” or click); content hidden from SR if toggled wrong; over-tall panels on laptops.
Apollo: organism MegaMenu = NavToggle + panel of MenuGroup (heading + MenuLink list) + optional FeatureCard (reuse T5 Card) + optional TabRail (reuse T4 Tabs). Variant prop: list | featured | tabbed | media. The tabbed variant is Tabs + panels composed — a good proof of the “compose, don’t re-invent” rule.

Dropdown menu (single-tier) & menu button molecule

What
A small panel of links/actions under a trigger. As navigation = disclosure of links; as actions (e.g. a row’s “⋯” or a “New ▾” split button) = the ARIA menu pattern.
Variants
Nav dropdown (links) · action menu (commands) · split button (default action + ▾ for alternates) · select-like listbox (that’s a form control — T3, built).
Use when
≤ one tier, a handful of items. More than that → mega menu or a page.
A11y — the fork that matters
Links → Disclosure (button + aria-expanded, Tab through links, no arrow-key model required). Commands → role=menu/menuitem (roving tabindex, arrow keys, Esc, type-ahead). Pick by content type — don’t mix.
Pitfalls
Using role=menu for a nav list (breaks SR expectations, hides non-interactive content); no Esc; losing focus on close.
Apollo: two molecules on one popover shell — NavDropdown (disclosure, links) & ActionMenu (menu pattern, commands). Shell = Popover primitive shared with D. Split button = Button (built) + ActionMenu.

Fly-out / cascading menu discouraged

What
Submenus that open to the side on hover/focus, cascading multiple levels.
Verdict
Avoid beyond two tiers — “a usability nightmare” (Microsoft), error-prone, hostile to touch/keyboard, and the diagonal-movement trap. Provide it only as a desktop enhancement over an accessible click/disclosure base, or replace with a mega menu / routing page.
If unavoidable
Click-to-open, generous hit areas, intent timing, full keyboard (arrows in/out, Esc up a level), and a mobile fallback that is a flat accordion.
Apollo: not a first-class component. If HSBC IA truly needs it, model as nested ActionMenu with a documented “prefer mega menu” warning in the usage guidance.

CSide / vertical navigation

Persistent left/right rails — the backbone of authenticated banking apps, dashboards and long IAs.

Side navigation (persistent / collapsible / tree) organism

What
A vertical list of destinations alongside content. The natural primary nav for post-login product areas where the top bar thins to identity + actions.
Variants
Persistent (always open, wide) · collapsible (toggle to a mini icon rail ↔ expanded) · multi-level / tree (expandable groups, accordion or disclosure) · left vs right (right rare, used for contextual/secondary) · flyout-on-hover for the collapsed rail.
Use when
Many peer destinations, deep IA, app-like tasks, dashboards. Scales far better than a top bar for 8+ areas.
Avoid
On thin/marketing pages; nesting past ~2 levels without a tree affordance; icon-rail with unlabelled icons (needs tooltips + accessible names).
Responsive
Persistent → collapsible rail (tablet) → off-canvas drawer or bottom tab bar (phone). The collapsed rail is a genuine responsive state, not a separate component.
A11y
<nav aria-label="Sections">; expandable groups = disclosure; aria-current="page" on the active item; collapsed icons need visible-on-hover/focus labels + aria-label; keyboard: Tab through, arrows optional for tree.
Pitfalls
Losing the current-location signal when collapsed; deep trees with no “you are here”; hover-flyouts that vanish before you reach them.
Apollo: organism SideNav = NavItem (icon + label + current/expand states) + NavGroup (disclosure) + RailToggle. One component, prop state: expanded | rail + levels. Reuses the disclosure + tooltip (T4) + icon-button (T1) atoms. Highest-value organism for a banking app.

DOverlay / transient — drawers, sheets, popovers

Navigation that slides in over content, then dismisses. The mobile home of primary nav, and a desktop home for secondary/contextual nav.

Drawer / off-canvas panel organism

What
A panel that slides from an edge (left/right/top/bottom) over a scrim, holding nav or secondary content. On mobile it’s where the collapsed primary nav lives.
Variants
By edge (left = primary nav; right = filters/account; bottom = mobile sheet) · modal (scrim + focus-trap, blocks page) vs non-modal / push (shifts content, no trap) · full-height vs partial sheet.
Use when
Mobile primary nav; secondary/contextual panels that shouldn’t take permanent space (filters, account, help).
Responsive
This is the responsive target for A and C on small screens. Its content = the mega/side nav re-expressed as accordions.
A11y — differs from a menu
A modal drawer is a dialog: role=dialog + aria-modal="true", focus trap, Esc to close, focus returns to the opener, background inert. A non-modal push drawer does not trap. (Note: this is the one place a focus trap is correct — plain menus must not trap.)
Pitfalls
No focus trap on a modal drawer (SR users escape into hidden page); background scroll bleed; no return-focus.
Apollo: Drawer extends the T1 Drawer we already built (motion, focus, scrim) — generalise it: props edge, modal, size. Its body composes NavAccordion (the mobile form of MegaMenu/SideNav). Big reuse win.

Popover (as nav shell) primitive

What
A small, anchored, dismissible overlay — the shared shell under dropdowns, action menus, and tooltips-with-actions.
A11y
Positioning + collision, outside-click/Esc dismiss, focus-return; role depends on content (disclosure vs menu vs dialog).
Apollo: primitive Popover underpins B’s dropdowns and much of D. Worth building early — many components depend on it.

EIn-page / secondary navigation

Wayfinding within a page or section — long articles, product detail, settings, legal/help.

In-page anchor nav / on-this-page / scroll-spy molecule

What
A list of jump-links to sections of the current page (“On this page” / table of contents), often sticky, often highlighting the section in view (scroll-spy).
Variants
Sticky left/right rail (desktop) · inline “jump to” list (top of page) · sticky sub-bar (horizontal, tabs-like) · collapsible ToC on mobile · scroll-spy on/off.
Use when
Long single pages: help articles, T&Cs, product detail, guides. Reduces scrolling and shows structure.
Responsive
Rail → collapsible “Contents” disclosure or a horizontal sticky sub-bar at the top.
A11y
<nav aria-label="On this page">; links move focus to the target heading (target has tabindex="-1"); scroll-spy updates aria-current; respect reduced-motion for smooth-scroll.
Pitfalls
Scroll-spy flicker; sticky header covering the anchored heading (use scroll-margin-top); JS-only (must degrade to plain anchors).
Apollo: InPageNav = list of AnchorLink + optional scroll-spy behaviour (CSS-first where possible). Related to your “you may also be interested in” — different intent, similar list atom.

Local / sub-navigation · Tabs-as-nav · Segmented-as-nav molecule

What
Navigation scoped to a section: a sub-bar of sibling pages, or Tabs used to switch routed views (URL changes), or a Segmented control to filter one view.
The distinction that matters
Tabs/segmented = one page, switching panels (tab semantics). Sub-nav = links to other pages (nav semantics). If the URL changes, it’s navigation (links), not a tabset — don’t use role=tablist for routing.
Responsive
Horizontal tabs → scrollable tabs → “More” overflow → select/segmented at the narrowest.
Apollo: reuse T4 Tabs and T3 Segmented — but document the semantic rule: routed switching = SubNav (links), in-page switching = Tabs. A common real-world bug worth gating.

FWayfinding — breadcrumb, pagination, steps

“Where am I, where can I go back to, and how far through am I.”

Breadcrumbs molecule

What
A trail from home to the current page, showing hierarchy and enabling one-tap ascent.
Variants
Location (IA hierarchy — most common) · attribute (faceted, e.g. filters) · history (rare, path taken) · truncated (Home › … › Current) · with dropdowns at each level.
Use when
Deep hierarchies (≥3 levels): product catalogues, support trees, docs. Redundant on flat IAs.
Responsive
Collapse middle to “…”, or reduce to a single “← Back to {parent}” on mobile.
A11y
<nav aria-label="Breadcrumb"> → ordered list; current page = aria-current="page" and not a link; separators are CSS/decorative (aria-hidden).
Pitfalls
Using breadcrumbs as the only nav; making the current item a link; separators read out by SRs.
Apollo: we built T4 Breadcrumb — extend with truncation + per-level dropdown variants and confirm the aria-current/ordered-list semantics against Carbon’s spec.

Pagination · “Back to top” · Step / wizard nav molecule

Pagination
Page-number navigation for long result sets. Variants: numbered · prev/next only · “load more” · infinite scroll (avoid for findable/legal content — no footer access, no position memory). We built T4 Pagination. A11y: <nav aria-label="Pagination">, aria-current="page".
Back to top
A floating return-to-top affordance on long pages; appears after scroll. A11y: real button, respects reduced-motion.
Step / wizard
Progress through a multi-step flow (apply for an account, transfer setup). Variants: numbered stepper · progress bar · vertical steps. Shows completed / current / upcoming; may or may not allow jumping back. Critical for banking journeys. A11y: aria-current="step", state announced.
Apollo: Stepper (journey) is a high-value new organism for banking flows — reuses the T1 Stepper control idea but at the page-flow scale (distinct component). BackToTop = trivial reuse of IconButton.

GContextual / related & footer

Navigation driven by content relationships rather than the fixed IA.

Contextual / related — “You may also be interested in” molecule

What
Cross-links to related content/products surfaced in-context: related articles, “people also viewed”, next-best-action, cross-sell. Compensates for the discoverability cost of any hidden nav.
Variants
Link list · card carousel · inline “see also” · end-of-article block · sidebar module. Content is usually algorithmic/editorial, so the component is a presentation shell fed data.
Use when
To deepen engagement and rescue findability. Especially valuable when primary nav is collapsed.
A11y
<nav aria-label="Related"> or a labelled region; if a carousel, full keyboard + reduced-motion + pause.
Apollo: RelatedLinks (list) and RelatedCards (reuse T5 Card + a carousel/track). Data-driven; the component owns layout + states, not the recommendations.

Footer navigation (fat footer) & sitemap organism

What
Comprehensive grouped links at page bottom — a full secondary IA plus legal, regional, social, regulatory. For banks this carries heavy compliance content (terms, security, accessibility, regulator statements).
Variants
Fat footer (multi-column groups) · minimal footer · with locale/region switcher · with newsletter/CTA · accordion-collapsed on mobile.
Responsive
Columns stack; groups often become accordions on mobile.
A11y
<footer> + <nav aria-label="Footer">; grouped with headings; accordion groups = disclosure.
Apollo: FooterNav = NavGroup (reused from SideNav) in a responsive column grid + legal/utility strip. Compliance-heavy, so a real HSBC need.

HMobile-specific

Patterns native to small, touch, task-focused screens — the HSBC mobile app’s home turf.

Bottom tab bar organism

What
3–5 primary destinations pinned to the bottom, always visible, thumb-reachable. The default primary nav for task apps (banking apps use this heavily).
Use when
≤5 flat, equally-important top destinations you want one-tap and persistent. Ideal for “Home / Accounts / Move money / Cards / More”.
Avoid
>5 items (last becomes “More”); using it for deep IA; hiding it on scroll if it’s primary.
Responsive
Mobile/app only; on wider screens promote to a top bar or side rail. The “More” tab opens a drawer/sheet with the overflow.
A11y
<nav aria-label="Primary">; each tab labelled (icon + text), aria-current="page"; 44px+ targets; safe-area insets.
Pitfalls
Icon-only tabs (label them); >5 items; competing with the OS home indicator.
Apollo: organism BottomTabBar = TabBarItem (icon + label + current + badge). Reuses icon + badge atoms. Pairs with the “More” Drawer.

Hamburger + accordion menu · Priority+ / “More” · Sticky action bar molecule/organism

Hamburger → drawer
The labelled toggle that opens the off-canvas drawer holding the full nav as nested accordions. Acceptable on mobile with a label; the drawer content is the responsive form of A/B/C.
Priority+ / overflow
Show as many top items as fit; roll the rest into a “More” menu. The evidence-preferred middle path between fully-exposed and fully-hidden. Great for a responsive top bar.
Sticky mobile action bar
Context actions pinned at the bottom of a task screen (e.g. “Continue”, “Pay”) — navigation-adjacent; keep distinct from the tab bar.
A11y
Labelled toggle (aria-expanded); drawer focus management (see D); overflow “More” = disclosure or menu per content; sticky bars must not obscure focused fields.
Apollo: NavToggle + Drawer + NavAccordion cover the hamburger path. PriorityNav is a responsive behaviour of GlobalHeader, not a separate component. Sticky action bar reuses the T5 actionbar + T1 button.

Accessibility model — in one place

Get these right once at the atom level and every nav organism inherits them. All normative (W3C ARIA APG / WCAG), so the least time-sensitive part of the catalog.

  • Landmarks & labels: every nav region in a <nav> with a distinct aria-label (Primary, Utility, Breadcrumb, Footer, On this page). One skip-to-content link, first in the DOM.
  • Disclosure, not menubar, for site nav: real <button> + aria-expanded + aria-controls. Reserve role=menu/menubar for command menus (actions), with their roving-tabindex + arrow-key model.
  • Current location: aria-current="page" (or "step") on the active item — a required state on every nav-item atom.
  • Keyboard: two-tier — Tab between components, arrows within a composite. Esc closes any open panel and returns focus to its trigger. General nav links stay individually tabbable (not one roving stop).
  • Overlays: a modal drawer/sheet is a dialog — trap focus, aria-modal, background inert, return focus on close. A menu/disclosure must not trap.
  • Motion: honour prefers-reduced-motion for slide/scroll/scroll-spy. Hover-revealed content meets WCAG 1.4.13 (dismissable, hoverable, persistent).
  • Targets & RTL: ≥44px touch targets; mirror horizontal nav, chevrons and drawer edges under RTL (Material bidirectionality).

Apollo decomposition + Tranche 7 scope

Atomise first, compose up — and it collapses to a surprisingly small set of atoms feeding a few organisms. Everything token- and mode-governed (mono / UI / SC), no hardcoded styling, motion in CSS.

The atoms & molecules almost everything shares

NavLink NavItem (icon+label+current+expand) NavToggle (disclosure) MenuLink MenuGroup (heading+list) Popover (shell) NavAccordion Brand TabBarItem

Reused from built tranches: IconButton, Tooltip, Tabs, Breadcrumb, Pagination, Segmented, Card, Badge, Drawer, Actionbar. The nav tranche is heavily a composition exercise — a strong proof of the pro-forma’s “compose, don’t re-invent” claim.

The HSBC-specific note

Your listed models map cleanly: mega-menu + variants → item 4; side nav → item 3; in-page anchor lists → item 7; “you may also be interested in” → item 8 (RelatedLinks); burger-only vs exposed top-level → the responsive states of item 2 (with the evidence steering you to exposed-by-default, labelled-collapse); fly-outs → deliberately down-weighted in favour of mega menus; drawers → item 5. Nothing in your list needs a bespoke component outside this set — which is the reassuring result.
