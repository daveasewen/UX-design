00Scope & why

Two audiences under one bank: retail/personal and institutional/markets. Weight retail, cover both.

HSBC spans everyday banking (balances, spend, budgets, net worth) and markets (rates, FX, portfolios, trading). Most Apollo screens are retail, so the kit leads with the everyday-money charts and treats trading/candlestick types as a second tier. The through-line for both: charts exist to answer a question and drive a decision — the visual is a means, not an ornament.

01Universal principles

The stable, decades-old core that every credible source repeats.

  • Start from the question, not the chart. Pick the type that answers the user's decision; don't reach for a favourite. Audience data-literacy sets the ceiling on complexity.
  • Encode to human perception. People read length and position accurately, but judge area and angle poorly — so bars beat pies, and a pie past a few slices is guesswork. (Cleveland/McGill lineage; the KB's 6-slice pie cap is the same instinct.)
  • Maximise the data-ink ratio (Tufte). Most pixels should be data. Strip chart junk — heavy gridlines, 3D, decoration. This aligns exactly with the HSBC "flat 2D fills, no 3D, no gradients" rule.
  • Be honest with scale. Don't truncate/compress axes to exaggerate trends; always show units in labels/titles ("$2.4M", not "2.4"). (KB {#dv-001}; bars need a zero baseline, lines optional.)
  • Consistency lowers cognitive load. Same colour = same meaning everywhere; uniform type, labels, and layout across a journey. (KB categorisation-consistency {#dv-014}.)
  • Colour with intent, never for decoration — use it to focus attention or encode a variable, never as a chart background.
The dashboard layout consensus: headline KPIs and exceptions at the top, related metrics grouped, and ~6–8 charts per view — more than that and the view stops being scannable. (Sigma; UXPin; Tableau.)

Beyond the four: KPI / stat cards ("metric + delta + spark" — the KB already lists this) are the most-used "chart" in banking and the natural first build; waterfall for P&L / balance bridges; progress bars/rings for budget-vs-limit; bullet for target-vs-actual.   • Bar / column: vertical · horizontal · grouped/clustered · stacked · 100%-stacked · segmented · diverging · butterfly / tornado (back-to-back) · range/floating · rounded vs square · bullet · waterfall · radial/polar bar.
  • Line: standard · multi-series · stepped · area · stacked-area · spark · range/confidence band · dual-axis · slope.
  • Composition: pie · donut · semi-donut/gauge · stacked-bar · Sankey · treemap.
  • Relationship / other: grouped bar · scatter · bubble · heatmap · candlestick/OHLC · progress ring · KPI stat card.
The method dossier's inventory will be variant-complete (bank-scale), each variant tagged by intent-family, retail-vs-markets, and build tier.

03Finance-specific conventions

Where money changes the rules.

  • Freshness is trust. In finance a stale figure reads as a broken figure — timestamp data, and show "as of" clearly. Real-time/near-real-time where it matters (rates, balances).
  • Units and precision are non-negotiable. Currency symbol, magnitude (K/M), and period must be explicit; ambiguous numbers erode trust faster in finance than anywhere else.
  • Scale honesty is a compliance-grade concern, not just taste — a compressed axis on a returns chart is a mis-selling risk. (Reinforces KB {#dv-001}/{#dv-003}.)
  • Drill-down is the default interaction. Summary card → detail: click a KPI or a category to expand into merchant-level / transaction-level breakdowns.
  • Consistency of colour-to-meaning across a journey is stronger in finance because users hold running mental models ("blue is revenue, orange is expense") across many screens.

04Retail / personal-finance patterns

The everyday-money vocabulary — the bulk of Apollo's charts.

  • Category breakdown — donut or stacked column of spend by category, with category totals + month-over-month comparison and merchant-level detail on drill-in.
  • Budget vs limit — progress bars/rings showing spending against a cap, with a clear over/under state.
  • Net-worth / balance trend — a continuous line with interactive points + tooltips; the "gold standard" for value-over-time.
  • Sparklines in tables — a tiny inline trend per account/holding row; high information density, minimal chrome. Pairs with the KPI stat card.
  • Money-flow — Sankey from income sources through spending categories; increasingly common in modern PFM apps.
Perception speed is the point: the brain reads a visual pattern in ~13ms vs far longer for text — for anxious money decisions, a glanceable chart lowers the stress of the task. Design for the 13ms read first, detail on demand.

05Gain / loss encoding

Ruling (Dave, 2026-07-16): the "never use red/green" orthodoxy is overblown — don't be precious about it.

Green-up / red-down is a well-understood finance convention and Apollo won't treat it as forbidden. The colour-blindness caution is real but overstated once luminance differs and a second cue is present. So use the conventional gain/loss colouring where it helps, and make it robust the way good charts do anyway —

  • Sign + direction: explicit +/− and up/down arrows (the primary, colour-independent signal).
  • Position: above/below a zero baseline carries most of the gain/loss meaning on its own.
  • Direct labelling of the value, so the number means the same regardless of hue.
One thing to confirm (it touches a documented brand rule): the HSBC KB currently states no red in charts, red reserved for brand/error (KB {#dv-017} / colour-standards). Using red for "loss" needs that rule explicitly overridden for gain/loss semantics — flagging so we relax it deliberately, not by accident.

06Accessibility

WCAG-grounded, and it aligns with HSBC's "lead the sector" bar.

  • Never colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1). Every series distinguished by colour must also be distinguished by shape/marker, position, or direct label.
  • Legends are fine — but pair colour with an alphabetic signifier. Label each series/segment with a letter on the chart element itself (bars A · B · C · D · E; segments/lines likewise), and show colour + letter + name in the legend — the letter is the colour-independent channel, so the legend reads without relying on hue. Direct labelling adjacent to elements is still ideal where it fits. (KB {#dv-006}.)
  • The data table is the accessible fallback — a real (optionally visually-hidden) <table> + a link to it. This satisfies both the KB "tabular alternative" rule {#dv-005} and screen-reader access in one move.
  • Programmatic meaning — chart has an accessible name/description; live regions (aria-live="polite") for updating values; ticks/gridlines hidden from the a11y tree.
  • Contrast — chart building blocks (axes, labels, gridlines) ≥3:1; any text ≥4.5:1. (KB {#dv-016}; matches the indicator-contrast gate.)
Direction — texture, sparingly (Dave 2026-07-16): willing to explore fill patterns, but sparingly and stylishly — e.g. a split / stacked bar could carry a single chevron-style treatment, never a zoo of competing hatchings. Guideline: at most one tasteful pattern per chart, still flat (no gradient / no 3D). Otherwise carry non-colour redundancy in shape markers, direct labels, position (+ the ≥2px block gap, KB {#dv-004}).

07Interaction & motion

Where digital earns its keep — Dave's "digital has an advantage."

  • Tooltips / reference points — on hover/focus, repeat the point's values on both axes without obscuring the chart. (KB {#dv-line-004}.)
  • Show/hide & filter series — toggle lines/categories; filter by date range, account, region. Essential once a chart has >2 sets. (KB {#dv-line-007}.)
  • Cross-filter & drill-down — selecting in one chart filters the dashboard; clicking a summary opens detail. The core analytical loop.
  • Entry/transition motion — bars grow, lines draw, donuts sweep on load or on data change; motion should clarify change, never decorate. Honour prefers-reduced-motion.
Apollo build note: all of this is achievable CSS-first — reveal/transition motion as CSS keyframes/transitions on SVG elements (stays DEF-003-clean, no JS-driven motion), with JS limited to behaviour (toggle, filter, tooltip position, data→geometry). This is why a canvas library is the wrong tool: it forfeits CSS styling, per-element motion, and the accessible DOM.

08What it means for Apollo

How the research lands on our decisions.

  • Approach — custom SVG + a token/CSS layer as the spine; borrow the table-as-source pattern for the simple types; a light scale/layout helper only for the hard maths. Reject canvas. (Detail in the method dossier.)
  • Palette — the supporting/"supercharge" palette (10 families × 5 steps). The open decision is the series assignment (V7) — which family/step is series-1/2/3 per mode; settle it with real renders in both modes, respecting the per-mode indicatorOK flags.
  • Gain/loss without red — sign + arrow + position + a non-red palette step; never red, never colour-alone.
  • Round-one kit (proposed) — KPI "metric + delta + spark" card · line (+ sparkline) · bar/column (+ stacked) · donut. These cover the bulk of retail banking and exercise the palette, the flat-fill rule, the table fallback, and CSS motion.
  • Gates — several KB rules are already BLOCKING-derivable and map to existing gates: palette-only fills (compose gate's 0-rogue-hex), building-block ≥3:1 (indicator-contrast gate), flat-fill/no-gradient (inspectable on SVG), ≥2px block separation. Charts become gate-able because they're SVG+tokens, not canvas.
