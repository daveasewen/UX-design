DP-08 · density · missed W1, missed W2

"Each headline metric shows value, signed delta against a named period, and a trend shape; the shape is a sparkline, never a gauge or ring."DP-08, DASHBOARD-PRINCIPLES v1

Two of the six tiles are not metrics. They are states wearing a metric's clothes.

Four tiles are true KPIs: value, signed delta, "vs yesterday", a sparkline. The other two — Awaiting approval and Undrawn facilities — are a count-of-total and a ratio-to-limit. Neither has a period to move against, so no honest signed delta exists. The template still gave them the sparkline slot, and "Undrawn facilities" fills it with twelve identical points: a flat black line that means nothing.

The row as shipped, 1440. Tiles 4 and 6 carry "14 payments · 5 need you" and "40% drawn · Reviewed 1 Sep" in the delta slot (page L283, L309). No signed delta, no named period.

The two status tiles at 2×. The "Undrawn facilities" sparkline is twelve points at y=23.0 (L316) — a slot forced a shape. This is the visual tell that they are a different kind of thing.

Your ruling s247-D3 already says where this goes: "if they are the only statuses shown they must not sit inside the other cards … their own surface, or in tandem with a status bar component at the top." The row is still hiding them. What is open is which surface.

DP-18 · rhythm · met on the template, missed on the page

"The chart fills its tile's width AND height … fit re-positions, never scales."s248-D2 · and s248-D3: every component in a tile must answer the tile

The chart got both axes on the template, then lost one on the composed page — because two engines fought over the same SVG.

At #248 the vertical fit (VFIT) was written into the dashboard template's hand-carried fit stub. The real chart component ships its own engine in the generated partial. On a composed page both select every svg.dv-fit: one re-derives the gridlines for a taller plot, the other re-pins the viewBox to 260 last, and neither moves the series. Grid and data disagreed, so the builder dropped the stub. Result: a 260px chart in a 438px tile, 44px of ground under it.

Mechanism. Two height authorities on one selector; last writer owns the viewBox. The fix is one engine that owns both axes, living where the x contract already lives: the FIT module of dv-behaviour.js, injected into all 14 dataviz members. Source: DIAGRAM.json, R-rerun finding 4, DP18 findings 1–2.

Shipped: svg 821×260, 44.1px of ground under the figure in a 438.1px tile.

Prototype, one engine: svg 821×304, slack 0, gridlines 274/187/101/14, every one of 36 marks on its line (deviation 0), glyph sizes unchanged. Not 415: the composed tile's head + legend take 86px more than the template's bar chart.

What VFIT lacked for a line chart was four y paths, not one. The stub only moved rect / line / text. A line chart also needs polyline points, circle centres, and the square and diamond marks — and the square must move its centre, not be re-scaled like a bar (the first prototype pass floated series C's diamonds 14px off their line). That is exactly the kind of defect a library engine with a test catches once for everyone; a per-template stub catches it never.
