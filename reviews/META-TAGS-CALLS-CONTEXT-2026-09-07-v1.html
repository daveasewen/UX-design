Where the brief did not name a value, the lane picked one and listed the pick. Under your rule — Claude authors, Dave vetoes (s251-D15) — those picks were shown to you and you kept all of them (s254-D1).

Before the calls · the six fields

What the fields are for

One sentence each. The metas are the canon; roles.json is a cross-check (s252-D1).

providesThe role this component fills in a page (chart-panel, headline-metric, wayfinding…). One role per component.
answersThe reader's question the component answers. For charts this equals the existing intent word (comparison, composition). For non-charts, nine new words were ruled in at s253-D3.
shapeThe shape of the data it takes. Two components with the same shape are candidates to swap for one another.
spanThe width band it needs on the 12-column canon, e.g. cols 6–12.
priorityWithin one role, which provider wins by default. Must be unique per role or the ordering is unreadable.
whenThe condition under which this component beats or yields to its siblings in the role.

Why these exist at all: so that a brief can ask for "a chart-panel that answers comparison, six columns wide" and a resolver can pick the component — rather than a human, or a model, guessing.

Page A · the eleven non-chart metas · eight calls

Calls A1–A8

Authored by lane A on the metas the bento template composes: kpi-tile, stat-card, chart-bar, summary, status-indicator, layout-utilities, breadcrumbs, headers, button, navigations, app-shell-top-nav.

A1

The nine answers words are phrased as reader questionsconvention

The alternative

Nouns: metric, status, location, actions, destinations.

What was chosen

Questions: one-number, status-of-one, where-am-i, what-can-i-do, where-can-i-go, what-is-this, what-holds-the-page, where-does-it-go, details-before-commit.

Why. The five existing chart intent words (comparison, change-over-time…) are already the chart's question. Keeping non-chart words in the same register means a brief reads as one vocabulary, not two.

If struck. The nine words in chart-intents.json are re-cut as nouns and the ten metas that carry them are re-edited. Your s253-D3 ruling admitted the words; this call is only their phrasing.

A2

summary answers details-before-commit, not a countcontent

The alternative

Follow roles.json, which files summary under status-surface as "n × count as a key/value list", and give it a counting word.

What was chosen

Follow the meta's own purpose prose — "confirming details before or after an action (a payment review, a receipt)".

Why. The brief said to read values off the meta, never off roles.json (s252-D1). The two disagree here, and the lane sided with the meta.

If struck. Either the word changes to a counting word, or — more likely the real fix — the purpose prose on summary is wrong and the component's role membership should be reviewed. This is the one place the two sources visibly conflict.

A3

kpi-tile answers two wordscontent

The alternative

One word per non-chart meta: one-number only.

What was chosen

[one-number, change-over-time] — the tile states a number and, via its embedded sparkline, the shape of its change.

Why. The second word is exactly the intent word the sparkline atom already carries. Dropping it would make kpi-tile and stat-card answer the same question, and the only thing separating them would be the when predicate (series present or not).

If struck. One edit on one meta. The resolver still passes; the tile just becomes less findable from a brief that asks for change-over-time.

A4

chart-bar took priority 84, not roles.json's 60superseded by B2

What happened. Lanes A and B ran at the same time and could not see each other's files. Lane B built a ladder for the thirteen charts from 92 down to 30 (see B1). Lane A, owning chart-bar, chose 84 — the free rung directly below chart-line's 88 — to keep chart-bar in the top tier without knowing B had proposed 92 for it.

Where it ended. The conductor enacted lane B's 92 at commit 6a41915. So 84 never survived to canon; this call is recorded for the history and is moot. See B2 for the decision that actually stands.

A5

No not-with promoted from prosescope

The alternative

Migrate every edges.mustNotNeighbour entry that names a resolvable slug — e.g. chart-bar's component:stat-card — into the new machine-readable not-with field.

What was chosen

Promote only the one exclusion the ROLES review named (kpi-tile ↛ status-surface). Leave the ~50 prose entries as a declared migration backlog.

Why. The brief authorised one. Migrating fifty on a lane's own authority would have been the overreach s251-D15 is designed to catch.

If struck. Say "migrate the resolvable ones" and a lane does it in a pass; the resolver's reciprocal check then gets real work to do. This is more a queued task than a veto.

A6

not-with on kpi-tile targets a role, not a metastructure

The alternative

Re-cut the exclusion against each meta in the role individually: kpi-tile ↛ status-indicator, kpi-tile ↛ summary. Every exclusion then has a symmetric partner.

What was chosen

Keep the exclusion as the ROLES page wrote it — against the role status-surface. The validator honestly prints "reciprocal NOT DERIVABLE (target is a ROLE, not a meta)".

Why. It mirrors what you signed off on the ROLES page. The cost is one exclusion with no reciprocal.

If struck. Two entries replace one, and a rule "exclusions are always meta-to-meta" enters the schema. Small, but it is a schema rule, so it should be yours.

A7

when is two halves separated by an em-dashstructure · same as B6

The alternative

Two fields: when (a machine-readable gate) and beats (prose naming the sibling). A schema addition.

What was chosen

One string: shape = one-measure-with-series AND series != none — beats stat-card whenever a series exists…. Left of the dash is the gate; right is the prose.

Why. The adopted research (R2, s253-D1) asked that each when names the sibling it beats or yields to. Putting it after a dash keeps a future parser able to read the gate alone without a schema change today.

If struck. A schema addition plus a 24-meta rewrite. Cheap now, dearer once anything parses the string. Note that nothing parses when yet — this is still open, see the last section.

A8

button and layout-utilities got shapes that mean "no data"structure

The alternative

Omit the shape field on components that carry no data.

What was chosen

Author no-data on button and arbitrary-blocks on layout-utilities, so every one of the 24 metas carries all six fields.

Why. Uniformity — a resolver that can assume the field is present is simpler than one that must handle absence. The cost is two vocabulary values that describe the absence of a shape.

If struck. Two field deletions and the schema makes shape optional. Nothing else moves.

Page B · the thirteen chart metas · ten calls

Calls B1–B10

Authored by lane B on every chart except chart-bar (lane A's). The charts are where priority matters most, because thirteen components share one role.

B1

The ladder is 92→30 in steps of 4structure

The alternative

Keep roles.json's numbers (60, 60, 60, 60, 60, 50, 50, 45, 40, 40, 40, 35, 30) and break ties by ones.

What was chosen

92 · 88 · 80 · 76 · 72 · 64 · 60 · 56 · 48 · 44 · 40 · 36 · 30. Steps of 4 so a new chart can be inserted between any two without renumbering.

Why. The resolver requires uniqueness within a role — roles.json's five-way tie at 60 was always illustrative (s252-D1). Starting at 92 leaves headroom above the top.

If struck. Thirteen numbers change and the resolver re-runs. Nothing downstream reads the absolute value; only the order matters.

B2

chart-bar sits at the top of the role: 92the one that matters

The alternative

chart-line first (88), chart-bar second (84). Lane A's number.

What was chosen · on disk at 6a41915

chart-bar first (92), chart-line second (88).

Why. chart-bar is the default answer to the most common intent (comparison) and the bento template composes it. When a brief asks for "a chart-panel" with nothing more, this is the component it gets.

What it decides. The default chart of the whole library. Everything else on these two pages is formatting or scope; this is the one call with a visible product consequence. If you would rather a dashboard's default panel be a line chart, this is the number to change: 92 → 84.

B3

The tie at 40: pie 48 > candlestick 44 > butterfly-h 40content

The alternative

Any other order of the three roles.json tied at 40.

What was chosen

Ordered by how often a brief reaches for the form: composition snapshot, then OHLC, then a mirrored pair.

Why. Their when predicates never overlap (composition vs OHLC vs two-series-mirrored), so the order is only ever consulted when a brief is silent. It matters almost never.

If struck. Three numbers. No consequence unless a brief asks for a chart-panel with no intent word.

B4

shape is written as <x-dimension> × <mark>structure

The alternative

A single label per chart: bar, line, donut.

What was chosen

A two-part string: time-series × 1–5-series, categories × two-series-mirrored, parts-of-whole.

Why. Equality of shape strings is what the resolver's instead-of grade compares. Donut and pie both say parts-of-whole on purpose, so the resolver knows they are substitutes. A label would never collide.

If struck. The substitute grade stops working until a different equality rule replaces it. Note page A's eleven non-chart shapes use a different grammar (one-measure-with-series); the two grammars are still unreconciled and neither is a closed list — see the last section.

B5

span is read off each meta's own responsive prosecontent

The alternative

A house rule: every chart-panel spans 6–12.

What was chosen

Charts whose FIT hooks are not wired get max 8 (boxplot, scatter); circles get 6–6 (DV-D02 excludes them from width compression); declared-fraction charts get 6–12; sparkline 2–4.

Why. Width past 8 columns on an unwired chart is empty stage. The span is what the component can actually do today, not what it should do.

If struck. The spans become aspirational and a dashboard may place a chart at a width it cannot fill. Better to wire the FIT hooks and then widen the span — that is queued work, not a veto.

B6

when two halves, em-dashsame as A7

Identical call to A7, made independently by lane B — the two lanes converged on the same shape without seeing each other. One strike covers both.

B7

not-with is authored in one direction onlystructure

The alternative

Write both sides: chart-pie ↛ chart-donut on pie, and chart-donut ↛ chart-pie on donut.

What was chosen

Write one side. The resolver derives and prints the reciprocal and writes it nowhere (s251-D6, ISO 25964).

Why. Two homes for one fact is what ADR-0017 forbids. If both are authored they can drift apart.

If struck. The schema gains a rule requiring both sides plus a consistency check. More text, more to keep in sync, no new information.

B8

The dv-014 colour-reuse edges were not promotedscope

Several charts carry a mustNotNeighbour entry reading "a second chart re-using these series colours for different data". It names no component — it is a rule about colour, not about a sibling — and s251-D6 makes a slug required on not-with. So they stay where they are, as backlog. The same reasoning as A5; nothing to strike unless you want colour rules modelled as exclusions, which they are not.

B9

Chart-bullet provides headline-metric onlycontent

The alternative

Let Chart-bullet provide both headline-metric and chart-panel, since it is drawn as a chart.

What was chosen

One role — headline-metric, at priority 40 beside stat-card and kpi-tile. Its answers stays comparison because for charts answers must equal intent.

Why. ROLES-REVIEW call 3, which you kept: one role per component. A bullet chart is a KPI tile with a target band, not a panel.

If struck. The one-role-per-component rule weakens for every component, not just this one.

B10

chart-stacked-area keeps a two-word answerscontent

The alternative

Collapse to one word — composition — and lose that it is also a trend chart.

What was chosen

[change-over-time, composition], copied from the meta's existing intent array, which the resolver asserts equality against.

Why. The intent array already said two words; inventing a new single word would have created the second home ADR-0017 forbids.

If struck. The intent field changes too, and the resolver's equality check either passes on the new pair or the assertion is loosened. Mirror of A3.

What "keep all" did not settle

These were declared unproven by both lanes and are not covered by s254-D1. Each will come back as its own question.

shape's storeTwenty-two shape values exist across two grammars (page A's one-measure-with-series, page B's time-series × 1–5-series) and nothing checks them. Whether shape becomes a closed list with its own resolver, and which grammar wins, is open.
when's field listEighteen field names appear on the left of the em-dash across 24 metas. Nothing parses them; nothing says which names are legal. Page A's count is a human reading — V-B found the script-computed figure is 11 fields on page B where the page says 12.
two priority homespriority-unique binds the metas only. roles.json still shows five chart-panels at 60 and 26 of 27 input providers with no priority. Lane C's Q1.
intent + answersFor charts these are two fields carrying one fact, held equal by assertion. ADR-0017's own target. Lane C's Q3.
sparkline as a panelWhether a brief asking for chart-panel may ever get chart-sparkline (priority 30). ROLES-REVIEW §03 left it in the role.
R1The masked-recovery calibration test from the KREPE research lane, deferred at s253-D1 ("2, 3 and 1 can wait I guess").
runway-barThe fifteenth intent carrier; neither lane touched it. Its role and priority are unauthored.
