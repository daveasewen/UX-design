Your instinct was right and it has a structural cause. Amber is an intrinsically LIGHT hue. Darkening it far enough for white text does not produce a dark amber, it produces brown — there is no path through that. Going lighter with dark ink is how amber wants to work: the incumbent #FFBB33 with the digital black already reads 10.28:1, comfortably AAA.

But that is exactly where the icons-and-arrows problem you sensed bites, and it is worth naming precisely.
Two different rules are in play, and they pull opposite ways for amber.
— Text on a filled ground (tag, badge) → WCAG 1.4.3, 4.5:1. A light amber fill with dark ink sails through.
— An icon, arrow or fill edge against the page → WCAG 1.4.11, 3:1. Light amber against a white page is 1.69:1 — it fails badly. The one amber that passes as an ink is the dark #C58720 you just rejected as a ground.

So amber may genuinely need two tokens: a GROUND and an INK. Red, green and blue don't, because they sit at mid luminance and work as both. Amber cannot be both. That is not a defect in the palette — it is the hue.

2 · The amber question, isolated

Option 1 — one light amber, dark ink, and accept it cannot be an ink. Tags and badges only. Arrows and standalone amber text are then not permitted — status is carried by the badge, never by a loose glyph. Simple rule, real restriction.

Option 2 — two tokens. rag/amber/ground light for fills, rag/amber/ink dark for arrows, icons and text. Honest to the hue; one more concept to teach, and the pair must never be swapped.

Option 3 — outline the fill. Keep the light ground and give the tag a darker amber border so its EDGE clears 3:1 while the fill stays light. Solves 1.4.11 without a second ink token — but adds a border to a square-cornered mono component, which is a look decision, not just a contrast one.

Note the inversion. On a dark page the amber problem REVERSES: light amber as an ink reads 11.16:1 against #111111 — it is the best ink of the four. So amber's ink and ground may legitimately differ BY MODE, not just by role. Dark-mode green #1AA05C on white text is still 3.37 and remains an open fix.
