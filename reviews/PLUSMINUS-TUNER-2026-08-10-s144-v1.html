Why this is text, not fill — one line

In the fills, meaning is carried by the label and the roundel glyph, so fill contrast is a salience lever, not a legibility requirement (R-D6). On the amount-display numeral, meaning is carried by the sign — U+2212 minus for negative, no sign for positive/zero — so colour there is also salience, not the only channel. But text carries a second obligation fills do not: it must be readable (WCAG 1.4.3), independent of what it means. That is the only reason these four values must clear 4.5:1.

Colour model

HSB is primary — Dave's words, the Figma convention. OKLCH drives saturation via chroma and brightness via lightness instead; the repo's own colour notes are already written in OKLCH (knowledge/tokens/semantic-colour.json $note fields, e.g. "L0.72 C0.070 H241"). Hue stays locked either way — whichever model is driving, the other model's numbers are shown live below the sliders, computed from the resulting colour (they may drift by a fraction of a degree/unit from the locked constant when the OTHER model drives — that is expected: HSB-hue and OKLCH-hue are different constructions and are not perfectly interconvertible).

Four cells — hue locked, drag saturation & brightness (or chroma & lightness)

Each cell's slider zero-point is its ruled seed hex reconciled to the shared locked hue (§ hue check, above) — for the two DARK seeds that reconciliation is exact (the dark fills are the hue source). For the two LIGHT seeds it shifts by ≤1 unit/255 in one channel (#B92F1E → #B9301E, #2B7E4F → #2B7E4B; contrast 6.02→5.99 and 5.00→5.01 respectively — both still comfortably clear AA 4.5, imperceptible on screen). Each "ruled seed" chip below shows the literal ruled hex for provenance; "Reset to seed" returns to the reconciled start, not a hue-drifted copy of that literal hex.
