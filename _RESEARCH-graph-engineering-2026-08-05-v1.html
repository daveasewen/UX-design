01

Provenance

The term is eighteen days old

Worth establishing before anything is compared: this is not a research development. It is a naming event, and its whole documented history fits in a paragraph.

  • 7 June 2026 — Addy Osmani publishes the essay that popularises “loop engineering”, building on Peter Steinberger's slogan and on Boris Cherny's line that he no longer prompts Claude, he writes the loops that do.
  • 18 July 2026 — Steinberger asks on X: “Are we still talking loops or did we shift to graphs yet?” Hours later Hamel Husain posts the obituary: “Loop Engineering Is Dead. Enter Graph Engineering.”
  • By 19 July — courses, roadmaps and tool stacks. Threads claiming Microsoft, Stanford and Anthropic had all discovered graph engineering simultaneously.
  • 20 July 2026 — Turing Post fact-checks the wave.
  • 22 July 2026 — LangChain responds directly, in a post titled around the three years they had already been doing it.

Loop engineering lasted six weeks. Someone has already proposed “metagraph engineering”. The useful posture is therefore not adoption — it is extraction: take the taxonomy, discard the frame.

“Graph engineering isn't a new idea. It's the latest name for a well established approach to building reliable agents.” Sydney Runkle & Harrison Chase, LangChain — 22 July 2026

02

Taxonomy

“Graph” now means four different things

This is the part worth keeping. Turing Post's separation is clean, and it is the frame the rest of this document uses. Anything connected qualifies as a graph, so the word is technically available to everyone — but the four describe entirely different problems.

TypeDescribesUsed for
Control graphWhat runs next, and under which conditionsOrchestrating agent workflows — LangGraph, Google ADK
Knowledge graphEntities and relationships inside dataImproving retrieval and reasoning — Microsoft GraphRAG
Execution traceWhat happened during one particular runDebugging and observability
Improvement graphLoops that optimise, check and audit one anotherControlling self-improving systems

What LangChain learned in three years of it

  • Agent graphs are usually not DAGs. Production needs cycles — retrying failed calls, asking for missing information, revising after validation, pausing for a human.
  • A loop is already a graph. Specifically, a directed cyclic graph. Loop engineering was never an alternative; it was the one-node case.
  • Dynamic transitions matter. You do not always know the edges up front. Map-reduce is the classic case: the number of workers depends on the input.
  • What is actually new is what fits inside a node. Early on a node was deterministic code or a single model call. Agents are now reliable enough that a node can be a full agent run. That is the honest delta, and it is a small one.

The claims that did not survive checking

Viral claimWhat the evidence shows

Stanford independently adopted itDSPy optimises language-model programs; it is not a knowledge-graph system

18% higher accuracy, 85% lower costFigures come from one paper applying GraphRAG to industrial engineering diagrams. Narrow setting, promoted into a general law.
When someone says it improved accuracy by 18%, ask three questions. What kind of graph? Improved compared with what? And where did that number come from? Turing Post FOD#159 — 20 July 2026

03

Comparison

Where Apollo and Memento already sit

Measured against the taxonomy rather than the slogan, three of the four are present and one of them is stronger than what the field is publishing.

TypeIn Apollo / MementoStanding
Control graph Runbooks, the gate suite, the six-beat ladder, model routing by phase. Encoded as procedure and CI gates rather than declared topology — the human stays in the loop by design. Partial, deliberately
Knowledge graph _DECISION-GRAPH.md — 100 nodes, 165 edges, seven typed edge types under ADR-0012, generated from an audited seed. Plus _GRAPH-REPORT.md — a token-to-component blast-radius graph over 67 components and 945 tokens. Ahead of the field
Execution trace _CHAIN.md, the session witness, _ENACTMENT-REGISTER.md under ADR-0016, and the gauge log. Present, rigorous
Improvement graph The optimiser is the build; the counter-metric is the context gauge and stop line; the audit loop is the wrap gate, the capture ritual and the dream pass. Enactment proof closes it — a ruling must be provably live, not merely inscribed. Present and enforced

The edge taxonomy is the standout

ADR-0012 defines seven edge types with qualifiers. Three of them make distinctions that did not appear anywhere in the 2026 GraphRAG or agent-memory literature reviewed for this document:

  • bounds(X, scope=…) — limits a ruling's scope without killing it. Scoped supersession, not binary supersession.
  • supersedes(X, claim=…) — kills one assertion inside a node that stays otherwise live.
  • diverges-from(X, reason=…) — a deliberate divergence the reconciliation gate must never try to resolve. Published systems have no vocabulary for “these two disagree on purpose”.

A conflict gate with no way to record intentional divergence flags that pair forever. That problem was found and solved here before the field named the discipline.

An external convergence worth knowing

Anthropic's Dreaming — a research preview announced at Code with Claude in May 2026 — lets an agent inspect its previous sessions, work out what it missed, and self-improve. The open-source OpenClaw Auto-Dream project implements the same idea as a scheduled cycle that scans, extracts, organises, scores, links and prunes a knowledge base asynchronously. The 2026 literature frames both as hippocampal replay: strengthen important traces during idle time, prune the rest.

That is the dream-pass lane, arrived at independently. The practical value is not validation — it is that there is now a body of external work to steal from, and a shared name to search under.

04

Finding

The graph is not on the retrieval path

One real gap, and it is structural rather than cosmetic. The 165 typed edges are inscribed, parsed, and rendered into a dashboard. Then nothing reads them again. Recall does not traverse them.

The Memento door and the DS-decisions door both sit on one retrieval spine, and that spine has exactly one scorer:

knowledge/_search_core.py — score_record() 
return matched_original * 2 + matched_expanded

Token overlap against a curated synonym lexicon, doubled for literal matches. No traversal. No hops. No awareness of whether a returned node has been superseded by something the corpus already knows about.

Why this matters more here than elsewhere

In a generic corpus, lexical retrieval returning a stale document is an inconvenience. In a decision corpus it is a specific and recorded failure mode: retrieval hands back a ruling that a later ruling already killed, with nothing marking it dead. The reader — human or model — then builds on it.

That failure has been hit before and treated at the symptom. The --all flag widens the net so the governing ruling is more likely to appear somewhere in the results. It does not tell you which of the results is the live one. The edges do. They are just not consulted.

This is precisely, and only, what GraphRAG's multi-hop claim amounts to — applied to a corpus that has already done the hard part, which is typing the edges honestly.

The corpus knows which ruling superseded which. The search does not ask it.

05

Worth taking

Five candidates, cheapest first

#ChangeWhat it fixesSize
1 One-hop edge expansion at retrieval. After the lexical hit, pull the supersedes / refines / bounds neighbours of every result and attach them. The structural version of the “retrieval hides the governing ruling” defect. Edges already exist in the audited seed JSON — this is a lookup and a merge, not a new data structure. Small
2 Supersession-aware ranking. Any node that something else supersedes is demoted and visibly marked in the result list. Stops a dead ruling presenting as live. Same file as #1; no new index. Small
3 Adopt the three questions as a claim gate. What kind of graph? Improved versus what? Where did the number come from? Generalises past graphs — it is a measurement-honesty rule that would have caught more than one prior claim. Costs nothing but a line in the runbook. Trivial
4 Probe the temporal half of ADR-0007. The ADR is literally titled temporal decision-graph; the main live thread in 2026 GraphRAG research is timestamped edges with validity intervals. Unknown until probed — the temporal half may be built, partly built, or ruled-not-enacted. Worth one grep before anything is planned on it. Probe first
5 Blast radius as pre-flight, not report. _GRAPH-REPORT.md computes that text/default reaches 55 components. A human reads that. A gate could. Turns a generated dashboard into a control edge — the one place where the knowledge graph would start doing control-graph work. Medium

Items 1 and 2 are the same change to the same function and should be priced as one piece of work. They are also the only two that close the finding in section 04.

06

Worth ignoring

What not to do about this

Both primary sources spend as much space on restraint as on advocacy, which is the most credible thing about them.

  • Do not rebuild the session runner as a declared control graph. Adding a graph framework means adding state management, routing logic, debugging work, and more places for the workflow to break. Where the path must be discovered as it goes, a harness beats a graph — LangChain's own deep-research product moved away from a predefined graph to an agentic core loop, and GPT Researcher made the same move.
  • Do not chase the performance numbers. They came from one industrial-diagram study and do not generalise.
  • Do not rename anything. Loop engineering lasted six weeks; graph engineering is on the same clock. The internal vocabulary — rulings, edges, gates, enactment — is more precise than the imported one.
  • Do not treat the taxonomy as a scorecard. A missing control graph is not a deficiency here. It is the deliberate consequence of keeping a human in the routing decision, which is the whole architecture.
“If the workflow is linear, keep it linear. A box-and-arrow diagram does not make an agent more advanced. Sometimes it only documents the traffic jam you designed.” Turing Post FOD#159

Verdict

You are doing this already — under better names, with one wire missing.

The honest read is that graph engineering is a name for a cluster of practices, of which Apollo/Memento independently implements three and skips the fourth for a stated reason. The typed-edge convention is more expressive than the published state of the art, because it was derived from a real corpus with real scoped supersessions rather than from a benchmark.

The gap is narrow and specific. The graph exists, it is typed, it is audited, and it is generated — and then recall reaches past it to a token-overlap scorer. Closing that is a small change to one function, and it is the only item in this document that changes what the system can do rather than what it is called.

The buzzword's claim

Build a graph and retrieval gets multi-hop, accuracy rises 18%, cost falls 85%. Adopt a framework; the topology is the product.

The version that survives

Typed edges are worth having because they record why a relationship exists — scoped, claim-level, deliberate. Traversal at recall time is then a small consequence, not a framework purchase.
