How we capture design decisions.

One design system feeds many projects. This is a simple, safe way to make a decision once — and roll it out everywhere without breaking things.

5-minute read 6 simple steps 4 decisions for you
01 — THE PROBLEM

One system. Many projects. No clear path.

Lots of teams build on our design system.

When we decide to change something, we don't have a clear, shared way to do three things:

1. Agree it.   2. Write it down.   3. Roll it out safely.

As the system grows, a missed step can quietly break another team's work. We want a path that scales.

Change the system once and it should flow out to every project — cleanly.

02 — WE PRESSURE-TESTED THE IDEA

The idea holds up. The research sharpened it.

Our idea: meet → AI drafts the notes → a person signs them off → the system updates → the team is told. The core is sound. The research confirmed one part and added two.

Your instinct holds: AI does the heavy lifting, a person signs off, and humans stay in control. The research confirmed that — and added two refinements.

What the research told us

✓ Confirmed · sign-off

Keeping a person to sign off the AI's draft is right — the AI never decides alone. The research strongly agrees, especially in a regulated setting.

Source: White & Case, Thomson Reuters
＋ Added · who approves

You don't need everyone to approve every change. A few people decide; the rest are told. That avoids bottlenecks and rubber-stamping.

Source: Nathan Curtis, EightShapes
＋ Added · the rollout

Tell people a change is coming and give them time to update. Don't just flip a switch under them.

Source: Versioning Design Systems, SemVer

So the idea stands. The proposal below builds it out into six clear steps.

03 — THE PROPOSAL

Six simple steps.

Same instinct as before, made safe. Read it left to right.

STEP 1

Propose

Anyone can raise a decision. Small, easy-to-undo ones skip the meeting.

STEP 2

Discuss

Talk it through on a call. AI takes notes and writes a first draft.

AI helps
STEP 3

Decide

A few named people agree it. We note who, why, and what it affects.

Person signs
STEP 4

Build

Teams make the change. Each one is checked automatically before it ships.

STEP 5

Tell people

Everyone who uses it is told — with time to adjust.

STEP 6

Check

If a decision keeps causing problems, we revisit it.

✎

AI drafts. A person signs.

The AI writes the first version. A named human makes it official.

①

Decide once. Build many times.

Agree the decision once. The changes that follow are just checked for quality — not re-argued.

◷

A few decide. Everyone is told.

A small group makes the call. Everyone else is kept informed — not asked to approve.

04 — FOUR DECISIONS, YOUR CALL

Four choices for you.

Each one shows the options side by side. I've marked the way I'd lean — but the call is yours.

1

Who gets to decide?How much sits with a central team versus the projects.

A. One central team

One team decides everything. Very consistent — but slow, and they become the bottleneck.

B. Each project alone

Every project decides for itself. Fast — but the system drifts apart over time.

C. Core decides, teams build

A small core agrees the decisions. The projects build the changes. Consistent and fast.

2

Where do we write decisions down?We already keep a log for technical decisions. Do design decisions share it, or get their own?

A. One shared log

Everything in one place. Simple — but design and technical notes get mixed together.

B. A separate design log

Design decisions get their own clear home. Different readers, different pace. Easier to find.

3

Who are the deciders?For a typical decision, who must agree, who is asked, and who is just told?

A. Everyone approves

All actors sign off every change. Feels fair — but causes hold-ups and rubber-stamping.

B. Decide · Asked · Told

A small named group decides. A few are asked for input. Everyone else is told. Clear and fast.

4

When do we hold a meeting?Not every change needs a formal call. Where's the line?

A. A meeting for everything

Safe, but heavy. People start skipping the process to get work done.

B. Match the meeting to the risk

Small, easy-to-undo changes are just logged. Big or hard-to-undo ones get the full meeting.

05 — WHAT I NEED FROM YOU

Your four answers.

If you're happy with my leans, this is a quick yes. If not, tell me which to change.

  1. Who decides?My lean: a small core decides, teams build.
  2. Where do we record it?My lean: a separate design-decisions log.
  3. Who are the deciders?My lean: a few decide · some asked · most told.
  4. When do we meet?My lean: only big or hard-to-undo changes.
