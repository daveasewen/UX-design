What this actually is

Four strata, by commit history. The repo's documentation describes stratum 1. Its value lives in strata 2 and 3.

01

Identity — the pivot nobody announced

The README describes a product that stopped existing on June 2.

The repo's stated mission — a portable, multi-discipline agentic harness with an orchestrator, typed spokes, and seven discipline pipelines — got one dry-run (runs/dryrun-001) and has not been touched since: harness/ last commit 2026-05-31, disciplines/ 2026-06-02. Meanwhile 53 of 54 commits touch knowledge/. What was actually built is a design-system compiler with enforcement: a token store, 38 gated reference components, a generated composition layer, and 15 build steps that withhold "done".

This isn't drift to be reversed — it's a correct pivot to be ratified. The dossier's founding insight ("encode senior design judgment as the scarce, defensible asset") is fully intact; only the vehicle changed. The cost of not naming it is concrete in your workflow specifically: every fresh session cold-starts an agent on README and AGENTS.md, and both currently tell it the wrong mission. The pipeline mental model even admits it — "harness = conveyor (barely built)". The harness ideas worth keeping (craft/taste gate split, error taxonomy, typed contracts) already migrated into the gates and runbooks; the scaffolding can be archived without losing them.

You didn't fail to build the harness. You discovered the knowledge layer was the product — six weeks ago. The paperwork just hasn't caught up.

02

Evidence — proven vs self-graded

Almost everything green was marked by the same hand that built it.

What is independently solid: the gate suite runs green from a cold start (I copied knowledge/ to a fresh directory today and ran all 15 steps — pass); the recompose proof showed 0 drift; the compose gate catches rogue hex and class redefinition by construction. That is real, deterministic verification and it is rarer than you'd think.

What is self-graded: the 9/9 scores, the dual critiques, the fitness-test verdicts — all generated and assessed by the same model in the same context window. You already have the proof this fails: the state-contrast blind spot, where 9/9 components hid real dark-mode contrast failures because the gate only checked author-declared pairs. The one time evidence arrived from outside the system — the payments-journey walk on June 24 — it invalidated the central assumption of three weeks of work in a single afternoon (12 × 9/9 ≠ one shippable screen).

External evidence to date: Dave's eye, and that one journey walk. Zero second users. Zero real projects. The discovery decisions locked on June 20 designed intake lanes for "non-expert users" — no non-expert has ever been observed touching any of this. The June 20 priority order put "prove the method on a real HSBC project" first; twelve days on, the project is still unpicked. That is the single most expensive open decision in the repo.

03

Software review — the gate suite

Good discipline, small and honest — resting on untested foundations.

Credit first: ~2,900 lines of zero-dependency Python, deterministic, dependency-ordered, exiting non-zero, with allow-lists that carry reasons and a clean warning/error split. Generators are idempotent; the composition layer is generated verbatim from reviewed snippets (the right fix after the hand-retype lost ~137 decisions). For a project that isn't "software", this is better engineering hygiene than most software.

05

Market — the window and the clock

The gap you're aimed at is real, open, and closing from three directions.

Validated: vocabulary-awareness is commoditising — Figma Make pulls components/variables automatically, Magic Patterns imports design systems, Code Connect is Figma's "#1 way" to get component reuse. But the ecosystem's own literature concedes the ceiling: systems are mapped, not enforced; models still emit one-off styles when the map runs out; there is no rendered-output verification anywhere in the loop. Blocking gates, rendered state-contrast checks, certified canon, register dials — nobody ships these. Your moat thesis survives contact with the market.

Also validated: spec-driven development went fully mainstream (30+ frameworks; spec-kit, Kiro, Tessl), with regulated-industry audit trails already a recognised wedge. Good news for the spec/eval-first discovery direction — and a warning that the generic version of the idea is no longer novel. The design-specific version (criteria that compile to design gates) is still unoccupied.

The clock: the durable asset is not the Python — a competent team replicates the gate suite in weeks. The durable assets are the criteria corpus (the decision comments, rubrics, charter, runbooks — judgment spent once and encoded) and a proof in a regulated bank, which no tool vendor can shortcut. That second asset only exists if decision 2 happens. Every week spent polishing single-tenant canon is a week the enforcement gap stays unclaimed publicly while Figma, Supernova ("the semantic foundation for AI agents") and the SDD wave each drift one step closer to it.

06

Focus — the strand economy

One person, six live strands, two customers, forty-six coordination docs.

Currently open: the component interaction review, the gap-pattern build, motion exploration, the fixed/flex governance work, the robustness/product-engineering track, the leadership transformation case, the Apollo product strategy — plus parked-but-warm: contextual dashboard, brand refresh, Graphify. Two of these are customers with different definitions of done: leadership buys an operating model backed by proof; Apollo buys a portable engine backed by proof. They share exactly one thing — the proof — which doesn't exist yet.

The June 19 reflection ruled "do not add more docs; curation is now part of the job." knowledge/ now holds 46 coordination and audit _*.md files. The self-awareness in this repo is exceptional — the blind-spot post-mortem, the procedural-debt note, the journey-proof correction — but every correction has come after an excursion, paid at full price. The structural fix isn't more discipline; it's a forcing function: one real project whose weekly needs decide what gets built, and both narratives written as by-products of it.

The project doesn't have a thinking problem. It has a sequencing problem: proof was ranked first on June 20 and has been outranked by polish every day since.

Verdict

Regroup, don't pivot. The thesis survived; the sequencing didn't.

Both available pivots fail the test. The contextual dashboard is correctly parked — same engine, different market, and it needs the design-time loop proven first anyway. Pivoting to "build the product tool" (renderer, web shell, zero-config) is premature for the same reason: it hardens plumbing around a value loop no outsider has yet run. And abandoning the enforcement thesis would mean walking away from the one gap the market research confirms is open. What's left is convergence: ratify the pivot that already happened, and put every remaining hour behind external proof.

The tempting path

Finish all 38 components to 9/9, build the five gap-patterns, engineer the bundled renderer, keep both narratives warm, pick the real project "when the canon is ready". Measurable progress every day; the proof recedes indefinitely; the market window narrows.

The recommended path

Pick the real HSBC project this week and let its journey scope all canon work. Run one public design system through the runbooks to prove the engine generalises. Put the gates under test + CI. Package agent-native. Write both narratives from what the proof shows.

Recommendations — eight decisions

1

Name it

Ratify the pivot: archive the harness, rewrite the identity

Move harness/, the five skeleton disciplines, and the v0.1 pipeline specs to archive/ with a short ADR ("the knowledge layer became the product"). Rewrite README + AGENTS.md around what exists: a governed design-system engine — canon, gates, composition, registers — operated by an agent through runbooks. Keep the harness's genuinely good ideas (craft/taste split, error taxonomy) where they already live: in the gates.

Effort: half a day. Payoff: every future cold-start agent starts on the true mission.

2

Prove it

Pick the one real HSBC project — this week, imperfect is fine

This has been the ranked-first goal since June 20 and is the gating input to everything else: it scopes the canon work (decision 3), grounds the leadership case, and is the demo Apollo actually needs. Selection criteria: small enough for one journey, real enough to have a stakeholder who isn't you, and payments-adjacent so the existing canon carries most of it. If no perfect candidate exists, the imperfect one beats another week of polish.

Effort: one decision + a kickoff brief. This is the highest-leverage item in this document.

3

Prove it

Cap the canon program: journey-driven, not alphabet-driven

The interaction review and remaining gap-patterns continue — but scoped to what decision 2's journey touches, in the order it touches them. The gallery-wide pass and tranche 6 wait for a journey that needs them. The June 24 proof established the principle; this just applies it to the current queue. (The craft pass you invited on portfolio-level interactions still happens — inside the proof project, where the wow lands on a stakeholder.)

Effort: zero — it's a re-ordering. Saves an estimated 1–2 weeks of pre-proof polish.

4

Prove it

Onboard a second design system — the generalisation proof

Run one public system through _RUNBOOK-onboard-code-library.md and the gated-component runbook, timeboxed to two days. GOV.UK Design System is ideal: public, accessibility-first, banking-adjacent rigour, and a brutal test of every HSBC-shaped assumption in the parsers and metas. Success = the gates run green on foreign content; every place they don't is precisely the engine/content boundary you need documented. This single exercise converts "a very good HSBC knowledge base" into evidence for "a machine that eats design systems".

Effort: 2 days, timeboxed hard. Do it after decision 2 is picked, not instead of it.

5

Harden it

Gate the gates: fixture tests + CI

A small pytest suite with deliberately-broken fixture snippets (drifted token, missing ARIA, failing contrast pair, undeclared state) asserting each validator catches each class — the bite-tests, made permanent. Then a GitHub Action running _build_all.py + the fixture suite on every push. Also: write the snippet authoring convention down as a schema (finding 03-3), so the input contract is validated, not folklore.

Effort: ~1 day. Closes the false-confidence class structurally instead of one post-mortem at a time.

6

Harden it

Resolve the two-machine breach

Real HSBC and Sutherland Figma exports live in knowledge/tokens/_raw/ on the home repo, against AGENTS.md's own rule. Either purge them (and re-derive on the agency machine), or — if the arrangement genuinely permits it — amend the rule to match reality. A governance product whose own repo ignores its written governance is a story a sceptical stakeholder only needs to find once.

Effort: an hour, plus one honest look at what the agency arrangement actually permits.

7

Ship it

Package agent-native; don't build the tool shell yet

The robustness bar in _ROBUSTNESS-PORTABILITY.md is the right end-state and the wrong next step. The runbooks + gates + KB already have the exact shape of an agent skill/plugin with an MCP surface — in that packaging, the host platform owns the renderer, ports, caching and sandbox, which is ~80% of the papercut list, for ~2% of the cost. Near-term: consolidate the render duct-tape into one owned script (server + cache-bust + bounded timeout + retry) and stop there. The bespoke zero-config tool is a decision to revisit only after decisions 2 and 4 have produced an external user.

Effort: 1 day for the render script; the deferred tool-shell is weeks–months avoided for now.

8

Ship it

Pick the primary Q3 customer; make the other a by-product

Leadership (the Digital Experience case) and Apollo (the portable engine) want the same proof but different framing, and each deserves different next-hours once the proof exists. My read of the repo: the leadership case has the nearer forcing function and the higher downside of silence — and a funded, reorganised team is the strongest possible platform to then productise from. But this is a taste call on information only you hold (Apollo's timeline, the agency politics). Decide it consciously rather than letting the commit history decide it for you — right now the commits are voting, and they're voting for neither.

Effort: one decision. Both documents get dramatically easier to write after decision 2 lands.

What is genuinely strong — keep, unchanged

For balance, the things this review found no fault with: verification-as-enforcement (market-validated and correctly implemented); the convergent/divergent bright line; retrieval-not-recall and the fixed/flex charter (the sharpest strategic writing in the repo); the runbook discipline; confidence tiers and blast-radius indexing; the payments-journey style of proof; and the habit — rare anywhere — of writing honest post-mortems against your own work. The engine's ideas are right. The remaining work is to let something outside the engine say so.
