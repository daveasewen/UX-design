#273 · lane LC · P-272-1 research

Nobody counts fields.

Thirteen design systems were consulted for the line between a list row and a card. Not one of them draws it on field count, and not one draws it on the number of columns inside a row. The two systems that ship a genuinely columnar list — IBM Carbon’s structured list and GOV.UK’s summary list — both call it a list, with column headers and key/value rows, and neither treats the columns as the thing that would make it a card.

Three predicates do appear, repeatedly, and they are about the record’s boundary, its own actions, and whether items in the set are the same shape as each other. The one number found anywhere (USWDS’s six) caps the size of a collection, not the contents of a record.

The three predicates, extracted

1 · The boundary (NN/g, USWDS, Ant, Carbon tile, M3)

A card owns a bordered, self-contained surface. A list row does not — it is separated from its neighbours by a rule or by nothing at all. This is the most-agreed predicate of the three, and it is the one our own canon already has a legal field name for: surface.

2 · Record-level actions and a title (GOV.UK, M3, Ant)

GOV.UK’s summary card exists for exactly one reason: a repeated record needs its OWN heading and its OWN actions. That is your list-card, shipping, in a system we already cite as canon-adjacent.

3 · Same-shape vs different-shape (NN/g)

NN/g’s strongest line is about the SET, not the record: cards are for heterogeneous collections; homogeneous records belong in a list because a list is scannable and a card layout is not. A ledger of transactions is homogeneous by definition — so on NN/g’s test, fintech records stay lists however many fields they carry.

Not a predicate anywhere: arithmetic

The only numbers found in thirteen systems are USWDS’s 6 items per collection, Polaris’s 50 items before pagination, and Carbon’s 3 paragraphs per row. All three measure the SET or the TEXT. None measures fields per record, and none measures columns.

The threshold sketch — where the evidence contradicts it

You wrote, explicitly not as a decision: “maybe we allow three columns and two rows in a list and anything beyond that is a stacked card”. Three things in the record cut against it, and one thing in it survives.

  • Carbon puts column headers on a list. A structured list is columnar by construction and Carbon still calls it a list; its exit is to a data table on nesting, never to a card. A 3×2 cap would make Carbon’s own default a card.
  • GOV.UK’s summary list has no row cap. A twelve-row check-your-answers list is a list. If rows > 2 made a card, GOV.UK’s most-used component would be a card everywhere.
  • Polaris puts media and per-item actions in a list row. So neither an image nor an action promotes a row to a card.
  • What survives: your instinct that a record with five datapoints plus a status is not the same animal as a two-field tappable row. Every system agrees with that. They just split it on the record’s BOUNDARY and its OWN ACTIONS rather than on a count — and on that split, your “stacked card” and GOV.UK’s summary card are the same thing.

Stated plainly: the arithmetic threshold has no external support in this record. The three shapes do. Recommending against the numbers is not recommending against the shapes.

cards — a provider, but not of this role

One correction to the brief that set this lane: cards is not $not-a-provider. It provides arrangement, when = “a bordered surface per item”. That is the FFF form axis — a container that holds other providers and carries no content of its own. It is not in record-list and, on the FFF reading, it should not be: a card is a surface, not a record.

Its meta ships five variants — Action, Link, Static, Media, Media borderless — and none of them is a repeated record. There is no card variant today that means one of many of the same thing.

account-card — genuinely $not-a-provider

It is listed in roles.json’s $not-a-provider line, alongside payment-card-visual and card-header-lockup. Its purpose reads as ONE account: label, balance, optional type tag and RAG chip — a single-record summary, so it answers no “show me the items” brief and provides nothing.

That is precisely the tension you named: an account card is authored as one record, but in the payments journey “account cards can stack like a list”. Nothing in the canon lets it. LC-3 is that question.

Where a five-field-plus-status stacked row goes today

Nowhere. Walked against all eight gates: list-items refuses it (≤ 3 fields); table and data-grid take it only by flattening the stack into columns, which is the reading you rejected; transaction-row, document-row and standing-order-mandate-row each require a specific record KIND; timeline needs time to be the structure; tree needs nesting. cards would host it as a surface but provides arrangement, so a brief asking for “show me the items” would never reach it.

The library’s own list-items meta already contradicts the 3-field gate in prose: its transaction variant is “Transaction row with tag or status detail” and its review-detail variant is “Review row with extra detail line”. The shapes are shipping; only the gate is missing.

Our own ingested guidelines (knowledge/guidelines/) are SILENT on the list/card line. The word “card” appears there three times, all in web-foundations.md, and all about elevation and padding — never about when a record becomes one.

Decisions

Four decisions. The recommendation is first in each.

Nothing here is ruled. Every option stays open; the marked option is the lane’s recommendation and carries no weight beyond that.

LC-1

THE LINE — what makes a row a card

The predicate that would gate record-list’s providers, written in the closed when grammar (knowledge/when-fields.json).

Field names this needs, and whether the grammar has them. surface is already legal (26 names, when-fields.json). rows is already legal but is defined as “how many rows the reading carries” — the rows of a LIST, not the rows inside one record; option (b) and (c) would either re-read it or need a second name. NOT legal, and needed: fields (datapoints per record) · columns (columns within one record) · actions (independent actions the record owns). Adding any of them is the $extension move that RD-3 is still open on — option (a) needs only actions, which is the cheapest of the three.

LC-2

THE SHAPES — three metas, three variants, or two metas

simple-list · column-list · list-card. Whatever LC-1 decides the line is, this decides where the shapes LIVE.

Why (a): the three shapes share a state set, a hit target and an accessibility contract; list-items’s purpose already covers “the tappable list-row family”. If LC-1 lands on (a), option (c) becomes the honest second choice — the surface would then be the one thing the two metas genuinely disagree about.

LC-3

ACCOUNT-CARD — does it join record-list

Your note: “account cards can stack like a list”. Today account-card is $not-a-provider and cannot be reached by a “show me the items” brief.

Why (a): adding a ninth kind-gated provider for every record type that happens to have a card-shaped presentation puts the library on a path to a provider per fintech noun. The variant carries the stacking without that. But (b) is not wrong — transaction-row is the precedent, and it is yours to weigh.

LC-4

THE CARDINALITY NUMBERS — do we import any

The only numbers thirteen systems offer: USWDS’s six per collection, Polaris’s fifty before pagination, Carbon’s three paragraphs per row. B-09 was ratified with your note and is still open on the number.

Why (a): a “complex relationship” (the #271 lane’s phrase on row B-20) and USWDS’s six-or-fewer are both unmachine-checkable at our gate, and s251-D3’s three-axis independence is the thing a soft number would blur first.
