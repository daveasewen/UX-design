Review for ruling

What actually separates a table from a list, and a radio from a dropdown

You asked two things today: whether we can be sure we have the criteria for these choices, and whether other libraries have the rules, since Figma is shut to you. Both answered below, measured, not remembered. Nine metas were read, nine public design systems and three NN/g articles were fetched, and not one file in knowledge/ was edited.

Your two sentences are quoted from today's chat. They are not yet in the Memento record — _quote_gate.py returns NOT FOUND for both — so they are cited as today's chat, never as verified quotes.

The answer

No, we cannot be sure — but the two families fail in opposite ways, and that is the useful part.

For record lists the criteria already exist and you already adopted them. roles.json role record-list carries a when on all eight providers: table is “default, read-mostly”, data-grid is “sort / filter / select / edit-in-place is required”, list-items is “≤ 3 fields per item, tappable”. They went in with the roles list at s252-D1. They are simply not on the metas and no gate reads them, so in practice nothing enforces them and nobody reading a meta ever sees them.

For selection controls there is nothing at all. The input role has 27 providers and 27 when: null — every null in the file sits in that one role. roles.json admits it in its own definition: “this role is large and will want sub-roles when forms get authored — not now.” The 470-rule guidelines index mentions a selection control 35 times and not once as a choice criterion — every hit is label placement, grouped-control semantics or button copy.

And yes, the other libraries have the rules. 49 external lines were captured with their URLs. The pattern in them is the finding: the KIND of criterion is close to unanimous, and every NUMBER is arbitrary.

The one decision with weight

Is a dropdown our default single-select field, or our last resort?

Every number on this page hangs off that one word. GOV.UK external says the select “should only be used as a last resort in public-facing services” and cites research that “some users find selects very difficult to use”. NN/g external says “If possible, use radio buttons rather than drop-down menus.” Our canon, meanwhile, treats Dropdown as the ordinary form field — it is what forms.md points at and what the sort control is built from.

Rule that word and eight predicates fall out of it. Rule it the GOV.UK way and radio becomes the default, dropdown needs an argument each time, and our Dropdown meta needs a line it does not have. Rule it our way and the radio ceiling is just a number.

Three smaller calls ride along, recommendations taken so you need only overrule: (a) one cut number, ruled once, rather than three — radio→dropdown and dropdown→combobox are the same decision twice; (b) record-list predicates get copied, not reinvented — they are already yours; (c) multi-select gets NO predicate until you rule one, because no external system has an opinion and a guess wearing a number is worse than a blank.

Family 1 — selection controls

Proposed in the s253-D1 shape: a gate half that a checker could read, then a prose half naming the sibling it beats and the sibling it yields to. Confidence is measured, not felt: Canon = already adopted in our repo · Consensus = three or more public systems state the same kind of criterion · One source = a single system, or the systems conflict on the cut · No source = proposed from our own meta prose, or a declared guess.

Family 2 — record lists

All three are marked Canon because the predicate is already in roles.json and adopted. What is proposed here is the gate half — the machine-checkable clause the adopted prose implies but does not state — plus the move onto the meta.

ComponentProposed whenSourcesThe one thing you must rule
tableCanon records >= 2 AND fields-per-record >= 3 AND the reading is COMPARISON ACROSS records on the same fields AND interaction = none

the default of the record-list role (roles.json when: "default, read-mostly"); beats list-items (roles.json when: "<= 3 fields per item, tappable") whenever the reader must compare the same field down a column; yields to data-grid (roles.json when: "sort / filter / select / edit-in-place is required") the moment any interaction is needed, and is never used for layout (our own antiPattern).

data-gridCanon records >= 2 AND fields-per-record >= 3 AND (sort OR filter OR row-selection OR edit-in-place) is required

Where the libraries disagree

They agree on the mechanism

Switch vs checkbox — five of five systems give the same single test: does the effect happen at once. M3 external: “The effects of a switch should start immediately, without needing to save.” Atlassian external: “Toggles should never require people to press a button to apply the settings.” Carbon, NN/g and Spectrum say it in their own words. This is the cleanest predicate found anywhere.

Radio vs checkbox — unanimous, and it is cardinality: one answer or several. GOV.UK external: “Do not use the radios component if users might need to select more than one option.”

Segmented vs tabs — Carbon reaches our own meta's line independently: “When navigating between distinct content areas like subpages, use tabs instead”. Our meta already says it. Two systems, no contact.

They disagree on every number

The radio → dropdown cut, across five systems: Polaris 4 · M3 5 · Ant 5 · Spectrum 6 · USWDS 7. GOV.UK, NN/g and Atlassian give no number at all and prefer radios regardless.

The dropdown → combobox cut: Spectrum 6, USWDS 15. A nine-option list is a combo box at Adobe and a plain select at USWDS. Carbon declines to count: “the list of options may be very long”.

And one system disagrees with the component itself. GOV.UK and NN/g both push users away from the select entirely. That is not a threshold argument, it is a different position, and it is the one worth your word.

Read the disagreement the right way round: it is not that the systems are sloppy. It is that option count is a proxy for the thing that actually matters — whether the options fit without scrolling, and whether the reader can recognise the right one on sight. Every system picked a number to stand in for that, and picked a different one.

Where everyone is silent

  • Multi-select as a distinct component. Not one of the nine systems separates a chip multi-select from a checkbox group. They all say “use checkboxes”. Our proposed threshold of ten options has no source and is marked as a guess in PROPOSED-WHEN.json, not dressed as a finding.
  • Account-selector. No external analogue — it is a domain component. Silence here is expected, not a gap. The only source is our own meta.
  • A segmented-control maximum. Carbon and M3 name no ceiling. The 5 proposed comes from the track width, not from a source.
  • The HSBC position, on all of it. The ten ingested source sets under knowledge/guidelines/_sources/ are brand, colour, typography, tone of voice, neurodiversity, visual assets and the two foundations pages. No component-choice prose is in the corpus. forms.md:40 points at the component specs, and the component specs do not carry the criterion. If HSBC has ruled any of this, it is in Figma, which you cannot open today — so this page is what we have, and the gap is declared rather than papered over.
