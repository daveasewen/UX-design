The other half of the claim

6

"Six months" is not in the repo. It has to come from somewhere, or go.

The measured side of the sentence is fourteen and a half weeks. The comparator is the half David Rice will test, and the repo cannot supply it. Three honest ways to fill it; one recommended.

A · Your own prior experience

"The last library I built by hand took ⬛."

Your number, your project, spoken as experience. It is the only comparator you can defend from memory in the room, and he can test it only by asking you about that project.

What it needs: one named prior build (yours), its rough scope (how many components, how many people) and its elapsed time. Say all three or none.

B · A named public benchmark

"Design systems of this size take ⬛ — per ⬛."

A published figure you cite by name. The repo has three industry lines already (digital-experience-transformation/strategy/: "40–60% faster", "~10x throughput", "91% of designers use AI") — all marked sourced but not ours, and none of them is an elapsed-time-to-library figure.

What it would need to say: a component-library or design-system build, comparable scope (~130 components, gated, two themes), elapsed calendar time, from a source he could look up during the meeting. This page does not supply one; do not use an uncited one.

C · Drop the comparator — recommended

"Fourteen and a half weeks, 102 days, from nothing to ten signed releases."

State the build alone and let the room supply its own comparator. He knows what a governed component library costs; a CAIO is likelier to trust a number he benchmarks himself than one you hand him.

Why this one: every word is checkable in the repo; the "six months" line has no owner; and the run-of-show's beat 5 already ends on "the graph underneath it" — a measured build is the graph's own story. Option A is the fallback if he asks "compared with what?" — answer from your own experience, not from a slide.

Whichever option: the repo never recorded person-hours. 234 numbered sessions exist, but no source states a session length, so hours are not derived here. Say "sessions" or "commit days", never "hours".

How a Chief AI Officer would test the number

Three attacks, and the answer the evidence allows

1

"Fourteen weeks of what? One person, part-time, on a side project isn't a benchmark."

True, and say so first. Git has two authors — one human (79 commits), one agent (1,193 + 7) — and 73 commit days out of 102. Hours were never recorded. The defensible unit is not effort but output under governance: 137 gated components, 444 dated rulings, ten hash-recorded releases, every one checkable in the tree in front of him.

Sources: git log --format=%an | sort | uniq -c · commit-day count · _rulings.json.

2

"You scored your own output. 3/3/3/2 is your number."

It is self-scored, and every point was earned by driving the page in a browser — reloads, filters, URL state — not by reading the source; the review pages carry the drive logs. The stronger answer is that the same method found the defects: the sixth run, on the release zip, dropped rich to 2 and failed both gates because a script was missing from the package. A scoring that only ever went up would be the suspicious one.

Sources: reviews/COLDRUN-258-2026-09-08-v5.html · notes/_subreports/2026-09-10-267-C6-cold-run-6.md § Scores, § Top three findings.

3

"Compared with six months where? Show me the baseline."

There is no baseline in the repo, and this page will not invent one. If Dave fills the comparator from his own prior build (option A) he answers from experience; if he uses option C he never made the comparison and the question does not arise. What the repo does hold is its own baseline: the first cold run scored 2/2/0/2 on 05 Sep before anything was fixed, and the growth curve above is dated to the commit.

Sources: reviews/BASELINE-246-2026-09-05-v1.html · knowledge/_kg_history.json · …266-T-timeline-trawl.md § 3.2.

The number the repo can defend is the build: 102 days, 73 of them with commits, to ten gated releases. The number it cannot defend is the six months. Recommendation: say the first, and let him supply the second.
