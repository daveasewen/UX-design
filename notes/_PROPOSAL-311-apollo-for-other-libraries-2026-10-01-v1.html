Session 311 · proposal · Apollo for other libraries · v1

Make the meta the spec, make the snippet the proof, and emit every target from the same source.

Your instinct is half right. The snippets should not be what the compiler reads. They should stay, as the fixture every emitter has to match, because they are the only framework-neutral test Apollo has and the only thing you rule on by eye.

Thursday 1 October 2026 Lane M3 (Fable), on M1's desk research (38 sources) and M2's read-only measurement of the repo at the seat. Nothing here is ruled until you say. Seven calls at the end.

01 · The answer on one screen

Not halfway. The half Apollo has is the half that governs. The half it lacks is the half that renders.

"is it right that relying on the way the compiler works now, might make this easy to implement, you can switch out the snippets and tokens for another library and you are half way there???"

No. Today the compiler's input is the snippets, so switching them out switches out the whole of what it reads: 95% of the shared stylesheet, every line of component markup, and about 5,800 lines of behaviour that live nowhere else. What would be left is the metas, and the metas hold what a component is, when to use it and what it takes, not how it is drawn. Swapping in another library's HTML would make canon.css a theme of that library. That is placement C, not a shortcut to placement A.

  • Two questions, not oneYour words hold two different jobs. S1: Apollo's own components delivered into a React, Angular or plain HTML stack. S2: Apollo working with a library a client already has, where Apollo governs and composes but does not own the components. They need different machinery and this page answers both.
  • S1, the answerDo it at source. The meta becomes the declared spec (it gains an anatomy tree, states and transitions as data, the events a part emits, and token bindings as references). Per-component CSS and behaviour modules move out of the snippets. The compiler becomes a set of emitters: the HTML+CSS one you have today, then Lit custom elements, then generated React and Angular wrappers. Tokens go to the W3C DTCG format under all of it.
  • S2, the answerA layer between Apollo and their library, but a manifest, not a code transform. One file per library maps Apollo roles and metas to their components, props to props, slots to slots, and their tokens onto Apollo's semantic tokens. Apollo's when-rules, gates and composition run unchanged; their components render. ADR-0008 already asks for this and four unverified bindings already exist.
  • The snippetsKeep them, change their job. From source to fixture. Each snippet becomes the HTML emitter's output for a documented example, committed, viewed in the showroom, read by the 35 gates that read it today, and ruled on by your eye. A round-trip gate refuses any emitter whose output does not match the committed snippet. This is what GOV.UK Frontend does with its fixtures.json and it has lasted ten years.
  • Where you are challengedThree places. First, "the snippets are for the user to view" undersells them: they are the acceptance test and the thing designers rule on, and the research says drop them from the pipeline only as input, never as oracle. Second, the compiler as it stands does not make this easy; it is a loop through the snippets with hand-written manifests in the middle, and the reversal you are proposing undoes ADR-0013 ruling 4, which says the snippets stay the source of truth. Third, "switch out the tokens" is the one part that is easy, and it is easy because the tokens are already close to a standard, not because of how the compiler works.
  • What the field saysNobody at design-system scale compiles React or Angular from compiled HTML. The two systems that compile once to many (Deutsche Bahn via Mitosis, Chakra via Zag) do it from a neutral spec. The big corporates (Carbon, Spectrum, Fluent, Nord, Shoelace) ship web components as the one runtime with thin generated wrappers and shared tokens underneath. React 19, Angular, Vue, Svelte and Solid all score 100% on Custom Elements Everywhere now, which removes the old objection.
  • Next stepRule the seven calls in 07. Phase 0 (tokens to DTCG) and phase 1 (extract the spec from the snippets into the metas) can start this weekend without touching the pipeline. Phase 2 reverses ADR-0013 ruling 4 and waits for your word.

02 · What Apollo depends on today · measured by M2 at the seat

The build is a loop through the snippets. The metas sit beside the loop, not inside it.

The Python build does not build a component from anything but its snippet. The snippet is the declared source of three things: the CSS, the markup and most of the behaviour. The meta is read for what a part is and when it is allowed, never for how it is drawn.

The loop reads left to right. Tokens are written into the snippets, the snippets are read back out into canon.css, and a composed screen gets its markup by copying a snippet. The meta is to the right, outside the loop: it is what the compose skill and the knowledge graph read, and it is where your rulings about what and when are kept.

  • The declared designThis is on purpose, and on record. gen_canon_components.py lines 3 to 7 say "The snippets are the source of truth (they are the final reviewed components); canon regenerates from them". ADR-0013 ruling 4 and gen_component_partials.py lines 9 to 11 say the same. Your instinct is a reversal of a ruled design, not a tidy-up.
  • What the meta holdsAbout half of a neutral spec, and it is the governing half. Props with types on 135 metas (594 props, every one typed, 393 with a default), variants on 125, the 12-role vocabulary on 107, when-rules on 45, an aria contract on 135. Typed slots on 20, a state list on 27. These are the fields your rulings land in.
  • What the meta lacksEverything an emitter needs to draw. No anatomy tree on any meta. No transitions, only state names. No emitted events (the 24 behaviour.events are DOM listeners, not what the part fires). Of 1,013 token entries, 239 are paths a machine can bind; 727 are prose. The real binding is the snippet's hand-written #token-manifest.
  • The prior recordMulti-framework output was decided, never built. ADR-0008 (accepted 2026-07-20): Apollo is the canonical core and consumers such as Sutherland React are reached "by machine-runnable adapters, not hand-ports". The onboarding runbook names codeBindings.<lib-id> as the spoke and lists web-components as an example library id. The designer skill says "React (preferred)" and there is no React component in the repo to wire to, and no customElements.define anywhere.
  • TokensClose to the standard; one generator away. $value, $type and $description everywhere. Four gaps: no {group.token} aliases (the alias sits in a non-standard $alias), light and dark encoded as path leaves, 150-odd non-standard $ keys that belong in $extensions, and dimensions as strings. M2's estimate is one generator with no information lost.

03 · What the field does · M1, sourced

The systems that serve many frameworks share tokens and CSS underneath, and ship one runtime or compile from one neutral spec. None of them compiles from HTML.

Eight systems read, all current as of 1 October 2026 unless dated. The pattern is consistent enough that the three placements you named can each be matched to someone who tried it.

  • IBM CarbonTwo hand-written flagships, one styles layer, parity by policy. @carbon/react and @carbon/web-components on a shared @carbon/styles; its contributor guidance says "Visual and functional parity are paramount, though the implementations themselves are allowed to diverge as necessary" [S4]. The vanilla HTML+JS library, the nearest thing to Apollo's snippets being the shipped artefact, was deprecated in 2023 and ended with v10 in September 2024 [S6, S26]. The cost is two teams; the drift is accepted in writing.
  • Adobe SpectrumOne CSS source, two consumers, no shared component code. Spectrum CSS depends on @spectrum-css/tokens; Spectrum Web Components import it directly, and moving to Spectrum 2 was a theme swap not a rewrite [S2, S3]. React Spectrum is a separate stack whose premise is "The main difference between design systems is styling" [S1]. What is shared is tokens and CSS.
  • Salesforce LightningReference HTML as the spec, at scale, for a decade, and their own warning. Blueprints are "framework-agnostic HTML or CSS"; the developer guide says "If SLDS updates the related blueprint, your component code isn't updated automatically" and tells you to look for any other route first [S27, S28]. SLDS 2 moved the visual contract to CSS custom properties that cross the Shadow DOM boundary.
  • MaterialThree parallel implementations and the one Google did not need was dropped. Material Web Components went to maintenance mode on 10 June 2024 because the engineers were reassigned [S9]; Angular Material carried on with Material 3 as CSS custom properties [S10]; MUI is a third codebase [S29]. If Google cannot fund parallel hand-written ports, Apollo cannot.
  • Fluent, Nord, ShoelaceWeb components as the one runtime, custom properties as the theme bridge. Fluent "do not create their own separate component model" [S11]; Nord pierces Shadow DOM with inherited custom properties [S13]; Shoelace ships React wrappers because React "treats all JSX properties as HTML attributes", which is why @lit/react exists [S17]. Stencil generalises wrappers into configured output targets for React, Angular and Vue [S15].
  • Deutsche Bahn DB UXThe purest compile-once-to-many in production. Components written once in Mitosis, emitted as React, Angular, Vue and Stencil packages on top of a CSS-only foundations package [S12]. The caveat: Mitosis is a Builder.io side project whose README is "actively looking for folks interested in becoming contributors" [S30]. Whatever emitter Apollo adopts, Apollo owns.
  • Zag and ArkBehaviour as the neutral source. State machines "that connects machine output to DOM semantics in a WAI-ARIA compliant way", with thin adapters for React, Vue, Solid and Svelte [S7, S8]. The machine is plain data; this is the model for the states-and-transitions field the metas lack.
  • GOV.UK FrontendThe fixture pattern Apollo's snippets should copy. Per-component fixtures.json holds options in and expected HTML out; porters are told to "pass options into your own macro and check the generated HTML matches html", ignoring known whitespace differences [S14]. GDS does not support the community ports [S37]. The reference is the oracle, not the source, and it has held since 2016 [S36].
  • Figma Code ConnectThe model for the S2 manifest. A small typed file that maps a design component to code components, one-to-many across frameworks, with typed holes so a boolean renders as the attribute or nothing [S31, S32]. That is what a per-library adapter needs from a meta.
  • The standardsTokens are solved; anatomy and behaviour are not. DTCG Format Module 2025.10 and the Resolver Module are stable since 28 October 2025, read by Style Dictionary since v4 and by Tokens Studio [S20 to S24]. Open UI's anatomy research gives part names but is not normative [S33]. The ARIA Authoring Practices' own front matter is the strongest argument against HTML as the spec: "A role is a promise" and "ARIA roles do not cause browsers to provide keyboard behaviors or styling" [S35]. React 19, Angular, Vue, Svelte, Solid and Preact all score 100% on Custom Elements Everywhere [S18, S19]; SSR of Shadow DOM and form participation were still caveats in late 2024 and no 2026 source was found closing them [S25].

Full research with every claim sourced: notes/_lanes/311/M/M1-FINDINGS.md. Pages that could not be fetched are listed there rather than filled in.

04 · The options · judged against solid, flexible, standards

Your three placements, for S1. Then three shapes for S2.

You asked whether to do it at source, to put a transform between the compiler and the output, or to put a layer between the prototype and the framework. For S1 those are placements A, B and C below. For S2 the "layer between" is right, but it is a manifest, not a transform.

A is what the systems that compile once to many actually do. B is unoccupied; the nearest relative warns against itself. C is what most corporate "design systems" quietly are, and it hands the DOM to someone else.

A

At source: the meta is the spec, each target is an emitter

SolidOne truth, every target regenerated from it, drift impossible by construction. The snippets become fixtures the emitters must match. FlexibleAdd a target by adding an emitter. The HTML+CSS emitter you have today is target one. StandardsDTCG tokens; custom elements for the runtime; APG patterns as the behaviour checklist; Open UI names for parts. CostThe emitters are yours forever. The missing half of the spec has to be written or extracted, 137 times.
Strong fit because the neutral spec already exists in part and because ADR-0008 already asked for machine-runnable adapters.
B

Transform the compiled HTML into framework code

SolidNo. HTML fixes one state; a transform has to guess props, slots, states and events from a string. Roles in the markup are promises with no keyboard behaviour behind them. FlexibleLooks flexible (any HTML in) and is brittle (every guess breaks on the next snippet). StandardsNone. Nobody has written one down because nobody does it. CostLow to start, unbounded to keep.
Ruled out. This is the placement your instinct rejects and the research agrees.
C

Adapt Apollo onto MUI, Angular Material or similar

SolidSolid as long as the host is funded and does not move. Material Web lost its engineers in June 2024 while first-party. FlexibleBounded by the host's component set, anatomy and ARIA choices. Theming bounded by what the host exposes. StandardsTheir standards, not yours. Apollo's gates stop where the host owns the DOM. CostCheap to start. Apollo stops being Apollo at the boundary.
A convenience export (a MUI theme generated from Apollo tokens), never the integration.

S2: a client already has a library. Three shapes.

Here Apollo does not own the components and should not try to. What Apollo brings is the governing half: roles, when-rules, props and slots as a contract, the gates, the composition. What it needs is a binding to their parts and a mapping of their tokens onto its semantic layer.

i

An adapter manifest per library

WhatOne file, adapters/<lib-id>.json, Code Connect in shape: Apollo role or meta to their component; prop to prop with typed holes; slot to slot; their tokens to Apollo's semantic tokens as DTCG resolver sets; an explicit list of what is unmapped. SolidApollo's when-rules, gates and composition run unchanged. A gate refuses a composed screen whose parts have no binding and no declared fallback. FlexibleOne manifest per library; React, Angular and plain HTML libraries are the same shape, only the target names differ. StandardsDTCG for the token half; the manifest schema is Apollo's, documented, small.
This is the hub-and-spoke the onboarding runbook already describes, with codeBindings.<lib-id> as the spoke. Four spokes exist today, all unverified; that is the gap, not the design.
ii

Theme only: Apollo tokens onto their library

WhatGenerate their theme file (MUI createTheme, Angular Material define-theme) from Apollo's tokens and stop there. SolidSolid for colour and type, silent on roles, when-rules, anatomy and gates. FlexibleOnly as flexible as their theme API. StandardsDTCG in, their format out. Style Dictionary does most of it.
Worth doing as the first deliverable of option i, because it is a day's work and buys trust. Not sufficient on its own: it is placement C from the other side.
iii

Replace their library with Apollo's emitters

WhatShip Apollo's React or Angular output and retire theirs. SolidYes, but it is not "working with" their library; it is S1 wearing a different coat. FlexibleOnly if the client agrees to move. Many will not. StandardsSame as A.
Keep it as the end state a client can choose once S1 exists. Do not lead with it.

05 · The recommended architecture

Four sources, five emitters, one manifest shape for the outside world.

The source of truth becomes the meta plus three siblings that move out of the snippet: a CSS file per component, a behaviour module per interactive component, and the tokens in DTCG form. Everything to the right of the sources is generated, including the snippets.

Left to right: hand-written sources, generated emitters, the gate that keeps them honest, and the two kinds of consumer. The snippet moves from the far left of today's diagram to the output of E1. Your eye still rules on it; the emitters now have to agree with your eye.

  • The source of truthThe meta, plus three files beside it. The meta gains four fields in meta.schema.json: anatomy (an element tree naming parts, slots, aria and the attributes each state sets), states (states, transitions and the keyboard map as data, Zag-shaped), emits (the events a part fires, with payload types), and bindings (the token manifest, moved in, as DTCG references). The 727 prose token entries are replaced by the manifest's real bindings.
  • What the snippets becomeFixtures, generated and committed. The HTML emitter renders the meta's documented examples through components/<slug>.css and the behaviour module into the same self-contained snippet file you have now, with a fenced header recording the options that produced it. The showroom, the 35 gates and your eye read the generated file exactly as they read the hand-written one today. The difference is that a snippet can no longer drift from the spec, because it is made from it.
  • TokensDTCG 2025.10 with the Resolver Module for light, dark and the four themes. $alias becomes {group.token} references; light and dark leave the path and become resolver sets; $note, $contrast, $confidence and friends move under $extensions.apollo so no tool drops them; dimensions become value objects. gen_canon_tokens.py is re-fed and its output is byte-equal. Style Dictionary reads the files as the proof that the format is real.
  • The emittersFive, in order of existence. E1 HTML+CSS exists and is re-fed from the new sources. E4 token exports exist for CSS variables and gain SCSS, JS and theme files for MUI and Angular Material as convenience outputs. E5 the A2UI catalogue is specced (s305-D45 route B) and is a reader of the same metas. E2 Lit custom elements is new: one <apollo-*> element per interactive meta, light DOM so canon.css styles it and the geometry gates still see it, behaviour from the module, events from emits. E3 React and Angular wrappers are generated over E2 from the meta's props and emits, @lit/react style, so the designer skill's "React (preferred)" finally has something to wire.
  • The S2 manifestadapters/<lib-id>.json, one per client library, Code Connect in shape. For each Apollo role or meta: their component name and import; a prop map with typed holes (a boolean renders the attribute or nothing); a slot map; a status (unverified, rendered, accepted); and a token map from their variables onto Apollo's semantic tokens as a DTCG resolver set. An unmapped list is required, not optional. A gate refuses a composed screen whose parts have no binding and no declared Apollo-native fallback. This replaces the four codeBindings spokes with something a machine can check.
  • What does not changeYour rulings and the knowledge graph. Rulings land in the meta as they do now. The graph's renderedBy edges keep pointing at snippet files, which still exist at the same paths. The dashboard, the gates and the release pack keep their inputs.
Technical · schema sketch, light DOM, and the gates by name
  1. Anatomy field. A tree of {part, tag, attrs, aria, slot?, children[]} nodes, with state attributes written as attrs["aria-selected"]: "{state.selected}" and prop holes as "{props.label}". Part names checked against Open UI's anatomy research where a page exists [S33]. The tree is extracted from today's snippet markup once by a script, then reviewed per cohort; 137 to do.
  2. States field. {states:[...], initial, transitions:[{from,on,to,guard?}], keys:{ArrowRight:"next", Home:"first", ...}}, the shape Zag machines take [S7]. Extracted from the <style> state selectors (1,556 pseudo, 502 .is-*, 209 [aria-*]) and the inline script; the APG pattern for the role is the checklist [S34].
  3. Emits field. [{name:"apollo-change", detail:{value:"string"}}]. Today behaviour.events lists listeners (blur, click, keydown); that field stays and emits is added beside it.
  4. Bindings field. The snippet's #token-manifest moved into the meta as {"--tab-ink": "{color.text.default}"}. gen_snippet_tokens and gen_theme_cascade read the meta instead of the snippet; the AUTO-THEMES block (12.3%) is regenerated byte-equal.
  5. Light DOM for E2. Recommended because canon.css already exists and 35 gates measure rendered geometry through it; Shadow DOM would need a custom-property bridge per component (Nord's pattern [S13]) and still carries the SSR and form caveats [S25]. Light DOM custom elements lose encapsulation but gain every existing gate. Call 4 puts this to you.
  6. Wrappers for E3. React via @lit/react createComponent with the events map generated from emits [S17]. Angular via a generated module declaring CUSTOM_ELEMENTS_SCHEMA plus a thin directive per element for typed inputs and outputs; Stencil's Angular output target is the model [S15]. Both generated, never hand-edited; a gate diffs the generated files against the tree.
  7. Gates by name. _validate_roundtrip.py (E1 output equals committed snippet; byte-level first, falling back to declaration-level where a generator sorts, per the rename-proofs lesson); _validate_spec_coverage.py (anatomy, states, emits, bindings present on every meta an emitter is asked to render, and the emitter refuses below coverage, so the count has a consumer); _validate_tokens_dtcg.py (schema-valid against 2025.10, Style Dictionary parses with zero warnings, token spine byte-equal); _validate_apg_keys.py (each E2 element answers its APG keyboard map in Playwright); _validate_wrappers.py (attributes, properties and events round-trip per wrapper, Custom Elements Everywhere style [S19]); _validate_adapter.py (every part on a composed screen has a binding or a declared fallback; status recorded).

07 · The calls · seven, recommendation first

Rule these in plain words. The bar at the bottom collects them.

1. What is a snippet from now on?

The recommendation: a fixture generated from the spec. Keeps everything you rule on by eye, keeps the 35 gates, and makes drift impossible. This reverses ADR-0013 ruling 4, so it needs your word, not a lane's.

2. Where does the neutral spec live?

The recommendation: in the meta, as four new fields in meta.schema.json. The meta is already where your rulings land and what the graph, the compose skill and Launchpad read. A separate file per component splits the record; a third-party DSL (Mitosis, Stencil) hands the source format to someone else.

3. What is the second runtime after HTML+CSS?

The recommendation: Lit custom elements, with React and Angular wrappers generated over them. One runtime for every host, idiom by generation, and every framework scores 100% on interop now. Hand-written React and Angular packages are the Carbon model and cost two teams. Mitosis is seeking maintainers. HTML-only with fixtures for porters is the GOV.UK model and inherits its disclaimer.

4. Do the custom elements use light DOM or Shadow DOM?

The recommendation: light DOM. canon.css already styles everything and 35 gates measure rendered geometry through it; Shadow DOM needs a custom-property bridge per component and still carries the SSR and form-participation caveats nobody has closed in print. Encapsulation is the trade.

5. How does Apollo work with a library a client already has?

The recommendation: an adapter manifest per library. Apollo keeps the governing half (roles, when-rules, gates, composition), their components render, a gate refuses unbound parts, and the token half is generated as their theme file on day one. Theme-only is placement C from the other side. Replacing their library is S1, not S2.

6. When do the tokens move to DTCG 2025.10?

The recommendation: now. One generator, no information lost, the token spine byte-equal, Style Dictionary as the proof. It is the one part of "switch out the tokens" that is as easy as you hoped, and every downstream tool reads the result.

7. What starts, and when?

The recommendation: phases 0 and 1 and the S2 schema this weekend, because none of them edits a snippet or changes an output; phase 2 after your ruling on call 1, cohort by cohort behind the round-trip gate; phases 3 and 4 later. Everything at once over one weekend run would put the reversal and the extraction in the same diff, and the round-trip gate cannot tell you which one broke.
