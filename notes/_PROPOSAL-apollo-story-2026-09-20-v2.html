The story in one line

An assembly line is only as fast as its parts bin, the skill of the assembly engineer, and the rigour of QC. Ours had a bin too small, engineers of every skill level, and QC that was rigorous but slow. We tried robots. They machined the missing parts and it showed. So we filled the bin, gave the engineer a brain, and put an inspector at the end of the line.

The plant is the board

Three problems, three parts of the line.

The analogy does the breakdown for us. Each of the three problems on the board is one part of the plant, and each part of Apollo answers one of them. Problem, breakdown, solution — in the same three words each time.

Seven beats · his words

The arc, in the order the room hears it.

  1. The line.

    An assembly line is only as fast as its parts bin, the skill of the assembly engineer and the rigour of QC.

    We have a parts bin that is too small, engineers with dramatically different skill levels, and our governance QC is rigorous but slow.

    No one has the full picture. Missing parts are machined all the time, by engineers without the skills to do so. The builds are unreliable and they have to go back down the line to be repaired.

  2. The robots.

    We introduced robots into the plant. The results were underwhelming: where the inventory was empty the robots tried to machine the parts, and they simply don't have the skills or knowledge for this.

    We watched our designers do exactly what the machine did. The problem was always there — trying to automate it is what made it obvious.

    Second paragraph is his ruling on the "his word" section, folded in: "this is the validation of a real phenomenon… when trying to automate, this problem became very obvious."

  3. The inventory.

    First we focused on the inventory and expanded it from 36 to 137 components — 101 new — including the page templates.

    Each one in code, built to our design and accessibility standards. Now there is no need to machine parts.

    Show · the library

    His text says "137 and 8 templates". The record says the 137 includes 12 templates; the 8 are foundations (grids, type, colour). Wording above follows the record — see the correction below.

  4. The assembly engineer.

    Because each product must be custom designed and built, what we are really creating is a custom shop that can build at the speed of a car plant. We needed the knowledge of a designer and an assembly engineer embedded in the system too.

    Standards, accessibility, rulings, governance, usage and theory — all in one graph, retrieved when needed, and with each build comes a record of account.

    Show · the explorer

  5. The inspector.

    Although quality is built into the parts and the assembly is powered by a vast knowledge base, QC is always the final check. Corrections are executed by the inspector, not sent back down the line.

  6. The build.

    One cold, repeatable testing prompt. The build, the inspection and the final product roll off the line.

    Show · live, with the recording as fallback

  7. The ask.

    Dave's, with his colleagues. Open.

    His word: "I need to work with my colleagues on this request." Held as a draft slot; research stays named as owed, another group's work.

Behind beats two and three

The robots are in the record, and so is the date.

The experiment he remembers is the July pair. Two arms, one brief: a governed arm that could only build from the library, and an ungoverned arm with no rules at all, run cold twice on Opus. The governed arm broke no rule and came out flat. The ungoverned arm found a real organising idea and read better — then needed a human to repair three invented icons and four accessibility failures before it could ship.

19 June
First pair, at component grain: Tabs built from the library alone versus unconstrained. The library‑only build failed dark‑mode contrast at 1.0 : 1 and had no focus indicator — "about 90% my invention", said the record.
30 June
The SME‑Payments brief, run four ways. "The canon was silent in exactly the places it had to invent." Invented charcoals, chart bars, easings; a cold run fabricated account numbers and status chips.
5 July
The pair at screen grain. Dave's verdict: "the canon works but probably no better than an AI model tied to a component library… I expected something like: unconstrained with the right styling."
7 July
Root cause, measured: the governed run was provenance‑perfect — zero invented colours, 26 library components — and still flat. The unconstrained run invented 56 colours and 219 local variables and read better. Rule‑following was already saturated; the library stopped at organism, with zero templates or page shells. It was never a rules problem. It was the bin.
10 July
Ruled: the two‑pass was "not all that successful" — a component, not the architecture. The inventory build‑out begins in earnest.
20 August

Two things the record corrects

Small, and worth getting right before the room does.

The templates. "137 and 8 templates" — the showroom index counts 137 components, of which 12 are page templates (auth, dashboard, list, detail, settings, report, error, empty and so on). The 8 are foundations. So it is 137 including the templates, on 8 foundations; or, if the templates deserve their own beat, 125 components and 12 templates.

The 36. Held, on your word: it is the Figma library's count and the repo's earliest reviewed count was 32 in code. 36 → 137 is 101 new, and nobody in the room will check the other four.
