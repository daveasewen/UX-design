Question 1 · the order

  1. Which comes first: what Apollo hands to hosts, or what it hands to developers?

    Recommend: hosts first, with the catalogue and renderer; minted CSS and the snippets second; adapters and emitters parked. Because it builds on what already exists, and the renderer needs a per-theme stylesheet anyway, so the second step rides on the first.

    Recommended · hosts first

    Catalogue and renderer, then minted CSS

    October goes on the proof of concept's first four steps: the catalogue from the parts' notes, gates as a service, the chooser, parts that take data.

    The mint step follows, because the renderer draws canon markup and needs a stylesheet per theme to do it.

    Fits the live proposal and the probe. Developers still get the snippets as the test from day one. Estimate: 6 to 8 sessions of lane work for steps 1 to 4.

    Or · developers first

    Minted CSS, then framework packages

    October goes on the per-theme stylesheet and a first React package, so a dev team can install Apollo.

    The catalogue waits.

    Answers your north-star question sooner for one team, but the emitters have no named consumer, and the MCP work you asked for on Saturday stops.

    Your words behind the two routes. On hosts, Saturday 13:29: "do you think we could create our own MCP-UI -- Apollo-MCP maybe or something that has the same capabilities as Google's A2UI and MCP-UI". On developers, 22 August: "we will publish the results of generated as dev ready code, it needs to be a set of primitives that can be wired to a stylesheet".

Question 2 · what reopens the parked two

  1. What should wake the adapters and the framework emitters back up?

    Recommend: one tripwire for both, a technology team naming itself as the owner of a consumer. Because an adapter or an emitter with nobody to use it is an instrument without a consumer, the programme's own named failure.

    Recommended · an owner appears

    Wake when a team says it will consume

    Both wait for a named team: the one behind the React library, the Common Toolkit, or any team that asks. That team's stack decides which adapter or emitter is built first.

    Parking is an order, not a reversal. The July decision that consumers are reached by generated adapters still stands.

    Or · keep today's tripwire

    Wake at the next release cut

    Your npm note is parked today on the release-cut event, which would fire at the next cut, planned for after Tuesday's sitting.

    It would reopen the emitters this week with no owner and no scope, and ask you the same question again.

    Your note, 16 September, kept whole: "before I forget. This is tangential but i want to explore this some time. could we store the tokens and components on a npm registry as a distribution mechanism for teams to use, we could use a script or something like the py component builded to output for other frameworks like angular and REACT".

Question 3 · how "build ready" is tested

  1. How do we find out whether what Apollo hands over is build ready?

    Recommend: hand the proof of concept's dashboard to a developer who has not seen it, and count what they had to redo, by kind. Because it turns your north-star question into a number, and every answer on this page can then be judged by it.

    Recommended · a cold developer

    One real hand-off, measured

    The June dashboard, as the proof of concept draws it, with its minted stylesheet and the reference snippets as the test.

    A colleague if one is free; otherwise a fresh seat that has never seen Apollo, declared as a stand-in.

    Each edit they make is logged by kind (restyled, restructured, rewired, re-typed) and counted. Fewer kinds and fewer edits means closer to build ready.

    Or · judge it by eye

    You look, and say

    You review the hand-over pack and rule whether it looks build ready.

    Quick, but it tests the look, not the hand-off, and gives the next answer nothing to beat.

    This test was proposed on 31 August, to run after the demo, and has not run yet. Estimate: one session to set up and one to run, after the proof of concept draws its first screen.
