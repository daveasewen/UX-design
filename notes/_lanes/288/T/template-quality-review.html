The verdict

Good page.
Stale paperwork.

The drawn page is the best-documented artefact in the tree and would not embarrass you in a one-shot. What would disappoint is everything wrapped around it: the template's own record card tells a builder to undo the layout you approved, its status line has been wrong for twenty sessions, and the inline-style problem is not where anyone has been looking.

Nothing in the markup is invented. Nothing in it dangles. Every colour and every type size resolves to a token. The one real colour drift is declared in the file and is a question for you, not a defect. The damage is in the metadata and the delivery path.

Reviewed — the snippet (1,842 lines), its meta (258 lines), its showroom card, the showroom generator, the shipped pack's build skill, and the 137 snippets it composes from.
Rendered — no. This seat has no browser; the render environment resolves to nothing here. Everything about 1024 and 1920 below is read from the CSS and labelled as such.
In flight elsewhere — the gutter delivery path (lane A). Not re-reported here.

Findings, ranked by how much each would spoil a one-shot

Seven things, worst first

Ranked by the damage a cold builder would actually do if they followed what is written, not by how hard each is to fix.

01 Would break an approved layout

The record card tells a builder to undo the wall you approved

The meta forbids, in as many words, the very spans the page now ships. Its anti-pattern reads: “Do not invent a span. Only 6 and 3 are square at every compiled band… A data-c="2" tile leaves one column empty at the 3-column band, and no gate sees it.” The wall as built is 6, then 4 + 2 — a data-c="4" group beside a data-c="2" group.

That 4 + 2 wall is the one you approved on sight. The rule that makes it legal was added later, as a band rule on the rail. But the meta was never amended, so the meta and the markup now say opposite things — and the meta is the file a builder is told to read for the contract.

03 Cannot be fixed by fixing the template

The showroom cannot say “proposed”, and the template can never reach “stable”

Two separate things, and only one of them is about words.

The vocabulary. The showroom's status set is fixed at three: stable, beta, deprecated. There is no fourth key and no free-text field. “Beta” is not the generator disagreeing with the meta — beta is the generator's only way of saying “proposed”, because it is the residual bucket. A PROPOSED marker on a meta actively demotes the card to beta by design.

The trap. To be stable a component must be in the gated set and carry no PROPOSED marker. The gated set is read from a frozen measurement file dated 2026-08-21. The template was built 2026-08-31. It is not in that file and never can be. 134 of 137 components are in it; this one missed by ten days. So even with a perfect status line, the card stays beta.

04 Would make dark mode unreadable if “fixed”

In dark mode the ruled page ground and the tile surface are the same colour

The ruled page ground for a dashboard resolves, in dark, to #1F1F1F. The tile surface also resolves, in dark, to #1F1F1F. Byte-identical — a contrast ratio of 1.00 to 1. With keylines off, which is the ruled default, the bento stops reading as a bento entirely: it becomes one flat dark rectangle with numbers on it.

The template does not ship that. It ships #1A1A1A instead, one rung darker, with the reason written into the file. That is a deliberate, declared, unruled drift off the token — and the derivation it drifts from was left expressly open when the rule was made.

05 Would look wrong on a wide screen

Nothing caps the page width, and the widest band was never measured

Read from the CSS, not rendered — this seat has no browser.

At 1024 wide the wall measures about 960 and falls to the three-column band. The rail takes the whole row it lands on and its two cards sit side by side. This is the behaviour you approved and the CSS says it holds.

At 1920 wide the wall measures about 1856 and stays at six columns. There is no upper bound anywhere: the page frame sets side padding and no maximum width, and the one layout primitive that would cap it — a 1120px container — is defined in the file but never used on any element. The summary rows in the rail deliberately drop their source component's 420px cap, on the principle that the container decides the width. At 1920 the container decides on roughly 900px, so a two-word label and a number sit at opposite ends of a very wide row.

The unmeasured band. The headline row is pinned to four columns at every band by a rule that deliberately opts out of the responsive machinery. Its own comment records it measured at 1440, 1100 and 820. The narrowest band, 520, is not in that list — and at 520 four tiles get about 125px each, which will not hold a formatted currency figure.

Where 596 came from. That count sweeps the snippet folder including two review scratch files that are not part of the library. The reviewed population is 566 attributes across 137 files. Within those, 87 carry a raw length with nothing overridable behind it — which is within one of the figure already in circulation, and I would keep quoting 87 as the actionable number.

What they are. Ninety-one raw declarations in total, and the split is the whole story:

ClassCountWould a theme switch break it?
Layout — gaps, margins, widths, heights91No. A theme changes colour and radius; these are geometry.
Type — six font sizes6Yes, under a size-ramp change. All six are specimen rows in two demo pages.

So the honest headline is this: the inline-style population is not a theming risk. It is a tidiness question. A third of it sits inside blocks the files themselves mark “showroom harness — never copy”. The single largest repeated value is a demo section spacer appearing a dozen times.

And the gate cannot see any of it. The hardcode gate does read inline attributes — that hole was fixed. But its population is eleven pro-forma files. The snippets folder is not in scope at all, and the gate's scope is spacing, radius and border width, which excludes the widths, heights and font sizes that make up most of the 87. So no gate is watching, and widening one would not catch most of them anyway.

The template's nine inline attributes

One positioning rule on a hidden sprite sheet, one opacity, and six animation delays on chart bars. Not a length among them.

Where the template's raw values actually live

129 raw lengths in its own stylesheet — 78 inside spliced component CSS, 22 in its instance dials, 6 in the generated bento block. That, not the inline attributes, is the template's hardcoding surface.

The command. The census is a short python pass over knowledge/snippets/*.reference.html matching style="…" across line breaks, splitting on semicolons, and counting any declaration whose value contains a bare px/rem/em number and no var(, excluding custom properties. It is quoted in full in the filed report.

Against the one-shot goal

Where it would disappoint

“I Either expect it would use the dashboard bento or it will ask if that is right.”Dave, the demo-day goal

The pass condition had four parts. The template is what makes part three — copy the markup, invent nothing — actually possible, and on that part it delivers completely.

Three ways it would still disappoint, in order:

  1. The builder may never reach it. The reader that the skill makes step one does not return it (finding 07). The prose rescue is real but it is at line 119 of a long skill.
  2. If they reach it and read the contract, they may unbuild it. The meta tells them the shipped wall is a defect (finding 01).
  3. On a wide screen it will look unfinished. No width cap, summary rows stretched to 900px, and the widest band never measured (finding 05).

None of those three is a problem with the drawing. All three are a problem with what is written around the drawing — which is, for a one-shot, the thing the machine actually reads.

Decision pack

Six questions

Each has its options and a recommendation. Nothing below is done; a skip on any one is an answer too.

  1. The inline-style rule — what, if anything, do we ban?

    87 attributes carry a raw length with no token behind it. None is colour. Six are type. No gate covers the population.

    • A. Ban raw lengths in inline styles across the snippet library, and widen the hardcode gate to cover it. Touches 87 attributes in 40-odd files.
    • B. Ban only what a theme can move — the six font sizes — and leave geometry alone.
    • C. Ban inline styles in shipped component regions only, and exempt anything inside a demo-harness block, which is a third of the population already.
    • D. No rule. Record the census and move on.
    Recommended: C, because the harness blocks are already marked “never copy” so the exemption costs nothing to define, and it aims the rule at the only markup a builder actually lifts.

  2. The template's status — proposed, or beta?

    The showroom has three status words and no room for a fourth without changing the ruled set. Beta is already the generator's way of saying proposed. Separately, the template can never reach stable because the gated-set snapshot predates it by ten days.

    • A. Add a fourth status word, “proposed”. Changes the ruled status set and one self-test.
    • B. Keep three words, accept beta as the expression of proposed, and fix the meta's status line so it stops contradicting the tree.
    • C. Re-measure the gated set at today's date so the template can reach stable on merit, and use the empty override seat in the meantime.
    • D. Declare it stable by override now.
    Recommended: B plus C, because B is a text fix with no ruling cost and removes the contradiction, while C repairs the date trap that would otherwise pin every component built after 21 August at beta forever — a problem larger than this template.

  3. The dark page ground

    The ruled token paints the wall and its tiles the same colour in dark. The template ships one rung darker instead, declared, unruled. Three readings are already rendered for your eye.

    • A. Ratify what ships — the darker ground — as the dashboard's dark page ground.
    • B. Keep the ruled token and turn keylines on in dark, so the bento reads by line rather than by ground.
    • C. Derive a new dark page-ground token properly, which is what the original ruling left open.
    Recommended: A, because it is what has shipped and been looked at for twenty sessions, and C can follow later without moving a pixel.

  4. May a snippet link the compiled canon stylesheet?

    None of the 137 does, so a snippet using a canon structural component has to copy the generated block. This one carries such a copy, and the projector then copies it back into canon — about 8KB of duplicated rules. Nothing compares the copy to its source, so it will rot silently.

    • A. Let snippets link canon. Changes the self-contained snippet convention for all 137.
    • B. Keep the copy and build the gate that byte-compares it against canon. Priced already.
    • C. Teach the projector to skip canon-owned selectors, removing the duplication in canon but not the rot risk in the snippet.
    Recommended: B, because the copy is the cheap half and the rot is the expensive half, and B buys the rot protection without reopening a convention that 137 files rely on.

  5. Do the other three themes' dashboard defaults belong in the snippet?

    Snippets are single-theme by convention. The dashboard's spacing defaults differ per theme. Only one theme's pair is written into this file; the other three live in a generator table.

    • A. Keep one theme in the snippet. The other three arrive through the generation arm when it is built.
    • B. Write all four pairs into the snippet, breaking the single-theme convention here.
    • C. Neither — the snippet should carry no spacing literal at all and read the selected theme's value.
    Recommended: A, because C is what the unbuilt generation arm is for and building it is already someone else's job this session; B would create a second home for a fact the generator owns.

  6. Does an overview dashboard have a maximum width?

    Nothing in the tree settles this. The page has no cap; the one primitive that would cap it is defined and unused; the summary rows deliberately dropped their own cap on the principle that the container decides. At 1920 the container decides on about 900px per row.

    • A. No cap. A dashboard fills the screen; density is the point.
    • B. Cap the wall at the existing 1120px container and centre it.
    • C. No cap on the wall, but restore a width cap on the summary rows so figures stay near their labels.
    Recommended: C, because filling a wide screen is right for a bento and wrong for a two-column key-and-value list, and C is the only option that separates the two. This wants a render at 1920 before you commit.
