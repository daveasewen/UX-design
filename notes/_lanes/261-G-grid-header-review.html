The column header

Dave's second nominated dashboard element. The header row was one full-cell sort button per column. It is now a four-part anatomy — label, sort, an optional per-column filter, a resize handle — under an optional spanning group row, with both header rows sticky, a comfortable/compact density, and a column that can say no. Nothing was bolted onto the passive Table: its header is a plain th scope="col" that collapses to cards, a different contract, and it is not shared with this one — Table is untouched.

G2 — what Dave asked for, verbatim. “I like the new one, but can we remove the bars from the end of the column headers. and the odd (I assume selected state) black lines, bottom and right.” Three changes and no others. (1) The resize handle no longer paints anything at rest — the affordance is untouched (24px hit area, col-resize cursor, pointer drag, Alt+arrow keyboard), and a 1px hairline appears only on hover or focus of the handle itself. (2) The sorted rule drops 3px → 2px and is the only edge the sorted state paints. (3) The filtered-active leading bar is gone: on the middle specimen Date is sorted and Payee is filtered, and Payee's black leading rule sat against Date's trailing edge — that was the “right” line. Filtered now speaks through its distinct glyph, a held ground on the filter button and its named chip. Driven in four themes × two modes: no state paints a right-edge bar; the only trailing rule left is the 1px table/border column hairline, and focus-visible is #305A85 / #4587A7, the focus/ring token, never a black box.

What moved at #261 G. The sort button gave up the whole cell and became one flex item, so a filter button and a resize handle have somewhere to live that no label can ever collide with. The label truncates and takes a title only when it measurably truncates. Numeric columns turn the label and its glyph to the right edge, over the tabular figures. Sorting a column now shifts its ground and draws a rule under it — the glyph alone was doing too much work at a glance across twelve columns.

7 · Two things worth Dave's word

  • The group row has no ground of its own. It binds table/column/background, which resolves to the same hex as table/header/background in all four themes today, so the row reads by its caption type and its rule, not by colour. Either a theme splits the two tokens, or the group row stays a typographic distinction. split or type
  • The disabled column is muted with alpha, not text/disabled. That token is #E1E1E1 and is illegible on the #F0F0F0 header ground — the repo's own .pbtn[disabled] precedent only works because pagination sits on the page, not on the header. alpha or token

8 · #261 G3 — the header checkbox IS the check component

Dave, verbatim. “the grid header is fine except the checkbox is rendered oddly, can't we just use the check component?” He is right twice over. What was wrong: this review page drew its own checkbox and pulled the tick through <use href="#g-tick">. A descendant selector cannot reach into a <use> shadow tree, so .box path{fill:none} never landed — the browser painted the path's initial fill:black and the open tick M3.5 9.5 L7.5 13.5 L14.5 5 closed into a small black triangle sitting in an unfilled box. What it is now: the grid does not draw a checkbox at all. Selection-controls is not registered as an injectable partial, so the composition rule applies — its token block and its rule block are carried across byte for byte under an alias layer, and its own two glyphs are used: the animated stroke-draw tick for checked and its own dash for indeterminate. The grid adds two lines and states both: the 44px label that carries the hit area (32px in compact, above the 24px floor, never below), and a stroke-dasharray on the dash, without which the offset is inert and the dash draws in every state.
