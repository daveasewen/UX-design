Your words · the ask

Six decisions.

One letter is an answer; a letter with a sentence attached is a sequel, and it is read back before anything is built on it. D-1 asks for a letter and, separately, one word. D-3 is the one every other decision waits on, and it now carries the thin slice.

D-1

The layers — five ingestion waves, or three views by force? One letter, then one word.

The five layers as built are the order the graph was landed in: base wiring, rulings, the WCAG corpus, the 470 HSBC rules, the 145 UX principles — each behind the chip its lane earned. They are not five kinds of knowledge. The proof is accessibility: it sits in three of the five today — 55 WCAG criteria in “guidelines”, 52 rules from the accessibility-*.md files in “guideline rules”, 20 standards-family principles in “UX principles” — with 0 edges between the three holdings (s275-D4 said so and deferred the joins to a named lane). The record called “governance” is measured, by the extractor’s own gov_target() test, as 435 of 593 rulings that govern only files, scripts and notes, 99 that govern only components, 59 both — a proxy from governs[] targets, not a reading of each ruling: about three-quarters of the record is how the system was built, one quarter is design precedent. This card asks two things and only two: a letter for the views, and a word for the name of the record.

Codex · Constitution · System management · or your own word. The record is the 593 rulings with their evidence and sessions; it is not one of the three views.

Recommendation. Option (a), and the word is yours. Your instinct is right about the symptom: the categorisation is the commit history, and accessibility does belong in design governance. The cut by force (chooses / obeys / consults) is lane A2’s preference argued in prose — any re-cut fixes the measured symptom; this one gives the reader (D-3) a single verb per view. Force is a property of the edge, not the node: an obeys, appliesTo or design-scoped governs edge is an obligation wherever it lands. That is how the view handles the one leak A2V found — the 14 obeys→ux: edges s276-D3 ratified (6 metas citing 4 of the six A-grade laws with a $why): the edge is drawn in Design governance, the principle node stays in Explanation, and no edge is renamed. Not asked on this card, and unratified — lane A2’s proposal for you to author or refuse later: (i) a precedence ladder (law, then ruling, then BLOCKING, then ADVISORY, then the principles) — nothing in _rulings.json orders a ruling against a WCAG criterion today, and s277-D3 left the one known conflict open; (ii) a derived scope on every ruling from its governs[] targets (435/99/59), which over-counts as “system” the rulings that shape the designer brain (s269-D3, s274-D12, s275-D4); (iii) the dozen fences written once as a document — four are named today (fence 3 of #261, the two-red law, nam-002, “an instrument without a consumer is refused”), the rest are not. What the agent does differently under (a): nothing until D-3 wires a reader — then the reader asks one question per view instead of learning four stores and 51 edge types.

Lane A2 (Fable) proposed; lane A2V (Fable) counter-argued and split the card; lane A2F rewrote it on A2V’s verdict. Figures re-driven by A2V from the extractor’s own gov_target().

D-2

How do the 470 HSBC rules reach a component?

Today a rule reaches a component only by an edge to that component: obeys on 10 metas, 107 rules, 8 of 34 guideline files — the 7 named after a component (s276-D5) plus the data-visualisation family file (s277-D3). The other 26 files hold 350 rules that bind a facet every component has: copywriting 55, tone of voice 40, colour 34, typography 32, icons and pictograms 31, neurodiversity 26 — 52 of the 350 are accessibility rules, and 51 of those already cite an SC that names components, so they are not facet rules. A designer knows the tone-of-voice rules apply to every label on the screen. The graph cannot say so, which is why 50 of the 59 BLOCKING rules bind nothing from the agent’s seat (4 of the 50 reach a component in two hops through cites → appliesTo; 46 reach nothing by any path). s277-D3 priced the per-component route for one family file and refused the all-to-all shape; the family-node option was declined because nothing could carry it.

Recommendation. Option (a). It is the single change that moves the most obligations into the agent’s reach for the least authored work, and it is the s274-D9 shape (one place for one fact) rather than the s274-D12 shape (inference wearing a ref) — provided the derived binding is labelled derived and carries its exception row. The join key it needs — which token groups a component uses — is already a typed field in the metas and is what D-5 turns into nodes. For the thin slice (Part A) this is the leg that changes most: today _compose_slice.py attaches BLOCKING rules by a hand-written vocabulary dictionary and marks them “routed”; under (a) the same attachment is a structural join and the label becomes “derived”.

Lane A2 (Fable); the BLOCKING count is measured over _rules-index.json against every meta’s edges.obeys; the two-hop and named-file counts are lane A2V’s.

D-3

The thin slice at build time — wire the reader, or keep reading the metas?

12 edge types carrying 3,110 live edges — 47% of the graph — are read by nothing, and 7 more are permitted by the schema and read by nothing. Not because they are wrong: four of the twelve (evidencedBy, appliesTo, ruledIn, definedIn) answer a designer’s question directly. Because the reader they were built for does not exist. The generate skill’s step 1 reads showroom/index.json (23K tokens) and then the metas it names — the library is 138 files, about 415K tokens (1.6 MB) — and names three edge types in total; the pack ships _rulings.json empty by design and does not ship the rule nodes or the principle nodes. One tool already does the right thing: knowledge/_compose_slice.py turns a task sentence into roles → components → edges → BLOCKING rules → anti-neighbours → token groups → rulings at 19.1K tokens against 111K for the 31 metas it replaces on a dashboard task (5.8×; 2.6× on a four-meta bar-chart task; 3.8× on a fourteen-meta form) — and it is marked PROPOSAL, ADVISORY, NOT WIRED, with the wiring recorded as yours to rule. Part A below is the thin slice in full: what is read today, what the slice reads, what each decision on this page does to it, and the contract.

Recommendation. Option (a). Every other decision on this page changes what the agent does only after this one; it is the decision, and the thin slice is its instrument. The answer rate the audit measures — 6 of 12 by graph path, 4 of 12 reachable at design time — is the gap between a graph that exists and a graph that is consulted. A3’s ASK door (D-6 a) is this same lane by another name: one instrument, two verbs — compose a screen, ask one question.

Lane A2 (Fable), on the consumer census in A1 §4 and the compose-path read in its §6; the slice re-run on three tasks and the step-1 figures are lane A2F’s (slice-measure-v2.json); the 415K and the “four” are lane A2V’s corrections.

D-4

The vocabulary — 51 storage types, and what the agent is told

A1 finds the real concentrations: thirteen component→component types and eleven ruling→ruling types. Both were ratified deliberately — s267-D3 retyped twenty ruling edges by hand into those eleven. The conductor’s suspected cluster (governs / boundBy / enforcedBy / under / appliesTo) is six different endpoint signatures, not one. The only true inverse pair is governs (1484) / governedBy (28), and that is two ends of one authored join, not a synonym. The 51 types cost the system a line each in the validator and the explorer; they cost the agent nothing, because the agent is told three.

Recommendation. Option (a). The cost of 51 types is in the reader, not in the agent; a merge undoes a hand-ratified set to tidy a census. The prose tier stays as it is — mentions dashed and advisory under s267-D3, cites kept solid but labelled “parsed citation” because the author wrote the SC number in the rule text. For the thin slice this changes the labels on the output, not its content: the slice’s sections become the twelve verbs.

Lane A2 (Fable), on A1 §4’s endpoint signatures and §5’s join classes.

D-5

Tokens enter the graph — at what grain, and when?

There is no token: node, so Q9 — what tokens does this component consume and what breaks if I change one — cannot be asked of the graph. Yet tokens is a typed block in 135 of 137 metas (316 distinct references across 128 groups; the top twelve — text/, rag/, tertiary/, form/, border-radius/, icon/ … — carry 70% of the 1,587 group-mentions; 10 live token files), and tokens/_blast-radius.json already computes reach — so an honest today-cost for Q9 is about 25K tokens (the meta plus the blast-radius file), not the 595K A3 charged by reading canon.css. s269-D3 ruled the grain — its ruled text, verbatim: “TOKENS ENTER THE GRAPH AT TIER GRAIN - semantic versus primitive - and NEVER as 932 leaf nodes.” Nothing was built. D-2’s facet scopes key on exactly these groups.

Recommendation. Option (a). It is the cheapest structural family left — a typed field in 135 metas that nobody has turned into edges — and it is the join key D-2 uses, so the two land together or D-2 lands weaker. For the thin slice it changes the source of the tokens leg, not its output: today tokens_for() regexes the group out of each meta’s token strings and looks the tier up in a map built from eight files at load; under (a) it walks bindsToken and reads the tier off the node.

Lane A2 (Fable); the group census is its measurement (A2-measure.json §tokens). The s269-D3 wording, the 792 live leaves, the ≈25K Q9 cost and the group-with-tier reading are lane A2V’s call (VERIFY.md Part 2, D-5).

Part A · the thin slice at build time · D-3's evidence


What the agent reads when it builds a screen — today, and under the graph.


Plain. The thin slice is the idea that the agent should be handed the few thousand tokens of design knowledge
a screen actually needs, in the order a designer would want them, instead of a library to browse. Today it does not
exist on the compose path. The generate skill's step 1 says find the components in showroom/index.json,
then read each one's meta, then copy its snippet: that is one 23K-token index, then a judgement
call about which of 138 metas to open (median 2,576 tokens each, 415K
for the library), then about 10K tokens of markup per component placed. The tokens are found by
opening canon.css — 588,102 tokens, because there is no smaller door. Nothing on that path reads a
graph edge: the skill names three edge types in prose and the agent reads them off the meta by eye. The rulings, the
rule nodes and the principle nodes are not read and, in the pack, not present.


The one tool that builds a slice, knowledge/_compose_slice.py, does it the way a designer would: the task
sentence is turned into roles and chart intents, the roles into components, the components into their edges, then the
BLOCKING rules first, the must-nots, the type composites, the icons, the token groups with their tier, and the
rulings behind each item — with everything it could not resolve declared as ref: null and a note,
never invented. Re-run this session on three tasks it comes out at 19,117 tokens for a dashboard against
111,468 for the 31 metas it replaces (5.8×;
22× against the library), 11,716 for a form (3.8×),
5,735 for a bar chart (2.6×). Nothing calls it. That is D-3.


A3 measured the other half: the twelve designer questions, asked one at a time, cost about 2.6M tokens
of file reads today when each question's reads are summed (about 1.0M if the same file is counted once and
Q9 is charged honestly), against 2,139 tokens for the twelve typed graph slices that answer them —
a few hundred tokens a question, under 1K each. So the thin slice has two verbs, one per table below: compose a
screen (the task in, the slice out) and ask one question (a node and a verb in, the answering edges out).
