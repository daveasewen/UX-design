Your words · the ask

Six decisions.

One letter is an answer; a letter with a sentence attached is a sequel, and it is read back before anything is built on it. D-1 carries the most weight and D-3 is the one every other decision waits on.

D-1

The layers — five ingestion waves, or three views by force?

The five layers as built are the order the graph was landed in: base wiring, rulings, the WCAG corpus, the 470 HSBC rules, the 145 UX principles — each behind the chip its lane earned. They are not five kinds of knowledge. The proof is accessibility: it sits in three of the five today — 55 WCAG criteria in “guidelines”, 52 rules from the accessibility-*.md files in “guideline rules”, 20 standards-family principles in “UX principles” — with 0 edges between the three holdings (s275-D4 said so and deferred the joins to a named lane). And the record called “governance” is measured as 432 of 593 rulings that govern only files, scripts and notes, 99 that govern only components, 62 both: three-quarters of it is how the system was built, one quarter is design precedent.

Recommendation. Dave’s instinct is right about the symptom and half right about the cure: the categorisation is wrong, and accessibility does belong in design governance — but the cut that fixes it is by force (chooses / obeys / consults), not by topic. Of his three words, codex fits the ruling record (bound, ordered, cited by id, with receipts); constitution fits only the dozen fences, which should be written down as the precedence ladder; system management is a scope on a ruling, not the name of the record. What the agent does differently: nothing until D-3 wires a reader — and then everything, because a reader over three force layers asks one question, “the obligations for this component, in precedence order”, instead of learning four stores and 51 edge types.

Lane A2 (Fable), from A1’s census and this lane’s ruling-scope and accessibility-holdings measurements.

D-2

How do the 470 HSBC rules reach a component?

Today a rule reaches a component only by an edge to that component: obeys on 10 metas, 107 rules, 8 of 34 guideline files — the files named after a component. The other 26 files hold 350 rules that bind a facet every component has: copywriting 55, tone of voice 40, colour 34, typography 32, icons and pictograms 31, neurodiversity 26. A designer knows the tone-of-voice rules apply to every label on the screen. The graph cannot say so, which is why 50 of the 59 BLOCKING rules bind nothing from the agent’s seat. s277-D3 priced the per-component route for one family file and refused the all-to-all shape; the family-node option was declined because nothing could carry it.

Recommendation. Option (a). It is the single change that moves the most obligations into the agent’s reach for the least authored work, and it is the s274-D9 shape (one place for one fact) rather than the s274-D12 shape (inference wearing a ref). The join key it needs — which token groups a component uses — is already a typed field in the metas and is what D-5 turns into nodes.

Lane A2 (Fable); the BLOCKING count is measured over _rules-index.json against every meta’s edges.obeys.

D-3

Wire the design-time reader, or keep reading the metas?

12 edge types carrying 3125 edges — 47% of the graph — are read by nothing, and 7 more are permitted by the schema and read by nothing. Not because they are wrong: nine of the twelve answer a designer’s question directly. Because the reader they were built for does not exist. The compose skill says “read the metas” — 137 files, about 730K tokens — and names three edge types in total; the pack ships _rulings.json empty by design and does not ship the rule nodes or the principle nodes. One tool already does the right thing: knowledge/_compose_slice.py turns a task sentence into roles → components → edges → BLOCKING rules → anti-neighbours → rulings at a self-measured 19,113 tokens against 111,468 for the metas it replaces — and it is marked PROPOSAL, ADVISORY, NOT WIRED, with the wiring recorded as yours to rule.

Recommendation. Option (a). Every other decision on this page changes what the agent does only after this one. The answer rate the audit measures — 6 of 12 by graph path, 4 of 12 reachable at design time — is the gap between a graph that exists and a graph that is consulted.

Lane A2 (Fable), on the consumer census in A1 §4 and the compose-path read in this lane’s §6.

D-4

The vocabulary — 51 storage types, and what the agent is told

A1 finds the real concentrations: thirteen component→component types and eleven ruling→ruling types. Both were ratified deliberately — s267-D3 retyped twenty ruling edges by hand into those eleven. The conductor’s suspected cluster (governs / boundBy / enforcedBy / under / appliesTo) is six different endpoint signatures, not one. The only true inverse pair is governs (1484) / governedBy (28), and that is two ends of one authored join, not a synonym. The 51 types cost the system a line each in the validator and the explorer; they cost the agent nothing, because the agent is told three.

Recommendation. Option (a). The cost of 51 types is in the reader, not in the agent; a merge undoes a hand-ratified set to tidy a census. The prose tier stays as it is — mentions dashed and advisory under s267-D3, cites kept solid but labelled “parsed citation” because the author wrote the SC number in the rule text.

Lane A2 (Fable), on A1 §4’s endpoint signatures and §5’s join classes.

D-5

Tokens enter the graph — at what grain, and when?

There is no token: node, so Q9 — what tokens does this component consume and what breaks if I change one — cannot be asked. Yet tokens is a typed block in 135 of 137 metas (316 distinct references; text/, rag/, tertiary/, form/, border-radius/, icon/ carry most of them; 10 live token files), and tokens/_blast-radius.json already computes reach. s269-D3 ruled the grain: tier, never 932 leaves. Nothing was built. D-2’s facet scopes key on exactly these groups.

Recommendation. Option (a). It is the cheapest structural family left — a typed field in 135 metas that nobody has turned into edges — and it is the join key D-2 uses, so the two land together or D-2 lands weaker.

Lane A2 (Fable); the meta count and group census are this lane’s measurement (A2-measure.json §tokens).

D-6

Which augmentation first?

Lane A3’s shortlist — 5 candidates in A3-shortlist.json, ranked as A3 ranked them (ASK, CLOSURE, SHAPES, TOKENS, NEIGHBOURS); the first is A3’s recommendation. Each is priced at one lane or less and tested against s274-D12. The full argument and A3's ten refusals are in A3-AUGMENT.md.

Recommendation. Take A3’s first, ASK, and take it as the same lane as D-3: a typed-question door over the graph and the wired compose slice are one instrument with two verbs, not two. A3’s TOKENS reaches the same gap as D-5 but at about 950 nodes — the leaf grain s269-D3 refused — so the two disagree on grain and not on need: take D-5(a) and A3’s aliasOf lands on the tier nodes, or take A3’s shape and s269-D3 is superseded by id, never silently. Its SHAPES is Part C of this page written into the validator — the two lanes converged without reading each other, which is the strongest signal either produced.

Lane A3 (Fable) ranked and priced the five; the convergence line is lane A2’s.
