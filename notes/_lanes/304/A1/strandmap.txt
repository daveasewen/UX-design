 01
 Strand one
 The designer pack and the Spider
 In flight 
 A designer installs a kit, and an agent builds with it.
 The pack is the design system in a form an AI can read — tokens, components, rules and skills. The Spider is the agent that reads it and builds screens. Version 1.0.13 is running on your work machine right now, on the HSBC CEO banking prototype.
 It is the only strand that has been used outside this repo, by a different model, on a real brief. That makes it the strongest evidence in the room and the one that needs the least work this week.
 What it proved, and what it got wrong. The Spider's own write-up of that build was checked line by line here. Five of its six factual claims about our system hold. The sixth — its 33-pass / 8-fail gate scoreboard — is arithmetically correct but cannot separate its own failures from ones it inherited, because it never ran the baseline the pack ships for exactly that purpose.
 Its one real analytical miss is the same miss twice: it read two deliberately-ruled numbers as defects. That is not a flaw in the Spider so much as a gap in what the pack tells an agent about which values are intentional.
 don't take it on face value, use your judgment, but what it did worked. Dave, 2026-09-19

 The pack ships with gates that are red before a designer writes a line — a first CI run is red for reasons that are not theirs. Unruled since #219.

 Nothing in the pack tells a reading agent that a value is ruled rather than accidental. Both of the Spider's misreads are that gap.

 02
 Strand two
 The bento canon and its dashboard template
 Open 
 The grid is ruled. The delivery of it is not.
 Bento is canon — promoted, with each theme carrying its own gutter tunings, and bentos nest inside bentos. The dashboard template is the page-scale piece that puts that grid to work. It is where the one-shot design lands.
 Your correction stands at the centre of this strand. There is no contradiction to resolve by picking a winner — there are two quantities, both deliberately per-theme.
 The problem with the gutters it that they are deliberately different for the themes and the gutters are also different for the inner and outer bentos we essentially have a structural bento and embedded bentos or tile groupings. Dave, 2026-09-19 
 What survives that correction is narrower and real. The template hard-codes one theme's pair of gutters — 40px outer, 4px inner — as literal numbers, rather than delivering whichever theme is selected. So a console or supercharge dashboard gets mono's spacing.
 The reason is single and named: the piece that would generate per-theme values was ruled into existence a month ago and never built. The manifest it would read declares itself groundwork-only in its own header and has sat unused since.
 What we have
 Two literals pinned in the compiled stylesheet, correct for one theme of four, with a source comment saying so.
 What Friday needs
 The generation arm built, so the template asks the theme for its outer and inner gutter and gets the right pair — and four themes rendered side by side for your eye.
 Mono's zero is doubted, not overruled. The ruled value for mono's bento gutter is 0, taken from your own tuner exports. You said you were not sure that is right. Nothing has been changed on that doubt — the value still stands until a review with your eye on it says otherwise. And whether that particular dial is even the structural gutter is still to be read from source, not assumed.
