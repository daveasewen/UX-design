The lens

A designer brain is a graph that answers a designer’s questions at build time.

The #277 audit wrote down the twelve questions a designer actually asks of a design system, and measured which ones the graph could answer. Six are answered in storage. Two are partial. Four cannot be answered by any edge that exists.

The orphans matter only where they sit on one of those twelve. Below, each set says which question it serves — and where a set serves none, the recommendation is to declare it and stop counting it.

The recommendation

Six moves, in this order.

  1. Wire the chip map — one lane, no question.MineSix edge types, 376 authored lines, no chip can draw any of them. Putting them in the System view lights the shapes, the intents and the roles (49 dots), and puts Q7 on the stage at 100%. Nothing new enters the graph; the page just stops hiding what is there.
  2. Say whether a research family may be a node.YoursThe pink cloud is a switch left off. Flipping it puts 32 family nodes on the stage — a new kind of thing. I recommend yes: a principle without a home is a card without a drawer, and the designer brain needs to walk from “Laws of UX” to its 26 principles, not only from 30 polarities to 45 of them.
  3. Say which family owns a file two records both name.YoursThree guideline files got claimed by the Constitution because a ruling listed them first, and 24 rules went dark for it. I recommend: a guideline file belongs to the guideline family whichever record names it first, and the builder declares the tie instead of resolving it by read order.
  4. Say where a component’s citation of a principle is drawn.Yours14 hand-written lines from ten components to four principles. Already ruled to stand; no chip draws them. I recommend they live in the Explanation view as “cited by a design”, not as a fourth provenance in Design governance — a principle is a reason, not an obligation.
  5. Put the line type the census cannot see.YoursQ3 — what principle underlies this rule — is the question a designer brain exists to answer, and there is no edge type in either direction. It orphans nobody, so the census never saw it. I recommend an authored rule→principle edge, started on the 59 blocking rules against the six A-grade laws.
  6. Declare the two that are not orphans.Mine, on your radio94 HSBC rules are dark only because their chip loads off; 8 lockups are dark because you ruled they should show as bound by nothing. Both are declared and leave the count.

If you take all six, the stage goes from six drawn designer questions to eight (nine once the rule→principle edge is authored), three lanes run (chip map · principles · file ownership), and the rule→principle edge becomes the next thing to author.

The sets

Eleven blocks. The ten from the census, plus the one it could not see.

Each block keeps the census figure and cause, adds which designer question it serves, and marks the answer I would give. The radio is yours; the marked one is a recommendation, not a default — nothing is pre-selected.

01

100 nodes

dark at all three settings

CauseThe source never emitted the edge

The edge type exists and the generator can write it; it was switched off, so nothing was emitted.

Designer questionQ6 for principles — what is this principle’s home, and what was it graded on. And the walk from a family to its principles, which no question on the list asks because it has never been possible.

The pink cloud — 100 of 145 UX principles

The Explanation view holds 145 principles and 30 polarities. Every polarity is wired; a principle gets a line only when a polarity names it as a party, and between them the 30 polarities reach 45 principles. The other 100 have no edge of any kind in storage.

The wiring is written. The principle generator can emit two more edge kinds, both held behind flags by the ruling that landed the family: one groups each principle under its research family (all 145 carry it, across 32 families — Laws of UX 26, NN/g heuristics 13, GOV.UK 11, WCAG 2.2 11, Tognazzini 10, deceptive design 8), the other links a principle to the sources it was graded on (all 145 carry it).

Turning the first on gives every dark dot a line the day it runs, and puts 32 family nodes on the stage.

Recommendation — put the question, answer yes

Families enter as nodes in the Explanation view, and both flags go on. A designer brain that can only reach a principle through a polarity is a library where you can only find a book by its argument with another book. The family is the shelf. The sources are the receipt — the same thing Q6 already gives every ruling.

What it costs: one ruling, then one lane that flips the flags, re-runs the validator and proves the count moved from 100 to 0.

07

dark until the Constitution chip joins

CauseThe relation has no declared home

Both ends exist and the line is drawn — but the far end was filed in a family that hides it.

Designer questionQ2 and Q5 — which rules bind me, and what was ruled about them. 24 rules answer only when the whole Constitution is switched on.

24 rules that need the Constitution to show a line

With every chip on, 24 rule dots are still dark. Their only line names the guideline file that defines them — and for these, that file was claimed by the Constitution family, because a ruling listed the file among its own artefacts before the rule family got to it. Three files do all the damage: data-visualisation (15 rules), accessibility-interaction-design (6), naming (3).

A guideline file is a guideline file whichever record names it first. The graph currently says otherwise, and three dots decide the fate of 24.

Recommendation — put the question, answer: the guideline family owns it

A file’s family is decided by what the file is, not by who cited it first. The builder should declare a tie when two records claim one file, and the tie-break should be a rule, not read order. This is a build-order accident with a visible cost and a one-line answer.

What it costs: one ruling, then a short lane in the builder. The lane is small; the ruling is the part that has to come first.

14 obeys lines from ten components to four principles, ruled to stand, never drawn.

Designer questionThe only component→principle chain the graph has. It is the seed of Q3 and Q4 — why does this design do what it does.

The 4 principles a design actually cites

Ten components carry a hand-written list of the rules and principles they obey, with a sentence attached to each, and 14 of those 168 lines land on a principle. It was ruled that those lines stand. The page has never drawn one.

Drawing it is not the same one-line change as the shapes and the roles. The Design governance view was fixed at three provenances (WCAG, HSBC rules, design-scoped rulings). A principle is not an obligation, so putting it there as a fourth is the wrong shape.

Recommendation — put the question, answer: draw it in the Explanation view

“Cited by a design” is an explanation, not a rule. The chip belongs beside the polarities, and the line reads component → principle with the designer’s own sentence on the edge. That keeps Design governance at its three provenances and gives the Explanation view its first door that does not start at a polarity.

What it costs: one ruling, then it rides lane 03 — the same chip-map line, one more family.

Designer questionQ3 — what principle underlies this rule, and how strong is the evidence for it. Zero of 470 today.

The line the census could not see

A designer brain is asked “why” more than anything else. Why must the pie stop at five parts; why does the form label sit above the field. The answer is a principle with a grade, and the graph holds 145 graded principles and 470 rules — and not one line between them. Q3 is unanswerable not because a switch is off but because the line type was never authored.

The audit’s proposal is a rule → principle edge, authored not parsed, started where it pays most: the 59 blocking rules against the six A-grade laws. That is the smallest set that makes Q3 answerable for the rules a design cannot ship without.

Recommendation — put the question; this is the next authored edge

Not a lane yet: authoring 59 links with a sentence each is judgment work, and the grade on the far end is what makes the link worth having. It lands after the chip map and the family nodes, because those two give it something to be drawn on.

If you say yes, the first move is a page like this one — the 59 rules, each with a proposed principle and the sentence — for your export.
