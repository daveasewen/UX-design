Dave, #170 (2026-08-14), on being handed a controller full of item IDs and buttons: *"codes and buttons don't work for this particular human… 'W-0b' doesn't mean anything to me. I'm a knowledge worker not a coder… I understand the concepts behind the code but I'm not a practitioner. I'm obviously a very visual human, I grok better with both words and images."*

He self-describes: very good UI/UX designer, ex web-designer (~25 yrs ago) so HTML/JS at a conceptual level; cross-domain (user research, CX) but not practitioner-level; technical from the abstract level. He understands SHAPE, how components should look/behave, how Memento should FEEL — and he notices errors by eye.

**Why:** the ID vocabulary (`W-0b`, `G14`, token paths) lives in MY context, not his. A decision surface written in that vocabulary asks him to do my translation work. He wants "empathy rules" written for this.

**How to apply:** every decision put to Dave gets translated into plain sentences about what the thing IS and what changes — swatches, rendered examples, before/after visuals — never raw IDs or token paths as the primary surface. IDs may appear as small print for the record. Controllers are fine ([[feedback-live-controller]]) but their labels must be human sentences, and a prose + visual walk-through must accompany them. This refines, not replaces, [[feedback-live-controller]] and [[comms-style-exec-summary]] (dyslexia: short, decision-first).
