01

What it is

In one sentence

The definition a builder and a reviewer both read.

An Overview dashboard is the standing first screen of a destination. It answers two questions without a click — is anything wrong? and what needs me? — and it lets the reader act on the second answer in place.

It is a monitoring surface, not a report and not a search result. The reader arrives already knowing their job; the page's work is to tell them what changed since they last looked, and to be quiet when nothing did.

Three things follow from that sentence, and everything further down is a consequence of them:

  • Everything read every visit is on the first level. Anything else is one deliberate, labelled step away — expand, view-as-table, popover — and that step happens where the question was asked, not on another page.
  • The page executes. An item the reader must act on carries its one primary action on the row. A list you can only look at is a report.
  • Quiet is a designed state. A page with nothing wrong shows no colour. Hue is a budget spent on exceptions, never on confirming that things are fine.

02

Who reads it, and for how long

The reading

If a page cannot name its reader it cannot be graded, because every decision below is "compared to what?".

An Overview dashboard is written for one named operating role — the person accountable for the thing on the page, not a platform owner and not an analyst. It is read two to five times a day for under ninety seconds, usually standing, usually between other things.

Two consequences that the grade actually turns on:

  • Order is by frequency of reading, not by size of number. The metric that role checks first sits first, left to right.
  • One destination, one task. A page that serves two roles is two pages. The Spider's report on the library already names the opposite defect — several destinations reusing one reporting wall instead of supporting distinct tasks.

⚠ A page therefore cannot be composed without a named role in the brief. If the brief does not give one, that is a question to ask, not a default to invent.

03

What it must carry — the five regions

In reading order. A page missing any of the five is not an Overview dashboard; a page with a sixth region has a brief that needs asking about.

01

The attention strip

A thin row above the wall carrying the exceptions as chips, each one anchor-linked to its item in the needs-attention region. It orients; it does not summarise. If nothing is wrong the strip carries one neutral line and no colour.

the user sees the strip and it orientates them to pay attention towards the detail … the user might even click on the chip and it anchor links to the needs attention panelDave · ruling s251-D1
02

The headline row

Three to six metrics in one row, never a 2×2 board. Each carries value, a signed delta against a named period, and a trend shape — a sparkline, never a gauge or a ring. The count is whatever the data dictates.

The headline row should simply be whatever the data dictates, 5 is even okay in this caseDave · ruling s249-D2
03

The dominant evidence tile

Exactly one tile is the widest on the wall, and it holds the evidence the role actually came for. No other tile matches its width. If two tiles compete for that job the page has not decided what it is for.

04

The needs-attention region

A standing region — present whether or not it has contents, with an empty state that reads as good news. Each item shows status, due, and a count against the total; each carries its one primary action in place. At most six actions on the page.

05

The supporting evidence

Two to four tiles answering the brief's remaining clauses. Widths are importance-weighted from the ruled span set, not split evenly. Each chart tile carries "view as table" in its own header.

—

And the filter

Optional, and when present it ships collapsed as a chip row with a count and a clear-all, and it sits with the wall it filters — never in the page title zone acting on nothing.

What it is not

  • Not a 2×2 KPI board. Two rows of tiles where one row would do pushes the first chart below the fold.
  • Not a symmetric 3+3 wall. Equal quadrants carry no hierarchy; irregular widths are how importance is said.
  • Not a page of green ticks. Healthy is unhued. A 100% signal rate is a 0% signal rate.
  • Not animated on entry. Motion is the rarest signal and is never spent on load choreography on a monitoring page.
  • Not a drill-down hub. If the answer is one click away on another page, it belongs here or it does not belong.

Source for the composition clauses above: the twenty-nine draft principles DP-01…29 at reviews/DASHBOARD-PRINCIPLES-2026-09-05-v1.html. ⚠ Those principles are DRAFT and none of them is ruled — the page says so on its own face. This definition treats them as the standard to grade against because nothing else exists; adopting them is Dave's, and it is question 1 in § 07.

Two parts the library does not have

Both are named here so a builder composes around them rather than inventing them, and so the definition does not quietly assume a component that is not there.

  • No needs-attention organism. There is no Needs-attention or Attention-list snippet. The region is composed from List-items + Status-indicator + Button inside a Card-header-lockup card. Whether that organism should be minted is a question, not a decision.
  • No attention-strip component. The strip is ruled a good pattern at s251-D1 and has no snippet; it is built from Status-indicator chips inside a landmark.

Both gaps were first measured at #288 lane P (notes/_subreports/2026-09-19-288-P-composition-probe.md, gaps G4 and G6) and are unchanged today.

Three traps that are measured, not theoretical

  • The outer gutter will silently be 0 if the instance dial is written the way canon documents it. canon.css tells the author to declare instance dials as .c-bento.my-wall{…}, but the rule that restores the outer gutter for a bento-of-bentos carries a :has() and out-specifies a bare class. The dial only lands when it repeats the :has(). Measured #288 lane P, finding F2.
  • A nested wall narrower than 1100px cannot carry its own column count. The container bands re-declare the column count on the grid and answer the wall, not the window. So an inner wall may only carry its own parameter set when it spans the full six columns. Measured #288 lane P, finding F3.
  • Overflow is clipped in silence. .c-bento{overflow:hidden} is structural, not decoration — a tile whose content exceeds its row is cropped with no error in the DOM and no console warning. Measured #288 lane P, finding F5.

06

What a one-shot has to do to pass

Nine conditions in two groups. Each one is a reading, not an opinion — so a page can be failed by a number rather than by a mood.

A · It composed, it did not trace

what is the point of building the KG if the agent just traces an existing file … this isn't Apollo its a dot-to-dot bookDave, 2026-09-19
P1 · MaterialsThe page's DOM carries eight or more distinct .cn-* component scopes. A page built from the library uses the library; a traced page uses its own markup. (Measured at #288: traced 0, composed 11.)
P2 · Zero inventionNo invented component, no invented variant, no invented token, no raw hex anywhere in the page's own CSS. Every visual value resolves to a token.
P3 · The five regionsAll five regions of § 03 are present, in reading order, and the needs-attention region carries at least one real action element per item.
P4 · Decisions are citedEvery structural decision carries the ruling, principle or token it rests on — or is marked a designer's call. Anything the system could not supply is written down as a gap, not invented over.

B · Alignment, spacing and dimensions

we need to do something about the sloppiness and, its all about alignment spacing and dimensionsDave, 2026-09-19
P5 · GuttersThe outer wall's computed column-gap and row-gap both read the theme's mainSpacing (40px in mono). Every inner wall reads subSpacing (4px in mono). Read from the live DOM, not from the source.
P6 · Stop setEvery bento spacing dial the page authors is on {1, 2, 4, 16, 24, 40}. One free value is a fail. ⚠ The stop set is scoped to the bento edit-pass vocabulary (_bento_edit_rails.json → rail.spacing_stops); component-internal padding is the component's own idiom and canon itself uses 8px and 12px throughout, so the condition is read on the dials, not on every declaration — and the page reports both numbers separately rather than blurring them.
P7 · No ragged layoutsEvery band closes on the column count with no orphan cell. The per-row sum of data-c is a whole multiple of the wall's columns.
P8 · Nothing clipped, nothing deadNo tile's content overflows its clipped container, and no tile carries a dead band under its last row. Measured as scrollHeight vs clientHeight per tile — because the clip is silent.
P9 · Both modes, no errorsRenders clean in light and dark at 1440 with zero uncaught page errors, and every custom property the page's own source references resolves in the scope it is used in.

⚠ P7 cannot be read by any gate in the tree on a page that links knowledge/canon/canon.css — knowledge/_validate_composition.py reads the grammar from the artefact's own inline @container blocks and returns UNPROVEN otherwise. On a linked page P7 is read by a bespoke DOM probe at render time and reported as such. That is a gap in the instruments, not a softening of the condition.

07

What this definition cannot decide

Five questions. Each one changes the definition above; none of them is a lane's to answer.

1 · The principlesDP-01…29 are draft and unruled, and this whole definition rests on them. Do they become the standard, as a set, or does the definition stand on its own and the principles stay advisory?
2 · Five regions, or fewerIs the five-region list the requirement for the class, or the recommended composition — i.e. is a page without an attention strip disqualified, or merely unusual?
3 · The two missing partsShould a needs-attention organism and an attention strip be minted as components, given both are composed by hand on every dashboard today?
4 · The page groundThe ruled page ground is grey and there is no semantic grey-page token — today it borrows --surface-hover. Mint a background/app-canvas role, or keep borrowing? (The dark page ground was left expressly open at s219-D1.)
5 · The barIs passing all nine conditions enough to show on the 25th, or is the bar your eye and these are only the floor?
