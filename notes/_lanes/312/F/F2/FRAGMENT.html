0N · The list rule, worked on its own · your call

A list is the default, and it may be searched, filtered and sorted. The table takes over when a field is read down a column; the data grid only when rows are selected in bulk or edited in place.

"we need to revisit this rule, lists can be filtered and resorted, and the general preference is for lists in most cases, lets work on this rule on it's own."

The banking demo already works the way you said. Its payment activity tile is a list under a toolbar: a search box, a market filter, a currency filter, a credits-or-debits switch, a chip for each filter you set, and pages. Twelve payments, six to a page; filtered to Europe it shows four. The sentence you rejected ("when nobody needs to sort, filter, select or edit them") would have made that tile a data grid. Below are the same twelve payments in the three shapes, drawn on canon as it stands, so the line between them is something you can see rather than read.

Three ways to word the rule. Each is a whole rule, not a patch on the old sentence.

  • Option 1 · toolbar does not change the shapeA list is the default for records of one kind, each read across its own row. It may be searched, filtered, sorted and paged from a toolbar above it; the toolbar does not change what it is. The table takes over when a field must be read down a column across records. The data grid takes over only when rows are selected in bulk or edited in place. This is what the demo does, and it keeps the one line to the table you already ruled (the comparison test, #274): read across, list; read down, table.
  • Option 2 · sort is the lineSearch and filter belong to the list; sorting by a field means reading that field down a column, so the moment a list is sorted by a field the table takes over, and the grid when rows are selected or edited. The stricter reading of the comparison test. It would push a "newest first" list into a table, which is the case you said you prefer as a list.
  • Option 3 · list until it is editedThe list is the default whatever the reader needs, search, filter, sort and select alike; the data grid takes over only when a cell is edited in place, and the table is for a read-only comparison. Your instinct at full strength. The cost is a checkbox column on list rows, which is a column read down, so selection in bulk starts to look like a grid anyway.
  • RecommendationOption 1. The list stays the default and keeps its toolbar; the table is chosen by reading direction alone; the grid is kept for the two things a list cannot honestly do, choose many rows at once and change a value where it sits. Selection goes with the grid because a column of checkboxes is a column, and the grid already has one.
  • What it changesThe list's when-rule loses "needs = none"; the data grid's when narrows from "sort / filter / select / edit-in-place is required" to "select in bulk or edit in place"; the #305 page's sentence is replaced. Nothing drawn here is a ruling; the row this closes is the list rule you asked to work on its own (opened #305).

0N · The page-header lock-up · your call

Keep the built lock-up as the default and give it a foot: on a page with sub-views the tab strip sits on the lock-up's bottom edge under the title and actions, and on a drill-down the breadcrumb replaces the eyebrow.

"we have to work on this lock-up though"

The lock-up is built as three separate arrangements: simple (eyebrow, title, actions), breadcrumb with tabs, and a badge count with a meta line. The demo uses the simple one, with a meta line and one action. Below is the demo's own header drawn three ways, in light and dark, using only the lock-up's own classes; the second option uses the tab strip you accepted on Wednesday, the one that takes its ground's colour.

At phone width the built rule already folds the actions onto their own row under the title, so option 3 is what every option becomes at 390 anyway.

  • Option 1 · as builtEyebrow, title and meta line on the left; the actions on the title row. Compact, 126px tall at 1440. It has nowhere for sub-views to go: on the demo the payment views live inside the tile as a switch.
  • Option 2 · a foot for the tab stripBreadcrumb or eyebrow above; title, meta line and actions on one row; the tab strip on the lock-up's bottom edge. 198px tall. The strip's track line becomes the lock-up's lower edge, so the header ends where the page's views begin, and the breadcrumb gives a drill-down its way back without a second component.
  • Option 3 · two rowsThe title alone on its row, full width; the meta line and the actions share the second. 162px tall. Gives a long title the whole width and puts the actions level with the small print, but on a desktop it spends a row for nothing the first option lacks.
  • RecommendationOption 2, as the shape of the whole family, not a fourth arrangement. One lock-up with three optional parts: a crumb or an eyebrow above the title, a meta line under it, a tab strip on the foot; the actions always on the title row; the phone fold as built. The badge-count arrangement goes: a count belongs in a tab label or in the content, not beside the title.
  • What it changesThe lock-up's arrangement choice (simple, breadcrumb-tabs, meta-badge) is replaced by the three switches it already carries (breadcrumb, tabs, meta), and the page-title rule you accepted on Sunday stands as worded. The row this closes is the lock-up work you asked for (opened #305). A drawn option is not a ruling.
