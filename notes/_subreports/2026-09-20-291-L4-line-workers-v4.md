## WHAT LANDED

**1 · The ribcage's top is a yoke, not a lid.** v3's ribcage was a tapered
block 88 tall: a flat top, and two top corners where the arms left. The block
now stops at **TOR_HB = 72** — just above the chest pockets, which did not
move — and a yoke lathe carries z 72 to 97. `latheP` gained one number per
ring, **e**, the superellipse exponent: |x/ax|^e + |y/ay|^e = 1, so e = 2 is
the ellipse the lathe always drew and e large is a rectangle with its corners
rounded off. That is what lets the yoke's bottom ring BE the block's own
rectangle (e 20, and 3 per cent proud, which is what swallows the block's top
corners so none of them pokes out of the dome) and then round, ring by ring,
into a shoulder:

```
z 72  ax 22.29  ay 38.95  e 20      the block's section
z 78  ax 22.60  ay 40.00  e 4.6     the shoulder mass, widest
z 84  ax 22.00  ay 38.30  e 3.0
z 89  ax 20.30  ay 34.20  e 2.7     (shoulder plane z 88: 20.6 x 35.0)
z 93  ax 17.90  ay 29.00  e 2.4     the collar band sits here
z 97  ax 13.50  ay 20.00  e 2.2     crest; top cap buried inside the neck
```

Both the block's lid and the yoke's own bottom cap are buried, so the seam
between them inks nothing, and the block's top corners are inside the yoke's
lowest ring, so the corner creases stop where the dome starts instead of
running out into a lid.

**2 · Twelve facets, not eight.** The first build used the body's usual N = 8
and the corner crease would not die: the two lowest bands inked at 0.47 and
0.58, which at the arm root read as a sharp notch, and no choice of e fixed
it. The reason is that the section is ELONGATED — ay/ax about 1.75 — and at
N = 8 a section that long turns about 0.55 from facet to facet whatever its e,
so it inks a crease down each side however round the maths says it is. At
**YOKE_N = 12** the same ring turns 0.80. Only the yoke is at 12; every other
solid in the figure is still at 8.

**3 · Each upper arm has a deltoid cap.** v3's upper arm was a frustum
starting 10 above its pivot with a buried flat cap, so above the shoulder it
ended in a blunt tube against the block's corner. It is now ONE lathe:

```
z -8.5  POLE            DEL_H above the pivot, no cap, triangle fan
z -6.5  r  6.5
z -3.8  r 11.0
z -1.2  r 13.9
z  2.0  r 14.6          DEL_R, the deltoid, just below the pivot plane
z 84    r 11.5          UP_R1, the elbow; the only cap, buried
```

Profile slopes 3.25, 1.67, 1.12, 0.22, -0.038 fall monotonically, so the solid
is convex; every dihedral inside it is above 0.80 and the 8-gon's flats meet
at 0.707, both above HARD, so nothing inside the cap inks. It is silhouette,
not a joint marker. **DEL_H was 11 first and read as a pauldron** — the crown
stood 2 above the trapezius; at 8.5 it lands at z 96.5 against the crest at
97, just under it, where a deltoid belongs. `UP_LIFT` and `UP_R0` are gone.

**4 · The garment follows the curve.** The collar notch and the straight
shoulder seam were seated on the block's TOP FACE and that face is gone. Three
seams are seated on the dome instead, and are curved because it is: the
coverall's **yoke seam at z 83**, the **collar band at z 93**, and the front
**opening** carried up from the placket's top, which now stops at z 70 rather
than 74. `faceAt()` names a lathe's facet the way BF_FRONT names a box's — it
returns the facet whose centroid is nearest a point — so each seam is emitted
facet by facet and gated on its own facet, and stops at the silhouette like a
line lying on a curved surface. The yoke seam is drawn across the BACK only:
wrapped right round it fought the placket, the buttons and both pockets, and
it replaces the straight rule that crossed the back at z 70. The buttons, both
chest pockets, the waistband, the back centre seam and the trousers are
untouched.

**Carried over untouched:** projection, camera, orbit spring, conveyor and its
creeping dashes, base plate, fit sweep, the hidden-line pipeline and its

rules, ONE red workpiece with two plain ones, the idle, the three construction

takes its half-extents from the block below TOR_HB and from the yoke above it

## RULING-SHAPED QUESTIONS

1. The yoke is round in both directions, so the shoulder has no corner at all
   above z 78. Is that the amount of rounding you meant, or should the corner
   run higher and turn more sharply — a yoke that is more box-with-a-chamfer
   and less dome?
2. The deltoid cap's crown sits just UNDER the yoke's crest. Should the arm
   mass instead stand slightly proud of the trapezius, which is what a heavy
   jacket or a padded coverall would do?
3. The yoke is drawn at 12 facets while every other solid in the figure is at
   8. Is that inconsistency acceptable for the one part that had to be smooth,
   or should the whole figure go to 12?
4. The coverall's yoke seam is now on the BACK only. On the chest the placket,
   three buttons and two pockets carry the garment. Does the front want a
   curved seam as well, at the cost of more ink across a busy chest?
5. The collar is a band round the neck root at z 93 rather than a notched
   collar with a visible opening. Is a band right for a coverall, or should
   the notch come back, redrawn on the dome?
6. The ribcage is now two solids — a box to z 72 and a dome above it. The box
   still has four hard corners below the shoulder. Should the whole ribcage
   round off, so the chest is soft too, or is the box the construction-method
   reading you want kept?
7. The deltoid's silhouette crosses the dome and draws an oval seam over each
   shoulder. It reads as a sleeve cap. Is that the right amount of seam, or
   should the arm lap far enough that no line crosses the shoulder at all?
