## Where I chose, and the strongest case for what I rejected

**RSQ 1 of the probe — the id on the CARD, not the row.** I put `id="dp08-na"` + `data-dp08-target` on the whole `stat-card`. *The strongest case for the ROW instead*: the strip has two items and only one of them is actionable, so a chip that lands on the card lands the reader on a container whose first line is the head row they just read — the finer target would put focus on the specific fact the chip named, which is what "strip items point at their detail" most literally asks for, and it would scale if a second chip ever became actionable (each chip owning its own row id rather than both pointing at one card). I rejected it because `s251-D1` makes Undrawn strip-only, so the card will only ever have ONE head row to target, and because focusing a row rather than the card would announce one line to a screen reader while the three item rows below — the actual work — go unmentioned. Focus on the card reads head row *and* items as one arrival.

**Head row vs no head row.** I built the promoted head row (Awaiting approval only). *The strongest case for NO head row*: the strip already carries `14 payments · 5 need you` in the header; repeating it verbatim inside the card is the double-reporting the probe measured (`Awaiting approval ×2` above the fold at 1440), and a reader who scans both surfaces reads the same sentence twice for no new information — the card could simply lead with its item rows, of which `USD payment run — 5 to approve`-style lines already carry the detail. I rejected it because at 1100 and 820 the card is 900.5 / 968.5 from the top and the strip is 277 / 345 — over 550px apart, never co-visible — so at the two narrow bands the head row is the ONLY place the count appears next to its `$2.7m` amount, and after an anchor arrival (which is precisely a jump past the strip) the head row is what tells the reader they arrived at the right thing. The `×2` cost is paid only at 1440, where the two are 240px apart and read as index and body.
