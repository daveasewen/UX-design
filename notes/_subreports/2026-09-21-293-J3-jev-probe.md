## 2 · The question set — what changed from J2's, and why

J2's Score was three abstract rungs ("Hides the gaps / Names the gaps / Names the gaps and
their boundary"). Against the skill's rule those describe **degrees of virtue**, not situations
a reader could match a page to, and there was no no-match outcome — every page on earth is
forced onto the scale. J3's set (verbatim in `notes/_lanes/293/J3/questions-as-sent.json`, which
is the file `probe.py --dump` emits and the file that was sent):

- **Noul A — `marks_every_gap`.** J2's premise, restated so high = yes, with `criteria` on both
  poles that anchor the judgment **at the capability itself** rather than anywhere on the page.
- **Noul B — `claims_unbuilt_as_available`.** *Independent*, not A's negation: a page can fail A
  without over-claiming (it just says nothing), and could in principle pass A while still
  over-claiming elsewhere. This is the "independently useful presence judgment" the skill asks
  for, and it is the question J2 never had.
- **Score — `absence_honesty`, four levels.** Level 0 is the **no-match outcome** ("does not
  present a list, grid or inventory of capabilities at all — a dashboard of figures, a single
  article or a component demo"). Levels 1–3 each name a concrete arrangement of markers, and
  each carries the qualifier *at the item itself* so the level stands alone without reference to
  its neighbours.

All three were sent **in one request per artefact** — the skill's "ask independent questions
over the same state together". `state` was a JSON object, not a bare string:
`{"artefact_path": …, "page_text": …}`, tag-stripped, cap 6000. **The cap never bit on any of
the three; all texts were sent whole** (2582 / 2496 / 2583 chars).

## 5 · Judgment

**Did the rubric discriminate P > M > N? Partly. P > N and M > N, sharply. P > M, no.**
2.99 vs 2.92 is not a separation; M put 0.93 of its mass on the *top* level, the one level it
was constructed not to satisfy. Noul A agreed with the Score (0.85 vs 0.82), so this is not a
Score artefact — **both questions saw M as essentially P**.

The cause is the instrument, not the rubric. **M's mutation is invisible in `state`.** Stripped
of tags, the only textual change is that one line, `Coming soon`, vanished and nothing replaced
it; the fade is CSS (`data-state`), which `html_to_state` discards by design. And M's key
section still reads *"Two are faded and badged: User Research & Insights, and CX Principles."*
So the text Jev was handed **still asserts both gaps are marked**. Jev answered the state it was
given, correctly. What J3 actually measured is the cost of `html_to_state` — the loss lane J2
published in its CANNOT block and listed in §8 as untested. It is now tested, and it is
load-bearing: *a mutation that a human sees instantly can be undetectable after stripping, and
the residual prose can actively vouch for the removed marker.*

**Did Noul B flip? Yes, cleanly — 0.13 → 0.89 → 0.21.** The independent premise is the strongest
result in the lane: it separates over-claiming from under-marking, which J2's single question
could not do, and it is the only signal that fired differently in kind rather than in degree.

**Did Score confidence leave 1.0? Yes — 0.99, 0.34, 0.92.** J2's exactly-1.0, the number it
said it would least want a threshold built on, is broken. N's 0.34 is a genuinely split answer
(0.48 / 0.39 / 0.13 across levels 1–3), and the split is informative: the *at the item itself*
qualifier only half held. Jev could not settle whether N's prose sentence naming the two gaps
counts as marking them. That is the rubric's remaining soft edge, and it is now visible instead
of hidden behind a 1.0.

**P scored 2.99/3 against a four-rung concrete rubric where J2 scored 2.0/2 against three
abstract rungs** — normalised, 0.997 vs 1.000. Rewriting the rubric to the skill's rules did
**not** move P. P's top score survived a harder scale, which is the one thing here that
strengthens J2's original claim.

**Latency, measured: 584.1 / 620.9 / 578.9 ms, mean 594.6.** J2 measured 703.3 / 683.6, mean
693.5. TypeSafe declares **70–500 ms**. **All five calls to date exceed the top of the declared
band** — J3 by 1.16–1.24×, J2 by 1.37×. J3's three were consecutive on a connection the session
had already warmed, which is the likeliest reason they sit ~100 ms lower; **that is a hypothesis,
not a measurement** — no warm/cold control was run, and n = 5 is not a distribution. Anyone
quoting a figure inside Apollo should say *~600–700 ms measured end-to-end from this sandbox*,
and say that the vendor's 70–500 ms is presumably server-side inference only.

### What this does not prove

- **n = 3, one question set, one run.** Nothing here says the answers are stable across repeats;
  no artefact was asked twice.
- **The artefacts were chosen by us, and two of three were built by us from the third.** N and M
  are derivatives of P, so this is a sensitivity test on one document, not a sample of Apollo's
  pages. A rubric that separates three variants of one page may not separate two unrelated pages.
- **Level 0, the no-match outcome, never fired.** It drew 0.0 on all three — correct, since all
  three are capability grids, but it means the no-match rung is **untested in the positive
  direction**. A dashboard was available (`notes/_lanes/292/D/overview-dashboard-oneshot-v1.html`)
  and the budget did not stretch to it.
- **Accuracy is still unmeasured.** The grep ground truth is mine, assembled by me, about a
  fixture I wrote. Agreeing with 0.89 is not validating it.
- **P > M being flat does not clear the rubric.** It has an innocent explanation that I believe,
  but the experiment that would separate "the rubric can't see it" from "Jev can't judge it" —
  re-running M with the contradicting key-section prose corrected — was not run.
