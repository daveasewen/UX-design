### 6 — the rigid-move claim (the core of the lane)

This is the claim worth attacking, and it survives.

**First, the framing in the brief is not testable as posed.** The brief asked me to confirm
`new = old×s + t` against `git show HEAD:...`. That cannot hold and should not: HEAD's
wordmark was itself destructively per-node snapped (47 of its 215 coordinates are exact
integers). Fitting new against HEAD gives a **max residual of 0.616 px** and independent
axis slopes kx = 0.98729, ky = 1.03097 — i.e. the *old* file is the distorted one. Reported
for completeness:

```
fit new = s*old(HEAD) + t :  s=0.99234  tx=0.36509  ty=-0.66507
                             max|residual| = 0.616158 px   rms = 0.161128
                             independent kx=0.9872857  ky=1.0309697
old coords that are exact integers: 47/215
new coords that are exact integers:  5/215
```

**The meaningful test** is the new master against the 315×85 Figma source, which is what the
rewritten `snap_wordmark()` actually maps from. I parsed every coordinate of every wordmark
path — on-path nodes **and** every Bézier control point — from both files, confirmed the
command/axis sequences match element-for-element (215 coordinates per file), forced the scale
to the exact ratio `s = h/85` with a single (tx, ty) per file, and measured the residual.

Across **all 20 masterbrand masters**, 215 paired coordinates each (4300 coordinates total):

```
WORST residual anywhere, s forced to h/85, single (tx,ty):  0.0000543 px
```

Per-file worst residuals range 4.80e-5 … 5.43e-5 px. That is exactly the floor imposed by the
generator's 4-decimal output rounding (5e-5). **There is no residual beyond serialisation
noise.** Best-fit independent axis slopes agree to within 1.3e-7 (`kx − ky` ∈ [−7.6e-7,
+7.5e-7]) — pure least-squares noise on a 215-point fit, not anisotropy. Sample:

```
masterbrand-light-colour-32.svg n=215 s=h/85=0.37647059 kx=0.37647074 ky=0.37647118  max|res|=0.0000498 px
masterbrand-dark-mono-40.svg    n=215 s=h/85=0.47058824 kx=0.47058811 ky=0.47058892  max|res|=0.0000480 px
```

The translation does what the docstring says: at 32 px the wordmark's left edge lands on
x = 69.0000 (integer column) and its cap line on y = 9.0000 (integer row); likewise 7.0 at
24 px and 12.0 at 40 px. Reading the diff of `_gen_masters.py` confirms the mechanism —
`quantise_runs()` is deleted outright, the `mark` set and per-node rounding are gone, and the
body is a single `for c in p.coords(): c[0] = c[0]*s + dx; c[1] = c[1]*s + dy` over nodes and
controls alike. **No per-node snapping, no kx≠ky. Claim upheld.**

| h | H crossbar | rows | B waist | rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 11.1114 → 12.7505 | 11–12 | 11.1806 → 12.6112 | 11–12 |
| 32 | 14.4818 → 16.6673 | 14–16 | 14.5742 → 16.4816 | 14–16 |
| 40 | 18.8523 → 21.5841 | 18–21 | 18.9677 → 21.3520 | 18–21 |

**The rows coincide at all three sizes.** The B's *outer* horizontals coincide even more
tightly: cap line 9.0002 vs H's 9.0000, baseline 22.3897 vs 22.3899 at 32 px — agreement to
3e-4 px at every size.

**Sub-pixel caveat, stated honestly.** Because nothing is snapped, the edges do not coincide
*exactly*: the B's waist is ~0.28 px thinner than the H's crossbar at 32 px and its edges sit
0.09–0.19 px inboard. In a 1× raster at midcolumn with a >0.5 alpha threshold, the H crossbar
reads as 3 rows (14,15,16) while the B waist's row-14/row-16 coverage falls to 0.43/0.48 and
reads as 1 row. This is **not a lane defect**: the same relationship exists in the 315×85
source (H crossbar 5.8052 units thick, B waist 5.0667, edges at 39.2437/45.0489 vs
39.4890/44.5557). The rigid transform reproduces the source faithfully, which is the point.
By contrast HEAD's snapped version forced both onto integer rows 15–17 — visually tidier at
one size, at the cost of the kinked bowls Dave caught by eye. The trade is the lane's stated
design and it is correctly executed.
