## In plain prose

Dave, on a screenshot of the pink UX family in explorer 1.18 — a cloud of dots with almost no
lines: *"do we have a plan to wire up the orphans etc?"* This lane answers the first half of that
honestly: it counts them, says why each one is dark, and prices the fix in the only two currencies
the record has — a lane, or one question to him.

**157 of the 4,618 nodes on the stage have no line that any chip can draw.** Four causes account
for every one of them, and the largest is the pink cloud itself: 100 of the 145 UX principles, 99
of which have no edge of any kind in storage — not a null, not a stub, nothing.

The pink cloud is not a gap in the vocabulary. It is a switch that was left off. The principle
generator can already emit two more edge kinds — a principle's research family, and the sources it
was graded on — and every one of the 145 principles carries both fields (32 distinct families).
`s275-D2` landed the family with those two behind flags, so the graph has never seen them.
Turning the first on gives all 100 dark dots a line on the day it runs, and it puts a family node
on the stage, which is a new kind of thing and therefore his word, not a lane's.

The second-largest set is the cheapest thing on the page: 94 base `rule:` dots that look like the
worst cloud at the page defaults and light up the moment the HSBC rule: chip goes on. Not orphans
— a default.

The rest split three ways. Three node sets (23 shapes, 14 intents, 12 roles) are dark because the
explorer's chip map has no family for the edge type that reaches them — the relations are
authored, live and unreachable by any chip. Twenty-four rules are dark until the Constitution chip
joins, because three guideline files were filed in the governance family when a ruling named them
among its artefacts first. Eight logo lockups are orphans on purpose, `s277-D7`'s declared nulls.

And one thing worth putting in front of him even though it orphans nobody: **376 authored lines
across six edge types that no chip anywhere can turn on** — `obeys` 168, `providesRole` 108,
`yieldsTo` 45, `answersIntent` 28, `hasDataShape` 26, `defaultActive` 1. The 14 `obeys`→`ux:`
lines inside that number are the whole of the component→principle chain `s277-D8` said stands, and
the page has never drawn one of them.

## Findings for the conductor

1. **The census re-implements the page's own predicates rather than trusting the stored `deg`.**
   Every node carries a build-time `deg`, and read naively it reports **99** orphans — it is
   chip-blind, and it disagrees with its own chip-blind recount for **345** nodes. The drawn
   count is **160** at the page defaults, **181** with every chip on and **157** with the
   Constitution. Any future orphan number has to name its chip setting or it means nothing.
2. **The ghost line is not a line.** An edge into a switched-off family is painted at alpha 0.04.
   It is not counted here, on lane LM's finding-1 precedent — but it is why a screenshot of the
   pink cloud looks slightly wired when it is not.

   governance family because a ruling listed it first is a build-order accident with a visible
   cost. Whatever the ruling says, the builder should declare the tie rather than resolve it by
   read order.

   figure is identical across both readings, and the page cites 1.19. A measuring lane reading a
   file another lane is rebuilding must state which build it read — this one does, in the page
