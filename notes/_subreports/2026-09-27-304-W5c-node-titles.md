## 1 · HOW A TITLE IS MADE

| node | read from | example |
|---|---|---|
| `ruling:<id>` (638) | the `ruled` headline; `says` when `ruled` is only a reference (`#167`, `GM-D7-am / #33`): 437 from `ruled`, 201 from `says` | `s245-D10 The radius candidate set from the tuner is ruled…` |
| `rule:<id>` (473) | the rule's `text` in `_rule_nodes.json`, its internal `CA-7 —` code dropped | `dv-009 2-dimensional, flat colour fills only` |
| `session:<n>` (92) | the rulings the page's own "What was ruled" tab lists for it (`ruled` is `#n` or the id starts `s<n>-`): date and count only | `#130 session of 2026-08-08, 6 rulings` |

The steps: take the first clause, cut at the first sentence end, `;`, spaced dash or `: ` that leaves at least 20 characters and more than one word. Drop record references that say nothing: id-bearing or long parentheticals, an id used as an adjective (`the s240-D2 ceiling`), and a leading id label. An id that is the object of a sentence (`built at #231`) is **kept**, because removing it leaves a broken sentence, and a broken title is worse than a code. Shouting caps are lowered to the casing the corpus itself uses for the word, so SC, WCAG and HSBC keep their caps and THE becomes the. Paths and identifiers (`_CHAIN.md`, `RATIFY_IDS`) are never re-cased. The result is capped at 60 characters on a word boundary with `…`, and a trailing weak word is trimmed. 386 of 1,203 titles are cut.

**A session has no text of its own in the tree.** Its title states only what can be counted: date and number of rulings. `_SESSIONS.jsonl` has 7 rows, and chat titles and git subjects are neither complete nor in the tree. I did not invent a topic.
