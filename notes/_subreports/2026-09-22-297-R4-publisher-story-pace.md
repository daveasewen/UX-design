## Where it jumps, repeats or sags

1. **The split diagnosis.** 06 lists five causes; 07–10 fix things; then 11 goes back to the causes, grouped as speed / consistency / quality, with "In truth it confirmed much of what we already knew". After the brain slide, "it" points at nothing, and one tick ("addressed first · the library") after four fixes reads like three problems left unsolved. Placed straight after 06, the same slide becomes the bridge: causes → three problems → "three things at once" → the fixes. It stays inside Evaluation, so the boss's chapters hold. (His #296 run order had the breakdown before the first improvement; the later moves carried it past the fixes.)
2. **Four, then three.** 07–10 count first, second, third, fourth; 12 says "The three main elements". The gates (09) vanish. A leadership room counts. Place them: the gates are how the three elements are held to standard — say it in 12's foot: "Parts, knowledge and judgement — with the gates checking all three at every step."
3. **Two decompositions back to back before the demo.** 12 (three elements) then 13 (twelve types of content, twelve file paths). 13 is the densest slide in the deck and sits in the slot where the room should be leaning forward for the demo. Move it after the close as a backup slide — show it if someone asks "what's in it?". Evaluation drops to seven slides and the demo arrives ~1:15 sooner.
4. **The turn into the demo isn't set up.** 14 says "One cold, repeatable testing prompt" — it doesn't say what is about to be built. The brief is a CEO's view of international banking for corporate clients: their world. Say so on the slide, and tell them what to watch for (a check catching something).
5. **The ask is a placeholder.** 15 carries a red DRAFT stamp, a line about Dave in the third person, and a foot saying the wording is a draft. "Help and support to develop it further." [his] is a wish, not an ask: nobody in the room knows what to say yes to. The data-and-research paragraph [his, tidied] is honest and worth saying, but on the ask slide it points at what Apollo doesn't do at the moment you want a yes.
6. **The close pays off.** "Automated product design you can bank on." [his] — lands after a demo in a bank. Keep.
