## Findings

1. The A page rule beats canon's band rules at every measured width: `--bento-cols-now` reads `4` at 1440, 1100 AND 820 (`probe251-P1.json`), so the row's band line is **4/4/4**, not the 4/2/1 the brief offered. At 820 that means 182px tiles.
2. Un-raggedness is exact, not approximate: `maxWdiff 0` and `lastRowVoid` 0 / −0.0 at all three widths and in dark.
3. The mutation to three tracks reproduces precisely the `s249-D5` defect and the probe names it (`rows 2 [3, 1]`, `lastRowVoid 682.7`) — the P1 proof is a proof.
4. The A strip wraps to two lines at 820 (`{'x':40,'y':316,'w':740,'h':44.3}` vs `h 24` at 1440/1100). One wrap of a flex row is not an orphaned cell, but it is the only geometry in A that changes shape across the bands.
5. B2's rail is legal at all three bands, and at 1100 both cards measure `h 310.5` exactly — equal heights, one row, `s248-D1` met with no seam.
6. The rail ORDER only matters at 1440: at 1100 B and B2 render identically apart from which card is left (`x 40`) and which is right (`x 552`).
7. `Template-dashboard-bento` is the cheapest member page in the `dataviz` group at `13,048` code-only bytes; a 1,753-byte anchor behaviour leaves `20,015` bytes of headroom under `PAGE_BYTES 34,816`.
8. The page cap cannot be crossed from this page by a partial that itself passes `MAX_BYTES` (needs ≥21,769 code-only; file cap is 16,384) — the mutation shows both caps failing together at 21,777.
9. The gate's page budget is summed WITHIN a `component-type` group (`check_group`, `consumes`-aware). `Template-dashboard-bento` appears in exactly one group with behaviours (`dataviz`); `Template-dashboard` sits in `segmented`, which declares none. A behaviour registered in a different group and loaded on the same page would not enter this sum — declared here as a property of the gate, not a defect claim.
10. The anchor address is broken in the built options: chip → `#p2` (a hidden tabpanel), card in `#p1` with no id (`target_found` true but `target_panel "p2"` for A; `target_found: false` for B2). Repointed at an id on the card, the behaviour works in one click with `#p1` still visible and reduced-motion honoured.

## RULING-SHAPED QUESTIONS

1. **Which id does the chip address?** — (a) an id on the Needs-attention CARD (`#dp08-na` as prototyped: focus lands on the whole card, one target for both strip items); (b) an id on the card's promoted STATUS ROW (finer target, but `s251-D1` says Undrawn is strip-only so only one of the two rows would ever be a target); (c) keep `#p2` and make the chip a tab switch (which is what it does today, and which contradicts "anchor links to the Needs attention panel"). Cost is identical; the difference is what gets focus. No recommendation.
2. **Which surface owns "Undrawn facilities" when both are present?** — `s251-D1` says strip-only, but B2's promoted head row carries it, which is where the `×2` above-fold repeat comes from. Dropping it from the card head, or dropping the head rows entirely (strip carries the state, the card carries the items), are both defensible readings of the same ruling.
3. **Which count wording is the ONE shared count** — `14 payments · 5 need you` (strip, A) or `5 of 14 need you` (card head, B2)? `s251-D1` requires one; the two built options disagree, and nothing measured here can choose.
4. **Is a seventh above-fold signal carrier acceptable** for A+B2 (22/7 measured) when DP-20's budget is three and the current page already scores 18/5? #249's question 4 is unchanged by this probe and is still Dave's.
