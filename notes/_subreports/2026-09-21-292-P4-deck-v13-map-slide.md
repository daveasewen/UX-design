## WHAT LANDED

Source: `cp` of v12 → `notes/_DEMO-SLIDES-apollo-2026-09-21-v13.html`. One
edit, made by `notes/_lanes/292/P4/build_v13.py` (kept; asserts on every anchor

**Ids were NOT renumbered, and that was measured first, not assumed.** The
chassis IIFE navigates positionally —

    var slides = Array.prototype.slice.call(document.querySelectorAll('.slide'));
    slides[Math.min(i+1, slides.length-1)].scrollIntoView(…)

— with the index taken from `deck.scrollTop / deck.clientHeight`. There is no
hash router, no `#sN` lookup, no id list. Ids are CSS and IIFE handles only
(`#s4 .draw`, `#s12 .close-line`, `getElementById('lw')`, …). So `s11` and
`s12` keep their names and their CSS keeps working, and the new card takes the
id **`s10map`** — deliberately outside the `s<N>` sequence, so nothing that
matches on `s11`/`s12` can ever collide with it.

**The card**, in the deck's own idiom:

    <section class="slide grey" id="s10map">
      <div class="inner">
        <p class="label rv">The design system</p>
        <h2 class="rv d1"><b>Twelve around one.</b></h2>
        <section class="dsm rv d2"> … twelve .tile + .hub … </section>
        <p class="foot rv d3">Ten of the twelve stand … Placeholder: not yet ruled.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="pagenum">10A / 12</div>
    </section>

`grey` because it pairs with 10, which is grey. The eyebrow, `h2`, `.foot`,
`.pagenum`, `.rv d1/d2/d3` reveal stagger and the `.inner` 1120px grid are the
deck's, unmodified — the map inherits the card, the card does not bend to the
map. The headline is the map's own title, as the brief allowed.

**What was carried from lane H's poster, and what was cut.** Carried: the 4×4
frame; the twelve on the perimeter in the same clockwise order 01…12; the hub
on the centre 2×2; the hairline grid made of the grid's *own* 1px gaps on a
rule-coloured ground; the inward tick as a stem with its own margin; the
`soon` state as reduced ink plus a badge; the ten citations, verbatim paths;
the masterbrand lockup with the wordmark re-bound by class; the six ring words
as a rule-separated mono strip. Cut, because a card is not a poster: the
masthead (the card carries its own eyebrow and headline), the two-panel key
and the footer (folded into one `.foot` line), and **each tile's description,
re-cut shorter** — lane H's 85–100-character lines ran to three lines in a
155px row, so they are re-written to ≤ 70 characters and two lines. The titles
and the citations are untouched.

**The tokens are re-declared locally, not imported.** `#s10map .dsm` declares
`--dsm-rule`, `--dsm-surface`, `--dsm-ink`, `--dsm-ink-2/3`, `--dsm-mute`,
`--dsm-band` and `--dsm-brand` from the deck's *own* `--g1/g3/g5/g6/g7/g8`,
which are the same canon values lane H cited. Nothing is pulled from
`knowledge/canon/type.css` (lane H's `<link>` is dropped — the deck is one
self-contained file, and a relative stylesheet would break the moment the deck
is moved). The type is the deck's `--font`, not the poster's `--uf`.

**Every rule is scoped to `#s10map`.** The tile heading is `h3`, not `h2`, so
the deck's `h2{font-size:clamp(34px,5.2vw,64px)}` cannot reach it. The style
block sits immediately after the section, `data-lane="P4"`, the way G3's block
sits after s12. Nothing declares on `.slide`, `.tile`, `.hub` or any bare
selector; there is no new global.

**It cannot overflow the card, by construction.** `#s10map .inner` is a flex
column with `flex:1 1 auto;min-height:0`, and `.dsm` is
`grid-template-rows:repeat(4,minmax(0,1fr))` inside it. The frame therefore
takes whatever height the eyebrow, headline and foot leave and no more — at
1440×900 that is four 154.8px rows, at any other height it is four equal rows
of that height. There is no fixed 268px row and no `transform:scale()`, so the
type stays crisp at every size.

**Print is declared, not silent.** At 1280×720 the card has ~180px less
content height than at 900. Inside `@media print`, `#s10map .tile .d` stands
down and the numeral, the name and the citation carry the tile; the headline
drops to 30px and the padding to 40px. That is a deliberate stand-down, named
here so it is not discovered later.

**Untouched:** every other slide, verbatim — s1…s10, s11, s12, their pagenums
(`01/12` … `12/12`), all five drawing IIFEs, the chassis, the print CSS, the
G3 style block, and the four D2 layout flags, which remain exactly as open as
they were.

## RULING-SHAPED QUESTIONS

1. **The counter.** The deck now has thirteen cards and every existing one
   still reads `NN / 12`. The brief said not to touch another slide, so the new
   card reads **`10A / 12`** — honest about being an insertion, and it disturbs
   nothing. The alternative is a sweep that renumbers all thirteen to `NN / 13`
   and makes 10A into 11. That is a one-line-per-card mechanical change and is
   the obvious next move once the map stops being a placeholder. Which?
2. **The id.** `s10map` keeps out of the `s<N>` sequence today. If the counter
   sweep above happens, does the id follow (`s11`, and everything after shifts)
   or does it stay a name rather than a number? Positional nav means either
   works; the CSS selectors are the only cost.
3. **The tile lines were re-cut.** Lane H's descriptions are longer and more
   careful; mine are shorter so they fit two lines in a deck card. Lane H's
   poster is unchanged and still carries the full text. Should the card's
   shorter lines be back-ported to the poster, or are they two registers on
   purpose?
4. **The card is theme-fixed.** Lane H's poster answers `prefers-color-scheme`
   and `data-theme`; the deck does not — its cards are white/grey/dark by
   class. So the map's dark roles are wired to `#s10map.dark` and are dormant:
   rendered light and dark, the card is **byte-identical** (234,382 B both).
   Is a dark map card wanted at all, or is the deck's own light/dark rhythm the
   answer?
5. **Placement against 10.** 10 is "Three things she is made of" (Parts,
   Knowledge, Proficiency); 10A is twelve parts of a design system. They are
   two different decompositions of the same subject, back to back, both on
   grey. Does the map want a bridging line, or does the sequence read?
6. **The masterbrand lockup is on the card.** Lane H put it in the hub of a
   standalone poster. This deck names no addressee anywhere and carries no
   brand mark on any other card. Does the lockup stay, or does the hub take the
   wordmark only?

Nothing above is settled; Dave has seen the map, not the card.
