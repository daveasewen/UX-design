## What was done, in plain prose

**Fifty-two rest on a principle; seven rest on convention.** The seven are `logo26-001` (a logo must
appear), `photo26-002` (the gen-AI and montage bans), `nam-001` (never possessive with product
names), `pict-010` (the 60 / 192 / 2px pictogram sizes), `type26-002` (brand-approved fonts, weights
and colours), `type25-020` (the gate-promotion record) and `webf-018` (the 4px base unit). Six of
those seven are genuinely conventions in the ordinary sense — identity, licensing, a chosen modulus,
an asset sourcing rule. The seventh, `type25-020`, is a different animal and is flagged below.

**The grade spread of the fifty-two recommendations is C 19 · L 16 · B 9 · A 8.** The audit's opening
proposal was the 59 blocking rules against the six A-grade laws. Held to that honestly, it does not
reach: only eight of the fifty-nine rest on a measured law, and all eight are `pr-fitts` (two target-
size rules) or `pr-graphical-perception` (six chart rules). The rest of the blocking canon is
accessibility obligation and practised guidance, which is what the ruling's "any grade is allowed"
clause exists to permit. This is a finding, not a disappointment: it tells a designer that most of
what they cannot ship without is law or professional agreement rather than measured psychology, and
the grade on each card says which.

**Eight principles carry more than two rules each.** `pr-wcag-1-4-3` (text contrast) carries 8;
`pr-graphical-perception` 6; `pr-tog-readability` 6; `pr-wcag-perceivable` 5; `pr-nng-consistency` 4;
and `pr-norman-signifier`, `pr-von-restorff` and `pr-wcag-1-4-11` carry 3 each. Those eight account
for 38 of the 52. The concentration is real and is the most useful thing on the page: three separate
guideline files ban red typography, and all three turn out to rest on the same principle — red only
works as a call-to-action cue while it is rare.

**The three links I am least sure of**, in order:

1. `pict-011` (never squash, stretch, skew or distort) → `pr-gestalt-similarity`. The principle is
   about set membership by shape, and a distorted pictogram does stop reading as a member of its set
   — but the real reason is probably closer to "the mark is an asset with a fixed form", which is
   convention. The null is offered on the card and I would not argue with it.
2. `type26-020` (left-aligned primary, never justified) → `pr-gestalt-continuity`. The rivers
   argument is a continuity argument, but the principle was written about elements on a line, not
   about the return sweep of the eye at a line break. It is a stretch of scope, and the alternative
   offered (`pr-tog-readability`) may be the safer home.
3. `webf-027` (all spacing in multiples of 4, mapped to the grid) → `pr-gestalt-proximity`. Proximity
   is why spacing means anything at all, so the link is honest — but the rule is mostly a systems
   rule, and its sibling `webf-018` is declared convention on the same page. A panel that reads them
   as one rule would put both in the null.

A fourth, named for completeness: `icon-006` (min 16px, max 48px, 2px increments) → `pr-tog-readability`.
Only the 16px floor has a perceptual reason; the ceiling and the increment are construction, and the
card's why says so.

## Ruling-shaped

1. **The verb `restsOn` needs, proposed here, not added.** The twelve-verb reading map already has
   the slot: `rests-on` in `knowledge/_kg_verbs.json` reads `obeys` (the ux branch) and `tensionWith`
   at force `should`, and its `$definition` ends "the future `restsOn` the audit named is NOT a type
   today and is not listed." The landing lane should move it out of that sentence and into
   `reads: ["obeys", "tensionWith", "restsOn"]`, keep the verb's force at `should` — a rule rests on
   a principle, it is not obliged by it, and the obligation is already carried by the rule's own
   destiny — extend `direction` with "rule s rests on principle t (`restsOn`, carrying the `$why`
   sentence and the grade at the time of writing)", and add a `count.restsOn`. Nothing else in the
   map moves. **Not done here: the Gates forbid it and the brief says propose it.**
2. **`type25-020` is not a design rule and probably should not be BLOCKING.** It records that three
   cost-0 checks were promoted straight to blocking and enacted as snippet-gate check 6. What it
   rests on is the three rules it enforces (`type25-003`, `type25-007`, `type25-008`), each of which
   carries its own proposal on this page, plus the ruling that promoted them. It is filed as a
   declared null with that explanation, but the honest reading is that the rule index is carrying a
   process record in the rule family, and a `restsOn → ref:null` is the wrong shape for it — a
   rule → rule line would be right, and no such type exists either.
3. **Does a `restsOn` line carry the grade, or read it live?** The ruling says the edge carries "the
   principle's grade", and `proposals.json` stores it. But grade is a field on the `ux:` node
   (`s237-D1`) and can be re-graded; a carried grade would then be stale. The alternative is that the
   edge carries only the `$why` and the reader joins the grade at read time. I have written the
   proposals with the grade stored **and** the uxId stored, so either reading is available to the
   landing lane, but it is Dave's to settle.
