# 291 · L2 — line-workers v2: the figures rebuilt as humans

Lane L2, Apollo session #291, 2026-09-20.

## VERDICT

The two figures are rebuilt from the construction method up — egg head with a
jaw, tapered ribcage and pelvis, ball joints, tapered limbs, contrapposto with
the feet placed and the legs solved to them — and dressed as coveralls off the
flat garment drawing. At 600×420 both now read as people in workwear rather
than as manipulators with heads: `line-workers.html` is v2 and v1 is preserved
whole. Four render-and-look passes, all four of them visual, no probe-only
changes. The standoff constant is **WK_D = 104**.

Provenance. Lane L built v1; Dave looked at the render and ruled, verbatim:

> in overalls, one is a drawing of overalls the other two are human bodies

## WHAT LANDED

**Why v1 read as a robot, part by part.** A cylinder head on a cylinder neck on
a box torso — the three shapes a toy robot is made of. Limbs that were the same
8-gon tube at both ends, so nothing narrowed from shoulder to wrist. An
attention stance: both feet flat and level, both legs vertical, hips level,
shoulders level, spine exactly vertical, head dead ahead, every limb parallel
to its opposite number. And a bib with crossed back straps, which at this size
drew an X on the chest — a crossed-out box.

**The body.** 408 units tall on a 56-unit head, 7.29 heads. From the crown
down, in heads: chin 1.00, shoulder plane 1.29, waist 2.86, hip joint 3.54,
knee 5.30, sole 7.29. Shoulder balls span 112 — exactly 2 heads. The head is a
five-ring lathe whose rings step forward and narrow below the cheek, so its
bottom cap is a 24×20 chin plate against a 20-diameter neck and that cap's rim
inks as the jaw line. Ribcage and pelvis are tapered blocks (half-width 40 at
the shoulders, 28 at the waist, back out to 33 at the hips). Shoulder, elbow,
hip and knee are 8-gon ball lathes; thigh 22→16, shin 16→12.5, upper arm
15→12.5, bare forearm 10→8.5. Boots are a sole block running forward past an
ankle-height upper, and the step between them is the heel.

**One new builder does all of it.** `latheP` stacks rings of an elliptical
section up a frame's local z. A quad between two such rings is planar, so
Newell and the half-space ray clip stay exact, and the cylinder, the frustum,
the joint ball and the egg head are all one function — every one convex, every
one landing in the existing pipeline exactly as the prisms did. `taperP` is the
same idea for a block. Joint balls use FOUR rings, not three: at three the
bands meet at 53° and the ball inks its own equator; at four they meet at 38°
and it does not.

**Buried caps are not inked.** `bury()` marks a face that lives inside another
solid — a thigh top inside a pelvis, a neck inside a head, a shin top inside a
knee ball. The face still bounds the solid for the ray clip and still fills
white; its edges simply never ink. This is new, and it is what makes a body
assembled from overlapping solids possible at all: without it every joint drew
a hoop across the part it entered.

**Posture.** Both feet are PLACED on the floor and both legs are then SOLVED to
them, so a boot never floats and a knee bend is a consequence of where the foot
is. Weight on one leg (22° of knee), the other set back with the heel up (33°)
on a toe-down boot frame. The pelvis is shifted laterally over the weight foot,
the hips tilt 5°, and the shoulder girdle rolls 9° the other way — the two axes
counter-tilt. The spine leans into the work and twists toward it; the head yaws
and pitches to look at the hands. A and B take opposite weight legs and
different arm jobs, so the pair is not one drawing and its mirror, and no two
limbs in the scene are parallel.

**Four rotations per limb, not three.** `solve3` takes a target in the limb's
own base frame and returns abduction, pitch and bend — abduction picks the
plane the two links swing in, so an elbow can go out to the side and a knee can
track over a foot that is not under the hip. Elbows take the forward root,
knees the backward one. The fourth rotation, `tw`, twists the upper link about
its own axis and aims the elbow hinge; without it the hand can only lie in the
plane the abduction chose, and the first attempt at a hand on the hip came out
as a plank across the chest. Solved reaches leave `tw` at 0; only the two
written-down poses use it.

**The garment is a COVERALL**, off the flat drawing: collar notch, centre
placket with three buttons, two chest pockets, waistband turned onto both side
faces, slanted hip pockets — and, because one figure is always seen from behind
at this camera, a back yoke, a back centre seam and two back pockets. Nothing
crosses anything. The rolled cuff is not a drawn line: it is a short wide
sleeve frustum with a visibly narrower bare forearm below it, so it reads in
silhouette.

**Standoff.** WK_D = 104 from the belt centre line, in from v1's 118 and a long
way in from the robots' 250. The torso front sits 82 out, 20 clear of the bed
edge at 62; the boot toe reaches 74, 12 clear. Shoulder to hand is 171 and the
reach onto the red part is about 150, so the elbow stays honestly bent. The
along-belt stations are still the robots' own, ARM_SA = −200 and ARM_SB = 66.

**The parts moved.** The red one still takes its station from B's solved hand
(69.3 at rest). The two plain ones straddle it at s = 0 and 200 rather than 20
and 250, which was the only arrangement that left clear white either side of
the red box — the belt runs away from the camera and compresses downstream, so
boxes at 110 or 150 crowded it in projection. Both are still past the s > −7
the painter's order needs, so there is still no re-seat anywhere in the scene.

**Carried over untouched:** projection, camera, orbit spring, conveyor and its
creeping dashes, base plate, fit sweep, the hidden-line pipeline and its
three-class ray classifier, painter's order by plain centroid depth, the ink
rules, the one red accent, the idle, the probe surface. `linePose()` now also
reports hands and feet in each figure's own floor frame, which is the frame the
poses are written in.

## RULING-SHAPED QUESTIONS

1. Does the pair read as two people in workwear at a glance, at 600×420, with
   no zoom?
2. The garment is a COVERALL — collar, placket, chest pockets, waistband, hip
   pockets, rolled cuffs — rather than bib overalls with straps. Is that the
   garment you meant by "overalls", or do you want the bib back without the X?
3. Worker A has his hand on his hip and the other on the belt rail; worker B
   reaches to the red part with his near hand. Is that the division of labour
   you want, or should both be working the belt?
4. The red workpiece is the one accent and it now sits between the two plain
   boxes rather than at the end of a run. Does it still read as the thing being
   handled?
5. At this camera A is seen from behind — yoke, back seam, back pockets — and
   only B shows a chest. Is one front and one back the right split, or should
   the composition be turned so both chests show?
6. The construction axes (standing line, shoulder transverse, one elbow) are
   still three per figure. On a human body rather than a machine, is that the
   right amount of construction ink, or is it now noise?
7. Should this pair and the robot pair be shown at the same camera and the same
   scale on the slide, as they are now, or does the human version want a closer
   crop?
