# #304 W3b — why quality is flat between v1.0.13 and the v1.0.14 candidate, and what would move it

provenance: 304 · Sun 2026-09-27 (overnight) · seat W3b (Fable 5.1, read-only judgment) · nothing committed, no git calls, no Project memory · writes only this file and `notes/_lanes/304/W3b/`
evidence: the six full-length 1440 overviews and the 390 montage looked at by eye (staged into the container); `review-data.json`; the harness `ext.json` per run; two seat probes written for this report (`notes/_lanes/304/W3b/probe_dead.py`, `geo-v1013-r2.json`)

## The answer in four lines

1. The measurement is mostly blind, and there is also mostly no difference. By eye the six pages are the same page with the furniture moved: every one carries the same chart-engine defects, and those defects are what Dave's eye lands on first. The candidate changed where the parts come from; it did not change the parts, and the parts are where the sloppiness lives.
2. The ceiling is set by canon and the chart engine, not by the skill or the prompt. Ranked by what it costs a page: charts that fill their tile and leave a 350px hole; axis labels cut and colliding; letters and dot markers stamped on every point of every series; raw floats in the donut centre; the KPI label crop; the 40px Common gutter; the 640px shell. A cold agent with vision off inherits all of these and reports "0 issues at 1440" because its own probes measure gutters, not ink.
3. Five changes would visibly move Tuesday's pages, four of them without a ruling: fix the ring-in-a-tall-tile and the axis gutter in the engine; take the letters and markers off dense series; format the donut total; key Common's gutter. The one that needs his word is whether a chart tile hugs its content or fills the row.
4. The rubric should stop scoring what the prompt already forces (rich · full · persistent · interactive all saturate at 10/12) and start scoring what he sees: dead ink, cut ink, collisions, size drift, ground and gutter, per view not per entry page. Until it does, "beat 10.0" is not a cut condition, it is a coin toss.

## 1. Blind, or no difference? Both, and it matters which is which

**What the numbers say.** Like-for-like 10.0 v 10.0. Geometry 1/0 on all six. Own-size 15–21 S1 findings on all six, every one the chart legend's key button at 16.7px against a 20px reference (`ext.json` own_size, all runs). Trace 0.38 → 0.18. Parts 6.3 → 14.7.

**What the pages say, by eye.** I looked at all six overviews full-length at 1440 in light, the 390 montage, and two sub-page pairs.

- The three v1.0.13 pages are one skeleton: KPI row, exposure bar, two side cards, a line and a donut. v1013-r3 (top nav, no shell) is the best-looking page of the six: its rows are level, its side cards are tidy, its donut is small enough to sit in a 432px column. v1013-r2 is a single stacked column with native `<select>` filters and a donut tile 1,221px tall holding a 200px ring (probe: 351px of nothing between ring and legend).
- The three candidates differ from each other, and two are organised under his three questions with section headings on the grey ground. That is the composition the plan wanted, and it is real. But cand-r2 is 4,731px long because two ring charts each take a 1,221px full-width tile (343px and 347px holes); cand-r1's filter toolbar runs past the content's right edge (the Export button is cut at the page edge) and its decision cards are a card inside a tile with raw bullets; cand-r3 has "0 of 0 records" in its filter line and a legend that reads A USD · B EUR · C GBP · A CNY · B Other (keys wrap at five and repeat).
- Shared by all six, and the first thing a designer sees: the y-axis tick labels are cut at the left on five pages ("00 £m", "50 £m"); 30 daily x labels collide on four; every line or area series carries a marker on every one of 30 points, and the stacked areas carry a letter on every point too, so cand-r3's liquidity chart reads "A A A A A A A" across its whole width; the donut centre prints 791.9000000000001 and 2990.8999999999996.

**Verdict.** The candidate is different in the way the plan intended (it composes; two of three answer the three questions as sections) and not better in the way Dave means (alignment, spacing, dimensions). Both readings are true at once. The harness is blind to the second because its four axes saturate on this prompt (see §4), and its geometry gate is blind to the largest single defect on the page (below). So: the like-for-like tie is honest, and it is also uninformative.

**A blind spot with a receipt.** `_validate_geometry.py` on v1013-r2 at 1440 reports G6 = 0 (`notes/_lanes/304/W3b/geo-v1013-r2.json`). My probe on the same page finds a leaf tile `div.c-bento__tile.stat-card` of 1,221px with a 351px empty band between the ring's last mark and the legend's first row (`probe_dead.py`, output in this report's lane folder). Same on cand-r2: two tiles, 343px and 347px. The gate's own docstring says a 48px band inside a leaf tile is G6. Either the tile is not being classed as a leaf, or something inside the figure is being read as ink; I did not find which. 4b should reproduce with this page before the gate is trusted on dead space, because dead space is the one clause that names Dave's #288 observation ("KPI tiles carry dead space").

## 2. The causes of the ceiling, ranked by what each costs a page

Cost is my judgment of how much of the page a designer would mark up, in order.

1. **A ring chart fills its tile.** ds-030 makes the ring a fixed diameter from `data-h` while `.dv-fit-on .dv-svg{width:100%;height:auto}` scales the svg box to the tile's width: a 1,014px-wide tile gets an 879px-tall svg holding a 200px ring. Any donut or pie in a wide or full-width tile produces a hole; in a row it also stretches its neighbours (v1013-r3's bar chart gains a 100px empty band at its top to level with the donut card). Hits 4 of 6 pages at 1440 and every sub-page that puts a ring in a wide tile (cand-r2 payments: two of them, 3,822px page). Engine, `dv-render-donut.js` + the fit CSS. No ruling needed to make the box hug the ring; whether a chart tile may be shorter than its row-mates is his (§3, change 5).
2. **The axis gutter is opt-in, so cold pages cut their labels.** ds-012(b) computes the left gutter from the widest label, but only when the svg carries `data-pl-fit`. The candidates have 12 svgs at `data-pl="46"` and only 6 with `data-pl-fit`; v1013-r3 has none. Result: "00 £m" on five of six overviews. Engine default. No ruling needed: the snippet's own svg carries `data-pl-fit="text.dv-label"` (Chart-bar.reference.html:486) — make the engine assume it when absent.
3. **Dense series are over-marked.** Line, area and stacked-area draw a marker on every point and a series letter on every point; at 30 points that is noise a designer strips first. The x-axis prints all 30 dates. Engine, `dv-render-line.js` / `dv-render-stacked-area.js` / the axis furniture. A rule of thumb needs no ruling (markers off above ~12 points, one letter per series at the line end, ticks thinned to every 5th day); Dave may want to look at the result, but it is not a ruling-shaped choice.
4. **Raw floats in the centre and the legend.** `String(total)` at `dv-render-donut.js`:135 and in dv-legend. Engine, one line each, no ruling. Small cost per page, but it is the defect a CEO sees first.
5. **Inherited Common canon: 40px gutter, KPI label crop, contrast, 640px shell.** R4s has these right and they are already on the fix list. The 40px gutter is the one that changes the whole page's feel (cand-r3 on 40 v v1013-r1 on 24 is the visible difference between heavy and comfortable). `gen_bento_role_vars.py` keying `common` needs no ruling; s219-D1 already says 24. The shell needs a full-viewport form, and the candidate's rule 3a ("never resize a part") is what kept it at 640 — the skill and the component have to move together.
6. **The skill's silence on charts as parts.** The skill says copy the snippet whole and never resize it; the chart snippets' figure is then handed to the tile and the row, and nothing tells the agent that a ring wants a narrow column or that a full-width tile wants a wide chart type. cand-r2 put two rings in full-width tiles because nothing forbade it. This is a when-rule (chart type × tile width), which is the graph's job and R4a's drafts do not cover it. Ruling-shaped, small.
7. **The model cannot see, and its own probes measure the wrong thing.** Every RUN-REPORT says "geometry: 0 issues at 1440" and "visual review unavailable". The agents measured gutters, edge sharing and overflow, which pass; they did not measure ink bands, label clipping inside svgs or marker density, which fail. The prompt's "the usual errors are spacing, double-check spacing" sent them to the wrong ruler. Prompt and skill, not a ruling: hand the agent `_validate_geometry.py` and `_validate_own_size.py` by name in step 8 and require the JSON in the run report. But note the gate's own blindness at item 1 in §1 before relying on it.
8. **The prompt.** It asks for wide desktop, ten views, every chart type, full interactivity and persistence, in one cold pass. Rich/full/persistent/interactive saturate because the prompt names them; taste is not named because he asked that it not be. That is a fair test and it should stay. What it does mean is that the rubric's four axes are measuring prompt compliance, not quality.
9. **390.** Nothing was built for it and the prompt does not ask for it. All six score 0 there and the KPI row stays four-across at 390 on every page. It is a true finding and a zero-information one for this comparison. Carry it, do not score it.
10. **The receipt address bug and dv-004.** Gate noise; they cost the candidate 2 harness points and cost the page nothing.

Not on the list because it is not a cause: composing from the graph. The candidates' section structure is the one genuine improvement, and it is invisible to every instrument.

## 3. Three to five changes most likely to move Tuesday's pages, with tests

Each names who can do it. "Claude" means no ruling is needed because a ruled rule already says so or it is a defect repair; "Dave" means it changes a design choice.

1. **Rings hug their ring (Claude, engine).** Give the donut and pie svg a box derived from `data-h`, not from the tile width: `height` pinned to the ring's diameter plus its leader ring, the legend beside it in a wide tile and below it in a narrow one. Test: re-render v1013-r2 and cand-r2 overviews; the tallest ring tile is under 520px at 1440; `probe_dead.py` finds no band over 48px in any ring tile; and the geometry gate, once 4b has found why it missed these, agrees.
2. **The axis gutter fits by default (Claude, engine).** Treat `data-pl-fit="text.dv-label"` as the default for every value axis when the attribute is absent; keep `data-pl` as the floor. Test: on all six overviews, the leftmost y label's ink starts inside the svg (G8 side-cut count on `text.dv-label` goes from 5 pages to 0); the plot never narrows below ds-012's 42% ceiling.
3. **Dense series are quiet (Claude, engine, Dave looks).** Markers off above 12 points; one series letter, at the line's end; x ticks thinned to a legible count from the label width. Test: render cand-r3's liquidity area and v1013-r1's line at 1440; no two x labels overlap (G7 on `text.dv-label` reads 0); marker count per series is 0 above 12 points; put the before/after pair on Tuesday's review page for his eye, because this is taste even though it is not a ruling.
4. **The four canon fixes R4s named, landed together (Claude).** Format the donut total (`ctx.fmt(total)`), key `common` in `gen_bento_role_vars.py` (24/4), fix the Kpi-tile crop (drop `overflow:hidden` or the 14px line box), and the KPI/nav contrast alphas. Test: cand-r3 re-rendered on the new canon shows 24px wall gutters, no "0000001", no G8 on `.kpi-lbl`, 4.5:1 on the label.
5. **Chart tile shape as a when-rule, and the shell's height (Dave, one line each).** (a) Does a chart tile hug its content or fill its row? Today the rails level every row, so the shortest chart stretches to the tallest; his #288 observation was dead space in tiles, which is the same question. (b) Does a ring chart get a narrow column by rule (a `when` of shape × tile width, the graph's job) or is that the agent's call? Test: the R4a drafts gain one row for chart-donut / chart-pie with a `layout.span` claim; one cold run on the new canon and the amended skill; the overview has no ring in a tile wider than half the wall.

Then one confirming cold run, on the repaired canon, with the run report carrying both gates' JSON. If it lands with no ring holes, no cut labels and no over-marked series, cut v1.0.14. If it does not, the fault is somewhere I have not named, and that is worth knowing before the cut too.

What I would not do by Tuesday: rewrite the skill again. R4a's rewrite did its job (trace 0.38 → 0.18, sections under the three questions). The skill is not where the ceiling is.

## 4. Should the rubric change? Yes, in three ways

**Drop the saturated axes from the headline.** Rich, full, persistent and interactive read 2–3 on every run because the prompt orders them. Keep them as a floor (must be ≥ 2 each) and stop summing them; a /12 that every run passes cannot rank runs.

**Score the sloppiness class, per view, from ink.** Five counts, at 1440, across all ten destinations, each normalised per tile so a long page is not punished for being long: dead ink (largest empty band per leaf tile, over 48px), cut ink (glyphs cut at any edge, inside svg included), collisions (ink on ink between text elements), size drift (own-size S1/S3 on any `cn-` part, including the legend key), and marker density (marks per point above a threshold). Report each as a count and a worst-case pixel figure. This is what he named at #288 and what he sees first on every one of these pages. R4b's gate has clauses for four of the five; the fifth is a chart-engine fact the gate can read off the DOM in one query. But fix its G6 blind spot first (§1), or the dead-ink count will read 0 on the worst page.

**Add two things the eye sees and no clause names, as declared judgment rows.** Section structure (does the overview answer the three questions as three visible groups: yes/no, read from headings under the wall) and chart-to-tile fit (is any ring chart in a tile wider than half the wall). Both are one line of DOM each and both separate the candidates from v1.0.13 today.

**And keep the render pair.** Nothing above replaces his eye. The review page should put the same view from the best v1.0.13 and the best candidate side by side at 1440 with the five counts under each, and let him say which is closer to Apollo.

## Disagreements with the reports, stated plainly

- R4s's recommendation (four fixes then "beat 10.0") is right about the four fixes and wrong about the test. The /12 cannot move above 10 on this prompt for reasons that have nothing to do with the pages; a confirming run should be judged on the sloppiness counts and his eye, not on the harness total.
- R4b's baseline says the geometry gate "names four of the conductor's five observations" on #288. On these six pages it does not name the largest one. The gate is good; it is not yet trusted on dead space, and the selftest should gain v1013-r2's donut tile as a real-page leg.
- R4c's harness can see interactivity and persistence, which is real work well done. It should stop presenting the /12 as the quality score; its own card already says taste, alignment and composition are "left to judgment", and that is exactly the part Dave is asking about.
- The plan's Run 4 framed the candidate as "the skill rewritten to compose rather than trace" and expected that to show in the score. It showed in the trace index and the section structure, which is what a skill can change. The quality Dave means is a property of the parts, and the parts are v1.0.13's in both arms.
