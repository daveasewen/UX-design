## WHAT LANDED

**1 · "we don't need the flat facet of the top of the head."** v2's egg was a
five-ring lathe whose top ring was a 24 x 22 disc, so `latheP` capped it with a
polygon and that cap's rim inked as a flat facet across the crown. `latheP`
now understands a POLE: a ring with `ax = ay = 0` is a single vertex and the
band into it is a triangle fan, so that end of the lathe has no cap and no rim
to ink. `HEAD_RINGS` is SEVEN entries — chin plate, jaw, cheek, brow, upper
brow, crown ring, pole — and the crown is still **56** above the chin, so no
proportion moved, the chin cap still inks as the jaw, the profile is convex at
every step on +x, -x and y, and the tightest band-to-band dihedral is **0.86**
against `HARD = 0.65`, so the skull does not ink its own facets either.

**2 · "reposition this body so its at the side of the conveyer."** v2 put A at
ARM_SA = -200 on a 280 half-length bed — 80 from the bed's end, 106 from the
end roller — at a standoff of 104, and at this camera the bed's end face and
the roller both crossed his body. Two constants move, both his alone.
**WK_DA = 126** (B keeps WK_D = 104, because B reaches ACROSS the belt and a
longer standoff would straighten his elbow): A's torso front is then 104 out
against a bed edge at 62, 42 clear, and his boot toe reaches 92, 30 clear.
**ARM_SA = -136** walks him 64 downstream into the middle of the bed's upstream
half. The new `lineClear()` probe tests every vertex of every one of his
solids, hands excluded, against the bed slab, the four legs and the two roller
cylinders: **minimum 14.9**, at his pelvis against the bed. B's figure is
10.5 clear by the same test. The near hand now RESTS ON the belt —
**RAIL_X = 70, RAIL_Z = 194** in his own floor frame, which puts the mitt
centre 6 past the near bed edge and its underside at 183, the belt surface —
and the mitt itself came down to **18 x 12 x 22** from v2's 20 x 14 x 26.

**3 · "this arm is coming out of its body."** Two things were wrong and both
are fixed. The pivot: v2 seated it at (y 42, z 78), TEN units below the
ribcage top on a block 38.6 half-wide there, and hung a 15-radius tube off it,
so half the tube lived inside the chest. It is now **SH_LZ = 88, SH_Y = 46** —
z 88 IS the ribcage's top face, and it is also where the proportion sheet puts
the shoulder plane, 1.29 heads from the crown = 336 = PEL_Z0 + TOR_LZ + 88 —
with **UP_R0 = 13**, so the arm's inboard surface sits at y 33 against a 39.7
half-width, tucked under the corner. The redundancy: a two-link arm reaching a
point has one degree of freedom left over, the elbow swinging on a cone about
the shoulder-to-hand axis, and `solve3` never chose it — it always put the arm
in the plane through the base frame's own z, which for B reaching across the
belt drops the elbow straight down the front of the chest. A sweep found that
NO target anywhere on the belt fixes this: the best of them still leaves the
axis 2.7 inside. So `solve3` gained a **swivel**: `swivelF` rotates the base
frame about the target direction before the solve, which leaves the hand on
exactly the same point (a rotation fixes its own axis) and walks the elbow
round the cone. **B's reach takes sw = -110**, elbow 54 forward and 56 out on
his own side; **A's belt hand takes sw = 20**. Measured with `lineClear()`,
all four upper-arm AXES are outside the ribcage block in both rest and orbit:
**A armL 6.0, A armR 6.1, B armL 6.0, B armR 4.9**. In v2 B's reaching arm was
11.3 INSIDE. A's hand on the hip had to be re-solved with it — v2 wrote that
pose down as three angles because solve3 alone could not reach a waist, and
those angles were tuned to the old pivot, so after the move the hand sat 11
high and 5 wide of the hip. It is a solved reach now, target **(10, -28, 216)**
at sw 0, which is already the akimbo: elbow 33 back, 46 out.

**4 · "the ankles could be columns rather than cubes."** v2's boot was a sole
slab plus an ankle-height BOX, and that box is the cube in the crop. The box is
gone. In its place a short frustum built in the SHIN'S OWN frame, so it
continues the shin's axis rather than sitting across it: **ANK_R0 12.6 to
ANK_R1 12.0**, starting **ANK_UP = 16** above the ankle — inside the shin,
which is 13.2 there — and running **ANK_DN = 22** below it into a sole block
made taller, **SOL_H = 7**, to receive it. Both caps buried, so the shin and
the column read as one leg into the boot, and the step where the sole runs
forward past the column is still the heel.

**5 · "lets remove the ball joints."** `ballP` is deleted; there is no sphere
at any shoulder, elbow, hip or knee. Those spheres existed to hide the seam
between two limb segments, and the seams are closed by OVERLAP instead, which
is the same trick the buried cap already made possible. Shoulder: the upper arm
starts **UP_LIFT = 10** ABOVE its own pivot, so it laps the ribcage's top
corner and makes the deltoid itself. Elbow: the sleeve cuff starts
**CUF_OV = 12** above the elbow and at r 14 is wider than the 11.5 arm end it
swallows. Knee: the shin starts **KNEE_OV = 14** above the knee and at
**SHIN_R0 = 17** is wider than the 16 thigh end. Hip: the ball was entirely
inside the pelvis, so deleting it changed no ink at all. No gap opens anywhere
in rest or orbit. The rolled cuff's lower rim still inks, so the sleeve still
reads.

**Carried over untouched:** projection, camera, orbit spring, conveyor and its
creeping dashes, base plate, fit sweep, the hidden-line pipeline and its
three-class ray classifier, painter's order by plain centroid depth with no
re-seat, the ink rules, ONE red workpiece with two plain ones, the coverall
lines, the idle, the three construction axes per figure. The red part's station
still reads off B's solved hand and lands at 74 (v2: 69.5), with the plain
parts still at 0 and 200 and still past the s > -7 the painter needs. Caption
is now `Fig. 291-L3`.

## RULING-SHAPED QUESTIONS

1. The head now closes on a pole. Is a fully smooth crown what you wanted, or
   should the skull keep SOME facet up there, just not a flat cap?
2. A stands at a standoff of 126 and B at 104 — the first time the two figures
   have not been the same distance from the belt. B is closer because he
   reaches across it. Is an asymmetric pair right, or should both stand at 126
   and B's reach be shortened instead?
3. A is at ARM_SA = -136 now. The conveyor's own upstream end ROLLER still
   projects against his far hip at this camera — it is scene furniture, not a
   ball joint, and the same drum is drawn at the other end. Is that adjacency
   acceptable, or should he walk further downstream, at the cost of crowding
   him toward B?
4. A's hand rests ON the belt top rather than on a rail or edge. Is a flat hand
   on the belt the gesture you meant by "at the side of the conveyer", or
   should he be holding a part?
5. The swivel is a new degree of freedom in the poses. Should the two
   written-down poses that remain — A's free-foot stance and B's hanging arm —
   be re-solved with it too, so the whole figure is solved and nothing is a
   magic number?
6. B's reaching elbow now points out and away from the camera, so the upper arm
   is foreshortened at the rest camera and reads as a narrow form. Does the
   reach still read at 600x420 without zoom, or should the whole figure be
   turned a few degrees?
7. The three construction axes per figure are untouched, and at A's shoulder
   they are now the busiest ink in that corner of the drawing. Is that still
   the right amount of construction ink?
