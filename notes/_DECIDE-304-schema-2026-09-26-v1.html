The answer first

Widen the schema for one thing only, the part states. Bring the other nine errors back to the schema. Then settle four shape questions, so a catalogue can describe a whole screen and not just its parts.

The meta schema is the rulebook every part's notes must pass, and it is the thing an Apollo-MCP catalogue would publish. 133 of 137 parts pass it today. The four that fail are the dashboard parts you nominated in September: the data grid, the filter bar, the KPI tile and the legend. Their notes grew richer than the rulebook.

13errors against the schema, all on those four parts
4of the 13 would be published in a catalogue: all are the part states
45parts with no field for their own words, the button among them
27parts that give a setting and a slot the same name

The schema is yours: the v1.0.6 brief says schema changes are yours, ratified at your eye before anything is filled in. Nothing on this page has been changed. Estimate for the whole page once you have ruled: 2 to 4 hours of lane work, plus your look at the result.

Question 1 · the part states

  1. Should the schema accept a part's states, such as ready, loading, empty, error and stale?

    Recommend: widen the schema, with one shape for states. Because a live screen has to draw loading, empty and error as well as ready, and a catalogue can only carry what the schema allows.

    Today the schema allows only two words here: "simple" or "full". The four new parts each describe their states properly, but each in its own way. These four errors are the only ones a catalogue would publish.

    Data grid · today 
    "stateModel": {
  "$scope": "the column header …",
  "states": ["rest", "hover", "focus-visible",
    "sorted", "filtered-active", "menu-open",
    "resizing", "disabled", "truncated"],
  "sorted": "aria-sort … a 2px underline …",
  …
}
    Filter bar · today 
    "stateModel": {
  "attribute": "data-ftb-state",
  "states": [
    {"name": "no-filters", "shows": "the total count …"},
    {"name": "filtered",   "shows": "N of M, the chips …"},
    … loading, empty, error
  ]
}
    KPI tile · today 
    "stateModel": {
  "states": ["ready", "loading", "empty",
             "error", "stale"],
  "loading": "aria-busy=true, four skeleton blocks …",
  "empty": "label + em-dash value + why …",
  …
}
    Legend · today 
    "stateModel": {
  "rest":    "every series shown; Reset disabled",
  "check":   "one or more swatches unchecked …",
  "isolate": "a label is pressed …"
}
    Recommended · widen, one shape

    A list of state names, and one sentence per state

    "stateModel": "simple" | "full"
  or
"stateModel": {
  "states": ["ready", "loading", …],
  "loading": "what the part shows",
  "attribute": "optional",
  "$…": notes
}

    The data grid and the KPI tile already fit. The legend gains its list of names. The filter bar's name-and-sentence pairs become a sentence under each name. Nothing is lost.

    Or · bring the four back

    Back to "simple" or "full"

    The four parts go back to one word each, and the states live only in the drawn snippet and its comments.

    The catalogue could then never tell a host that a part has a loading or an error state, and a live screen would have to guess.

Question 2 · the other nine errors

  1. For the other nine errors, do the parts come back to the schema?

    Recommend: yes, bring all nine back, as listed below. Because a catalogue never reads these fields, and the schema already has a legal place for each of them.

    PartWhat the schema refusesErrorsIn a catalogue?Recommend
    Data gridIts six companion parts (checkbox, filter bar, pagination, search, tags, skeleton) are written as plain names.6NoBring back. Each becomes a named entry, with its note kept beside it, in the form you ruled on 7 September.
    Data gridA list of the parts it is built from, placed inside its relationships.1NoBring back. Move the list to the field that already exists for a part's inner parts.
    LegendIts source is written "hand-authored", which is not one of the allowed sources.1NoBring back. Its source is "code", since it points at the canon legend script. The data grid made the same move in September.
    LegendIts one companion carries a "when" sentence the schema has no field for.1NoBring back. Keep the sentence as a note and mark the companion as recommended.

    With question 1 widened and these nine brought back, all 137 parts pass the schema and the build's integrity check turns green on this point. Estimate: under an hour of lane work, one verifier.

Question 3 · where a part's words come from

  1. Where does a part's own text come from: a field on the part, or a separate text part placed inside it?

    Recommend: a field on the part, such as the button's label and the header's title. Because the words are checked with the part: the label's length, its wrap, the 44px target and the name a screen reader hears.

    45 parts describe how they look but not what they say. The button, the header, the status dot, the list and the summary have no field for their words. In the snippet the words are simply typed into the markup.

    Recommended · a field on the part

    The button carries its label

    { "id": "pay", "component": "button",
  "variant": "primary",
  "label": "Confirm payment" }

    Apollo's gate sees the words as the button's own and can check them with it. Each of the 45 parts gains one or two plainly named fields: label, title, items, rows, trail.

    Or · a text part inside

    The button holds a separate text part

    { "id": "pay", "component": "button",
  "variant": "primary", "child": "pay-text" }
{ "id": "pay-text", "component": "Text",
  "text": "Confirm payment" }

    This is how A2UI's own basic button works, so it costs nothing to write. But the words become a loose child the gate cannot tell apart from any other text.

Question 4 · a part that holds several parts

  1. What holds four parts side by side on a screen: Apollo's own bento wall, or A2UI's rows and columns?

    Recommend: give Apollo's bento wall a slot that takes several tiles. Because the spans, the gutters and the no-orphan rule are ruled on the bento wall, and A2UI's rows and columns know none of them.

    No part in the dashboard set can hold more than one child today. The layout part declares no slot, and the page shell's content slot takes a single child. So a dashboard of four parts cannot yet be written as a tree from the catalogue alone.

    Recommended · Apollo's own container

    The bento wall takes tiles

    { "id": "wall", "component": "bento-wall",
  "tiles": ["cash", "cutoff", "queue", "trend"] }

    The slot accepts parts by what they provide, not by a list of names, as your 9 August ruling on slots requires. Every screen the catalogue describes passes through the bento rules and gates.

    Or · import A2UI's rows and columns

    Borrow Row and Column from A2UI's basic set

    { "id": "row", "component": "Row",
  "children": ["cash", "cutoff", "queue", "trend"] }

    Quickest, and every A2UI host already draws them. But a screen built this way steps round the bento spans and gutters, so our gates would have to re-learn them.

Question 5 · a setting and a slot with the same name

  1. When a part names a setting and a slot the same, such as a chart's "data", which one is it?

    Recommend one rule: data is a setting, bound to a data shape; a slot only ever holds another part. Because chart data modelled as child parts is the open gap the probe named, and the chooser reads data shapes, not children.

    27 parts give both a setting and a slot the same name. A catalogue entry can only have one of them. The probe let the slot win on every part and logged 195 losses. Applied to the 27, the rule sorts them like this:

    Becomes a setting · 16

    • All 14 charts: data, series, slices, bins, sessions, categories, rows, ranges, outliers
    • KPI tile: target
    • Account selector: accounts

    Stays a slot · 7

    • Action bar, banner, confirmation, drawer: actions
    • Cards, popover: content
    • Side rail shell: flyout

    Needs your eye · 4

    • Data grid: columns, filters
    • Lightbox: items
    • Stepper: steps
    • Tab bar: items

    The sort is this seat's reading of each name, not a measurement. The four in the last column could go either way and are put to you by name.

Question 6 · proposed parts in the first catalogue

  1. Do the five dashboard parts still marked as proposals go into the proof-of-concept catalogue?

    Recommend: yes, all five, marked as proposals, with every screen's record naming any proposal it used. Because the June dashboard cannot be drawn without the page shell and the layout, and the proof of concept never leaves your machine.

    Each of these still carries "your eye owed" in its notes. Putting them in the proof of concept is not a ruling on them. For the KPI tile in particular, your words still stand: "might be a partially built trend card with option, I haven't decided yet". Any catalogue that leaves your machine would carry settled parts only.
