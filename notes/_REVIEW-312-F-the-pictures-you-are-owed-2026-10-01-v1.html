01 · Container types · your call

Keep two container words: section, a named region of the page that a screen reader lists, and panel, as it is defined today. Drop division and sector.

"So extending this do we need a series of container types like section, division, sector, panel, etc"Your page note, Tuesday 29 September, 18:56

Section and panel drawn on the banking demo. Orange is a section, blue is a housing (the tile, with its cell written as columns by rows), the dashed line is the frame. Today the demo marks every tile as a named section: three of its four named sections are tiles, and the three bento groups are plain unnamed boxes. The recommendation turns that round. The page header and each bento group become the sections, and tiles stop being landmarks.

  • SectionA page region with a name. A screen reader lists it, so a person can jump to it. On a dashboard: the page header and each bento group.
  • PanelStands as defined: a sheet that docks beside the page and narrows it, rather than floating over it.
  • Division, sectorNo behaviour of their own. A division is any box, which every part already is; a sector is a section by another name. Two words with nothing to do become two words people use differently.
  • What it changesTiles stop being named regions, so the demo's screen-reader list drops from tile by tile to four regions. Closes the container-types question from your cards-and-tiles page.

Which container words stand?

Recommended: section and panel; drop division and sector.

A check that counts a dashboard's tile groups reads the three navs as one group, because they answer the same question: where am I, where can I go. Drawn both ways on the dashboard template. Left: the navs are frame, and the wall has three groups, as the designer drew them. Right: the navs counted as a fourth group. The check is built and held until you answer.

08 · The list rule, worked on its own · your call

A list is the default, and it may be searched, filtered and sorted. The table takes over when a field is read down a column; the data grid only when rows are selected in bulk or edited in place.

"we need to revisit this rule, lists can be filtered and resorted, and the general preference is for lists in most cases, lets work on this rule on it's own."The loose-ends page, 28 September

The banking demo already works the way you said. Its payment activity tile is a list under a toolbar: a search box, a market filter, a currency filter, a credits-or-debits switch, a chip for each filter you set, and pages. Twelve payments, six to a page; filtered to Europe it shows four. The sentence you rejected would have made that tile a data grid. Below, the same twelve payments in the three shapes, drawn on canon as it stands.

  • Option 1 · toolbar does not change the shapeA list is the default for records of one kind, each read across its own row. It may be searched, filtered, sorted and paged from a toolbar; the toolbar does not change what it is. The table takes over when a field must be read down a column across records. The data grid takes over only when rows are selected in bulk or edited in place. This is what the demo does, and it keeps the line to the table you already ruled: read across, list; read down, table.
  • Option 2 · sort is the lineSearch and filter belong to the list; sorting by a field means reading it down a column, so a sorted list becomes a table. It would push a "newest first" list into a table, the case you said you prefer as a list.
  • Option 3 · list until it is editedThe list for everything but in-place editing. Your instinct at full strength; the cost is a checkbox column on list rows, which is a column read down, so bulk selection starts to look like a grid anyway.
  • Why option 1The grid is kept for the two things a list cannot honestly do: choose many rows at once and change a value where it sits.
  • What it changesThe list's rule loses "needs = none"; the data grid's narrows to "select in bulk or edit in place". Closes the list rule you asked to work on its own.

Which wording stands?

Recommended: option 1, the toolbar does not change the shape.
