Session 311 · review page · Why the night was slow

Your Mac runs one command at a time. Twelve lanes queued for it, and the queue ate their clock.

Both faults are proven and measured. The cut calls were not cut short; they waited in a single-file queue for the seat and were given the minutes left on a 180-second clock that started when they joined it. The lock is older than last night: the mounted folder refuses every delete, so git cannot remove a lock it took and did not use, and it has left 948 of them behind since session 263.

Thursday 1 October 2026 Your words at 08:04: "we need to understand what went wrong this will be a constant issue otherwise". Measured from the 2,182 seat calls in the overnight transcripts, the lane reports, a seat test at 07:09 and a lock test in a throwaway repo. Nothing in the product was changed.

01 · What happened · the timeline

From 21:08 UTC the seat had twelve lanes asking for it at once. Every call then spent about two minutes queueing and one call in eight was cut.

Until 21:00 one or two lanes ran at a time and a typical call returned in under a second. Then the overnight wave started twelve lanes together. The median call went from 0.7 s to 120 s of wall time. 143 calls were cut with between 1 and 152 seconds of run time, 38 never ran at all, and the commit script, which needs 81 to 165 uninterrupted seconds, landed 23 of 147 attempts. Lane H1 held the commit lock for three hours over nineteen tries. The last cuts came at 03:27, when only two lanes were left and both were rendering.

  • Calls2,182 device_bash calls from the conductor and 30 lanes, 30 Sep 18:23 to 1 Oct 04:44 UTC.
  • Cut143 cut by the wall. Their run time before the cut: a quarter under 10 s, half under 32 s, three quarters under 70 s, 128 under 100 s. Their wall time from submission: 178.7 to 182 s in 142 of 143.
  • Never ran38 returned "this call waited (behind another command for the same session, or for the workspace) until its time ran out. Nothing was executed."
  • Commits23 of 147 runs of _git_commit.sh landed. 41 were cut mid-run, 14 refused to stage because a lock was in the way, the rest were honest gate refusals. A clean run needs 81 to 165 s with the seat to itself (median 87 s).
  • Queue costAbout 44 lane-hours were spent in flight beyond the solo norm while four or more lanes were calling. The seat itself can only have executed 6.5 hours of work in that window, because it runs one command at a time.
  • Also21 calls failed with "Service Unavailable: server draining" between 21:41 and 03:15: the cloud end restarting, not the seat. Not part of either fault.

02 · The cut calls · proven

The seat runs one command at a time, and a call's 180-second clock starts when it joins the queue, not when it runs. Twelve lanes meant two minutes of queue before every command.

Nothing was cut at 4 seconds. A call that reported "timed out after 4,928 ms" had a wall time of 180.5 s: it waited 175.6 s behind other lanes' commands, then ran for the 4.9 s left. The sum of run time and queue wait is 180 s in all 143 cut calls. The 38 that "never ran" are the same thing with nothing left. Lane CS measured it live: a bare sleep 35 was killed at 23.6 s. The seat was never slow; it was busy with someone else.

This morning's test at the seat, alone, says the same from the other side. One sleep 150 ran to completion. Two issued together ran one after the other: the second started one second after the first ended and also ran its full 150 s, 301 s after it was asked for. Four sleep 40 issued together ran strictly in series, the last finishing 162 s after submission, none cut. From one lane the calls are issued one after another and the clock starts at execution; from twelve lanes the calls arrive together, the server queues them, and the clock starts at submission. Either way: one command at a time.

The number that follows is two. A commit run needs about 90 uncontended seconds and a render 60 to 160. With one other long call ahead of you the wait is survivable; with two it is not. Measured: with three or fewer lanes calling, 1 call in 539 was cut; from four lanes up the cuts begin; at eleven and twelve, one in eight was lost and the median call waited two minutes. Last night eight of the twelve lanes (H1, H2, C0, CS, E0, D1 to D3, B5) were reading and writing text; they did not need the seat's shell for anything but reading files and landing them.

Which fix?

The recommendation is first. The cap is measured, not chosen.

Technical
  1. The server's own wording, from 38 results: "device_bash did not run: this call waited (behind another command for the same session, or for the workspace) until its time ran out. Nothing was executed; it is safe to run it again." The queue is per session or per workspace: all 30 lanes were subagents of one session on one linked computer, so the two cannot be told apart from last night's data. Whether a second claude.ai session linked to the same Mac gets its own slot is untested.
  2. Arithmetic, all 143 cut calls: wall 178.7 to 182.0 s (142 of 143); reported run time 1.08 to 178 s; timeout_ms asked for made no difference (asked 20,000 to 178,000, or none). Wait = wall − run time. First eight cut calls: wait 0.7, 71.4, 172.4, 101.7, 175.6, 128.3, 139.4, 98.9 s.
  3. Seat test, 1 Oct 07:09 to 07:20 UTC, lane X alone, timeout_ms 178000 on every call. N=1: start 07:09:09, end 07:11:39. N=2 issued in one message: a 07:11:45 to 07:14:15, b 07:14:16 to 07:16:46. N=4 × sleep 40 in one message: starts 07:17:24, 07:18:05, 07:18:45, 07:19:26, each ending 40 s later. N=8 was not run: the N=2 and N=4 shape already shows the single-agent client serialises its own parallel calls and starts the clock at execution, so an N=8 from one agent cannot reproduce the overnight queue; that needs calls from several agents at once, which this lane cannot issue.
  4. The lane reports agree: CS "every device_bash call was being cut at about 15–25 s regardless of timeout_ms (a bare sleep 35 was killed at 23.6 s)"; H2 "calls cut at 1.8–33 s while queued behind another command for the same session"; E0 "the shared device shell gave this lane 2–40 s a call (queued behind eleven lanes)".
  5. Commit runs, overnight: 147 attempts, 23 landed, 41 cut by the wall mid-run, 14 "could not stage" (a lock in the way), the rest gate refusals. H1 held /tmp/apollo-commit.lock 22:10 to about 01:26 over nineteen attempts (H2's report); H2 waited for it 23:20 to 02:29.
  6. Whether device_stage_files and device_commit_files share the bash queue is untested; the recommendation assumes they do not, and the conductor should test it with two agents before the next wave.

03 · The lock · proven

The mount refuses every delete, and git releases an unused lock by deleting it. So every no-op git command strands a lock, and every commit strands two.

Reproduced this morning in a throwaway repo on the mount, with a control in /tmp. A no-op git add, git add --dry-run, git status, git update-index --refresh, an empty git commit and a clean git reset all leave a 0-byte lock behind, with git's own warning "unable to unlink … Operation not permitted". A commit that lands leaves HEAD.lock and objects/maintenance.lock every time. The same commands in /tmp leave nothing. This is not a twelve-lane fault: the repo holds 948 moved-aside locks from 48 sessions, six of them from #310's quiet day. Twelve lanes only made it bite, because a lock one lane leaked is a fatal "File exists" for the next lane's git add: fourteen commit runs died that way.

The commit script already knows: clear_locks moves locks aside at five points and the commit line greps away "unable to unlink". But it also runs git status --porcelain after staging (line 984), stages every named path with a bare git add whether or not it changed (line 981), and between its clear_locks and its git add any other lane's git status can drop a lock. Moving the index out of the mount (GIT_INDEX_FILE) stops the index lock and nothing else: HEAD.lock and the branch lock still land in .git on every commit. The git reset -q that last night's bridge note recommends leaks two locks of its own.

Which fix?

The recommendation is first. It is the only one that reaches the cause.

Technical
  1. Mount: fuse (rw,nosuid,nodev,relatime,user_id=0,group_id=0,default_permissions,allow_other); rm z.tmp → "Operation not permitted"; mv and overwrite work. Git 2.34.1 at the seat.
  2. Why git deletes: a lock file is written beside its target and renamed over it on success; on "nothing changed" it is rolled back with unlink(). git add and git status write the index with SKIP_IF_UNCHANGED, so a no-op always takes the rollback path. After a commit, the symbolic HEAD.lock is always rolled back, and objects/maintenance.lock belongs to the post-commit auto-maintenance; both are unlinks. git add of changed content also leaves objects/*/tmp_obj_* files, which are harmless but accumulate.
  3. The throwaway repo: outputs/311/X/lockrepo (ignored tree); the probe moved each stranded lock aside after measuring it, into outputs/311/X/aside-*. Full probe output in notes/_lanes/311/X/lock-probe.txt.
  4. Stranded locks on record: 377 in notes/_lanes/_orphan-locks/ (by session: 263 ×6, 294 ×18, 295 ×38, 296 ×30, 297 ×17, 298 ×3, 299 ×7, 300 ×4, 301 ×11, 302 ×9, 303 ×7, 304 ×18, 305 ×5, 306 ×21, 307 ×26, 308 ×66, 309 ×29, 310 ×6, 311 ×50), 561 in _to_delete/_stale_locks/, 10 in .git/_stale-locks/.
  5. In the overnight transcripts: 11 × "fatal: Unable to create '…/.git/index.lock'", 13 × "could not stage … git refused it", 4 × "lock survived the mv-aside — do NOT stage".
  6. What device_request_delete_permission changes (not called by this lane): one approval for the connected folder root enables unlink/rmdir under its whole subtree for the rest of the session, from the next device_bash call. It does not survive the session, so it belongs at the opener. The commit script's own rm -f "$L" fallback (line ~304) would then also work.

04 · What else the night taught · seven rules

Seven one-line rules for the parallel-conductor runbook.

  1. A chain conductor with no Agent tool is a worker: before spawning anything, ToolSearch select:Agent; if it is absent, hand back in one line and do not take the brief.Chains 1 and 3 both opened with "this seat has no Agent tool" and none of their lanes ran until the conductor relaunched them at 22:15; the night lost its first hour.
  2. The pre-push survey runs the committed tree first, without --include-mutating, exactly as CI reads it; the mutating pass is the second run, never the only one.With --include-mutating step [118] rebuilds the memento index before [119] checks it, so the check always passes locally; CI does not ask [118] and went red on 69848bbe for the stale index C0's 70 meta changes left. Lane K's fix: efd0e89f.
  3. The seat takes one command at a time: never put more than two lanes on it, and never ask it for anything a cloud shell can do.Section 02. The twelve-lane wave spent about 44 lane-hours queueing for 6.5 hours of seat time.
  4. The seat-wide commit mutex is one empty directory; a holder that cannot finish inside one call says so and releases; a holder older than 30 minutes is removed by whoever finds it.Markers inside /tmp/apollo-commit.lock made rmdir fail, so the stale rule could not be applied; it was held past 30 minutes three times and lanes committed around it.
  5. Never commit knowledge/_state.json whole from a lane: name rows, or leave the store to the one committer.A7's d1901fa2 carried E0's, F2's, H1's and CS's uncommitted rows into HEAD before their reports existed.
  6. Read git without locking: git diff --quiet, git diff-index --quiet HEAD, git ls-files -m -o, git log, git rev-parse. git status locks, including the one on line 984 of the commit script.Measured in section 03.
  7. "Service Unavailable: server draining" is the cloud end restarting: wait a minute and resend the same call; do not diagnose the seat.21 calls, 21:41 to 03:15, in bursts.
