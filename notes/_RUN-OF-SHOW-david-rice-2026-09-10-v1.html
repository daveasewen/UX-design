07 · Dead air A

Two minutes, what inspired it. Beats, in order: the first version was a bespoke pipeline, and abandoning it was the good decision. On 2 July we archived the runtime and ratified the pivot: generation is a commodity, and this is the layer around any generator. Within hours of that we pointed the engine at GOV.UK, a completely different design system, and it still worked. So it was never about writing a better generator — it was about writing down what we already know well enough that a machine can be held to it.

Sources: docs/decisions/ADR-0005-ratify-knowledge-engine-pivot.md; second-system-govuk/, git 2026-07-02; README: “Generation is a commodity — this repo is the layer around any generator.”

08 · Dead air B

Ninety seconds, how it developed. Four acts: June, we took the inventory — twenty-odd components with criteria in a week, then the checks acquired the power to withhold “done”. July, the pivot, and the project started keeping a record that could prove its own honesty. August, it became a product with releases — ten of them since 26 August. September, one sentence: get the dashboard one-shottable. That is the sentence you are watching run.

Sources: git 2026-06-17/18/19 and 2026-06-22→29; docs/decisions/ADR-0007-project-memory-decision-graph.md; apollo-spider/dist/; _DECISION-HISTORY/2026-09-05-246-the-library-dictates.md (Dave: “the focus is getting the dashboards one-shotable.”).
