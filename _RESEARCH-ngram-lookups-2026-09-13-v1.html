01What it is

An n-gram is a short run of adjacent things — two words in a row (a word bigram), or three letters in a row (a character trigram). An n-gram index is a lookup table from each run to the records that contain it.

Why it matters here: the record is made of sentences you spoke. Sentences carry their meaning in word order, and a bag of single words throws the order away. A phrase lookup keeps it.

Two families, two jobs. Word n-grams answer "who said these words in this order". Character n-grams answer "what word did he mean" — they survive typos, plurals and hyphens because most of the letter-runs still match.

What it is not. It is not a vector database and not an LLM. It is exact, cheap, explainable, and it refuses honestly — the same fail-loud contract the spine already keeps.

02How Memento matches today

Substring, then count. The spine lowercases the record, splits the query into single words, and scores a record by how many words appear anywhere in it. Word order is invisible; "six weeks" scores the same as a record that says "six" on line 3 and "weeks" on line 90.

Spelling is handled by chopping. "fonts" finds "font-size" because the first six letters are tried as a substring. A single-letter slip ("ratifed") finds nothing.

Synonyms are hand-fed. The lexicon grows on miss, one entry at a time, 93 lines today. Nothing proposes entries.

Nothing in the repo uses n-grams. A grep for n-gram, BM25 or tf-idf across the Python and notes returns zero. This is untouched ground, not a rebuild.

Six uses, ranked by weight

1 · GATE

Quote verification — "are these his words?"

Before a lane, a wrap or a deck card presents a sentence as yours, look the phrase up verbatim. Found: green with file and line. Not found: red, with the nearest real phrase. This is the instrument for the conflated-fix law — it tests against your own words instead of a memory of them.

Measured: "6 month project may become 6 weeks" — 2 records. My paraphrase "six month…" — 0. "we are getting somewhere" — 8. Brute-force over 24.6M chars is already fast enough; a suffix array is not needed at this size.
2 · RANKING

Phrase-aware ranking

Score a record higher when the query's words appear adjacent, not just present. "six weeks on the clock" should surface the five records that say it before the 44 that merely contain both words.

Measured: 434,902 distinct word bigrams over the corpus, built in 2.9 s. Runs on the same spine, so _consult.py (the Apollo door) gets it for free.
3 · TOLERANCE

Typo and spelling tolerance

Index the 18,650 words the corpus actually uses as character trigrams. A misspelt query word is mapped to its nearest real word before search runs — silently, with the substitution shown in the receipt.

Measured on four slips: descendor→descender, salutaton→salutation, ratifed→ratified (clean); chromuim tied with "chromatic" (would need a second signal — edit distance — to break the tie).
4 · DREAM PASS

Near-duplicate detection for consolidation

Eight-word shingles and a Jaccard score find records that say the same thing twice. The dream pass could open with a measured list of candidates to merge instead of reading the archive by eye.

Measured: 22 pairs above 0.5 overlap among GM/LS archive sections and briefs — e.g. the 08-28/08-29/08-30 GM batches at 0.56–0.58. Candidates only; the merge stays yours.
5 · KG

Edge candidates with a measured overlap

At #267, 52 of 78 generated edges were wrong. Shingle overlap between two nodes is a cheap, explainable signal to rank candidates before they reach you — a number on each proposed edge, not a guess.

Not measured this session; the same shingle code as use 4 applies to pattern-node and context-node records (602 of them). Advisory only — never runs gen_kg_edges.py on its own.
6 · LEXICON

Synonym suggestions from co-occurrence

When two words keep appearing in the same bigram neighbourhoods ("descender" / "crop", "amber" / "ochre"), propose them as lexicon entries. Grow-on-miss becomes grow-on-evidence, with you still the one who accepts.

Not measured; lowest weight. Worth doing only once uses 1–3 are on the spine, because it reuses their index.

Comparison

Keep it on the spine, in pure Python, at this corpus size.

The internet-scale tools are the wrong size. Infini-gram and its FM-index successor index trillions of tokens with suffix arrays; they exist so researchers can count phrases across the whole web. Our corpus is 25 MB. A Python dictionary built in three seconds at index time does the same job with no new dependency — which matters because _search_core.py ships byte-identical inside the memento-package.

Hybrid retrieval literature agrees on the shape. The 2026 guides on RAG retrieval put character n-gram overlap alongside BM25 as a lexical signal and fuse the rankings. We do not need the dense-vector half: the record is small, exact, and the fail-loud contract wants explainable hits, not similarity scores.

Today — substring spine
Single words, any position, count the matches. No phrase, no typo tolerance, no way to prove a quote is verbatim. Fast enough already.
Proposed — n-gram layer on the same spine
Word bigrams for phrase rank and quote proof; character trigrams for spelling; shingles for near-duplicates. One index build step, one file, no external library, same two-stage refs-then-fetch shape.

"Retrieval-not-recall now reaches the project's own memory: quote the record, never reconstruct it." — the spine's own docstring. An n-gram lookup is the first instrument that can check the quote.

Costs and cautions

  1. Index growth (estimate). 435K bigram keys plus postings will add roughly 5–15 MB on disk to a 25 MB index; needs one measurement in _build_memento_index.py before it is a number.
  2. The quote gate must be advisory first. A gate that goes red on every legitimate paraphrase would block wraps. It should report, not refuse, until its false-red rate is seen on a real session.
  3. The lexicon file is hand-authored by rule. Use 6 may only propose entries into a review page; it never writes the lexicon.
  4. Nothing here touches the KG generator. Use 5 ranks candidates; it does not create or ratify edges.

Next step
