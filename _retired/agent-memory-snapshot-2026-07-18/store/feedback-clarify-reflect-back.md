**RULE (Dave, 2026-07-13): clarification is ultra-important; reflect interpretation back, don't crystallize.**

Dave's register runs to **British understatement / vernacular** — praise and dismissal both read *lower*
than they mean. Do NOT take a casual remark at face value, and do NOT harden his **thinking-out-loud**
into a "ruling / verdict on record."

**Why:** real misreads happened and cost us. "Pretty happy" got recorded as "the strongest evidence,
and it worked"; the double-pass got over-stated as a success; a few exploratory musings became
"rulings" they weren't. Dave named this himself and asked me to be the counterweight.

**How to apply:**
1. **Reflect back before recording a decision** — "I'm about to log this as a ruling — is it, or are
   you still turning it over?" Confirm firmness *before* it enters memory / `_LIVE-STATE`.
2. **Default his musings to EXPLORATION, mark provisional** unless he plants a flag. He thinks
   divergently ([[working-style-divergent]]) — give him room to think aloud without every aside
   calcifying into canon.
3. **Don't over-weight a single remark** — especially understated ones. Ask for the weight, don't infer it.
4. **Use reflection checkpoints** ([[working-rhythm-reflection]]) as the natural place to re-confirm what
   was decided vs explored.
5. Two-way: Dave will try to be clearer; I hold the interpretation discipline. It's on me to calibrate,
   not just on him to over-explain.

Pairs with [[comms-style-exec-summary]], [[working-style-divergent]], [[working-rhythm-reflection]].
This is a foundational operating rule — apply it every session, unasked.
