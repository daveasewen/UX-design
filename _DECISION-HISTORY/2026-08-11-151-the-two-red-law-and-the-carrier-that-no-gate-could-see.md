## ① The premise Dave withdrew, and the law that replaced it

The queue item asked which of three narrowings applied to "red text is used exclusively with dark
ink". Dave's answer was that the sentence itself was wrong. What he ruled instead — `s151-D1`, and
he called it *"final solution"*, so it is FIRM — is **background-keyed, not usage-keyed**:

- **`#DA1A00` on white**, for text *and* atoms, and
- **`#F6604C` everywhere else**.

Two reds, and which one you use is decided by what is behind it. Nothing about the *kind* of
element decides it.

Two riders came with the law and they are the part most likely to be mis-carried later:

- **Coloured text is confined to monetary values carrying a symbol.** Labels are always default
  ink. Status-seated symbols are default ink.
- **The governing standard is *"colour alone must not carry meaning"*, not a blanket 4.5:1.** This
  matters because it is what a gate can actually check, and #151 built the gate that checks it.

★ **The lesson worth keeping: the option set was well-formed and still wrong, because all three
options inherited a premise from the session that raised them.** A queue item ages into a premise;
the premise ages faster than the rule [[premise-ages-faster-than-rule]].
