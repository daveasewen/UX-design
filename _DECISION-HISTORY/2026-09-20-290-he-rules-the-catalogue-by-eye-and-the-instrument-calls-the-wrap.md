## 1. Why there is a dossier for a session with zero lanes

A short Sunday. Nine sentences from Dave, one commit, no delegated seats. The mechanical account
fits in a banner. What does not fit is **why the session ran the way it did**, and there are three

## 2. The catalogue: four notes, four one-constant changes, and one of them was a mis-diagnosis

His first note bundles two asks — *"one page is upside down, and it would be better is it wasnt
flat maybe a 60 degree angle"*. The interesting half is the first one, because **the page was not
upside down in the sense the word implies**: the left leaf had been generated by rotating the
right leaf 180°, which flips its text as well as its position. The fix is not to un-rotate it but
to **MIRROR it across the spine** — which is what an open book actually does, and which reads as
"the right way up" without any rotation being involved at all. The lesson is the usual one in this
family of drawings: *the defect the eye reports and the defect in the construction are different
objects*, and the second is the one you change.

His third note is the only one that offered a choice: *"the pages seem to turn up in the middle,
either this should be on the outer edges, or bent into the book at the middle"*. Both are legal.
The conductor took **bent into the gutter**, for a reason that is physical rather than aesthetic:
an open book's leaves are held at the spine and fall away from it, so they dip toward the gutter,
not away from it. The page block was rebuilt as two convex prisms sharing the plane `x = Lg`,
`CURL` at 5.6, with the head and tail leaf-edge lines following the dip so the stack reads as
paper rather than as a solid.

One thing was **tried and removed**: a dashed hidden outline of the wedge stand behind the book.
It is a legitimate technical-illustration convention and it fought the grid — two competing line
weights in the same region, both dashed, neither dominant. It is recorded here because a reverted
experiment that nobody names is indistinguishable from one that was never tried, and the next
person to think "the stand should show through" deserves to know it was attempted.

## 3. Why this was done in seat, and why that is published rather than smoothed

The delegation rule (`s204-D1`, restated at #284) says this work goes to a lane. It did not. The
conductor's stated reason, carried verbatim rather than paraphrased into a justification: **each
of Dave's notes was a one-constant geometry change with him waiting on the render, and the whole
exchange cost less than one lane brief.** That is a real argument — a lane brief for "rotate this
by 60 degrees and re-render" is more expensive than the edit — and it is also exactly the argument
that, accepted silently, would erode the rule.

So the session's departure is **declared as a departure**, in the banner, the delta, the stratum
and the carry set. The question of whether a *"one-constant edit with Dave waiting"* exception
should exist is put to Dave as a question, not answered here. Until he speaks, the rule stands as
written and #290 is a recorded exception to it rather than a precedent for it.
