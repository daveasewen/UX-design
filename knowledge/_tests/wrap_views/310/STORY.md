## @why
### 1. The arrow was answered by the drawing he already had
`_HANDOFF-160` put the arrow's shape first. The conductor asked the plain question, thin arrow or the filled triangle back, and he answered in one line: *"the rhin arrow from the icon assets"*. Before inscribing it the seat checked what "from the icon assets" meant on disk: the thin arrow is the library's `direction` set, and Metric and the banking demo already carried its paths byte for byte, from lane D's move at #309. So the ruling moved nothing. The render that proved it also found a page where the arrow draws nothing at all: the receipt page names a symbol it never defines. That was left as a found item rather than fixed on the spot, because it sits with the older looks on the same page (W-309g4).
### 2. Colour means news, not direction
The second call was which metric should be the first "up is bad" one. The conductor named Net FX exposure: exposure is risk, so its fall is good news. His answer gave the reason as well as the yes: *"its positive and negative news, not up and down"*. That sentence is the rule `s310-D2` carries. The arrow keeps pointing the way the number moved; the colour says whether that was good. It was built in the seat with one class on one tile, and the glyph colour was measured before and after in both modes.
### 3. The dark ground: measure first, then ask
His #309 note said there was no tile colour in dark mode. The seat measured it rather than taking the complaint as the diagnosis, and he was right: in every theme the ground and the tiles were the same colour. The review page showed today, his reading (dark grey ground, black tiles) and the reverse, side by side. He liked both: *"lets have the mirror as default but the other is an option both are valid"*. That made the job bigger than a colour change. A second reading needed somewhere to live, and there was no register of page-level options, only component attributes. Lane A put it in the theme register, under a new `options` key, because that is the file the cascade and every theme picker already read. The fork-ban gate then read the option as sixteen forks. Lane A did not add sixteen entries to the fork ledger, because the ledger is for forks nobody has ruled; it taught the gate that a registered option is a sanctioned axis, and made the test bite both ways.
### 4. What black tiles broke, and how each was put to him
Black tiles changed 51 of 137 components in dark, and three consequences were not his to guess. Supercharge's pressed and active tiles had always been its darkest colour, which was now the resting tile, so a press showed nothing. White text on pure black contradicted the brand's own note about digital black. And tab strips, which paint the tile colour, now drew a black band on the page. Each went onto lane A's review page as one call with a recommendation. His answers split them: the pressed tile moved one step lighter *"for now"*, because he has specs he cannot reach; the option's name took a plain yes; the white ink and the tab strip he wanted to see, not approve on a sentence.
### 5. The white ink: his instinct, then the repo's own tool
His 16:24 line guessed the answer: *"we can avoid halation by choosing a different white ink maybe"*. Lane B built one page with five inks and the strip on four grounds. At 16:56 he pointed at a tool already in the repo, the RAG bloom model, and the conductor ran it: the ink is the lever, the tile barely moves the bloom (pure white scores 29 on black or grey tiles, #E1E1E1 scores 16.5). That settled why the recommendation kept his black tiles and softened the ink instead of reversing his earlier choice. Supercharge keeps its own warm white, because following to its step 12 would drop the contrast to about 11:1. The strip was recommended to take its container's colour, because it banded in light too, wherever it sat on anything but a tile; light only looked right because its page is also white. He took both recommendations at 17:35, and his page note, *"i need to see this"*, means neither stands until he has looked at it built.
### 6. The red and the fill: the conductor skipped its own checks
The one CI red was the conductor's push, not a lane's: two rulings inscribed without regenerating the graph's node titles, pushed without the pre-push check that `s309-D7` had just written for lanes.
