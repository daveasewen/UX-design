Colour
We use colour to create brand awareness, recall, visual balance and consistency across all our media and touchpoints.

Key takeaways
HSBC uses white and black, applying HSBC red tactically, in order to draw the user's attention to drive their journey
When combining or layering colours, eg text or icons on a background, ensure correct colour contrast is met
Use the correct colour palette for the right use case, eg use the illustration palette only for illustrations, data vis palette only for charts and graphs

Usage
Colour can be used creatively in a number of different ways. We can create a world that represents our brand through introducing our colours to backgrounds and illustrations, and we can use textured colours in photography.
Our colour palettes act as the ‘visual glue’ that helps to deliver consistency and provide familiarity across an increasing number of channels and touchpoints.

Colour palette types
We have a range of colour palettes that we use for our brand communications:
Brand palette: made up of Core, Complementary Red, and Complementary Grey palettes
Illustration and Complementary Illustration palettes: for creating illustrations
Data Visualisation palette: for representing data, eg in graphs
RAG palette: for communicating status or severity levels in UX journeys through the red – amber – green traffic light system

Brand palette
Our brand palette is made up of three colour palettes: Core, Complementary Red, and Complementary Grey.
Core palette
This palette is made up of our most recognisable and distinctive colours and is used for all our communications.
Complementary Red palette
These reds are used to support the Core palette and to add more depth across our owned channels.
Complementary Grey palette
The greys in this palette support all our palettes and add depth and clarity.
More about the Brand Colours

Illustration colour palette
Illustration colour palette
The colours of the main Illustration palette were chosen to create harmonious compositions with the Brand and Complimentary Red palettes and should be the main colours used.
Complementary Illustration palette
The Complementary Illustration palette is used to depict a diverse range of skin and hair tones. It can also be used more widely in our compositions where appropriate, for example, illustrating coffee or a mountain.
More about the Illustration Colours

Data visualisation and RAG colours
Data Visualisation palette
These colours are used only for graphical representations of data.
RAG palette
These colours (red, amber, green and the additional blue) are based on the traffic light system and are used in that very functional manner – to communicate warnings, or status or severity levels within data visualisation and messaging.
More about the data visualisation colours
