Typefaces

A cohesive, multilingual font system that stays clear, accessible and recognisably HSBC in every market.

Univers Next for HSBC
Univers Next for HSBC is our latin brand typeface, built on the Univers typeface designed by Adrian Frutiger. A neo-grotesque, sans serif typeface known for its clarity and modernity. Univers Next carries that disciplined approach forward, giving HSBC a confident, highly-legible voice across channels and platforms. [Download Univers Next]

MYingHei
Available in Traditional and Simplified Chinese cuts, MYing Hei is HSBC's Chinese typeface, chosen for its clean, contemporary forms and strong on-screen legibility. It's designed to visually align with Univers Next for HSBC – matching overall tone, stroke weight, and proportion – so Chinese and Latin text sit together naturally in the same layout. This pairing helps us maintain a consistent hierarchy and a recognisably HSBC look and feel across channels and markets. [Download MYingHei]

Univers Arabic for HSBC
We use Univers Arabic for HSBC, selected for clear, confident readability and accurate Arabic script rendering across digital and print. It's designed to harmonise with Univers Next for HSBC by matching overall proportion and weight so Arabic and Latin content can sit together seamlessly in the same layout. [Download Univers Arabic]

Cyrilic [sic], Japanese, Armenian
We use a variety of other fonts for our communications in Cyrilic, Japanese and Armenian that are visually similar to Univers. [Download our full suite of fonts]

Portfolio of licensed fonts
Univers Next for HSBC can be used for multiple languages – you can find out which ones in the language support section.

Creative headlines

Visual guidance and examples for headline typography – covering size, weight, spacing, alignment and how headlines sit with imagery.

Principles for headlines

Elegant weights
Use the lightest weights (Ultra Light, Thin, Light) of type at larger sizes to create impactful headlines that still provide space and clarity to supporting images. Keep headlines concise, with comfortable letter spacing and clean line breaks, so they feel confident without overpowering the layout. Use heavier weights only when you need a clear hierarchy shift or a single point of emphasis.

Balanced dual language
Our creative often uses both Latin, Chinese and Arabic text to reflect the bank's international footprint and the heritage that connects East and West. When bilingual typography is used, we aim to give both languages clear presence and comparable prominence, so the message feels unified, rather than 'translated' or secondary. Layout, hierarchy, spacing and line length should be carefully balanced to maintain readability, to ensure the overall composition remains distinctly HSBC – confident, simple and inclusive across markets.

Give the imagery room to breathe
We position headlines to work with the image, not against it – using the type to frame and draw attention to the most important focal point. Place copy in areas of clean negative space, or along natural lines in the composition, so it guides the eye towards the subject rather than covering it. The goal is a clear visual path: headline first, then the key detail in the imagery, with both staying legible and uncluttered. We use grids and the rule of thirds to give focus to both the headlines and the focal point of an image.

Centre aligned: If the photography is more ambient and lacks a clear focal point, the Iconic Hexagon should be placed centrally. It can be used either with or without text.
Thirds intersection points: The Iconic Hexagon sits on intersection points to create visual balance. When positioned away from the centre, it should always be accompanied by text.

Types of headlines

Over photography: For use on content within the brand system, where brand cues are already established through Open or Cropped Hexagons, photography, or surrounding assets.

Magnetic headline (with Iconic Hexagon): We use large headlines, featuring an Iconic Hexagon, when the brand is a key part of the message, or when the channel needs the logo elevated into the communication so the brand presence is immediate and unmistakable. Eg in our advertising, or on the cover of a brochure.

Layout

Over photography
Placing creative headlines over photography can add energy and clarity. It needs to feel intentional with clear visual hierarchy so the headline lands fast, supports the story in the photo, and stays readable across channels and devices.
Watch-outs:
- Legibility first: Avoid busy areas, high-contrast edges and faces. Use safe placement, sufficient contrast and (where needed) a subtle gradient/overlay, rather than heavy effects.
- Respect the subject: Don't cover key focal points (especially faces), brand cues, or important contextual details – let the image do its job.
Layout considerations: Headline top left corner, focal point right · Headline aligned to right-hand third, focal point left third · Both headline and focal point aligned centrally.

With Open Hexagons
When type sits with Open Hexagons, it should feel integrated – using the shape to guide the eye and add structure – without turning the layout into a puzzle. Treat the Hexagon as a framing and flow device: it can anchor the message, create emphasis, and help organise information while keeping the headline effortless to read.
Watch-outs:
- Don't force the fit: Avoid squeezing or warping type to follow the hexagon edges. Keep type set cleanly and let the shape support it, not dictate it.
- Mind the collisions: Give the both [sic] the type and the Open Hexagon breathing room – those angles can create visual 'pinch points' that reduce legibility. The type shouldn't obscure the clarity of the Hexagon shape.
Layout considerations — outside the Open Hexagon: top left corner · aligned right third · centred. Inside the Open Hexagon: right third · two lines centred · two lines right third.
Don't position typography overlapping the Cropped Hexagon edge.
Don't go out of the safe area with the content.
Do place creative headline typography inside the Open Hexagon, but keep it simple and concise.
Don't place long-form copy inside the Open Hexagon.

With Cropped Hexagons
Using type alongside Cropped Hexagons brings pace and modernity to a layout. The crop creates tension and direction, so the typography should feel deliberate – anchored to the geometry, easy to scan and never competing with the cut.
Watch-outs:
- Avoid awkward tangents: Don't let letterforms or key words 'kiss' the crop edge. Keep clear spacing so the type doesn't look accidentally clipped or crowded.
- Protect readability near edges: Crops can create high-contrast intersections. Maintain strong contrast and consistent line spacing, and avoid placing small text where the crop makes the background visually noisy.
- Keep alignment intentional: Align type to the hexagon's dominant angles or a clean grid – random offsets can make the layout feel unbalanced and reduce hierarchy.
Layout considerations — outside the Cropped Hexagon: top left corner · top aligned to vertical centre · top right third. Inside: top left corner · bottom right corner · bottom right intersection.
Don't position typography overlapping the Cropped Hexagon edge as this will affect the legibility of the typography. Try to keep this minimal.
Don't squeeze the typography within the shapes provided by the Cropped Hexagon. Give both room to breathe.

Magnetic headline (with Iconic Hexagon)
Magnetic type is our headline construct where an Iconic Hexagon sits at the centre of the line, acting as the visual anchor for the message. The typography is 'magnetised' to this central hexagon, so the words before and after it feel intentionally pulled into place, creating a clear pivot point and balanced rhythm. This approach elevates the brand mark within the headline while keeping the overall composition clean, legible and tightly integrated with the layout.

Aligning to the Iconic Hexagon
Vertical alignment: Try to align headline text using the geometry of the Hexagon as a guide. Text can be either left aligned or right aligned to the 5 vertices of the Hexagon.
Horizontal alignment: The baseline of the headline text can also be aligned guided by the geometry of the Hexagon.
Examples: headline aligned left of Hexagon · bilingual headline left, second line right · top and bottom headlines aligned to Hexagon centre · aligned right · aligned to opposite points · aligned to vertical lines within the Hexagon.

Make the photography the hero
It's key to ensure that the subject of the photography is given space within the layout so the narrative is clear. The magnetic type can move accordingly to ensure that both the Headline construct and the photography have equal balance within the messaging. The magnetic layout can be centralised if the photography is a more ambient scene with no clear focal point. If the subject matter sits in the bottom left or top right corner, the stacked magnetic type can be used. The magnetic type can also be positioned on one side of the ad to allow for the subject of the photography to be clear.

Use of space
It's ideal when the Iconic Hexagon aligns with an intersection point, but this isn't a strict rule. Depending on the format, imagery, and headline length, exact alignment to the Rule of Thirds may not always be possible—however, it should still guide the overall layout.
Examples: aligned to central vertical line (top headline standing on the line, second hanging from it; ambient backgrounds) · both parts centrally aligned (ambient backgrounds) · left aligned top left corner · stacked on right third · centred over focal point · right aligned over left third · left aligned on right third · stacked at bottom right intersection.
