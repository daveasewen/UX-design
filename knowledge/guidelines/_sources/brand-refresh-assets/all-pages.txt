Logos (2026)

The HSBC logo symbolises strength, stability and global connectivity. Its bold geometric design reflects HSBC's commitment to clarity and trust in financial services.

Key takeaways
- Our logo is integral to our brand and has built trust with our audience.
- An HSBC logo should appear at least once, on every piece of HSBC communication or customer journey
- Always use original logos as supplied by the Global Brand Design team
- Our logos should never be recreated / edited and should always be used in their entirety
- Do not alter the sizing or positioning of the components
- Our logos must always be readable / legible
- If sharing with a third party, please follow the TMLA application process

The Hexagon isn't just our logo — it's a foundational creative device. It scales and flexes seamlessly across every platform and touchpoint, from the smallest favicon to bold, expressive hexagon-led layouts in campaigns and experiences. With its origins from our flag, the Hexagon provides a flexible and scalable construct which underpins the whole of the Create Design System. It informs our creative direction which, in turn, builds on consistency and trust over our 160+ year history.

Usage
- Across our branch and workplace network: signage, airports, high-visibility locations — the logo acts as a beacon of trust... [examples: Global headquarters · Wealth Centre reception · Branch]
- Marketing, events and social: instantly recognisable, builds trust, unifies communications. [examples: Global brand campaign · OOH · partnership events · social]
- Merchandising: reinforces identity and visibility internally and externally.
- Digital channels: the logo signals a trusted, secure environment. [examples: public websites · internal platforms · within email]

Types of logos

HSBC Masterbrand — the core symbol of our identity... used on all primary brand materials. Using the logo on all customer journeys ensures a consistent, recognisable brand experience.
- Masterbrand – full colour: our primary logo. Three colours: HSBC Red, black and white. For use on lighter-coloured backgrounds.
- Masterbrand – full colour negative: a variant of the primary logo. Two colours: HSBC Red and white. For use on dark-coloured backgrounds.
- Colour variants: single-colour version, Hexagon and logotype the same colour; version depends on legibility against the background colour or image. A white infill should not be used on these versions.
- Mono reversed: single colour. For use only in printing on darker backgrounds, images or on red.
- Mono: black. For use only in printing on lighter backgrounds; should not be used on HSBC Red.

Regional variations
- Non-English: translated/typeset for regional markets — Traditional Chinese · Simplified Chinese · Bilingual Traditional Chinese and English · Bilingual Simplified Chinese and English. Same sizing/positioning rules; only as supplied by Global Brand Design; TMLA for third parties.
- Masterbrand regional variations: adapted with additional text for a specific region/market — restricted to the examples shown, where there is a legal requirement.

Do try to use the full colour Masterbrand logo, giving prominence to the red and white hexagon.
Don't use a full colour logo on a red background, so the hexagon can't be seen.

HSBC proposition logos — identify specialist products/services (HSBC Asset Management · Private Bank · Life · Innovation Banking) under the Masterbrand umbrella. Do think about which audience you're targeting and what stage of the Brand Effect Model. Don't create a proposition logo outside of those agreed by Group Brand.

Further signposting — Identifiers: how we signpost products, departments and internal programmes, in support of the Masterbrand.

Brand Promise — 'opening up a world of opportunity', reflects purpose, strategy and values. It's MANDATORY at the Prime and Engage stages of communications. [Masterbrand + Brand Promise lock-up · Proposition + Brand Promise lock-up]

For use on third-party materials — protect customers and brand; name/logo used in line with reputational, brand, domain, trademark and digital accessibility procedures.

Brand Partnerships — all logos designed and managed by HSBC. Where HSBC is well known in the market, the Hexagon is used on its own; where less known, the full Masterbrand logo is adopted. [examples: LIV Golf · Rugby Sevens · Championships · Abu Dhabi Art Fair]

Resources — Masterbrand logo files (do not alter sizing relationship/positioning; TMLA for third parties) · End frames (logo + Brand Promise + sonic branding for video) · Brand architecture.

Photography (2026)

Our principal visual communication method, we use photography to tell authentic human stories and connect to our customers, clients and colleagues.

Key takeaways
- Our photography should frame stories with a keen eye, and portray the world in a cinematic way. It should be immersive, human and above all authentic.
- We use red tactically throughout, capturing warmth in the lens through to naturally in a scene
- We don't need to show people directly, but all images should be captured from a human perspective
- Always lead with photography as your principle [sic] creative medium
- Don't use overtly staged, posed or clichéd photography.
- Don't use Generative AI, mixed media or CGI rendered elements within your imagery

Our cinematic imagery feels curated rather than generic...

Do use photography to capture a moment or a scene to help provide a supportive narrative to your content.
Don't use overtly staged or posed shots – our photography should look naturally captured and authentic.
Don't use photography that is too vibrant in tone. Our colour palette in photography is muted and sophisticated.
Don't use mixed media or computer generated graphics or overlays.
Don't use montages or composite images made up of more than one shot.
Don't use text as a mask, as this will affect the legibility of the copy and obscure the image.

Types of cinematic shots
- Cinematic: elevating the everyday into carefully crafted, narrative-led visuals; considered lighting, composition, colour and grading.
- People: relatable, grounded, emotionally resonant; human perspective. Frame authentic human stories (real people, genuine moments, no artificial poses). Diversity and inclusion: wide range of backgrounds, ages, cultures; always show disability in a positive light. Natural warmth through the lens, especially skin tones and ambient light; red appears naturally in the scene (warmer tones), not as a forced accent.
- Landscapes and cityscapes: from landmark-led to human, cinematic perspectives; realistic lived-in viewpoints; natural framing, depth, atmospheric lighting. Wide immersive shots; real locations (no overly retouched/fantastical scenes); textural and architectural detail (light on glass, water, foliage, metals).
- Objects: lower down the funnel; beautiful curated depiction linking to products/services; storytelling objects with context/meaning; cinematic dramatic natural lighting; textural/premium quality, craft and detail.
- Textural: macro photography, abstract textural scenes, immersive close-ups, textural depth, authenticity (no artificial enhancements).
- Abstract: evokes emotion/atmosphere through texture and detail; sets the mood; based off photography (no forced/artificial elements); ambient and non-distracting.

Cultural relevancy — authentic moments and diverse stories worldwide: Europe and North America · Middle East, North Africa and Türkiye · Eastern Asia · Southern Asia · South America.

Usage — across all channels/platforms. Marketing (distinct cinematic style; see the We're There global brand campaign) · Internal comms · Digital channels (consistent link back to marketing).

Sourcing photography — Global Image Library 2026 first (pre-paid, sourced by Global Brand, diverse perspectives/abilities/values/cultures; licensed globally in perpetuity, any platform or channel; gaps → get in touch) → stock providers (Getty Images, royalty-free + research/lightbox: keyuk.service@gettyimages.com) → shoots/spec shoots (Untold Fable · La Plage).

Creative Hexagons (2026)

Our most recognisable asset sits at the heart of our visual identity, building trust and opening up opportunities in diverse and compelling ways.

Key takeaways
- Our Creative Hexagons should anchor our viewer, drawing focus directly to key moments in storytelling.
- The Iconic Hexagon should never replace the logo
- Ensure the hexagon shape remains recognisable – avoid overly obscuring or distorting it
- The Iconic Hexagon is now used at a smaller size than previously
- The Cropped Hexagon is available in two versions: one-edge and two-edge crops (previously three- and four-edge crops were also used)

As the HSBC Hexagon is our single most recognisable asset, it must be central to all our communications. It is the core DNA of our visual identity. The Creative Hexagon is derived from our logomark, but it is not the logomark itself — adapted and tweaked versions designed for more expressive and flexible applications while staying rooted in the core identity.

Types
- Iconic Hexagon: unedited, timeless, 'classic'; bold and confident.
- Open Hexagon: deconstructed; a simple but powerful canvas; can act as a window, framing content.
- Cropped Hexagon: abstract; a bold canvas framing assets (imagery, typography, illustration) while distinctly referencing the brand.

Usage — across every stage of the customer journey + internal platforms. The type depends on where the audience is in their journey: Iconic leads at the start; further along, shift to more abstract interpretations (Open or Cropped). The Brand Effect Model (BEM) breaks the journey into points of communication; platform-specific standards carry the per-touchpoint guidance.
Please note: Creative Hexagons should not replace our logo; they support it.

Per-channel notes: Airport advertising — Iconic always (outside the immediate HSBC environment). OOH — Iconic (broad audiences, public spaces). Social media — any type depending on use case. Emails — depends on email type. Zoom backgrounds — external-facing, further down funnel, any type. Credit/debit cards — card category determines Iconic vs Open. Branches/offices — Open or Cropped (careful with Iconic to avoid too many hexagons in one area).

Hexagon within a Hexagon — where brand equity is low (region/channel), the Iconic Hexagon can be included within the Open/Cropped visual. Does NOT replace the Masterbrand logo requirement. Open+Iconic balances expressive storytelling with brand presence; Cropped+Iconic combines dynamic partial framing with a recognisable anchor.

Graphic treatments — techniques applied to Iconic/Open/Cropped for visual interest when photography or illustration alone is not sufficient. Guidance on the 3D Hexagon, Graphic Hexagons, the Keyline Hexagon and other treatments "will be released soon". [SOURCE GAP]

Hexagons in motion — two motion types for Creative Hexagons: opening and advancing. [links to motion standard]
