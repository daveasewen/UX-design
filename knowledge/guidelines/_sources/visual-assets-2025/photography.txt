Key takeaways
Always lead with photography as your principle creative medium
Don’t use overtly staged, posed or clichéd photography. Our photography should be premium with a clear, single point of focus
Don’t use Generative AI, mixed media or CGI rendered elements within your imagery
Please review our Global Image Library, as well as pre-approved light boxes from our preferred vendors in the 'How to source photography' section, before buying any new images

Subject matter
Diversity and inclusion
We are an international bank reaching all corners of the world, we want to make sure our photography represents diverse perspectives, abilities, values, and cultures. HSBC’s global presence drives a need for ethnic representation in imagery, from the indigenous population in Canada, and Hispanic and African Americans in the USA, to the wide diversity of peoples and cultures across Asia and Europe. Our photography should cross borders and tailor to different markets.
Inclusion is in the DNA of our imagery; we want to show people from all backgrounds in their everyday lives. Celebrating diversity is when we celebrate what makes every person unique regardless of race, age, ability, gender, or sexuality.
Localisation: Regional sensitivities should be considered when curating images for use in all markets.
Do represent gender and age from all perspectives
Don’t show stereotypes or clichés
Do represent the LGBTQ+ community
Don’t show groups of people all from the same background, if possible
Do portray people with disabilities living their lives
Don’t make someone's perceived difference the focus of the image (eg, don't focus on a model's wheelchair)
Sustainability
Our photography is built around the story of helping businesses transition to a more sustainable future. It focuses on positive changes that communicate innovation, nature-based solutions and an optimistic and forward-facing future.
Do show the positive actions taken to combat climate change
Don’t show clichéd ‘green’ imagery
Do use well understood semiotics of sustainability
Don’t focus on the negative images associated with local sustainability

Creative considerations
Please note:
Including one or more of the below creative considerations makes our imagery distinctive, however it's not mandatory to do so.
Colour balance
Colour plays an important role in our photography. Choosing imagery with hints of red can reinforce our brand, making it distinctive and ownable. Photography can be manipulated to include red, but this shouldn't feel forced or unnatural.
Do include natural hints of red within photography
Don’t force red into the imagery where it feels unnatural
Use of angles
Reflecting the geometry of the Hexagon by integrating angles into photography will help create a distinctive look and feel. Angles can range from soft and subtle to more bold and overt, which creates dynamic and unexpected compositions.
Do show subtle angles through the subjects' stance, the landscape, architectural shapes and the treatment of light and shade
Don’t force a 45-degree angle – angles should always feel natural, subtle and never contrived
Playing with scale
To reflect our Brand Promise of ‘opening up a world of opportunity’, wider framed photography gives a sense of 'opening up' and a broader view on the world. Tighter crops and close-ups allow us to capture intimate moments and detail.
Do create a sense of space and allow the image to breathe when capturing wide frame photography
Do show close-ups – capture candid moments as if you were part of the scene

Things to consider when sourcing photography
Cropping
As well as having a central focal point for creating iconic hexagons, chose photography that can be cropped into different standard ratios that work with changing layouts and screen sizes.
Point of focus
By choosing simpler or more focused imagery, we can present clearer concepts. Point of focus can also be achieved by using colour, contrast and density within visual assets.
Do try to use simpler more focused image.
Don't use photography without a single point of reference.
Quality
Always use sharp, high quality visual assets. Users associate the quality of imagery with the quality of the product or brand. Visual assets should be optimised and tested for our supported devices, including both low and high definition screens.
If you don’t have the quality of assets required, it’s better not to use any visual assets at all rather than risk creating a bad impression of the product or brand.
Do use high quality images
Don't use images with poor resolution
Image types
Always use photography that is real, authentic and true to life. Avoid overly retouched images, the use of CGI and mixed media.
Do not use digitally manipulated photography
Do not used mixed media

How to source photography
If you've already looked through the Global Image Library and can't find the right image, your next step would be to reach out to our global stock image/footage providers. We've got existing relationships with EyeEm and Getty Images, who offer royalty-free photography and stock video footage, as well as research and lightbox creation services as part of our agreements with them. We've worked closely with each of these agencies to define global pricing models and each know our photography guidelines to help you find the images you need.
Our image partners
EyeEm – hsbc@eyeem.com
Getty Images – keyuk.service@gettyimages.com
Free image libraries
We don't use free image libraries or images under Creative Commons, as these can pose different forms of risk to HSBC and its reputation. Even though the images are marketed and sold as “free”, there are often restrictive conditions that wouldn’t allow us to use them. When these conditions aren't met, we’re breaching the licensing agreement.
The important thing to remember is that HSBC is a global corporation, passionate about giving back to the creative communities that serve us. By purchasing images through the correct channels, we ensure we’re sourcing images that reinforce our brand messaging, as well as ensuring that those photographers whose work we use are being paid and treated fairly.

Uploading your photography
Once you’ve downloaded your photography assets from our stock partners, please make sure you upload them to the Assets section of create.hsbc. By uploading the asset, you are helping to create a library of pre-purchased, on-brand photography that can be used by the rest of the business globally. It ensures we’re getting the most out of our investment and that we have a rich diverse library of images to pick from.
Hero images
If you want to restrict the usage of the image for a single product or service, think about where, when, and how long it’s going to be used for. If an image has been picked to act as a hero and you don’t want others in the bank to use it, make sure you enter it in the notes section of the metadata. You'll need to specify an end date for the hero exclusivity, after which time the image will be usable by the rest of the HSBC network.
