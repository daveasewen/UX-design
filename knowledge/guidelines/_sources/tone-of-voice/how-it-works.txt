How it works — Get started here, whether you're briefing someone, or starting a new project yourself.

== It all begins with a brief ==

Start with a good brief, and everyone – you, plus anyone reviewing and signing the work off – will know exactly what the point of your piece of writing is. A good brief leads to purposeful, relevant, concise writing. Jot down answers to these questions even without a formal brief. [Standard briefing form available.]

Ask yourself: who's the audience?
- Someone you know well? First communication? Internal, or customer, client or agency?
- If customers: Retail, WPB or Business Banking?
What channel are you using? Email, letter, text — or adapted for different channels?
What do you want them to know? What the reader absolutely NEEDS to know – not everything you want to tell them.
How do you want them to feel? Informed? Inspired? Valued? Reassured? (Never confused, angry, or ignored.)
What do we want them to do? If you want someone to do something, make that absolutely clear. Don't hide it at the bottom. Put it in plain sight.

**Only start writing when you have the answers to these questions.**

== Help with your writing ==

Always have the tone of voice principles to hand. Once you've got to a good place, use the tone of voice checklist to make sure your writing is measuring up.

== Measuring impact ==

Think about what you can measure to prove the difference tone of voice makes: click rates, open rates, NPS scores, call handling times — or something simple like showing the readability score improved.

Word's built-in readability checker: File > Proofing > Options > 'Check readability statistics' > OK. The number of interest is 'Flesch Reading Ease' — score out of 100, 100 most readable.

Type of writing → Score to aim for:
Internal email 60-70+
Customer email 60-70+
Customer letter 60+
Social media 70+
Report 50+
Technical report 45+
Ts and Cs 40+

== The review process: the how and the who ==

Make a list of who you need an okay from and agree who has the final say. **Factor in two to three days for each round of review.**
To get the most useful feedback, ask your reviewer to break comments into:
- content – what it says
- structure – how it's ordered
- tone – how it sounds.

If writing to customers, Compliance must review, with two things in mind: (1) does it protect customers and make sure they're fully informed? (2) is the bank guarded against claims that our communications are misleading or incorrect? Legal checks law/regulation compliance. [Cross-reference: communication review and approval requirements.]
