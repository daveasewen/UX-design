Preferred terms
Consistent language across our regions and products.

What are 'preferred terms'?
Our preferred terms help us to speak a common language across the entire group. It means our customers feel at home with HSBC, no matter where in the world they find themselves.

How to use and apply preferred terms
The bank’s Global Content discipline is responsible for maintaining and updating this guide. If you are a writer and would like to propose an update to this guide, please email customer.design.team@hsbc.com.

Core preferred terms
Please see our global English language core preferred terms list below.

international |
|
Take care when using terms such as
‘abroad’ and ‘overseas’. These are
acceptable in markets where they’re
commonly used, but ‘overseas’ in
particular can be problematic.
Would it make sense for a
land-locked country or region such as
Armenia, for example? And might a
customer living in England find
‘overseas’ confusing? After all,
Northern Ireland is part of the UK but
literally over the sea from mainland
Great Britain?
For clarity, use ‘outside the UK’ or
‘outside Malaysia’ etc (as appropriate
to the relevant market).
In EU markets and the UK, there are
also regulatory requirements as part
of the Payment Account Directive.
The HSBC UK glossary of terms on
this states: “Where possible, the
terms ‘outside the UK’ and ‘inside
the UK’ should be used to describe
geographical location.”

payment / payments and
transfers |
These terms should not
be used interchangeably. |
The terms 'payments' and 'transfers'
are often used interchangeably - but
they are slightly different.
Here are some guidelines to help
you use them correctly:
Transfers should be used when: you
send money to your or a payee's
personal account. It's an umbrella
term and can include payments -
all payments are transfers, but
not all transfers are payments.
Payments should be used when: you
send money to major businesses for
the purposes of paying bills (eg,
utility bills, credit card bills, loan
repayments) for the purpose of
paying for goods or services.
Refunds are considered a
payment as goods would often need
to be given back as well as part of
the exchange.
