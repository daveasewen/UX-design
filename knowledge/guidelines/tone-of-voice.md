# Tone of voice

> Source: create.hsbc brandhub (authenticated) — `foundations-and-identity/Tone_of_Voice.html`. Captured 2026-06-18 for RAG. Summarised, not verbatim. The four named principles + copywriting / how-to detail live on sub-pages (pull on demand — see below).

## In one line

Speak to customers **conversationally — the way we'd like to be spoken to**: understandable, warm and human.

## Key takeaways (operational rules)

1. **No jargon, not overly formal.** Keep language understandable, warm and human.
2. **Structure your copy.** Put the main point first and highlight what's important.
3. **Don't be vague — get to the point quickly.**
4. **Signpost clearly.** Give the reader clear next steps / solutions for what happens next.

## Brand narrative ("Opening up a world of opportunity")

HSBC's global reach is its unique strength and shapes how it thinks, acts and grows. The voice is **optimistic and open** — "difference doesn't faze us, borders don't stop us," at home in an ever-changing world. Writing should **connect people across boundaries, borders and experiences**, and read consistently whether it's an internal note, a customer message, a smartphone screen or a posted letter.

Use **inclusive language** to reflect HSBC's borderless approach to banking — connecting people, ideas and capital. (Detail on the Inclusive language sub-page.)

## For the copywriting pipeline

These takeaways are the actionable ruleset for generated/checked copy:
- prefer plain, warm, human phrasing over formal/technical;
- lead with the main point; front-load importance;
- be concise and specific;
- always tell the reader what happens next.

Pair with **UX copy** component-level rules (button labels, error messages, empty states) where those exist, and with the per-component `antiPatterns` (e.g. links: avoid "click here"; notifications: error text not colour alone).
