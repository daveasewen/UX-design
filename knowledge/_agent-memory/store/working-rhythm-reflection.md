Dave wants **reflection built into the rhythm of the work**, not left to memory.

Two standing habits:
1. **Proactively suggest reflection checkpoints.** Don't wait to be asked. At natural inflection points — after finishing a batch, before committing to a big new direction, and especially when I notice us in a "productivity bubble" (building satisfying things instead of proving value) — pause and offer a short, honest assessment. Name real risks, not just progress.
2. **End-of-session morning briefing.** When a session wraps, take the initiative to generate something that greets Dave in the morning: warm, but substantive — where we landed, the honest state of things, what's queued, and any open question. Distinct from a dry work-plan/brief; this is the human-facing "here's your day." **Delivery (Dave's choice): write it as a file to the workspace at session end** (e.g. `GOOD-MORNING.md` or dated) — reliable, ready when he next opens the folder. Not a scheduled task.

**Why:** Dave explicitly values the honest critique (e.g. the [[promenaut-fitness-test-plan]] pivot came out of one) and worries — rightly — about productivity-for-its-own-sake. He named these reflection points as "crucial" and wants them to become a habit on both sides.

**How to apply:** Be candid and willing to push back; he treats me as a peer giving a real view, not a cheerleader. Keep reflections concise and concrete. Greet in the morning with genuine warmth. Related: [[promenaut-project-status]].
